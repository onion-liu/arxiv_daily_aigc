eDMD: Endpoint Distribution Matching for Few-Step Diffusion Distillation

Distribution matching distillation (DMD) has emerged as a leading paradigm for distilling diffusion models into few-step students, where an auxiliary fake score model tracks the student's distribution and the discrepancy between the teacher and fake predictions drives the student toward the teacher. In multi-step distillation, although only the student's final output is delivered, existing methods train the fake model on intermediate states or across all denoising steps. Consequently, the student's update signal becomes entangled with intermediate distributions, which can distort the matching direction and remain nonzero even after the student's final outputs already match the teacher distribution. To resolve this mismatch, we propose Endpoint Distribution Matching Distillation (eDMD), which trains a single shared fake model exclusively on the student's final outputs and uses it to correct every denoising step. Our theoretical analysis proves that this endpoint reference guarantees that the distribution matching term vanishes if and only if the student's final output distribution matches the teacher's, regardless of intermediate distributions. Beyond the fake reference, we further strengthen teacher supervision through a precise teacher (PT) that replaces single-step guided predictions with short multi-step trajectories on the final noise interval, paired with a calibrated query density for intermediate steps. Experiments on SD3-Medium, FLUX.2-klein (4B and 9B), and Qwen-Image-2.1 demonstrate that eDMD consistently outperforms existing distribution matching methods for 4-step generation.

TLDR: eDMD improves few-step diffusion distillation by exclusively using the final output distribution as the matching target and introducing precise teacher supervision, leading to superior performance on large-scale models like FLUX and SD3.

TLDR: eDMD通过仅将最终输出分布作为匹配目标，并引入精确教师监督，改进了扩散模型蒸馏，在FLUX和SD3等大模型上实现了更高效的少步生成。

TokenStream: Multi-Subject Personalized Video Generation with Token-wise LoRA and Guided Attention

We introduce TokenStream, an optimization-based framework that composes independently learned subject identities in text-to-video generation without a reference-video training dataset or joint optimization of each new subject combination. Learning from a single reference image makes subject appearance difficult to separate from static-image bias, while merging independently trained adapters can mix identities. Token-wise LoRA (ToRA) restricts low-rank updates to the cross-attention value projections of subject-associated text tokens, providing a separate adaptation path for each subject. A T2V-guided stream copies queries and keys from the frozen base model during early denoising to reuse its layout and motion priors while retaining personalized values. We also introduce Multi-DB, a 30-subject, 122-prompt benchmark with appearance-free prompts and varied interactions. On Multi-DB at 1.3B, TokenStream improves identity preservation from 0.422 for DreamBooth to 0.467 and textual alignment from 0.652 to 0.688.

TLDR: TokenStream is a video generation framework that enables multi-subject personalization by using Token-wise LoRA (ToRA) to isolate subject identities and a T2V-guided stream to preserve motion priors from a frozen base model.

TLDR: TokenStream 是一个视频生成框架，通过 Token-wise LoRA (ToRA) 分离主体特征并利用 T2V 引导流保留基础模型的运动先验，从而实现无需联合训练的多主体个性化视频生成。

PhysLoop: Harnessing LLMs' Physics Awareness for Self-Improving Video Generation Models

Video generation models produce visually realistic videos but still struggle with physical dynamics and causal consistency. Learning from generated videos requires fine-grained physical supervision, yet reliable quality judgments, correction pairs, and controlled variations are difficult to obtain. Large language models (LLMs) possess physical knowledge, but struggle to reliably guide video generation models in producing their training videos. We present PhysLoop, a unified harness designed to support physically grounded self-improvement in video generation models. The harness guides and constrains LLMs throughout video generation, selection, and correction, distilling their physical knowledge into physically consistent videos. The framework supports controllable training-data construction and inference-time video repair. By identifying and correcting failures, PhysLoop marks a key milestone toward recursive self-improvement (RSI) in video generation models with limited human intervention. We release 100K videos spanning diverse, realistic indoor and outdoor scenes, along with generation tools and two evaluation datasets: PhysLoop-Gen and PhysLoop-Repair. We evaluate controllable generation and localized repair on these datasets, respectively, and assess physical behavior on the external Physics-IQ Verified benchmark.

TLDR: PhysLoop is a framework that leverages LLMs as physical reasoning engines to guide the generation, selection, and repair of physically consistent videos, enabling recursive self-improvement in video models.

TLDR: PhysLoop 是一套利用大语言模型（LLM）的物理推理能力来指导视频生成、筛选与修复的框架，旨在通过递归自我改进机制提升视频模型的物理一致性。

When Chekhov's Gun Fires Anyway: Over-Realization in Text-to-Image Models

In text-to-image generation, a target that the prompt mentions often appears in the generated image in a way the prompt does not call for. We call this failure over-realization. Mental-spaces theory points to the situations where it can happen: under a negation, inside what a character believes or imagines, as the vehicle of a figurative comparison, or behind a viewpoint that hides the target. Prior work has studied only negation in depth, and the conventional alignment and preference metrics do not penalize the failure. We build OverReal to measure it: OverReal-Gen, 698 prompts covering the four situations, on which we measure each generator's over-realization rate; and OverReal-Det, 2,841 generated images human-labeled for whether and how the target is over-realized, on which we verify the automatic judge that produces this rate. Our evaluation of eight text-to-image systems reveals that all of them over-realize, and that conventional metrics such as CLIPScore reward the failure rather than penalize it. Prompt expansion only partly mitigates the failure. Inside the generator, the diffusion backbone attends to a mentioned target almost as much as to a requested one. The cue words that should hold the target back receive less attention than ordinary words.

TLDR: The paper identifies 'over-realization' in text-to-image models, where concepts mentioned in prompts (like negated or hypothetical items) incorrectly appear in the image, and introduces the OverReal benchmark to quantify this failure across various diffusion systems.

TLDR: 本文探讨了文生图模型中的“过度实现”现象，即提示词中提及的否定或假设性目标被错误地渲染在图像中，并提出了OverReal基准来定量分析这一普遍存在的缺陷。

UniSpark: Exploring Hybrid Attention for Multimodal Understanding and Generation

We present UniSpark-9B, a unified multimodal model that investigates hybrid attention for visual understanding and generation. Built on a Mixture-of-Transformer-Experts backbone with approximately 9B parameters in each branch, UniSpark combines full attention for explicit token interactions with GatedDeltaNet linear attention for compact context propagation. Autoregressive text prediction and visual flow matching jointly support visual question answering, text-to-image generation, instruction-guided editing, and interleaved text-image prediction. In interleaved generation, noisy visual targets are isolated from subsequent predictions, while clean visual representations provide context for later outputs. A blockwise protocol enforces this distinction through attention masks in full-attention layers and recurrent-state retention rules in linear-attention layers. The selected training sources contain 159.7M generation samples, 213.4M understanding samples, and 624.8K interleaved sequences. A four-stage curriculum jointly trains multimodal understanding and visual generation, refining answer accuracy and instruction following while extending image synthesis to 2K. UniSpark scores 2470.3 on MME-S and 0.83 on GenEval. Qualitative examples further illustrate its capabilities in interleaved image generation, video synthesis, and video editing. Together, these results demonstrate that hybrid attention provides an effective backbone for unified multimodal understanding and generation.

TLDR: UniSpark-9B is a unified multimodal model utilizing a hybrid attention architecture (full attention combined with GatedDeltaNet) to bridge visual understanding and generation tasks like interleaved synthesis and editing.

TLDR: UniSpark-9B 是一款统一的多模态模型，通过结合全注意力机制与 GatedDeltaNet 线性注意力机制的混合架构，实现了视觉理解与生成任务（如交错式图文生成与编辑）的统一。

VBot Exo2Ego Policy: Lightweight Data Collection and Exocentric-to-Egocentric Video Generation

We present VBot Exo2Ego Policy, a video generation model that turns third-person videos into first-person observations of a selected actor, offering a practical way to expand first-person operation data from abundant real-world footage. To support this goal, we introduce a lightweight data collection pipeline and mobile app that support first-person recording, third-person recording, and synchronized capture of both views. This setup combines accessible single-view collection with paired RGB and camera geometry for learning cross-view correspondence. Building on a pretrained video diffusion transformer, our model incorporates geometric guidance and auxiliary supervision from semantic masks and hand keypoints to facilitate optimization and improve the fidelity of hand-object interactions. Camera geometry is injected stochastically during training and gradually removed, allowing inference without target-camera calibration. To accommodate the diversity of Internet videos, we combine source-video perception and reasoning, optional actor references, and multi-shot adaptation to identify the intended actor and follow an operation across camera cuts. The collection system and generation model provide a foundation for expanding first-person operation data across tasks, scenes, and participants.

TLDR: VBot Exo2Ego is a framework that leverages a lightweight data collection system and a diffusion-based video model to transform third-person videos into first-person egocentric views for data augmentation.

TLDR: VBot Exo2Ego 提出了一套轻量级数据采集系统和基于视频扩散模型的方法，旨在将第三人称视频转换为第一人称视角，从而扩展具身智能任务中的数据规模。

WorldDraft: If Code Builds the World, Can Video Models Just Render It?

Multimodal coding agents decouple 4D world construction from visual generation, repurposing video models from world generators toward generative renderers. This division of labor raises a fundamental question: if code builds the world, can video models just render it? A reliable generative renderer hinges on two complementary criteria: (i) world preservation, retaining prescribed 4D kinematics and camera paths; and (ii) visual realization, persistently binding text-specified target attributes over time. To evaluate this capability, we introduce WorldDraft, a benchmark of $432$ strictly paired test cases across $48$ executable 4D world families spanning six dynamic challenge categories. Under a $3 \times 3$ factorial design, WorldDraft orthogonally crosses draft granularity (abstract proxy to previs) and appearance alignment (neutral to conflicting) under identical dynamics. Extensive evaluations across $12$ representative models reveal that: (I) world preservation and visual realization remain in stark operational tension; (II) richer draft scaffolding anchors structure but paradoxically impairs appearance fidelity; and (III) conflicting source cues trigger severe source leakage (up to $64\%$), exposing fragile cross-modal disentanglement. To mitigate these bottlenecks, we propose WorldDraft-Primer, a plug-and-play visual prompting strategy that consistently improves both objectives across frozen video renderers. Together, WorldDraft and WorldDraft-Primer lay a foundation for bridging programmatic 4D simulation with video generation, advancing physically grounded generative rendering.

TLDR: WorldDraft proposes a framework for using video models as generative renderers for code-based 4D worlds, introducing a benchmark to evaluate world preservation and visual realization, alongside a prompting strategy to mitigate existing bottlenecks.

TLDR: WorldDraft 提出了一种将视频模型作为代码生成 4D 世界的渲染器的方法，构建了一个评估基准来衡量世界保真度与视觉表现，并提出了相应的视觉提示策略来改善模型性能。

Iris-Z: Scaling One Generative Model Past Specialist Geometry Models

Language moved from many task-specific models to one unified model: a single generative model with a single generative training task that handles all of them within the same backbone, and keeps improving with data, parameters and compute. We believe the bitter lesson applies just as much to computer vision, 3D and spatial understanding, in the end to visual intelligence: the effort should go into general architectures that solve all these tasks in a unified way, and into scaling them, rather than into task-specific designs. We test this on monocular geometry, a task that has belonged to specialised architectures for years. Iris-Z is a general image generator that produces appearance and geometry together. A single backbone denoises, reasons about and understands the image and its depth at the same time, and one set of weights generates images with their depth, estimates depth from photographs and paints images onto a given depth. At enough scale it surpasses state-of-the-art specialists on established depth benchmarks while keeping the generative abilities of the backbone it started from without specialized layers. We release two models for the community.

TLDR: Iris-Z introduces a unified generative model that achieves state-of-the-art performance on monocular depth estimation by treating geometry as a generative task alongside image synthesis, effectively replacing task-specific architectures with a single backbone.

TLDR: Iris-Z 提出了一种统一的生成式模型，通过将几何估计视为与图像生成并行的一项任务，在单目深度估计任务上超越了专用架构，验证了通用视觉模型的可扩展性。

TRACE: Trajectory Synchronization for Training-Free Resolution Extension in Video Generation

More text-to-video models are available, but many remain limited to low resolutions. Training them for higher resolutions is costly, while existing training-free methods still struggle with fine detail, scene structure, and efficiency. We propose TRACE, a training-free method that synchronizes low- and high-resolution generation trajectories. During early denoising, TRACE aligns predicted scene content at corresponding noise levels. It computes high-resolution updates from low-resolution predictions and the current high-resolution state, without high-resolution model inference. The high-resolution trajectory retains fine-grained variation for generating new details. Local window attention then refines fine details in a shared high-resolution latent state. TRACE checks suspected duplicate subjects against the corresponding low-resolution layout and corrects selected regions and their solver history while preserving detail elsewhere. Experiments across all kinds of video generation backbones and resolutions show improved perceptual quality and text-video alignment over FreeSwim, a state-of-the-art training-free method, with 2.0-2.9x end-to-end speedups. Our code and models will be released at https://github.com/libozhu03/TRACE.

TLDR: TRACE is a training-free video super-resolution method that synchronizes low-resolution trajectories with high-resolution refinements, achieving superior detail and efficiency without additional model training.

TLDR: TRACE 提出了一种无需训练的视频分辨率扩展方法，通过同步低分辨率生成轨迹与高分辨率细化过程，在无需额外训练的情况下实现了更高的视频细节质量和生成效率。

VibePrevisBench: Evaluating Agentic Coding for 4D Scene Generation in Blender

Controllable real-world video generation models can condition on a rendered graybox proxy video, which fixes layout, motion and camera. Coding agents can build such videos by reconstructing a 4D scene in Blender, yet no benchmark checks both these videos and the long, multi-shot scenes behind them. We introduce VibePrevisBench, a benchmark for 4D scene reconstruction: an agent turns a brief into an executable 4D scene and renders its proxy video. Its 242 tasks span four input kinds, including scripts, photos, continuations, and reference videos. Each delivery is scored along two evaluation dimensions. Rule-based Scene Check re-runs the code and compares the exported 4D scene with hidden ground truth facts in nine dimensions. VLM-based Screen Check asks a VLM judge whether the facts that pass can be recognized in the proxy video. We analyze the evaluation results and trajectories of 11 frontier coding agents and identify three key observations. First, multimodal understanding separates the agents, especially in understanding space and camera motion. Second, all agents seem to already master basic camera work and staging, and fail at challenging requests. Third, agents inspect their own renders but fail to correct their errors. Agents thus need better visual understanding of space and camera, explicit checks for challenging requests, and item-by-item verification of renders against the brief.

TLDR: VibePrevisBench is a new benchmark designed to evaluate agentic coding capabilities in 4D scene reconstruction for Blender, bridging the gap between brief descriptions and controllable proxy video generation.

TLDR: VibePrevisBench 是一个旨在评估智能体在 Blender 中进行 4D 场景重建编码能力的新基准，旨在弥合从简短描述到可控代理视频生成的差距。

Sprint3D: Accelerating End-to-End 3D Asset Generation through Pipeline Optimization

Image-to-3D generative models now produce asset-quality textured meshes, but at minutes per asset they remain slow to iterate with and costly to deploy. Prior work has mostly accelerated the sampling of the networks, and where it also optimizes the mesh processing, it trains its own generators. We instead start from a publicly released pipeline and ask how much of its end-to-end time can be saved. Profiling the public Pixal3D pipeline shows that its latency is dominated by what happens after the networks: the four rectified flows take 14% of the wall-clock time and mesh post-processing takes 85%, most of it in CPU-based UV unwrapping. We therefore first rebuild the mesh post-processing on the GPU and take it off the critical path, then cut the per-evaluation overhead of the flows and distill them from 76 to 8 network evaluations. The resulting pipeline, Sprint3D, reduces the median time per asset from 133 s to 4.05 s on one H200 GPU at the same export settings (33$\times$). At the same time, on 2,653 held-out Toys4K objects it improves every input-view normal metric of the Pixal3D protocol, with a slightly lower mean silhouette IoU, lower point-cloud alignment scores and less fine texture detail. The rebuilt post-processing alone also speeds up TRELLIS.2, which uses the same export code, by 6.7$\times$ without a detectable change in the evaluated metrics.

TLDR: Sprint3D accelerates end-to-end 3D asset generation by 33x by optimizing post-processing workflows and distilling generative flow networks, achieving sub-5s inference without training a new model.

TLDR: Sprint3D 通过优化后处理工作流并对生成流网络进行蒸馏，将端到端 3D 资产生成速度提升了 33 倍，在无需训练新模型的情况下实现了 4 秒以内的快速推理。

ATune: Unsupervised Photo Retouching Through Image-Editor Controls

Photo retouching models are trained on pairs of original and retouched photographs, usually edited by experts. Expert edits are costly, so the two main public sets, FiveK and PPR10K, hold only 16,161 originals. Good photographs on their own are far easier to find: commercial stock collections and free ones such as Pixabay and Unsplash hold millions. We learn retouching from them alone: we damage each photograph the way a camera or a careless edit would, which gives a worse and a better version of the same photograph. From these pairs, our model, ATune, learns how 13 measured attributes, such as brightness, contrast and sharpness, should change to raise a photograph's quality. The edit is declarative: ATune sets target values for the attributes, and a simple search adjusts the sliders of an out-of-the-box photo editor until the photograph reaches them. Every edit is a list of slider values a person can read and adjust. The same model can drive any editor that makes those changes; we test RawTherapee and darktable. We train on 10M photographs from a commercial stock collection and test on FiveK, PPR10K and SPAQ. Nine quality judges score every edit, and in a user study 157 human raters made 9,253 choices between our edit and another method's edit of the same photograph. When Claude Opus 5 and GPT-6 Astra edit the same photographs in the same editor, raters prefer our edits to GPT-6 Astra's (54.3%) and rate them even with Claude's (50.5%). We also compare with supervised retouchers on their home ground: NeurOP on FiveK and AdaInt on PPR10K, each trained on that set's own expert edits. Raters chose our edits in 46.3% of those comparisons. ATune, a 54M-parameter model trained without a single expert edit, now matches frontier models at setting an editor's sliders.

TLDR: ATune is an unsupervised photo retouching framework that learns to adjust image attributes by training on synthetic 'damaged' versions of millions of stock photos, bypassing the need for expert-annotated datasets while achieving parity with frontier models.

TLDR: ATune 是一种无需专家标注数据集的无监督照片修图模型，它通过模拟损坏照片并学习调整参数，实现了与前沿模型相当的修图效果，且具有极高的可解释性。

Split-and-scale Latent 3D Representations

Latent 3D representations have significantly expanded the capabilities of modern shape modeling, enabling compact encoding of 3D scenes and supporting powerful 3D generative models. Despite this progress, current representations struggle to capture the fine details of complex objects, fundamentally limited by the capacity of their latents. However, scaling latent capacity at training time is computationally expensive and often infeasible under memory limits, and naively increasing the number of latents at inference fails to make effective use of the added capacity. In this paper, we address both challenges with \textit{split-and-scale}, a method that effectively expands latent capacity at inference while keeping training cost tractable. Our key observation is that by representing 3D scenes as continuous functions of 3D coordinates, a single pretrained tokenizer can be applied at inference to arbitrarily small sub-regions of a scene, allocating the same latent capacity to a smaller spatial extent and thus capturing far higher detail. We validate this insight by conducting extensive ablations to study the trade-offs between compute, memory, latent capacity, and reconstruction quality, demonstrating our inference-time scaling method achieves quality competitive with much larger models that exceed single-GPU memory limits. We further demonstrate that split-and-scale supports high-quality generative modeling, training an autoregressive 3D generator that performs competitively with state-of-the-art baselines.

TLDR: The paper introduces 'split-and-scale,' a method that improves 3D latent representation detail by applying pretrained tokenizers to smaller spatial sub-regions at inference time, enabling high-fidelity 3D modeling without expensive training-time scaling.

Where to Generate Touch: Do Image Latents Preserve Material Recognition?

Generated touch could extend robot learning beyond recorded contacts. A tactile generator needs a latent space, and we choose one by testing whether a material classifier trained on real GelSight presses still works on encode-decode reconstructions from pretrained image models. On ObjectFolder Real, the classifier loses 6.5 to 9.7 points on reconstructions from three 16x discrete tokenizers and 0.3 points on those from the Stable Diffusion VAE (SD-VAE). The 16x tokenizers again lose far more on Touch and Go and on DIGIT object recognition (YCB-Slide). Retraining the classifier on reconstructions, with no new labels, narrows the discrete gap. SD-VAE reconstructions also keep object identity, with 76.7% object-matching accuracy against 78.9% for real touch and 67.0% for MAGVIT-v2. We therefore select SD-VAE latents and show a photo-to-touch generator trained in them. Code will be released upon acceptance.

TLDR: The paper investigates the suitability of pretrained image latent spaces for tactile generation, identifying the Stable Diffusion VAE as superior to discrete tokenizers for preserving material and object identity in tactile reconstructions.
