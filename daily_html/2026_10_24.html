UniAction: Unified Action Conditioning for World Models

Actions that condition world models are highly heterogeneous, and thus existing action-conditioned world models are typically built for individual action types, fragmenting already limited action-annotated data across separate models. We introduce UniAction, a unified action-conditioning framework that represents any action as a sparse set of action elements, each with a discrete semantic identity and a continuous spatiotemporal grounding coordinate. By mapping these coordinates directly into the pretrained video model's native positional space, UniAction allows heterogeneous actions to condition a shared world model within the same spatiotemporal structure. We further introduce cardinality-calibrated attention to address the bias from differing action representation granularities. We evaluate UniAction on four representative action forms: force, camera control, hand pose, and robot pose. UniAction outperforms conditioning alternatives in action following, and a single jointly trained model surpasses the action-specific experts, showing that heterogeneous action data benefit from unified model and joint training.

TLDR: UniAction proposes a unified framework for world models by representing heterogeneous actions as sparse sets of semantic elements and coordinates, enabling a single model to perform across diverse action types.

TLDR: UniAction 提出了一种统一的世界模型动作条件框架，通过将异构动作表示为语义元素与时空坐标的稀疏集合，使单一模型能够处理多种类型的动作输入。

EditJev: System One Routing for Image Editing Models

Instruction-based image editing models exhibit pronounced sample-specific performance variation: different models can produce substantially different results for the same source image and editing instruction, and the best-performing model often varies across requests. These models also differ substantially in inference latency, making fixed-model deployment inefficient. We therefore study image editing model routing, which dynamically selects an editor from a candidate pool given an input image and editing instruction. We introduce EditJev, a System One routing model that directly predicts sample-level preference scores over candidate image editing models in a single forward pass. A lightweight preference head on a vision-language backbone produces a fixed-dimensional score vector without autoregressive model-name generation or response parsing. To train EditJev, we construct pairwise preferences from offline candidate outputs and optimize a pairwise ranking objective to learn relative model suitability. At inference time, EditJev combines the predicted preference scores with model-level speed scores to balance editing quality and generation latency. Experiments on ImgEdit and GEdit-En show that EditJev improves aggregate quality over the strongest fixed editor, from 4.540 to 4.592 and from 8.194 to 8.255, respectively, while reducing estimated end-to-end latency by 60.8% and 31.7%. These results demonstrate the effectiveness of direct, structured model routing for efficient image editing.

TLDR: EditJev is a lightweight System One routing model that dynamically selects the optimal image editing model for a given request, balancing generation quality and inference latency via a preference-trained head.

TLDR: EditJev 提出了一种轻量级的系统一路由模型，通过在单次前向传播中预测模型偏好，动态选择最优图像编辑模型，从而有效平衡了生成质量与推理延迟。

Fast and Useful: Adapting Distilled Generators for Visual Learning

Distilled text-to-image models generate images quickly, but classifiers trained on those images can perform substantially worse than classifiers trained on images from their slower base models. We adapt released distilled generators to produce better training data while retaining fast generation. We fit a low-rank update to the student's first prediction so that it reaches an intermediate state produced by its unguided base, then disable the update and let the unchanged student finish generation. The base supplies training targets and is absent at inference. Guided targets and shorter jumps give classifiers lower accuracy in our ablations, showing why the state used for supervision matters. Across three generator families, the adapted images train better classifiers than images from the released students. With class-name templates at full ImageNet-1k scale, ResNet-50 top-1 rises from 8.4% to 34.2% for FLUX.2-klein and from 12.7% to 32.1% for SD-Turbo, using two and three denoiser evaluations per image, respectively. Fixed adapters also improve results on additional classes, fine-grained datasets, and other learner architectures and training recipes. Template-based adaptation remains useful against richer-prompt alternatives, while the benefit of combining adaptation with LLM generation prompts depends on the generator. Adapting a single generation step makes fast generators substantially more useful for training visual models.

TLDR: The paper introduces a method to adapt distilled text-to-image models by injecting intermediate states from unguided base models, significantly improving the quality of synthetic training data for visual classifiers while maintaining high generation speed.

TIE: Training-free CLIP Embedding Guidance for Compositional Text-to-Image Alignment

Text-to-image (T2I) diffusion models have advanced notably, but still fail to satisfy prompt conditions, resulting in attribute mismatches and missing objects. These errors normally fall into two categories: concept loss, when an object or attribute (i.e., concept) goes missing, and concept confusion, when an attribute is assigned to the wrong object or multiple objects blend together. Recent studies suggest that these challenges are caused by the limited capacity of CLIP text encoder to capture fine-grained semantic details. Although several methods have been proposed, concept loss and concept confusion persist in recent T2I models when handling multi-object, multi-attribute prompts. In this paper, we conduct an embedding-level analysis to develop a training-free, inference-time solution for concept loss and concept confusion across CLIP-based T2I models. We find that (1) concepts mentioned later exhibit higher embedding entropy, reflecting less concentrated representations that are more often associated with concept loss, and (2) the first CLIP attention layer captures the strength of binding between each object and its attribute. Guided by our findings, we introduce TIE, a method that improves semantic alignment through a single text-embedding update. TIE addresses concept loss via entropy-aware singular value amplification and resolves concept confusion through interpolation-extrapolation binding based on CLIP attention scores, all in a training-free manner. Extensive experiments demonstrate that TIE enhances semantic fidelity in multi-concept scenarios with minimal sampling overhead. The project page is available at https://hong-sujung.github.io/TIE/.

TLDR: TIE is a training-free inference-time method that improves text-to-image alignment by addressing concept loss and confusion through entropy-aware singular value amplification and attention-based binding in CLIP embeddings.

TLDR: TIE 是一种无需训练的推理时方法，通过基于熵的奇异值放大和基于注意力机制的绑定，解决文本生成图像模型中的概念丢失与概念混淆问题，提升语义对齐能力。

UniWorld: A Unified World Model for Real-Time Visual World Simulation

Continuous visual world simulation requires coupling evolving scene geometry with temporally persistent appearance. We present UniWorld, a unified framework that integrates depth trajectories and reference appearance within a shared video generation model. The central design maintains visual and geometric histories on a common timeline. Noise-aligned overlapping continuation places generated history at the prescribed sampling noise level and commits only newly generated latents. Rolling depth latent state preserves the corresponding geometric controls, incrementally reads newly available geometry, and keeps the conditioning fixed within each sampling window. Together, these mechanisms ensure exact preservation of latent history while bounding the active depth state by the window size. We evaluate continuous generation, paired history interventions, and long-horizon behavior across diverse scene categories. The same generator supports estimated, native, and on-demand simulator depth, while cached and incremental execution paths produce bitwise-identical outputs. Appearance changes and geometric control branches operate through the same interface without modifying committed history. The measured steady-state DiT execution path achieves real-time generation throughput, demonstrating the efficiency of UniWorld for continuous visual world simulation.

TLDR: UniWorld is a unified world model that integrates depth trajectories and visual appearance into a shared video generation framework, enabling temporally persistent, real-time, and long-horizon visual simulation.

TLDR: UniWorld 是一种统一的世界模型，将深度轨迹与视觉外观集成到共享视频生成框架中，实现了具有时间持久性、实时性及长序列的视觉模拟。

ACWM-RM: A Reference-Free Reward Model from Complementary Mismatch Preferences

Reinforcement learning has gained attention for improving action-conditioned world models (ACWMs), as it enables reward-driven optimization of visual quality (VQ) and action-following fidelity (AF) beyond flow-matching optimization. Realizing this potential requires reward signals that reliably rank videos by both VQ and AF. However, existing rewards capture only one dimension: metric-based rewards assess VQ but require ground-truth videos and do not directly assess AF, while inverse dynamics model (IDM) rewards assess AF without references but do not guarantee VQ. Moreover, IDMs, typically trained on real videos, may provide unreliable rewards for generated videos containing visual artifacts and physical inconsistencies. In this work, we introduce the Action and Video Mismatch Benchmark (AVM-Eval), comprising 67.2k pairs that evaluate how reliably IDMs assess AF on real and generated videos. We then propose ACWM-RM, a reward model that jointly assesses VQ and AF without ground-truth videos during reward computation. To train it, we first construct the Action and Video Mismatch Preference Dataset (AVM-Pref) with 506.3k pairs providing complementary supervision: action-mismatch pairs supervise AF, while video-mismatch pairs supervise VQ and expose the model to generated videos. We then pretrain an action-conditioned predictor on real videos to learn forward dynamics before jointly training it with a patch-weighted score head on AVM-Pref to transfer these dynamics to AF assessment on generated videos. Evaluation on AVM-Eval reveals the limited reliability of IDMs on generated videos, while downstream RL experiments show that ACWM-RM outperforms reward baselines in both criteria.

TLDR: The paper introduces ACWM-RM, a reference-free reward model for video generation that jointly optimizes visual quality and action-following fidelity using a novel preference dataset (AVM-Pref) and evaluation benchmark (AVM-Eval).

TLDR: 本文提出了ACWM-RM，一种无需参考视频的奖励模型，通过构建AVM-Pref偏好数据集和AVM-Eval评测基准，实现对视频生成中视觉质量和动作遵循度的一体化优化。

$Ψ$-0.6: Adaptive Streams of Thought in a Unified Vision-Language World Model

Humans reason about the physical world by flexibly combining different forms of inference as a problem unfolds. Achieving this flexibility is a major open challenge in AI: capabilities often remain separated across specialized models, limiting how they can be composed and jointly adapted to a task. We introduce $Ψ$-0.6, a unified vision-language world model that adaptively composes perception, language, geometry, and simulation within a shared reasoning process we call streams of thought. These streams interleave images, text, and explicit representations of geometry and motion, all of which the model can generate and interpret through a shared token interface. $Ψ$-0.6 surpasses leading open-weight models zero-shot on multi-view spatial reasoning, with further gains from reasoning through geometric intermediates. It also supports counterfactual simulation: generating and interpreting imagined scenes to determine, for example, whether moving an object would obstruct a pathway. Reinforcement learning from task feedback jointly optimizes the selection, generation, and use of intermediate representations, improving visual question-answering accuracy with gains that transfer to unseen benchmarks. $Ψ$-0.6 offers a path toward adaptive physical reasoning -- not only predicting the physical world but also choosing the representations through which to reason.

TLDR: Ψ-0.6 is a unified vision-language world model that integrates perception, geometry, and simulation through 'streams of thought' to perform adaptive physical reasoning and counterfactual scene generation.

TLDR: Ψ-0.6 是一个统一的视觉语言世界模型，通过“思维流”整合了感知、几何和模拟，实现了自适应的物理推理与反事实场景生成。

ImageRSI: Verification-Driven Recursive Self-Improvement for Agentic Image Generation

Complex text-to-image requests remain difficult to satisfy reliably. Verifying and repairing an image can fix an individual output, but a successful repair does not necessarily carry over to other requests. We present ImageRSI, a framework that keeps the image generator frozen and combines three components: five-dimensional verification and repair within each task, an experience library shared across tasks, and offline updating of the judge. Candidate experiences enter the library only after paired A/B validation, and the judge is updated with supervised fine-tuning and GRPO on accumulated image-judgment records and evaluated before reuse. We also introduce ImageReq-Bench, a benchmark of 961 requests built around hard cases in real-world image-generation requests, each paired with an evaluation checklist. ImageRSI achieves a Checklist Strict Accuracy (CSA) of 52.6% on MindBench (with Nano Banana Pro as the generator) and 65.66% on ImageReq-Bench, and we compare system outcomes across judge-training configurations.

TLDR: ImageRSI is an agentic framework that enhances text-to-image reliability through recursive self-improvement, utilizing a shared experience library, multi-dimensional verification, and an offline judge updated via GRPO.

TLDR: ImageRSI 是一个通过递归自我提升来增强文生图可靠性的智能体框架，利用共享经验库、多维度验证以及通过 GRPO 更新的离线判别器来优化生成结果。

Concept Acupuncturist: An Efficient and Precise Concept Erasure Method for Diffusion Models via Sparse Autoencoders

Diffusion-based image generation models produce high-quality images but may generate harmful or policy-violating content, motivating machine unlearning (MU) to selectively remove target concepts while preserving general capabilities. However, existing MU approaches often struggle with the distributed and entangled nature of concept representations, leading to inefficient optimization and imprecise edits when applied through large-scale fine-tuning over full diffusion trajectories. To bridge this gap, we propose Concept Acupuncturist (ConceptACP), a novel MU framework that integrates interpretability-guided analysis with inference-free fine-tuning for efficient and precise concept removal. By establishing mappings between semantic concepts and cross-attention blocks in the U-Net denoiser and localizing the most concept-relevant block for targeted optimization, ConceptACP avoids full diffusion trajectory optimization, thereby improving fine-tuning efficiency. To mitigate concept entanglement, we propose a feature-level correction mechanism based on Sparse Autoencoders (SAEs), where SAE-derived sparse, semantically meaningful feature activations are manipulated to construct targeted correction signals, which are then projected back to the output space of the corresponding block and used as supervision for precise concept removal. Consequently, ConceptACP eliminates the need for external inference-time modules while achieving efficient and fine-grained unlearning. Instantiated on SDXL-Turbo, ConceptACP improves the forgetting success rate by 36.34 percentage points in the standard one-step generation setting on average over the best-performing baseline, while preserving image quality and prompt fidelity, and generalizes effectively across other Stable Diffusion backbones. Our code is available at https://github.com/Eran-Xia/ConceptACP.

TLDR: ConceptACP is a novel machine unlearning framework that leverages Sparse Autoencoders to precisely localize and erase specific concepts in diffusion models without requiring full trajectory fine-tuning or inference-time modules.

TLDR: ConceptACP 提出了一种新型机器遗忘框架，利用稀疏自编码器（SAE）精确定位并擦除扩散模型中的特定概念，无需进行全轨迹微调或推理阶段的额外模块，实现了高效且精准的模型净化。

ACVA-Bench: Beyond Prompt Following in Abstract Concept Visualization Ability for Text-to-Image Models

Text-to-image (T2I) models have achieved remarkable progress in image quality and instruction following, prompting growing research interest in their capacity for deep understanding that goes beyond literal prompt matching. However, without an explicit visual scheme provided, it remains unclear and has not been systematically evaluated whether T2I models can autonomously translate abstract concepts lacking a fixed visual referent, such as "loyalty," into visual representations that are accurate, diverse, and distinguishable from closely related concepts. We define this capability as Abstract Concept Visualization Ability (ACVA) and introduce ACVA-Bench, a category-balanced benchmark grounded in psycholinguistic norms of abstract concepts, which examines models' ACVA along three dimensions semantic sufficiency, representational diversity, and conceptual distinctiveness under a low-intervention setting that withholds concrete objects, scenes, or visual metaphors. Since abstract concepts lack a unique reference image, conventional evaluation metrics that rely on reference-image matching are difficult to apply. To address this, we propose a fine-grained visual-semantic evaluation method constrained by concept semantics, which decomposes concept semantics into independently verifiable visual-semantic questions to assess the semantic representation quality of generated images, with its validity further confirmed by human evaluation. Benchmarking results on mainstream T2I models show that these models rely on a small set of prototypical visual patterns, leaving room for improvement in representational diversity and conceptual distinctiveness for abstract concepts, thereby providing an interpretable and reproducible evaluation foundation for future research.

TLDR: ACVA-Bench is a new benchmark designed to evaluate how text-to-image models visualize abstract concepts (e.g., 'loyalty') by assessing semantic sufficiency, diversity, and distinctiveness without relying on concrete visual cues.

TLDR: ACVA-Bench 是一个旨在评估文本生成图像模型对“忠诚”等抽象概念视觉化能力的新基准，通过考察语义充分性、表征多样性和概念独特性，填补了该领域的评估空白。

LOCI: Localized Continuous Control for Multi-Subject Image Editing

Recent instruction-following image editors such as Flux.1-Kontext enable text-driven editing but lack fine-grained control over where and how much an edit is applied. Existing approaches provide either continuous but global control or spatially localized editing at a fixed strength. To our knowledge, none jointly supports explicit spatial localization, continuous per-region strength, and independent simultaneous control of multiple subjects. We present LOCI (LOcalized Continuous control for multi- subject Image editing). Given a multi-subject image with per-subject regions and instructions, LOCI edits each subject at an independently chosen, continuously adjustable strength, for instance gradually aging one person while adding a beard to another with minimal cross-region leakage. LOCI pairs subject-level attention binding for spatial isolation with a Regional LoRA layer that replaces the LoRA's scalar coefficient with a per-token scaling vector aligned to the spatial layout. A single adapter is trained at one suppression point, and continuous edit strengths follow by rescaling its regional residual at inference. Experiments on multi-subject scenes demonstrate that LOCI achieves strong edit fidelity with significantly reduced cross-region interference compared to baselines, while maintaining continuous control over edit strength

TLDR: LOCI is a novel image editing framework that enables independent, spatially-localized, and continuous-strength control over multiple subjects within a single image using regional LoRA adapters and attention binding.

TLDR: LOCI 是一种创新的图像编辑框架，通过区域 LoRA 适配器和注意力绑定技术，实现了在单张图像中对多个主体进行独立、空间局部化且强度连续可调的编辑。

Scaling Transformer Energy-based Models For High Quality Image Synthesis

Energy-Based Models (EBMs) are a flexible class of generative models that decouple probabilistic modeling from the generation process. This decoupling directly enables compositional generation and inference-time scaling, where sample quality can be refined simply by increasing sampling steps. However, severe challenges in high-dimensional sampling and training instabilities with deep architectures have restricted EBMs to low-dimensional regimes. To resolve these bottlenecks, we introduce the Transformer Energy-Based Model (TEM), a scalable transformer energy function operating entirely within a latent space. On top of this backbone, we extract energies at multiple layers and sum them, composing complementary constraints from local texture to global semantics. Furthermore, two components make this high-quality at scale: Positive Representation Alignment (PRA), which supervises TEM's intermediate representations with a vision foundation model, and advanced sampling strategies that improve sample quality at inference. TEM-XL achieves an FID-50K of 3.82 on class-conditional ImageNet 256$\times$256 without classifier-free guidance, showing that a contrastive EBM can generate high-fidelity images. On ImageNet 512$\times$512, TEM achieves an FID-50K of 8.24, demonstrating that the approach scales to higher resolution. We further validate the core promises of EBMs: compositional generation, zero-shot inpainting, and out-of-distribution detection.

TLDR: The paper introduces the Transformer Energy-Based Model (TEM), which leverages latent-space transformer architectures, multi-layer energy summation, and Positive Representation Alignment (PRA) to achieve high-fidelity image synthesis at scale.

SyncVid: World-Equivariant Diffusion for Synchronized Video Generation

Generating multiple videos of a dynamic scene from a single reference video requires synthesizing both observed and unobserved content consistently across viewpoints. Existing camera-control methods for video diffusion typically rely on architectural mechanisms, including camera embeddings, cross-view attention, and geometric conditioning. Although effective for static scene content, these approaches often struggle with the fine-grained, dynamic geometry that must remain coherent across views and over time. We synchronize the diffusion process itself by sampling the Gaussian noise prior in a canonical 3D space derived from the source video and rasterizing it into target cameras, rather than sampling independently in each 2D image plane. Our method, SyncVid, lifts a monocular video into a canonical 4D representation, associates noise with scene geometry and unseen space, and rasterizes the resulting structured noise into arbitrary views. Because rendering can introduce local correlations, we further propose a cross-view-consistent whitening procedure that decorrelates neighboring pixels while preserving correspondence across views. The structured noise serves as the diffusion prior for a LoRA-adapted pretrained video diffusion model, requiring no architectural modifications. By enforcing spatiotemporal and cross-view coherence at the source of stochasticity, SyncVid generates synchronized videos and improves both perceptual quality and cross-view consistency in our experiments. Project page: https://qualcomm-ai-research.github.io/SyncVid-Webpage.

TLDR: SyncVid introduces a world-equivariant diffusion framework that ensures cross-view consistency in video generation by sampling Gaussian noise in a canonical 3D space rather than independent 2D image planes.

AlphaAnything: Extending Pretrained RGB Generators to RGBA

Existing RGBA generation methods commonly learn a transparency-specific representation through reconstruction, introducing an additional training stage and potentially altering the RGB decoding function inherited from pretrained models. We present AlphaAnything, a lightweight framework that extends pretrained RGB generators to RGBA directly in their original latent space, without RGBA reconstruction pretraining. AlphaAnything inserts LoRA modules into the generative transformer and attaches an alpha branch to the frozen RGB VAE decoder. Unidirectional RGB-to-alpha feature sharing allows opacity supervision to shape the generative latent while guaranteeing that, for any fixed latent, RGB decoding remains identical to that of the pretrained decoder. We further introduce Endpoint-Aware Alpha Loss (EA Loss), which derives opacity supervision from the complementary foreground--background weights used in compositing. It supports exact opacity endpoints and continuous partial transparency while emphasizing localized alpha errors. Experiments on text-to-RGBA and image-to-RGBA tasks across multiple pretrained RGB backbones demonstrate competitive generation quality, and controlled ablations validate the shared-latent training design and EA Loss.

TLDR: AlphaAnything is a lightweight framework that extends pretrained RGB generators to support RGBA output by injecting LoRA modules and an alpha branch without altering the original RGB decoding function.

TLDR: AlphaAnything 是一个轻量级框架，通过在预训练的 RGB 生成模型中引入 LoRA 模块和 alpha 分支，无需改变原始 RGB 解码功能即可实现 RGBA 生成。

PASTA: Prompt-Aware Annealed Diffusion Sampling

While Diffusion models produce high-quality images, real-world visual design requires intentionally preserving clean regions for headlines, logos, or UI elements. Existing layout-aware text-to-image methods struggle to strictly preserve reserved regions, often introducing unwanted content that complicates downstream editing. Existing approaches either manipulate attention to displace content or suppress conditioning via noise-level interventions. However, these methods sacrifice visual coherence, producing flat regions or noticeable style inconsistencies between reserved and non-reserved areas. To address this limitation, we propose a Prompt-Aware Shared-Latent Training-Free Annealed Diffusion Sampling (PASTA), a training-free framework that enforces reserved-region constraints by selectively applying semantic guidance to object-allowed regions during sampling. PASTA operates on a shared latent trajectory, where full-prompt and context-prompt guidance are fused through mask-guided sampling. An annealed schedule suppresses semantic leakage during early denoising stages while gradually restoring visual consistency in later stages. Extensive experiments demonstrate that PASTA improves reserved-region fidelity while preserving overall image realism and perceptual consistency.

TLDR: PASTA is a training-free framework for diffusion models that enforces reserved-region constraints (e.g., for UI/logos) using mask-guided semantic sampling and an annealed schedule to balance constraint fidelity with visual coherence.

TLDR: PASTA 是一个无需训练的扩散模型框架，通过掩码引导采样和退火调度机制，在保留特定区域（如 UI 或标志）的同时，确保生成图像的视觉连贯性和语义一致性。

SceneBloom: Agentic Indoor Environment Generation with Realistic and Diverse Layouts for Embodied Learning

Training generalizable embodied agents requires realistic, varied, and simulation-ready indoor environments. Language-guided methods support semantic planning and object-level reasoning, but precise spatial grounding remains difficult.
