When a Larger Guidance Scale Repairs Text-to-Video Failures and When It Does Not

When a text-to-video diffusion model ignores part of a prompt, the usual first fix is to raise the classifier-free guidance scale. This paper is a measurement study of when that fix works. On Mochi, the same increase repairs failures on rare compositions ("a snowy beach") beyond what generating again does, does not repair negations ("a highway with no vehicles"), where it fixes fewer failures than generating again does, and changes nothing for events that must happen late in a clip ("a sandcastle that a wave washes flat"). Which of these happens can be known in advance, from the prompt and one inexpensive score. The score, which we call demand, measures how strongly the denoiser reacts to the part of the prompt that the failed video leaves out, and it is read from the first quarter of denoising, before any video exists. Our main finding is a condition: raising the scale is effective only when the prompt contains no negation and no event tied to a specific time, and when demand is sufficiently high, with recovery clearly higher above a fitted threshold than below it. Published corrections for the three failure types, used as fixed probes, behave as this account predicts. A short decision rule follows: before generating again, a user can tell whether the most common fix is worth trying, and what to use when it is not.

TLDR: This paper investigates when increasing the classifier-free guidance scale effectively repairs text-to-video failures, introducing a predictive 'demand' score to determine if scaling is useful or if other strategies are needed.

Rescaling OLS-Merged Adapters To Improve Personalized Image Generation

Merging multiple adapters into a single image generation model reduces deployment costs while preserving diverse task capabilities or personalization modes. State-of-the-art methods such as RegMean, Mix-of-Show, and Subspace Routing fit the outputs of the merged model to those of the individual adapters using ordinary least squares (OLS). We show that this reconstruction objective systematically shrinks task-specific signals whenever the activation distributions of different tasks overlap, producing a merged adapter that is too weak for downstream use. We derive a decomposition that quantifies this shrinkage and propose rescaling the merged update to restore signal strength. On multi-subject personalization with up to 30 merged adapters, rescaling substantially improves subject fidelity over unscaled baselines. Our approach also improves results on style transfer, concept erasure, image classification, and LLM-based text classification.

TLDR: The paper identifies that OLS-based adapter merging suffers from signal shrinkage and proposes a rescaling technique to restore task-specific signal strength, significantly improving personalization fidelity and generalizability.

Hierarchical Diffusion Transformer for End-to-End Image Generation

Pixel-space diffusion learns visual representations and image generation jointly in a single end-to-end training stage, without a separately pretrained visual encoder. However, plain pixel-space Transformers still trail leading two-stage latent diffusion models in generation quality and training efficiency. We hypothesize that part of this gap arises from retaining one token per patch throughout the backbone, which does not explicitly encourage a visual hierarchy that summarizes related content at progressively coarser levels. To address this, we introduce Hierarchical Diffusion Transformers (HiT), which progressively group related tokens and represent each group with a single token. The main Transformer bottleneck processes these compact group tokens, and ungrouping restores features to their original image positions. A compression budget regulates the average compression ratio across the batch, allowing group memberships and counts to vary across images and diffusion timesteps during both training and generation. The model exhibits emergent semantic structure, learning to group coherent objects and object parts without segmentation labels. In text-to-image generation, HiT further learns to group caption words with image patches depicting the same concepts, without region annotations. HiT achieves 1.48 FID on ImageNet $256\times256$ and reaches comparable generation quality to JiT using approximately 40\% of its cumulative training FLOPs. For text-to-image generation, HiT exceeds UniLiP's average score across five benchmarks with roughly $6\times$ fewer denoiser parameters and $3\times$ fewer forward FLOPs. These results suggest that learning a visual hierarchy within the generator breaks down the barrier to stronger image generation without external visual pretraining.

TLDR: The paper introduces Hierarchical Diffusion Transformers (HiT), which improve end-to-end image generation by dynamically grouping tokens to learn a visual hierarchy, achieving superior performance with significantly reduced computational costs.

Code World Model 2.0: Towards Infinite Controllable Video Generation

Visual proxies decouple explicit world-state simulation from photorealistic video synthesis, offering a controllable interface for both offline clip creation and real-time interactive streaming. Yet a practical renderer must address two challenges. Upstream proxies vary from coarse layouts to fine articulated geometry depending on the interaction, and streaming inference must operate in a few causal steps without accumulating drift across self-generated history. We present Invoker-Render, pairing a bidirectional creation model with a causal four-step streaming model under a shared conditioning interface over structural proxies, an appearance reference, and language. Trained on an aligned approximately 200-hour multi-granularity corpus, Invoker-Render honors fine geometric constraints when supplied while synthesizing plausible local motion under coarse proxies, enabling runtime selection of input detail. To bridge bidirectional creation and real-time streaming without compounding step compression and rollout drift, we introduce Progressive Forcing (ProForcing). ProForcing first trains a causal predictor and distills it into a four-step renderer using histories derived from training videos. Training then shifts to continuous self-generated rollouts. A pipelined serving stack supports efficient streaming inference. Experiments show versatile control across proxy families, granularities, and camera paths, consistent gains over recent streaming baselines, and stable 30-minute racing rollouts that preserve scene and subject appearance.

TLDR: Invoker-Render is a video generation framework that uses structural proxies for controllable synthesis, introducing 'ProForcing' to bridge bidirectional creation with stable, low-latency, causal streaming inference.

LOCUS: Causal Support Windows for Attribute Control in Autoregressive Image Generation

Unified autoregressive (AR) image generators struggle to satisfy multiple attribute constraints in a single prompt. Although these attributes are decodable from image-token latents, editing those latents at test time remains difficult. Adding a fixed attribute direction to the prefix can reinforce a requested attribute but rarely overrides a conflicting one, such as turning a car red when the prompt specifies blue. We investigate how the effect of an intervention depends on where it acts in the image-token sequence. We introduce the causal support profile, which measures, for each attribute, how often patching image-token states within a given window flips the rendered attribute. Structural attributes (count and position) are most responsive in early, narrow windows, while appearance attributes (color, size, and material) respond over later, broader windows. A budget-matched comparison of early and middle windows shows that the effect of window location depends on the target attribute, and two-dimensional support maps show that the responsive regions track spatial content. Based on these profiles, we propose LOCUS (Latent Optimization in Causal Support windows), which diagnoses which attribute failed and routes test-time latent optimization to its corresponding support window. With the generator, optimizer, reward, verifier, and token budget held fixed, support-routed windows outperform late windows by 23.6 percentage points in GenEval++ macro accuracy. With the generator frozen, LOCUS raises Janus-Pro's GenEval++ accuracy from 24.6% to 46.8% overall and from 19.5% to 40.5% on multi-attribute tags. On GenEval, LOCUS repairs 48.1% of failed generations in one rollout, compared with 37.4% for prefix optimization, and needs 14% fewer rounds on average to succeed.

TLDR: LOCUS introduces a causal analysis method to identify specific 'support windows' in image-token sequences for effective attribute control, enabling significantly improved multi-attribute adherence in autoregressive image generators.

Beyond Co-Generation: Rethinking Jointness in Joint Audio-Video Generation

Our aim is to advance the understanding of genuine audio-visual coordination in joint text-to-audio-video (T2AV) generation, with particular emphasis on semantic consistency. Evaluating audio-visual consistency in T2AV generation is nontrivial, as detailed text prompts obscure whether such consistency stems from genuine cross-modal coordination or merely from independent text following. To isolate the former, we introduce intrinsic jointness: the ability of audio and video to produce the same realization for details left underspecified by the prompt. Using semantically underspecified prompts (e.g., "musical instrument playing" rather than "electric guitar playing"), we investigate four recent models and reveal their limited intrinsic jointness. To address this limitation, we propose JointREPA, a simple representation alignment-based post-training method to facilitate cross-modal coordination. Since existing benchmarks use semantically detailed prompts, we introduce UNSAIDBench, a benchmark designed to evaluate intrinsic jointness under semantic underspecification. Experiments across two distinct models and three benchmarks, including UNSAIDBench, demonstrate that JointREPA consistently improves semantic consistency.

TLDR: The paper introduces 'intrinsic jointness' to measure audio-visual semantic consistency in T2AV generation and proposes JointREPA, a post-training alignment method, along with a new benchmark, UNSAIDBench, to address current limitations in cross-modal coordination.

Pixel is All You Need

Text, images, audio, touch, and even robot actions can, in principle, all be rendered onto a single canvas, suggesting an ambitious goal of unifying all modalities within vision. Language provides the natural first test. To subsume it, a visual model must reason directly over text in pixels. Across a wide range of image, video, and unified multimodal generators, however, we find that their visual generation modules cannot perform symbolic reasoning over text on the canvas. They succeed only when an underlying text module or prompt rewriter already supplies the answer, effectively reducing the generator to a mere renderer, a limitation that post-training does little to resolve. To test whether this failure stems from pixels themselves or from current training paradigms, we train a family of purely pixel-space generative models from scratch without any text pathway. With images as their sole input and output, these models read questions, write intermediate steps, and produce answers directly on the canvas. Remarkably, they exhibit hallmark behaviors of large language models. Performance improves predictably with scale following a power law. Writing intermediate steps frame by frame substantially outperforms single-frame answering, echoing chain-of-thought, while reinforcement-learning post-training further strengthens reasoning. Moreover, sharing one canvas allows the same model to reason across modalities, a capability that text-only language models lack. To the best of our knowledge, this is the first systematic study of these phenomena in purely pixel-space generative models free of text tokens.

TLDR: The paper demonstrates that purely pixel-based generative models, trained without explicit text pathways, can perform symbolic reasoning and exhibit LLM-like behaviors, suggesting a path toward a unified visual-centric multimodal architecture.

TLDR: 该研究表明，在没有文本路径的情况下，纯像素生成的模型能够执行符号推理并表现出类似大语言模型的特征，为实现视觉中心的多模态架构提供了新路径。

Evolve in Panorama, Render in View: Panoramic World State Evolution for Video World Models

Video world models should maintain a world that keeps evolving as the viewpoint changes. Yet when the camera turns away and returns, current models often freeze the unobserved scene, lose objects, or change their appearance, because content outside the view has no representation that keeps changing. We present PanoEvo, which uses a Panoramic Evolver to evolve the world state in the latent space of a panoramic video diffusion model, where every viewing direction is present at every time step, so the scene evolves whether or not it is observed. Existing panoramic video models are not trained to evolve a scene from a single image or the objects within it, so we build PanoEvo-Data, an event-centric dataset from real 360° videos, and train the Panoramic Evolver coarse-to-fine as an image-to-panoramic-video model. Since each view covers only a small part of the low-resolution panorama, directly projected views are blurry and often show low-quality details or structural errors. A Perspective Renderer, built on a perspective video diffusion model, reads this state via latent-space projection and direction-restricted cross-attention to render sharp, high-quality views. PanoEvo achieves the best overall score among 23 publicly evaluated models on WRBench and the highest total score on STEVO-Bench. Code, models, and data will be released.

TLDR: PanoEvo introduces a dual-stage framework using a panoramic latent evolver and a perspective renderer to maintain consistent, evolving world states in video world models, addressing object loss and temporal inconsistency in unobserved regions.

ConserveEdit: Source-Anchored Transactions for Drift-Bounded Iterative Image Editing

Instruction-driven image editors produce convincing local edits in one pass, but inside an iterative refinement loop every accepted output becomes the next input, so unintended changes outside the requested region accumulate. We present ConserveEdit, an editor-agnostic controller that treats each refinement step as a transaction against a contract compiled once from the source image. The contract separates what may change (the authorized region of each target, with a bounded halo for shadows and reflections) from what must survive (protected content with explicit tolerances). Candidates from a frozen editor are composed through a source-anchored mask and committed only if target progress, drift on protected content, and image quality pass as separate tests; otherwise the step is rolled back and repaired. For any editor, this keeps every pixel outside the authorized region bit-exact and bounds drift independently of the number of iterations, whereas no fixed scalar trade-off reproduces the separate tests. On all 1053 MagicBrush test edits with a frozen Qwen-Image-Edit, masking is necessary but not sufficient. A direct call already alters the rest of the image (12.1 dB PSNR outside the mask); composed through the source-anchored mask, it stays bit-exact there and its DINO similarity to the ground truth rises from 0.67 to 0.85, matching ConserveEdit, so this gain comes from the mask. Next to automatic masks, however, masked direct editing changes a protected face or text on 48% of turns, and masked scalar selection avoids this mostly by abstaining (23% constrained success). Because its drift test names the violated entity, ConserveEdit shrinks the support instead (2% violations, 53% constrained success); in a simulator with known ground truth it raises constrained success from 55.0% to 61.8%. Automatic grounding remains the main bottleneck. Code and logs will be released.

TLDR: ConserveEdit introduces a transaction-based control framework for iterative image editing that uses source-anchored contracts to prevent unintended 'drift' or content degradation outside of target regions.

TLDR: ConserveEdit 提出了一种基于事务处理的图像迭代编辑控制框架，利用“源锚定合约”机制有效防止了编辑过程中目标区域之外的图像内容退化与漂移。

PCRG: Training-Free Pruning and Caching of Reference Tokens for Video Generation

Reference-to-video diffusion transformers pack the variational autoencoder (VAE) latents of all references and the generated tokens into one full-attention sequence, in which reference tokens make up about half. Because attention cost grows quadratically with sequence length, these tokens take up an even larger share of the attention computation, yet most of them are static, smooth or irrelevant to the output. To accelerate reference-conditioned generation, we propose pruning and caching of reference tokens for video generation (PCRG), a training-free, plug-and-play method that uses two signals the model already computes to assign each reference token to one of three tiers, namely pruned, cached or kept. The first is a low-level redundancy r from VAE latents, since a token whose latent barely changes over time or across its neighbors adds little new content. The second is a high-level importance g from the vision-language-model (VLM) conditioner, where the attention that the prompt and any first or last frames pay to a token tells whether the output needs to read it. Tokens that are not redundant are kept, and redundant tokens are cached if important and pruned otherwise. Pruned tokens leave the sequence before the first step with all rotary position embedding positions unchanged. Cached tokens have their keys and values computed once and reused, and kept tokens are processed as usual. On MiniMax-H3, already distilled to 8 timesteps and quantized to int8, PCRG is 1.57x faster end to end on our V2V-Bench and 1.49x faster on the public VACE-Benchmark, while the quality of VBench drops by only 0.24% and 0.17%, respectively. PCRG shortens the sequence rather than changing how attention is computed or which steps are run, so it also stacks on step caching and sparse attention. On V2V-Bench it reaches 1.86x with TeaCache, 1.82x with Spark at nearly unchanged fidelity and 2.15x with Veda.

TLDR: PCRG is a training-free, plug-and-play optimization method for video diffusion transformers that accelerates generation by dynamically pruning and caching redundant reference tokens based on VAE latent stability and VLM attention importance.

TLDR: PCRG 是一种无需训练的视频扩散模型加速方法，通过利用 VAE 潜在表示的冗余度和 VLM 的注意力信号，动态剪枝和缓存参考 tokens，从而在几乎不损失质量的前提下显著提升生成速度。

REVEAL: Reconstructing Video Amodal Content via Language and Flow Guidance

Amodal perception enables humans to perceive entire objects even when parts are occluded, a remarkable cognitive skill that artificial intelligence struggles to replicate. Recent diffusion-based methods have extended amodal completion from images to videos, yet they lack auxiliary priors to guide the reconstruction of heavily occluded content and to maintain temporal consistency over long sequences. We present REVEAL (REconstructing VidEo Amodal content via Language and flow guidance), a unified diffusion-based framework that integrates complementary motion and semantic priors for video amodal completion. A user-provided text query serves a dual role: it drives open-vocabulary video segmentation to obtain visible masks, and it provides semantic guidance for texture reconstruction. For amodal segmentation, we introduce optical flow guidance: by warping visible masks from previous frames to the current frame, the flow-warped mask propagates visible content into occluded regions and approximates the object's current shape, providing a strong shape prior even under simultaneous deformation and occlusion. For texture completion, text guidance constrains the reconstruction of occluded appearance while providing a stable semantic reference that helps maintain visual coherence throughout the reconstruction. We also contribute LAVAT, a new benchmark featuring long video sequences paired with text descriptions, enabling evaluation of text-guided video amodal completion under heavy occlusion. Extensive experiments demonstrate that REVEAL achieves state-of-the-art performance on existing benchmarks while maintaining high-quality temporal consistency over extended sequences. The project page is available at: https://uark-aicv.github.io/REVEAL/.

TLDR: REVEAL is a diffusion-based framework for video amodal completion that leverages optical flow for shape propagation and text queries for semantic texture guidance, supported by a new benchmark, LAVAT.

Motion-Aware Reward Modeling for Instruction-Guided Video Editing

Reward models for instruction-guided video editing can favor temporal consistency at the expense of motion. We study this failure, termed \emph{motion collapse}, using controlled motion-suppression variants of real edits. On VE-Bench, the evaluated composite reward prefers a motion-suppressed candidate in $97\%$ of cases. Learned video rewards and a VLM judge also struggle with mild suppression, while latent optimization and reward-guided fine-tuning exploit this weakness to increase reward as motion declines. A linear probe of our base reward backbone performs near chance on mild suppression, motivating an explicit inter-frame dynamics input. We propose a motion-aware reward model that combines an RGB frame-difference stream with preference training against motion-suppressed and fabricated-motion negatives. A distillation anchor preserves alignment with human quality judgments. On the VE-Bench motion ladder, our model reduces the rate of selecting motion-suppressed candidates to $0\%$ while achieving a Spearman correlation of $0.57$ with human quality ratings. It also detects unseen suppression variants and preserves motion during reward-guided fine-tuning on two editing pipelines. These results support explicitly modeling motion in rewards for video editing.

TLDR: This paper identifies 'motion collapse' in existing video editing reward models and proposes a motion-aware reward model that incorporates inter-frame dynamics to significantly improve temporal movement preservation.

Sample-Aligned Decoders for Latent Generative Models

Latent generative models synthesize images by decoding generated latents, yet their decoder is trained only to reconstruct encoder latents and never sees the latents it decodes at test time, so the residual errors of the generator are rendered directly into the final image. Existing remedies retrain the generator, add cost at every sampling step, or make the decoder robust to perturbed real latents, assuming that generator errors resemble such perturbations. We find that they do not. Generated latents lie on the encoder latent manifold as closely as real ones, yet follow a different distribution. The decoder should therefore be adapted to the distribution of generated latents, which raises two challenges: (1) unpaired supervision, since a generated latent has no ground-truth image, and (2) latent-space drift, since a decoder freed from paired targets forgets the latent space it must decode. We propose Sample-Aligned Decoders (SAD), a post-training framework that fine-tunes only the decoder of a frozen generator at no inference cost. Generated-Sample Distribution Alignment matches decoded generated latents to unpaired real images in a frozen foundation-model feature space, and Encoder Anchor Preservation keeps the decoder close to the original one on encoder latents. Our analysis shows that the alignment is effective only on generated latents, where it corrects each sample in proportion to how much more often the generator produces such latents than the encoder; on encoder latents it can only work against the anchor. SAD improves four state-of-the-art ImageNet generators across three tokenizers, e.g., FID-50K from 1.15 to 0.98 for REPA-E, complements discriminator guidance, and extends to text-to-image generation. Our code and models are available at https://torpedo2003.github.io/SAD/.

TLDR: The paper introduces Sample-Aligned Decoders (SAD), a post-training framework that aligns the decoder of frozen latent generative models to the distribution of generated latents using unpaired supervision and anchor preservation, improving image quality at zero inference cost.

Do Diffusion Models Learn to Generalize Basic Visual Skills?

Diffusion models generate high-quality images, but how well they generalize is hard to measure. Current evaluations rely on human preference and prompt-following benchmarks for text-to-image models and on distribution-level metrics such as Fréchet Inception Distance (FID) and Inception Score (IS) for class-conditional models, but these cannot tell whether models learn basic visual skills or largely match training patterns. To address this gap, we introduce the Visual Generative Lab (VGL), a controlled experimental framework for studying how diffusion models learn and generalize basic visual skills, including size, position, rotation, count, color, shape, and their compositions. By generating synthetic training data for each skill from explicit rules, VGL allows precise measurement of generalization with task-specific, rule-based metrics. Training models from scratch on these data reveals three patterns in this controlled setting: (1) with our default design, extrapolation is limited: size and position degrade within a few pixels of the training range, count reaches only about 50% accuracy on out-of-range counts, and rotation extrapolation accuracy varies widely across seeds; (2) for compositional generalization, the number of distinct basic visual skill combinations matters more than dataset size; (3) skills mostly fail independently, except position. Code and data-generation scripts: https://github.com/AmishSethi/visual-generative-lab.

TLDR: The paper introduces the Visual Generative Lab (VGL), a synthetic framework for evaluating how diffusion models generalize basic visual skills like counting, rotation, and spatial positioning.
