Matrix-Game 4.0: Harnessing Video Models for Real-Time Playable Game Worlds

Moving from controllable videos to genuinely playable worlds requires persistent world state, executable game rules, and low-latency interaction. We outline a roadmap for the next generation of Matrix-Game that separates game execution from visual generation through an executable game harness, and introduce Matrix-Game 4.0 as the first system built on this design. A lightweight game engine maintains world state and executes interaction rules, a multimodal coding agent translates player ideas into game code and dynamic scene captions, and a renderer based on autoregressive video model transforms engine-rendered low-poly proxies into detailed visuals, conditioned on dynamic captions and memory references. This framework provides continuous guidance for visual generation and enables natural-language creation and modification of scenes and gameplay. Our renderer is trained on aligned low-poly and high-fidelity video pairs and distilled into a causal generator that generates in four-frame chunks with one or two denoising steps. Together with targeted inference optimizations, Matrix-Game 4.0 enables 480p, 60 FPS gameplay on a single NVIDIA RTX5090 with a key-press-to-display latency of 112ms. Single-step generation achieves 83FPS steady-state throughput with 18.2GiB of peak GPU memory usage. With fixed prompts, steady-state throughput also reaches 67FPS on an RTX5080 and 58FPS on an RTX5070Ti. We demonstrate customizable shooter and driving games with engine-enforced mechanics, and release code, model weights, and low-poly game assets to support research and creation of playable game worlds.

TLDR: Matrix-Game 4.0 introduces a decoupled architecture for playable AI game worlds, combining a lightweight game engine for state management with an autoregressive video model renderer to achieve low-latency, 60 FPS interactive experiences.

TLDR: Matrix-Game 4.0 提出了一种解耦的交互式游戏世界架构，通过轻量级游戏引擎管理状态，并利用自回归视频模型进行渲染，实现了低延迟的 60 FPS 游戏体验。

Joints, Camera, Action! Co-Generating 3D Motion and Video for Arbitrary Rigs

Video generation models can now depict remarkably realistic motion. Yet it is unclear how to use this motion beyond pixels: to animate a specific 3D asset and replay the animation in its 3D scene. For instance, a video model can show a monkey jumping over a crossbar, but it cannot return that jump as replayable 3D motion placed in the 3D scene. Here we present a model that directly co-generates the video and 3D motion for any supplied rig, along with a camera that registers this motion to the scene. Prior video-motion models either handle only human skeletons, often adding billions of parameters, or triangulate 3D motion from generated 2D pose maps. We instead keep 3D motion in a separate model that supports arbitrary skeletons, and connect it to a pretrained video model through a small set of cross-attention layers. This separation lets the motion model first learn how skeletons move from motion-only data, which is easier to obtain and augment than paired video. Additionally, our model learns to locate each joint in the video frames, which lets us recover the camera and place the 3D motion in the scene. To train across many skeletons, we use a generative video editor to place animated 3D assets in scenes suited to their actions, curating the edits so their known motion remains valid. Together with human and robot video, this yields nearly 200K training pairs. Our evaluations demonstrate that our model animates diverse rigs from text more faithfully than video-first pipelines, and its motion responds to the surrounding scene. It also turns video into rig motion, rig motion into video, and enables keyframe-based control of the 3D motion. Videos and additional results are available on our project page: https://research.nvidia.com/labs/par/projects/jca/.

TLDR: The paper introduces a framework that co-generates 3D motion and video for arbitrary rigs by coupling a motion-specific model with a pretrained video model via cross-attention, enabling scene-consistent 3D animation and bidirectional motion-video translation.

AEON: Unifying Video and 3D World Models

Current world models for explicit world simulation typically follow two distinct pathways: (1) video generative models (e.g., Sora), which simulate realistic appearances but fail to preserve 3D consistency; and (2) 3D generative models (e.g., Marble), which generate geometrically consistent scenes but often yield static, less realistic observations. We present AEON, a unified framework that bridges video-based and 3D-based world models and combines the benefits of both. The key insight of AEON lies in a generative model that operates within the text- or image-conditioned video latent space while producing renderable 4D graphics primitives---specifically, 4D Gaussian Splatting (4DGS)---as the explicit world representation. We train a 4D reconstruction model to predict both 4DGS and camera poses from monocular videos, serving as our initialized 4D decoder. Furthermore, we design a domain adapter that translates generative video latents into reconstructive 4D latents through a distillation process on the video VAE. AEON naturally supports video-based world modeling by rendering 4DGS at predicted camera trajectories, and constructs a persistent 4D environment with scene dynamics for realistic world simulation. Consequently, AEON empowers a diverse range of world modeling applications and achieves state-of-the-art performance on standard benchmarks. Project Page: https://weiqi-zhang.github.io/AEON

TLDR: AEON is a unified world modeling framework that bridges video generative models and 3D reconstruction by generating 4D Gaussian Splatting primitives from video latents to ensure both visual realism and geometric consistency.

TLDR: AEON 是一个统一的世界模型框架，通过将视频生成模型潜在空间映射为可渲染的 4D 高斯溅射（4DGS）图元，解决了视频模型缺乏 3D 一致性与 3D 模型缺乏真实感的矛盾。

SolarRenderer: Few-Step Generative Reference Rendering

Game engines and coding agents can specify the layout, motion, and camera of a scene, yet turning such coarse structure into realistic video requires a renderer that follows the structure and completes the appearance it leaves open. Existing renderers are tied to the representation of one engine, need tens of denoising steps or a costly distillation of their own, and learn from conditions that are not aligned with their target videos. We present SolarRenderer, a structure-conditioned video renderer that adapts a frozen MiniMax-H3 33B backbone with two lightweight LoRAs. Structural videos enter the native reference pathway of the backbone at the positions of the frames they describe, without a type identifier and described only in plain text, so that a single control LoRA follows arbitrary structural representations. Starting from a pretrained four-step LoRA, structure-guided distribution matching renders in four steps, 12.3 times faster than 50-step sampling, while preserving the learned control. A block-causal variant extends the same design to streaming and renders 768p video in real time. To train the model, we build a frame-aligned corpus of 136K clips from game engines, simulators, and YouTube videos. Experiments on an in-distribution test set and a generalization benchmark of 16 structural representations show that SolarRenderer achieves state-of-the-art results across both, and generalizes to 11 unseen representations, including pose skeletons, drawings, and point clouds.

TLDR: SolarRenderer is a structure-conditioned video generation model that leverages a frozen 33B backbone with lightweight LoRAs to render realistic video from arbitrary structural inputs (like game engines or pose skeletons) in just four steps.

TLDR: SolarRenderer 是一种结构条件视频生成模型，它通过在冻结的 33B 主干模型上使用轻量级 LoRA，实现了仅需四步即可将任意结构输入（如游戏引擎或姿态骨架）转换为高质量视频的目标。

MindView: Benchmarking Generative Multi-View Spatial Intelligence

Spatial intelligence has recently been assessed through generative tasks such as spatially grounded image editing, yet language-guided generation of spatially consistent views from partial multi-view observations remains underexplored. We introduce MindView, a benchmark for generative multi-view spatial intelligence with 1,500 samples from 513 scenes across three indoor datasets. Its seven tasks form two families: geometry-dominant tasks specify changes in camera position and orientation, while semantics-dominant tasks require inferring target viewpoints by grounding referred objects and interpreting spatial relations and reference-frame cues. Evaluation of eleven generators reveals substantial room for improvement on MindView. A paired context analysis further shows that all eleven generators obtain lower overall scores under the expanded context setting. These findings motivate us to investigate whether task-specific supervision can improve target-view generation and whether models can use additional multi-view evidence effectively. We therefore fine-tune BAGEL on the MindView training dataset, improving its overall score by 24.3 points and yielding gains across all seven MindView tasks. Beyond these generation gains, the fine-tuned model also yields higher scores on three spatial-understanding benchmarks without training on their annotations, suggesting that learning to generate spatially grounded views may benefit spatial understanding. To improve evidence utilization under expanded context, we propose MindCue, a training-free spatial evidence selector guided by a target-view hypothesis. MindCue reverses the drop in overall score under expanded context and outperforms the standard-context baseline. Overall, MindView provides a concrete testbed for generative multi-view spatial intelligence.

TLDR: MindView is a new benchmark for generative multi-view spatial intelligence, introducing seven geometry- and semantics-based tasks and a spatial evidence selector called MindCue to improve spatial consistency in generative models.

TLDR: MindView 是一个用于评估生成式多视图空间智能的基准测试，包含七个几何与语义任务，并提出了 MindCue 空间证据选择器，以提升生成模型在多视图环境下的空间一致性。

Learning the 4D World through Point Trajectory Forecasting

Reliable multi-view forecasting requires videos from different cameras to describe the same evolving 3D scene. To this end, we introduce Video-Point Model (VPM), a world model that conditions on multi-view video histories and a language instruction to jointly forecast future videos from arbitrary viewpoints and the 3D trajectories of persistent scene points. We extend a pretrained world model with camera-conditioned multi-view processing that integrates complementary observations across cameras, and add a trajectory head that predicts how physical points move through time. Each trajectory tracks a single point across all views, and supervising these trajectories alongside video generation therefore anchors appearance changes in every camera to a shared description of 3D motion. To support training and evaluation, we build VPM-Data, a simulation corpus pairing synchronized multi-view videos with persistent point trajectories, and VP-WorldBench, a forecasting benchmark spanning simulated and real environments. We further extend VPM to robot control by adding robot actions as a token modality in the generator. Action tokens share the point-supervised backbone and are denoised jointly with future videos, thereby grounding the policy in the same 3D-aware representation. On VP-WorldBench and existing 4D generation benchmarks, point supervision improves both video fidelity and 3D motion accuracy over a video-only counterpart, and in simulated and real-robot experiments, it also raises the manipulation success of the action-enabled model.

TLDR: The paper introduces the Video-Point Model (VPM), a world model that jointly forecasts multi-view videos and 3D point trajectories to ground video generation in physical 3D motion, also enabling downstream robot control.

Continual Concept Personalization in Diffusion Models via Residual-Space Adaptation

Text-to-image personalization aims to equip pretrained diffusion models with the ability to learn user-specific concepts from only a few reference images. Existing methods, however, typically train each concept independently or assume that all concepts are available simultaneously. In practical applications, concepts often arrive sequentially, requiring models to continually learn new subjects, objects, or styles while minimizing interference with previously acquired concepts. We address this continual concept personalization setting by extending residual-space adaptation to the stochastic adaptation process of pretrained diffusion models. Our work achieves the highest concept fidelity among competing continual personalization methods while maintaining near-zero forgetting. Beyond these empirical gains, the learned residual-space dynamics reveal where adaptation capacity is concentrated within the diffusion model. Restricting adaptation to the identified layers by our method preserves nearly the same performance while reducing the number of trainable parameters by up to 72\%, demonstrating that residual-space dynamics can identify the layers that contribute most to continual concept personalization and directly guide more parameter-efficient adaptation.

TLDR: This paper introduces a residual-space adaptation method for diffusion models to enable continual concept personalization without catastrophic forgetting, significantly improving parameter efficiency.

OverSeer: Multi-Agent World Models Beyond What Agents See

Despite the success of video world models, extending them to multiple agents is difficult because it requires generating several agent-specific video streams that are consistent in time and between agents. To address this gap, we introduce OverSeer, a multi-agent world model that augments egocentric agent views with auxiliary camera views. We suggest that, by fixing the auxiliary views in space, they act as a proxy for a world-centric representation: Unlike the agents' views, which can change entirely due to a simple change in agent position, changes in the auxiliary views directly map to localized changes in the world state. We show that these fixed views substantially help the model to maintain consistency. We also show that, due to their redundant and semi-static nature, the auxiliary views can be efficiently compressed with a delta-VAE that encodes only the changes relative to a static reference image, reducing the number of tokens required to process them. Experiments in Minecraft and Doom demonstrate improved world and agent consistency, with qualitative results on real-world multi-camera captures indicating that the approach is not tied to game engines. Our compression analysis further shows that delta-VAE substantially reduces the computational overhead of auxiliary-view generation. We conclude that these fixed views provide a simple and efficient way to improve multi-agent world models.

TLDR: OverSeer is a multi-agent world model that achieves temporal and spatial consistency by incorporating fixed, auxiliary camera views as a world-centric proxy, optimized via a delta-VAE for efficient token compression.

TLDR: OverSeer 是一种多智能体世界模型，通过引入固定的辅助视角作为世界中心表示，利用 delta-VAE 对其进行压缩，从而提升了多智能体生成过程中的空间与时间一致性。

Latent Bridge: From Video Latents to 3D Geometry via Feature Alignment

Video generation and feed-forward 3D reconstruction typically use separate representations, requiring RGB decoding and visual re-encoding to recover geometry from generated latents. We propose Latent Bridge, which connects video generative latent spaces with geometric feature spaces to directly predict 3D geometry from video latents. We develop representation-specific bridges for Wan2.1 and V-RAE, enabling feed-forward prediction of cameras, depth, and 3D point clouds without the intermediate RGB pathway. Each bridge is trained on RGB videos through feature distillation, while the video encoder and geometry model remain frozen. Training requires neither ground-truth geometry nor execution of the downstream geometric aggregator or prediction heads. At inference, the same video latents support both RGB decoding and geometry prediction. The same trained bridge can be reused for image-to-video and text-to-video generation within the same video latent space, without bridge retraining. Experiments on Co3Dv2 and WildRGB-D demonstrate competitive geometric reconstruction quality, efficient end-to-end generation and reconstruction, and faster latent-to-geometry decoding, highlighting latent alignment as a practical and reusable interface between video generation and 3D reconstruction. Our code is available at: https://github.com/hustvl/Latent-Bridge.

TLDR: Latent Bridge enables direct 3D geometry prediction from frozen video generative latents by distilling geometric features, bypassing computationally expensive RGB decoding and re-encoding steps.

TLDR: Latent Bridge 通过特征蒸馏实现从视频生成模型的潜空间直接预测 3D 几何结构，无需经过 RGB 解码和重编码，从而提升了生成与重建的效率。

Code2Video-Compass: A Comprehensive Benchmark for General-Purpose Code-Based Video Generation across T2V, I2V, and V2V

Code-based video generation offers fine-grained control, targeted editing, and component reuse compared with direct video generation. This approach requires multimodal large language models (MLLMs) to translate visual and temporal requirements into executable programs that produce complete videos. However, existing code-based benchmarks primarily target specialized tasks, leaving complete video creation across diverse creative categories and input modalities insufficiently evaluated. We introduce Code2Video-Compass, a general-purpose benchmark for code-based video generation across text-to-video (T2V), image-to-video (I2V), and video-to-video (V2V) settings. Code2Video-Compass comprises 2,104 tasks spanning 10 categories, 24 subtypes, and three complexity dimensions covering construction, dynamic animation, and state dependency. It combines tasks curated from textual instructions and visual references with corresponding T2V, I2V, and V2V tasks derived from complete professional and user-created videos. We propose a dual-level evaluation framework combining 12 objective metrics and five subjective metrics. Objective evaluation covers operational performance, video quality, text-video alignment, and reference fidelity, while subjective evaluation uses an MLLM judge to evaluate instruction following and holistic video quality. Extensive evaluation of 15 recent proprietary and open-weight MLLMs from nine families reveals that even leading models face challenges in reliably generating high-quality complete videos aligned with textual and visual requirements, with performance generally declining as construction, dynamic animation, and state-dependency complexity increases. We anticipate that Code2Video-Compass will advance reliable, coherent, and controllable code-based video generation and inspire broader progress in programmatic visual creation.

TLDR: Code2Video-Compass is a new benchmark for code-based video generation that evaluates MLLMs across T2V, I2V, and V2V tasks, addressing the limitations of existing specialized benchmarks.

TLDR: Code2Video-Compass 是一个针对基于代码的视频生成的综合基准测试，涵盖了 T2V、I2V 和 V2V 任务，旨在解决现有基准测试在多模态视频生成评估上的不足。

StreamEval: Evaluating Interactive Streaming Video Generation Models

As streaming video generation advances, enabling real-time interaction with generated video is becoming increasingly important. We introduce StreamEval, a unified benchmark for interactive streaming video generation spanning five tasks: avatar dialogue, avatar performance, world generation, instruction editing, and reference editing. StreamEval comprises 300 scripted test cases lasting 15-300s, each specifying initial assets, incrementally arriving multimodal requests, and explicit requirements for state changes and content preservation. Performance is assessed along four evaluation dimensions: task execution, state consistency, visual fidelity, and audiovisual coordination, instantiated through ten task-specific metrics on a 0-100 scale. Evaluating 37 distinct models across 300 test cases, we find that high visual quality and identity retention do not necessarily coincide with reliable request execution or sustained state consistency. In particular, request execution remains challenging: the highest task-specific scores are 36.90 for Following among seven avatar-performance models, 25.93 for Following in text-conditioned world generation, and 15.00 for Edit Success among three reported reference-editing models. These findings identify reliable request execution under timing and preservation constraints as a central challenge for advancing omni-modal real-time interactive systems. The leaderboard is available at https://streameval.ai

TLDR: StreamEval is a comprehensive benchmark designed to evaluate interactive streaming video generation across five key tasks, highlighting that current models struggle significantly with request execution and state consistency.

Auto-Director: Leveraging Multi-Agent Collaboration for Autonomous Video Editing

Autonomous video editing from open-world resources remains a challenging problem in multimedia content creation. Retrieval-based methods are constrained by fixed source material and lack creative flexibility, while generative models struggle with long-horizon coherence and often diverge from user intent. Both paradigms fail to replicate the structured, multi-stage reasoning that underlies professional post-production. To address these limitations, we present Auto-Director, a tool-augmented multi-agent framework that mirrors professional editorial workflows: a Planning Agent decomposes abstract topics into fact-grounded scripts via retrieval-augmented planning; a Material Agent indexes open-world footage through shot segmentation and visual tagging; an Editor Agent assembles semantically aligned clips with MLLM-driven spatial adaptation; and a Critic Agent governs an iterative Reflect-and-Revise loop that checks scene-level alignment and pacing. To rigorously evaluate this new paradigm, we introduce VEdit-Bench, a benchmark of 100 expert-curated topics with human-annotated Golden Scripts and a five-dimensional metric suite covering Narrative Logic, Factual Accuracy, Semantic Alignment, Compositional Quality, and Stylistic Cohesion. Extensive experiments demonstrate that Auto-Director significantly outperforms baseline methods in both automated metrics and human evaluation, effectively bridging the gap between amateurs and professional-grade video production.

TLDR: Auto-Director is a multi-agent framework that automates professional video editing by decomposing the process into planning, material retrieval, editing, and iterative critique stages.

TLDR: Auto-Director 是一个多智能体框架，通过将视频编辑流程拆解为规划、素材检索、剪辑和迭代审查四个阶段，实现了专业级的自动化视频创作。

ABACUS: A Benchmark for Image Editing Models to Assess Object Category and Counting Under Scene Conservation

Instruction-based image editing models can now add, remove a specified number of objects or change their category while preserving the scene. Yet these models often fail to produce the correct number, and these failures grow sharply as scenes become more complex and the requested count increases. Current benchmarks largely miss this problem: they score edits with holistic similarity metrics or with vision-language model (VLM) judges that are themselves poor at counting, so numerical errors go unnoticed or are graded incorrectly. As a result, the true numerical ability of today's editing models has never been accurately measured. We address this issue with ABACUS, a new benchmark, evaluation protocol and dedicated scoring metric for numerical image editing. Our protocol is judge-free: no VLM or MLLM scores an edit or counts objects (a VLM only confirms the category of rare ambiguous crops). Instead, it verifies each edit at the instance level against panoptic ground truth confirmed by detector consensus, checking both that the exact requested count was achieved and that the rest of the scene was preserved, across the add, remove and change operations. By evaluating leading image editors, our method reveals substantial numerical editing failures that worsen with increasing scene complexity and object count; failures that prior benchmarks often overlooked or misjudged.

TLDR: ABACUS is a judge-free benchmark designed to rigorously evaluate numerical accuracy in instruction-based image editing by using panoptic ground truth verification instead of unreliable VLM-based scoring.

TLDR: ABACUS 是一个旨在评估图像编辑模型数值准确性的基准测试，通过基于全景真值的验证方案（而非依赖视觉语言模型）来准确衡量模型在添加、删除和修改对象时的计数能力。

SplatFlux: Feed-Forward 4D Gaussian Reconstruction and Generation from Video Latents

This work presents SplatFlux, a feed-forward latent-to-4DGS framework for both video-based 4D reconstruction and conditional 4D generation. Our key observation is that observed videos and conditionally generated content can be represented in the same pretrained video latent space, allowing both tasks to be formulated as latent-to-4DGS decoding. To construct compact and temporally coherent 4D scenes, SplatFlux introduces a static-dynamic decoupled 4D Gaussian representation that explicitly predicts dynamic probabilities for Gaussian primitives. This representation separates static scene structures from moving regions, enabling complete 4D scene construction in a feed-forward manner through static aggregation and dynamic motion interpolation. To learn static-dynamic decomposition and dynamic Gaussian motion without costly annotations, we further develop an auxiliary distillation strategy that provides pseudo dynamic masks and optical-flow cues. Extensive experiments across 3D and 4D reconstruction and generation demonstrate that SplatFlux achieves state-of-the-art performance.

TLDR: SplatFlux is a feed-forward framework that unifies video-based 4D reconstruction and generation by decoding pretrained video latents into a static-dynamic decoupled 4D Gaussian representation.

Attractive Faces Skew Text-to-Image Reward Models and Leaderboards

Human preference is now central to the evaluation, guidance, finetuning, and ranking of text-to-image models. However, the susceptibility of human preferences to bias in image generation remains underexplored. We identify a key observation: for human faces, perceived attractiveness overrides image quality in preference judgments, even when comparing real photographs of identical fidelity. In a controlled user study, annotators rank more attractive faces as higher quality, even when unattractive real faces are compared with attractive AI-generated ones. We examine the downstream consequences of this bias. Firstly, simulating Elo rankings from user study data shows attractiveness can invert a leaderboard's ranking, elevating synthetic attractive faces over real, unattractive ones. BUMP: Body-driven Unified Multi-object Physics for HOI Synthesis

Generating human-object interactions (HOIs) with multiple objects is challenging because the resulting motions arise from different underlying causes. Human motion reflects semantic intent, intentionally manipulated objects must remain coordinated with the human, while surrounding objects move primarily as physical consequences of contact and collision. We present BUMP, a text-driven framework for multi-object HOI generation based on a simple principle: learn intentional motion and simulate physical consequences. Our method first generates full-body human motion from text and scene context, then predicts trajectories for a small number of intentionally manipulated driver objects conditioned on the human motion. We then obtain responder object motions through physics simulation, allowing the framework to handle varying numbers and configurations of surrounding objects without expanding the learned trajectory output space. To address the scarcity of paired full-body multi-object HOI data, we combine heterogeneous supervision from human-only motion, full-body HOI, and hand-centric manipulation data augmented with plausible body motion. We further introduce outcome-conditioned interaction editing, which infers a local modification of the causal driver object trajectory from a desired responder object behavior without requiring an explicit target driver object trajectory. Experiments demonstrate strong performance on both single-object and multi-object HOI benchmarks. Qualitative results showcase diverse scenarios involving inter-object relationships and edits, highlighting the advantage of our unified model formulation.

TLDR: BUMP is a text-driven framework for multi-object human-object interaction (HOI) synthesis that decouples intentional human motion from physics-based object interactions to handle complex scenes efficiently.
