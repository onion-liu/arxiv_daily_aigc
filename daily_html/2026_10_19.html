UniState: Unifying Memory and Intervention for Controllable World Simulation

World models offer increasingly rich environments for exploration, yet intervening in a world while observing the consequences from freely chosen viewpoints remains challenging. We tackle these challenges by introducing UniState, a framework for controllable world simulation built on a unified world-state representation. This representation unifies spatial memory and user interventions. Users specify object motion directly within spatial memory, while independently choosing the camera trajectory from which the resulting world state is observed. This unification makes the relationship between the scene and the requested changes explicit, removing ambiguity between object motion and viewpoint change from the control specification. Crucially, specifying an intervention does not require prescribing every resulting movement: the video model can synthesize additional motion within controlled objects and plausible interactions with their surroundings. We develop training and sampling strategies that help generated videos follow the specified object motion and camera trajectory while preserving this capacity to generate responses beyond the prescribed transformations. Experiments demonstrate more accurate joint object and camera control than the existing methods, alongside qualitative evidence that intervention-induced scene changes persist across rollouts.

TLDR: UniState is a unified world-simulation framework that decouples object motion and camera trajectories through a spatial memory representation, enabling controllable video generation with persistent physical interactions.

TLDR: UniState 提出了一种统一的世界模拟框架，通过空间记忆表示将物体运动与摄像机轨迹解耦，实现了具有持久物理交互的可控视频生成。

MT Forcing: Towards Stable and Consistent Multi-Turn Image Editing with Autoregressive Diffusion

Multi-turn image editing aims to modify an image according to a sequence of instructions, enabling more effective interactive visual content creation for users. However, existing methods often exhibit noticeable drift after only a few editing turns, manifesting in two aspects: 1) unstable visual quality, with accumulating artifacts and degraded details; and 2) inconsistent image content, with unintended changes to preserved regions and prior edits. To address these challenges, we propose MT Forcing, a novel training paradigm for multi-turn image editing with unified multimodal models (UMMs) based on autoregressive diffusion. Specifically, we propose Single-Turn Distribution Matching Distillation (Single-Turn DMD), which supervises student self-rollouts with a teacher that applies cumulative edits to the source image in a single turn, providing visual-quality guidance without recursive error accumulation. Additionally, we introduce Teacher-Forcing Guidance, which supervises history-dependent transitions on reference trajectories during warmup and as a regularizer during distillation, encouraging cross-turn content consistency beyond what per-turn distribution matching alone can ensure. Extensive experiments demonstrate that our approach significantly improves the multi-turn image editing capabilities of UMMs, yielding more stable visual quality and stronger cross-turn consistency.

TLDR: MT Forcing introduces a training paradigm using Single-Turn Distribution Matching Distillation and Teacher-Forcing Guidance to mitigate drift, quality degradation, and inconsistency in autoregressive multi-turn image editing.

TLDR: MT Forcing 提出了一种基于单步分布匹配蒸馏和教师强制引导的训练范式，旨在解决自回归多轮图像编辑中的视觉质量下降和内容不一致问题。

A 3D-Driven Framework for Moving-Camera Perspective-to-360° Video Generation

Turning a moving-camera perspective video into a 360° video requires more than just generating content outside the input frame: observations revealed over time must be preserved, unseen regions completed, and the resulting scene kept consistent as the camera viewpoint changes. Existing methods assign these different requirements to a single conditional video model, requiring them to be jointly learned from panoramic video data. We instead present a modular, 3D-driven framework that distributes these three responsibilities across specialized components, all connected through a shared scene representation. Input observations are reconstructed explicitly in 3D, while a pretrained panoramic image model completes unobserved content from a selected panoramic render. This generated content is lifted into the shared 3D scene, where it remains spatially anchored across camera viewpoints. Without requiring any additional panoramic-video training, our framework achieves state-of-the-art input-view fidelity, perceptual quality, and geometric consistency on a static-scene subset of the Argus evaluation set. These results support our decomposed, 3D-driven formulation as a promising alternative to direct video generation in this setting.

TLDR: This paper proposes a modular, 3D-driven framework for converting moving-camera perspective videos into 360° content by leveraging explicit 3D reconstruction and a pretrained panoramic image model, avoiding the need for panoramic video training.

ATLAS: Attention-Guided Test-Time Latent Selection for Compositional Visual Autoregressive Generation

Visual autoregressive models produce convincing images but can misassign attributes and spatial relations. Searching multiple generation paths can improve composition, yet completing every candidate is costly. We introduce ATLAS, a training-free framework that uses the frozen generator's internal attention to guide search before final images are available. Within-prompt diagnostics identify attention-derived scores that predict final compositional preferences on Infinity, with category-dependent transfer to Switti. ATLAS uses these on-the-fly signals to progressively concentrate computation on promising trajectories. It maintains multiple coarse alternatives, prunes candidates without decoding at intermediate scales, and reserves image-space evaluation for a final choice among partial trajectories. ATLAS scales effectively on both backbones, achieving higher mean scores on the evaluated T2I-CompBench and GenEval categories as more computation is devoted to search. At the largest tested budget, the targeted T2I-CompBench mean improves by over 10% relative to each backbone. Separate profiles show over 50% less generation time than completing and evaluating all candidates at equal initial counts, alongside lower peak allocated GPU memory. Relative time savings are larger at the higher tested candidate count. Together, these results support scalable compositional search: additional sampling expands exploration, while attention-guided selection limits continuation cost without parameter updates or gradient-based optimization.

TLDR: ATLAS is a training-free framework for visual autoregressive models that uses internal attention maps to prune suboptimal generation trajectories early, significantly improving compositional accuracy while reducing computational costs.

TLDR: ATLAS 是一种针对视觉自回归模型的无需训练框架，通过利用内部注意力图在生成早期剪枝低质量路径，在显著提高组合生成准确性的同时降低了计算成本。

Painting Change Map as guidance for generating painting processes

Digital art generation has undergone a massive paradigm shift, moving from static image synthesis to dynamic video generation that can mimic the human painting process. While existing models can synthesize visually stunning time-lapses of paintings being created or "unpainted," they largely treat the creative process as a black box. Current conditioning mechanisms are either too rigid, relying on static geometric cues like Canny edges or depth maps, or too holistic, utilizing simple scalar progress variables that offer no localized control. Consequently, users lack the agency to steer how a painting evolves over time. In this work, we argue that true control over the video generation of artwork requires a joint understanding of two fundamental dimensions: where an artist acts, and when those actions take place. To achieve this, we introduce the Painting Change Map, a novel spatio-temporal control mechanism that explicitly tracks normalized structural changes across a 1,000-timestep continuum, a resolution sufficient to capture fine-grained stroke-level changes while remaining computationally tractable. By mapping localized visual differences into an encoded RGB space, our representation enables fine-grained guidance for both the forward painting and reverse unpainting processes. Extensive benchmarking demonstrates the efficacy of our approach. We outperform existing control baselines and achieve competitive or superior results compared to state-of-the-art painting methods. Furthermore, under oracle conditions, our method surpasses all baselines by a significant margin. Code is available at https://github.com/Markus-Pobitzer/painting-change-map.

TLDR: The authors introduce the 'Painting Change Map,' a novel spatio-temporal control mechanism that enables fine-grained guidance for painting and unpainting processes in video generation by tracking structural changes across a 1,000-timestep continuum.

TLDR: 本文提出了一种名为“绘画变化图”（Painting Change Map）的时空控制机制，通过在1000个时间步长内追踪结构变化，实现了对数字绘画视频生成过程中“哪里”以及“何时”进行绘画的精细化控制。

MotionShift: Environment-Compatible Motion Transfer via Planner-Evaluator Synergistic Adaptation

Existing motion transfer methods excel at exact, rigid motion replication from reference videos to target scenes, whereas they often fail to account for the compatibility between the transferred motion and the target scene environment in accordance with physical laws. In this work, we propose MotionShift, a novel motion transfer framework that achieves high-fidelity motion transfer while ensuring physical compatibility with the target scene. In particular, we design a planner--evaluator iterative motion adaptation mechanism, in which a Large Language Model (LLM) acts as the planner to generate motion trajectories by leveraging its strong semantic reasoning capabilities, while a Vision-Language Model (VLM) serves as the evaluator to rectify the motion based on its superior visual perception and scene understanding. Through this planner--evaluator synergistic reasoning process, the motion is adapted to the target scene in a training-free manner. The adapted motion is then used as a generative condition for an image-to-video (I2V) model to render videos in the target scene. Extensive experiments demonstrate that MotionShift achieves high-fidelity and physically compatible motion transfer across diverse and complex scenes. Moreover, we show that MotionShift can be readily integrated into existing trajectory-conditioned I2V models, endowing them with realistic motion transfer capabilities.

TLDR: MotionShift is a training-free framework that utilizes an LLM-planner and VLM-evaluator to adapt reference motion for physical compatibility in target environments before rendering it via I2V models.

TLDR: MotionShift 是一个无需训练的框架，利用 LLM 作为规划器、VLM 作为评估器，在将参考动作渲染到目标视频前，对其进行物理兼容性调整。

Holo4D: Holistic 4D Reconstruction as Geometric Control for Video Diffusion

Synthesizing a monocular video from a new camera trajectory requires preserving the observed scene geometry and motion while completing regions that become newly visible. Feed-forward 4D reconstructors recover the structure of the observed scene, whereas video diffusion models provide a generative prior for completing unseen content. We introduce Holo4D, a video reshooting framework that uses reconstructed 3D trajectories as a holistic source-view 4D scaffold. We project these trajectories into the requested views and encode their geometry and source appearance to guide a pretrained video diffusion model. Together with the source video and target cameras, the scaffold grounds generation in reconstructed observations while leaving unsupported regions to the generative prior. Experiments demonstrate the value of trajectory-based conditioning for camera control and novel-view synthesis. The framework accommodates different 4D reconstructors without retraining the video generator, allowing it to benefit from future advances in source reconstruction. More details can be found in our project page.

TLDR: Holo4D is a video reshooting framework that utilizes reconstructed 3D trajectories as a geometric scaffold to guide pretrained video diffusion models, enabling consistent novel-view synthesis from monocular inputs.

GeoART: Geometry-Aware Refinement via Test-Time Scaling for Camera-Conditioned Video Diffusion

Camera-conditioned video diffusion models often suffer from inaccurate camera control and geometric inconsisten- cies because camera constraints and estimated geometry are only weakly enforced during denoising. Existing reme- dies often require costly geometry-aware fine-tuning or ad- ditional 3D supervision. We introduce GeoART, a training- free self-refinement framework that improves camera con- trollability through geometry-aware test-time scaling. At selected denoising steps, GeoART generates multiple can- didates within the Predict-and-Perturb cycle and eval- uates them using a geometry prior, without directly modify- ing the denoising trajectory. A confidence gate suppresses unreliable geometric evidence, while rank-calibrated prob- abilistic selection combines score magnitude and relative rank to choose the next refinement state. Experiments demonstrate consistent improvements in camera controlla- bility, multi-view consistency, and visual quality over exist- ing sampling and self-refinement methods, without training or modifying the pretrained video diffusion model

TLDR: GeoART is a training-free, geometry-aware self-refinement framework for video diffusion models that improves camera control and consistency through test-time candidate selection and ranking.

GeoV2V: Geometry-Grounded Video Diffusion Model for Driving Scene Generation

Novel view synthesis under trajectory changes is essential for autonomous driving simulation, yet existing methods struggle to generate consistent videos when extrapolating to unseen viewpoints such as lane shifts. Reconstruction-based approaches maintain geometric consistency but degrade in visual quality under large viewpoint changes, while reconstruct-then-restore methods often produce visually plausible frames yet suffer from geometric and temporal inconsistencies. We present GeoV2V, a geometry-grounded video-to-video diffusion framework that synthesizes temporally coherent driving videos along novel trajectories by integrating LiDAR and depth-derived geometric priors with full-video conditioning in the Wan 2.1 backbone. A key challenge in learning cross-lane transformations is the lack of synchronized multi-trajectory supervision in real-world datasets. To address this, we also introduce Para4D, a synthetic dataset capturing synchronized multi-lane driving videos within dynamic scenes, which provides explicit supervision for lane-shift generation and serves as a strong training prior for geometry-consistent view synthesis. Experiments on Waymo, nuScenes, and Para4D demonstrate state-of-the-art performance in visual quality, geometric consistency, and robustness to complex lighting dynamics under large trajectory shifts. Project page: https://xiyuche.github.io/GeoV2V/

TLDR: GeoV2V is a geometry-grounded video diffusion framework that leverages LiDAR/depth priors and a new synthetic dataset, Para4D, to achieve temporally consistent novel view synthesis for autonomous driving trajectories.

TLDR: GeoV2V 是一个几何引导的视频扩散框架，通过利用 LiDAR/深度先验以及名为 Para4D 的新合成数据集，实现了自动驾驶场景中轨迹变换下的高质量、高一致性视频合成。

Map2Vid: Embodied Navigation Video Generation with Human-Friendly Motion Control

Controllable video generation has recently achieved impressive visual quality, yet existing control paradigms remain poorly suited to embodied navigation. Text prompts are spatially ambiguous, while camera-trajectory control relies on low-level motion specifications that are unintuitive for human users and difficult to manipulate. In this work, we introduce Map2Vid, a novel task that reformulates embodied navigation video generation as a BEV-map-to-video synthesis problem, together with a large-scale benchmark for systematic evaluation. By using BEV maps, which provide a visual representation of global scene layout and intended navigation routes, Map2Vid enables human-friendly navigation video generation. To support this task, we construct a dedicated dataset with over 100K navigation sequences across 61 indoor scenes, each paired with egocentric videos, BEV maps, camera trajectories and language instructions. We further develop a unified framework that integrates multiple control signals, together with a curriculum training strategy that progressively weakens non-map inputs, enabling flexible inference under both full conditioning and map-only input. Extensive experiments across multiple foundation generation models demonstrate that controllable navigation video generation via BEV map is a viable and promising direction.

TLDR: Map2Vid introduces a novel BEV-map-to-video synthesis framework for embodied navigation, utilizing a large-scale dataset to enable human-friendly control of egocentric video generation.

TLDR: Map2Vid 提出了一种基于鸟瞰图（BEV）的具身导航视频生成框架，并通过构建大规模数据集实现了更符合人类直觉的场景导航视频合成。

Reflection-Guided Dynamics Rectification for Text-to-Image Generation

Fine-grained rectification of existing generations under a limited additional budget remains a key problem in test-time optimization (TTO) for image generation. As for prior approaches, they could identify residual errors, but could not directly ensure that the model acts on the feedback during reasoning. Such errors stem from multiple sources, such as the initial noise or text prompt, and ultimately induce an execution gap of failing to handle potential deviations along the trajectory generating. However, classic models generally cannot remedy this gap. To narrow it, we propose Reflection-Guided Dynamics Rectification (RGDR), which turns reflections on completed outputs into corrective interventions on the process of sampling dynamics at the trajectory level. Specifically, RGDR could impact every sampling step by adapting to instantaneous intervention. Moreover, RGDR generates flexible candidates while adjudicating between them and the reference. As experiments show, rectification from RGDR starts from the existing base trajectory and reflection, and RGDR is substantially more effective than former trajectory exploration. Under matched budgets for additional image generation, RGDR's gain in the cross-model average final checklist score over the reference is $1.51\times$ that of TIR prompt refinement and $1.81\times$ that of Dynamic CFG.

TLDR: The paper introduces Reflection-Guided Dynamics Rectification (RGDR), a test-time optimization method that uses reflection to intervene in and correct the diffusion sampling trajectory for higher-quality, error-corrected text-to-image generation.

TLDR: 本文提出了一种名为反射引导动力学校正（RGDR）的测试时优化方法，通过将对生成结果的反射转化为对扩散采样轨迹的实时干预，有效纠正生成偏差并提升图像质量。

Goal Images Are Control Targets: Spatially-Grounded Goal-Image Synthesis for Robotic Manipulation

Goal-conditioned manipulation policies are steered by a goal image, a picture of the desired post-manipulation scene synthesized from the current observation and a language instruction. The prevailing assumption is that better image-editing quality yields better goals, so goal generators are built and compared using general image-editing metrics. We show this assumption is false. Across two independent goal-conditioned policies on CALVIN, the image editors that top aggregate editing-quality leaderboards (Gemini 3 Pro Image and GPT Image 1.5) produce worse control targets than our proposed far smaller finetuned open models, and the gap widens sharply for the multi-step (long-horizon) tasks robots actually face. A per-dimension analysis explains why. Downstream policy success is governed almost entirely by manipulation adherence (selecting the right object and the right final state; Spearman $ρ\approx 0.9$ with success), while photorealism and aesthetic fidelity, precisely where large proprietary editors excel, are uncorrelated with success ($ρ\approx 0$). This dissociation holds on both CALVIN and the real-world-aligned SIMPLER-Bridge benchmark. We then present SGE-Goal, a spatially-grounded decomposed editing framework that predicts initial and final 2D edit-region masks before goal-image synthesis. SGE-Goal turns open base models (FLUX.1 Kontext, FLUX.2 klein) into the strongest goal generators we evaluate on CALVIN, and second only to Gemini 3 Pro Image on SIMPLER-Bridge. Base models fine-tuned with SGE-Goal also hold up under chaining far better than the best proprietary editor, sustaining roughly seven times the success rate at the longest (five-step) chains. We release code, datasets, checkpoints, and a human-aligned diagnostic evaluator. Our results argue that goal generators must be selected by downstream utility, not by editing-quality leaderboards.

TLDR: The paper challenges the reliance on general image-editing metrics for robotic goal generation, demonstrating that task-specific manipulation adherence is far more critical than photorealism, and proposes SGE-Goal to optimize image synthesis for downstream robotic control.

TLDR: 本文揭示了机器人目标生成中“视觉编辑质量”与“下游控制任务成功率”的脱节，并提出了一种名为SGE-Goal的空间接地分解编辑框架，通过优化任务相关性显著提升了多步操作任务的成功率。

SpriteStage: Long Horizon Animation Generation via Progressive Scene Programming and Replay

Video generation models synthesize visual sequences from text and image inputs. Extending video generation to long animations requires actions, dialogue, camera coverage, and narrative developments to remain coordinated across scenes. Executable scene programs make these decisions available for inspection. Constructing an entire story before observing its execution can leave later scenes dependent on unrealized events. We present SpriteStage, a progressive scene programming framework that builds animation through iterative enactment and engine reorchestration. Iterative enactment generates each scene from an accepted world checkpoint and narrative snapshot, executes it on a shared spatial stage, and uses runtime and visual feedback to revise the relevant actions or story beats. The accepted outcome becomes the starting state for the next scene. Engine reorchestration maps the resulting draft to production assets and engine capabilities while retaining its events, geometry, and timing. Inspection of the assembled sequence and frozen replay carry the accepted performance into the final video. Three complete animations, ranging from approximately 9.9 to 14.3 minutes, demonstrate the framework's effectiveness in producing extended narratives through scene programming and engine execution.

TLDR: SpriteStage is a progressive scene programming framework that enables long-horizon animation generation by iteratively enacting narrative scenes and reorchestrating them within a shared spatial environment.

IECLA: Interpretable Emotion Contribution Learning for Affective Image Editing

Affective image editing (AIE) aims to evoke a target emotion while preserving the content and structure of a source image. Existing methods improve emotional expression or visual fidelity, but the target emotion alone does not reveal which source regions should be changed and which should remain intact, especially in complex scenes. We introduce Interpretable Emotion Contribution Learning for Affective Image Editing (IECLA), a framework that separates source-emotion attribution from target-aware editing. IECLA constructs signed object-level supervision through counterfactual interventions and trains a context-conditioned contribution predictor to localize evidence that supports or opposes the source emotion. At inference, an image-level evaluator estimates the source emotion directly from the input, while the predictor identifies editable and preservation evidence without repeated interventions. A target-aware editing agent then translates this evidence and the desired emotion into localized, content-aware operations for existing editors. Extensive evaluations show that IECLA predicts counterfactual contributions more faithfully on both in-domain and cross-dataset tests, while external VLM and independent human references support the quality of its localized evidence. Across six editing backbones, IECLA consistently strengthens target-emotion expression while preserving image content and structure, and user studies further show improved perceived target expression and naturalness.

TLDR: IECLA is a framework for Affective Image Editing that uses counterfactual interventions to learn which image regions contribute to specific emotions, enabling localized, content-preserving editing.

TLDR: IECLA 是一种情感图像编辑框架，它通过反事实干预学习图像中不同区域对特定情感的贡献，从而实现内容可控且具有局部针对性的情感编辑。

Only Means Only: what a harmonization benchmark score averages over

Image harmonization methods adjust the colour and lighting of an object pasted into a photograph so that it looks native to its new scene, and they are ranked by average error on one benchmark whose test images are real photographs with one object recoloured. One photograph can yield several such test images, and the benchmark promises that no photograph appears in both the training and the test split. We find that 1,399 photographs do, and the composites built on them are a quarter of the test set. Duplicate checks over file names or bytes miss this, because every composite is a different file. On that quarter the three methods we score end to end read two to five times lower error than on the rest, but most of the gap is composition rather than the sharing: the shared composites come almost entirely from one of the benchmark's four source collections, and inside that collection the gap falls to about one and a half times. An official score is therefore an average over two populations, mixed in a proportion the benchmark does not report. We release a test list of 5,581 composites whose construction identities do not appear in training, with four confirmed near-duplicate pairs disclosed rather than removed, adoptable without retraining at the price of one rescore per checkpoint. We leave open whether the sharing itself changes what a model learns.

TLDR: The paper identifies a significant data leakage issue in common image harmonization benchmarks, where identical source images appear in both training and testing sets, leading to inflated performance metrics. It provides a cleaned test list to enable more accurate model evaluation.

TLDR: 本文指出图像合成基准测试中存在严重的数据泄露问题，即训练集和测试集中包含相同的源图像，导致模型评估指标虚高。作者提供了一份清洗后的测试集，以实现更公平的模型评价。

Stable Signer V3: 4D Deformable Primitive Splatting Is All You Need for Sign Language Production

We present Stable Signer V3, an interactive high-resolution sign language avatar system built on 4D Deformable Primitive Splatting. It addresses three limitations of existing avatars: restricted facial expression, inaccurate identity-specific body deformation, and loss of fine appearance details. Shared Face Primitive Splatting adds transferable superquadric primitives for richer expression control; Test-Time Body Primitive Splatting learns an identity-specific virtual muscle layer for more faithful motion; and Test-Time Tunneling Super-Resolution uses TunnelingNet to recover identity-specific high-resolution details. Both test-time modules are optimized once per signer identity; inference then runs entirely from the stored identity parameters. Together, they reduce facial geometry error by 71.4%, body-boundary error by 35.1% across ten identities, and high-resolution RGB reconstruction error by 24.2% on held-out evaluations, while retaining interactive streaming.
