UniAVC: Unified Audio-Video Creation via Progressive Training and Asymmetric Scheduling

Unified audio-video creation requires a single model capable of text-conditioned joint synthesis, bidirectional cross-modal generation, and instruction-guided editing across both modalities. Existing methods rely heavily on paired corpora, which are scarce for joint generation and even rarer for editing. Consequently, they are spent learning single-branch generative and editing capabilities that abundant unimodal data could readily teach, rather than on the cross-modal coupling. Furthermore, prevailing systems apply a single shared noise schedule to both modalities, neglecting two critical limitations. First, due to the significant discrepancy in information density, video requires prolonged high noise to organize spatial and motion structure, whereas the sparser audio signal can settle earlier during the synthesis process, the dynamics a uniform schedule might compromise. Second, lockstep denoising leaves asymmetric workflows, such as conditioning on one modality to synthesize or edit another, untrained. We present UniAVC, which addresses both obstacles. It first trains each branch on unimodal dataset to full generative and editing competence, and subsequently dedicates paired data to exploit cross-modal correspondence. Modality-decoupled schedulers meanwhile warp the noise schedule independently from a shared timestep, while subtasks with one clean modality directly supervise asymmetric configurations. These settings respectively enable intent-aware editing, which preserves one modality intact while enlisting it to constrain the other, and post-inference self-refinement, which regenerates only an unsatisfactory branch against an accepted one. UniAVC thus spans multiple creation tasks from a single set of weights without task-specific adapters.

TLDR: UniAVC introduces a unified audio-video generative framework using progressive unimodal-to-multimodal training and modality-decoupled noise scheduling to enable flexible cross-modal synthesis and editing without task-specific adapters.

TLDR: UniAVC 提出了一种统一的音视频生成框架，通过从单模态到多模态的渐进式训练以及解耦的噪声调度策略，实现了无需特定适配器的灵活跨模态生成与编辑。

Design Anything, Design at Scale

As multimodal resources are transformed into media outputs, agentic AI is expected to stay aligned with design intent over long horizons. Designers follow briefs specifying goals, constraints, and deliverables, with later work building on earlier decisions. Recent work reuses design experience across tasks, yet alignment among artifacts within one project remains challenging. Agents therefore need to preserve and apply this intent across domains and as projects grow. We introduce Design Anything, a graph-based harness that preserves design intent outside the model and binds deliverables to the workflow steps that produce them through an intent-execution graph. A task contract retains constraints and decisions, selected designs become explicit inputs to later generations, and a deterministic controller uses bindings and execution records to check output types and counts, identify outstanding deliverables, and guide subsequent turns. We also introduce DesignAnything-Bench, with 228 tasks across 19 design domains, including 38 project families at scales of 6, 14, and 24 artifacts. With the LLM held fixed, Design Anything satisfies 85.0% and 92.5% of the brief's factual constraints with DeepSeek V4.1 Flash High and GPT-5.6 Sol High, respectively, exceeding the strongest same-model baselines by 15.4% and 14.4%. These gains reach 25.1% and 23.0% on 24-artifact tasks. Under each model, Design Anything leads or ties in constraint satisfaction in 18 of 19 domains. Professional designers also prefer its complete deliverable sets over those of same-model baselines in a blind evaluation.

TLDR: Design Anything introduces an intent-execution graph framework to maintain design consistency and constraint satisfaction in long-horizon, multi-artifact agentic design workflows.

TLDR: Design Anything 提出了一种基于意图-执行图的框架，用于在多阶段、多产出的代理设计工作流中保持设计意图的一致性与约束满足度。

CorrFlow: Self-Correcting Flow Matching

Flow-matching models generate samples through iterative velocity predictions, but typically do not revisit these predictions using the intermediate representations that produced them. We introduce CorrFlow, a self-correcting flow-matching framework that reuses this information through a lightweight refiner. Under rectified-flow interpolation, the error of the one-step clean prediction equals the realized velocity residual scaled by the noise level. Motivated by this identity, the refiner learns to predict residual corrections from the base model's clean prediction, conditioning, noise level and hidden states from the same forward pass. The correction is added to the base velocity before each sampling update, so subsequent predictions are made from the corrected state. This requires no additional base-model evaluations and only one lightweight refiner evaluation per step. Our theoretical analyses establish conditions under which feature reuse outperforms raw-input affine predictors and refiner training achieves greater residual-risk reduction than continued base-model training under a matched training compute budget. Experiments on class-conditional image generation, text-to-video generation and image-to-video generation show consistent improvements over the corresponding base models, including gains in visual quality and physical consistency. Project Page: https://fogradio.github.io/corrflow-project

TLDR: CorrFlow is a self-correcting flow-matching framework that uses a lightweight refiner to iteratively adjust velocity predictions during sampling, improving image and video generation quality without increasing base-model evaluations.

TLDR: CorrFlow 是一种自校正流匹配框架，通过轻量级细化器在采样过程中对速度预测进行迭代修正，从而在不增加基础模型评估次数的前提下，提升了图像和视频生成的质量。

Editing Images into Reusable Layers via Compositional Edit-Prior Distillation

High-quality image editing requires precise modification of target regions while preserving the content of unrelated regions. Existing editing models produce flattened outputs that limit precise local manipulation and layer reuse, while decomposition methods typically optimize layer recovery separately from instruction-driven editing. To this end, we introduce LayerEditor, a unified framework that couples instruction-guided image editing with controllable generation of reusable RGBA layers. At its core, In-Context RGB-Alpha Token Modeling jointly represents the edited canvas and an ordered sequence of RGB-Alpha layers within a single token sequence, enabling semantic edits and explicit control over individual layer content. To retain the pretrained model's editing capability during adaptation to layered outputs, we propose Compositional Edit-Prior Distillation, which transfers its editing prior to both the edited canvas and the image composed from the predicted layers. To support training and evaluation, we construct LayerEditor-100K, a large-scale paired image-editing dataset with ordered RGBA layer annotations, and introduce dedicated benchmarks for layered editing. Extensive experiments demonstrate that LayerEditor maintains strong editing fidelity while generating accurate RGBA layers, supporting both flexible layer-level manipulation and multi-layer decomposition.

TLDR: LayerEditor is a unified framework that combines instruction-guided image editing with RGBA layer decomposition using a novel In-Context RGB-Alpha Token Modeling approach and a specific distillation technique.

TLDR: LayerEditor 是一个统一框架，通过引入“上下文 RGB-Alpha Token 建模”和专门的蒸馏技术，实现了在指令驱动图像编辑的同时生成可复用的 RGBA 图层。

Knowledge Diversion: A Novel Paradigm for Pre-training Decomposable Image Generation Models

Large-scale pre-trained models have become foundational to modern machine learning, yet conventional pre-training objectives primarily optimize source-domain performance without explicitly considering downstream requirements. Consequently, transferable and task-specific knowledge remains entangled within a shared parameter space, limiting selective knowledge reuse and efficient downstream adaptation. This limitation is particularly consequential for image-generation models, which must accommodate diverse conditions and domains. To address this challenge, we introduce Knowledge Diversion (KDiv), a condition-aware pre-training paradigm for learning inherently decomposable generative models. Before pre-training, KDiv factorizes randomly initialized weights into rank-one Knowledge Units, which are organized into learngenes and tailors. During pre-training, continuous condition-aware routing consolidates knowledge shared across conditions in learngenes while directing condition-dependent variations to tailors. This structured organization enables learngenes to capture transferable generative priors and tailors to develop condition-specific expertise, allowing the resulting model to be selectively recombined for diverse downstream conditions. Extensive experiments across class-, text-, and image-conditioned generation demonstrate that KDiv consistently matches or surpasses dense baselines at comparable pre-training cost and achieves the strongest average downstream performance with a trainable parameter budget comparable to low-rank adaptation methods.

TLDR: Knowledge Diversion (KDiv) is a novel pre-training paradigm that factorizes model weights into shared 'learngenes' and condition-specific 'tailors' to enable disentangled, efficient, and modular image generation.

TLDR: Knowledge Diversion (KDiv) 提出了一种新的预训练范式，通过将模型权重分解为共享的“learngenes”和特定条件的“tailors”，实现了图像生成模型中知识的解耦、高效重用与模块化适应。

BEM: Learning Bregman Divergences for Concept Erasure in Flow Matching Models

Concept erasure removes harmful, private, or copyrighted concepts from text-to-image models while preserving their other abilities. Leading models such as FLUX and Stable Diffusion 3 are trained with flow matching, and erasure methods for them fine-tune the model toward a guided teacher's velocities under a Euclidean loss. Yet the squared distance is only one member of a larger family: Bregman divergences are exactly the losses under which flow matching recovers the correct velocity field, and each member shapes differently what is erased and what is preserved. We introduce BEM (Bregman erasure matching), which is, to our knowledge, the first to learn this divergence through bi-level optimization, so that the fine-tuned model forgets the concept on unseen prompts while its unrelated generations stay unchanged. In the experiments we target the suppression of nudity, violence, real identities, and copyrighted characters. Under the evaluation protocol of GEM, BEM removes more of the concept than GEM on most settings, cutting the unsafe rate on bloody gore from 53.9% to 14.4% and the FID on nudity from 8.64 to 6.64, while retention holds on some concepts and degrades on others.

TLDR: BEM introduces a novel framework using learnable Bregman divergences within flow matching models to improve concept erasure, effectively suppressing harmful content while preserving model generation quality.

TLDR: BEM 提出了一种在流匹配模型中利用可学习 Bregman 散度进行概念擦除的新框架，能在有效抑制有害内容的同时保持模型原有的生成能力。

BodyBridge: Grounded Body Layers for Sim-to-Real Translation of Vulnerable Road Users

Simulation-to-real driving video diffusion models can translate photorealistic scenes, but they typically represent pedestrians and cyclists, vulnerable road users (VRUs), as generic object classes. This is insufficient for vulnerable road users whose safety-relevant appearance depends on articulated pose and identity. We present BodyBridge and Grounded Body Layers (GBL), a simulator-derived, pixel-aligned representation that combines scene geometry, canonical per-pixel body correspondence, and exact VRU masks. GBL provides explicit articulation to the video diffusion model, emphasizes VRU regions during control-branch post-training, and supports additive composition with scene-geometry control at inference. It also enables per-joint evaluation of generated VRUs and a failure taxonomy for identity errors. On CARLA-to-real translation, BodyBridge improves near-range PCK@0.5 by 22% over the released depth-conditioned baseline. Replacing normal-map body control with canonical correspondence reduces body flips by 18%, while composition reduces near-range facing flips by 27%. These results show that explicit body grounding complements scene-level control and improves pose-faithful VRU generation. All code, data, and model checkpoints will be made publicly available to support reproducible, pedestrian-aware autonomous driving research.

TLDR: BodyBridge introduces Grounded Body Layers (GBL) to enhance video diffusion models for autonomous driving by providing explicit body articulation and canonical correspondence for vulnerable road users, significantly improving pose accuracy in sim-to-real translation.

TLDR: BodyBridge 引入了“地面身体层”（GBL），通过为视频扩散模型提供显式的身体关节信息和规范化对应关系，显著提升了自动驾驶中弱势道路使用者（VRU）在模拟到真实场景转换中的姿态准确度。

ZeroDSS: Semantic Segmentation from Class Names Alone

Semantic segmentation depends heavily on real images with pixel-level annotations, making dense supervision a major obstacle to deployment in new domains. We study task-specific data-free semantic segmentation, where training uses generic pretrained generative priors but has access to no target-domain images, masks, or captions--only a predefined category set. We introduce Zero-Data Semantic Segmentation (ZeroDSS), a feedback-driven approach that treats synthetic supervision as an adaptively controllable training distribution rather than a direct surrogate for real data. ZeroDSS constructs a semantically structured prompt distribution through LLM-guided compositional generation and adaptive class rebalancing, then converts the prompts into image-mask pairs using diffusion attention. Its feedback-driven distribution refinement consists of a two-phase closed-loop training procedure with a single loop-back step. In Phase I, a segmenter is trained using confidence-weighted supervision and dual-scale consistency. In Phase II, model feedback is used to adaptively target underperforming categories and refine diffusion-derived masks using model predictions. After this refined initial training, a teacher-student framework is utilized in the final phase to iteratively improve the fused supervision and segmenter through exponential moving average (EMA) updates. Using synthetic training data only, ZeroDSS achieves 35.0% mIoU on MS COCO with DeepLabV3, and 66.3% and 63.5% mIoU on PASCAL VOC with DeepLabV3 and Mask2Former, respectively. These results show that explicitly constructing and iteratively refining the synthetic supervision distribution can substantially reduce the synthetic-to-real gap in dense prediction.

TLDR: ZeroDSS introduces a feedback-driven, data-free semantic segmentation framework that uses LLMs and diffusion models to generate adaptive synthetic training data, bypassing the need for manual pixel-level annotations.

TLDR: ZeroDSS 提出了一种反馈驱动的零数据语义分割框架，通过大语言模型和扩散模型生成自适应的合成训练数据，从而摆脱了对真实图像人工像素级标注的依赖。

SERO: Semantic Prediction for Single-Step Object Removal

Object removal and semantic inpainting of missing regions remain inherently ill-posed, as multiple plausible completions may exist for a given scene. Recent diffusion-based methods have demonstrated strong potential for high-quality object removal; however, they still face two key limitations. First, although they can synthesise sharp and high-resolution images, they often struggle to complete removed regions in a semantically consistent and contextually meaningful manner, where the generated content should agree with the surrounding scene layout and object relationships rather than merely appear visually realistic. Second, their iterative denoising process makes them computationally expensive, limiting their practicality for downstream applications. We propose SERO, a semantic prediction framework for single-step object removal. Our model predicts a hidden-region semantic prior in embedding space, which guides a latent bridge-matching generator toward plausible scene completion by providing both where to edit and what structure is likely to exist behind the removed object. This enables visually realistic and contextually coherent completions in one forward pass. Experiments on the RORD and OBER datasets show that SERO achieves competitive or state-of-the-art performance while requiring only a single generation step.

TLDR: SERO is a single-step object removal framework that uses a semantic prior in the embedding space to guide latent bridge-matching, achieving semantically coherent completions without iterative denoising.

TLDR: SERO 是一种用于单步物体移除的语义预测框架，通过在嵌入空间中预测语义先验来引导潜在空间匹配生成，在无需迭代去噪的情况下实现了语义一致的图像修复。

MOMO: Human Motion from Moving Sounds

Human responses to sound depend not only on its semantic content, but also on the evolving spatial relationship between source and listener, conveyed through spatial audio and its temporal dynamics. Modeling human reactions to such dynamic spatial audio is important for character animation, human behavior analysis, and robotics. As of yet, existing audio-driven motion generation methods primarily focus on non-spatial or static acoustic conditions, leaving this important problem largely underexplored. To bridge this gap, we formulate the novel task of dynamic spatial-audio-driven human motion generation and introduce two motion-captured datasets. We introduce the Dynamic Spatial Audio and Motion dataset (DySAM) as the first dataset and benchmark for this task, providing synchronized binaural audio, 3D human motions, and moving sound source trajectories. Given the importance of dynamic spatial cues of sound sources, we further provide the Dynamic Spatial Audio Localization dataset (DySAL), an auxiliary binaural localization dataset that pairs diverse acoustic events with head poses and moving source trajectories to support spatial audio perception. For benchmarking, we propose MOMO for human Motion generation from Moving Sounds, a diffusion-based framework guided by three coupled principles: localization, understanding, and generation. MOMO performs localization by estimating head-centric spatial direction cues of the moving source directly from raw binaural audio, avoiding reliance on ground-truth source coordinates. It performs understanding by extracting frame-wise audio-content representations, and performs generation by conditioning motion diffusion on both semantic features and dynamic spatial cues. Experiments show that MOMO outperforms existing baselines in motion fidelity, audio-motion alignment, and physical plausibility.

TLDR: MOMO is a novel diffusion-based framework that generates human motion driven by dynamic spatial audio, utilizing two new datasets (DySAM and DySAL) to bridge the gap between acoustic spatial cues and physical motion.

TLDR: MOMO是一个基于扩散模型的创新框架，旨在通过动态空间音频驱动人体运动生成。研究引入了DySAM和DySAL数据集，解决了现有音频驱动运动生成中忽略空间动态特性的问题。

HumanSkill-4D: Training-Free Language Querying of Animatable Gaussian Avatars in Space and Time

We introduce avatar referring segmentation, which selects regions of animatable Gaussian avatars from language queries. A query can also specify a pose or motion to select matching intervals. This lets users interact with avatars for local editing and motion inspection. However, making these selections with existing language Gaussian splatting methods poses two challenges: adjacent parts and garments are hard to separate and may be referred to indirectly (region ambiguity), while fine-grained motions can look alike in RGB (motion ambiguity). We address these challenges with HumanSkill-4D, a training-free framework that shares a multimodal large language model (MLLM) and geometric operators across avatars. For each avatar, a semantic memory links body-part and garment categories to persistent Gaussian regions and appearance descriptions. The MLLM uses category names and descriptions to select categories by appearance, relation, or function. To find matching intervals, it composes operators into programs that check query conditions on driving joint trajectories stored in a motion memory. These category choices and operator outputs make selections traceable, while composing or adding operators supports new motion conditions. To evaluate this task, we introduce three human-annotated benchmarks and show that HumanSkill-4D outperforms the baselines in part segmentation and 3D and 4D referring segmentation, with compact memories and only minutes of setup per avatar.

TLDR: HumanSkill-4D is a training-free framework for language-guided segmentation of animatable Gaussian avatars, enabling precise selection of body parts, garments, and motion intervals through semantic memory and MLLM-based geometric operators.

TLDR: HumanSkill-4D 是一个无需训练的框架，通过多模态大模型和几何算子，实现了基于语言查询的 3D/4D 虚拟人分割，支持对身体部位、服饰及动作片段的精确定位与编辑。

ULIFLOW: Unified Latent Human-Object Interaction Flow

Text-conditioned 3D human--object interaction (HOI) generation remains challenging because individually plausible human and object motions do not necessarily form a coherent interaction. We argue that a key limitation lies in conventional skeleton-based representations, which compactly describe body kinematics while leaving surface-level interaction geometry largely implicit, often requiring additional contact-specific regularizers. We propose ULIFlow, a framework that explicitly models continuous human--object relations within a unified latent space. To this end, we derive continuous marker--object relations from sparse body-surface markers and treat them as an explicit interaction modality alongside human motion and object dynamics. A Mixture-of-Transformers (MoT) autoencoder is then designed to jointly model these heterogeneous streams while preserving their modality-specific structures. Importantly, we let the relation stream directly participate in human and object reconstruction, encouraging the shared latent representation to preserve both individual dynamics and the interaction geometry that coordinates them. To further achieve variable-length generation, we introduce a blockwise Flow Forcing scheme with global sequence-progress conditioning, enabling progressive continuation from fixed-length training windows while maintaining consistent long-horizon dynamics. Extensive experiments demonstrate that ULIFlow consistently outperforms prior work in overall generation quality and human--object coordination, while preserving coherent interactions over requested durations beyond the training horizon.

TLDR: ULIFlow introduces a unified latent framework for 3D human-object interaction (HOI) generation by explicitly modeling continuous marker-object relations as a core modality alongside human and object motion.

TLDR: ULIFlow 提出了一种统一的潜在空间框架，通过将连续的人体标记-物体关系作为核心模态与人体和物体运动共同建模，解决了 3D 人机交互生成的协调性难题。

NV-Generate-Body: 3D Latent Diffusion Model forControllable Anatomy, Tumor, and Demographic Body Mask Synthesis

Curating large labeled CT datasets is expensive and constrained by patient privacy. We present NV-Generate-BodyMask, a 3D latent diffusion model that synthesizes whole-body CT segmentation masks with precise, joint control over organ anatomy, tumor burden, and patient demographics. The model operates in the compressed latent space of a frozen mask autoencoder, generating $256^3$ volumetric label maps conditioned on a 19-dimensional vector via Adaptive Group Normalization and cross-attention. A size-adaptive ROI loss with spatial halo weighting and a paired tumor injection pipeline enable accurate tumor size conditioning despite the scarcity of annotated tumor cases. Trained on 8,571 masks across 26 cohorts spanning 132 labels, the model achieves Spearman $ρ= 0.70$--$0.92$ for organ size, 85--100\% tumor recall for lung, pancreatic, colon, and bone lesions, and strong demographic alignment (BMI $ρ= 0.78$, sex-conditioned perfect prostate control).

TLDR: NV-Generate-BodyMask is a 3D latent diffusion model that synthesizes controllable, large-scale volumetric CT segmentation masks conditioned on anatomy, tumor burden, and patient demographics.

BLANC: Adaptive Temporal Scheduling for Sign Language Production

Sign language production (SLP) aims to synthesize expressive signing motion from written text. Modeling signing motion presents two key temporal challenges. First, fine-grained hand articulations demand higher temporal resolution than relatively slower body movements. Second, signing events unfold over variable durations, calling for adaptive temporal scheduling during generation. To address these challenges, we first introduce a Multi-Rate Vector Quantizer (MRVQ), an articulation-decoupled motion tokenizer that represents the body and both hands as separate discrete streams at distinct temporal resolutions. Building on this representation, we propose BLANC (BLock-wise Adaptive diffusioN with Articulatory Clock), a gloss-free SLP framework that generates variable-duration motion blocks causally while denoising tokens in parallel within each block. At its core, a Latent Articulatory Clock (LAC) learns temporal structure from multi-scale motion-derived partitions to predict block durations and sequence termination during generation. We further introduce inference-time body-conditioned hand refinement to improve coordination across articulators. Extensive experiments on PHOENIX-2014T and CSL-Daily demonstrate that BLANC achieves state-of-the-art performance in sign language production.

TLDR: BLANC is a gloss-free sign language production framework that uses a Multi-Rate Vector Quantizer for articulation-decoupled motion and a Latent Articulatory Clock to manage variable-duration motion generation.

TLDR: BLANC 是一种无需词汇标注的手语生成框架，利用多速率向量量化器处理解耦动作，并通过潜在关节时钟来管理可变时长的动作生成。
