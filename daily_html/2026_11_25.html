Linear Probes Can Guide: Training-Free Control of Representation Generators

Representation-space generative models, such as Representation Autoencoders (RAEs), leverage pretrained vision foundation models (VFMs) primarily to accelerate training and improve sample fidelity. Yet VFMs are more than reconstruction tokenizers, as they feature rich representation spaces that are highly organized and linearly readable. We observe that this removes a key obstacle of post-hoc guidance: by linearity of expectation, a clean probe applied to an ideal denoiser's prediction yields the exact posterior mean of the probe scores at any noise level. This regime, which we call Semantic Mean-Space, turns semantic guidance into a Bayesian linear inverse problem, bypassing the need for training-time conditioning. With this foundation, we propose LiSM, a unified framework for Linear Guidance in Semantic Mean-Space. To validate the framework, we developed and adapted different solvers, and characterized them based on how they estimate probe-space uncertainty and propagate the correction through the representation. In addition, we propose Fit-Path, a solver designed to achieve strong global and local control. Our results in four control setups (class, attributes, foreground, and keypoints) show that LiSM methods, and Fit-Path in particular, provide effective, lightweight control across global and local tasks, outperforming noise-aware and pixel-space guidance.

TLDR: LiSM introduces a training-free guidance framework for representation-space generative models by leveraging the linear readability of foundation model spaces to perform semantic control as a Bayesian inverse problem.

PhysMend: Verification-Guided Program Refinement for Physics-Aware Video Generation

Video generative models synthesize realistic scenes, yet their interactions often violate physics. A ball dropped onto sand should displace grains and settle into a crater, whereas a generated video may show it passing through the surface or leaving the sand flat. Post-hoc methods typically score generated videos with a vision-language model (VLM), but such scores offer limited guidance on which object or parameter to modify, and each correction costs another synthesis. Simulation-based pipelines instead run fixed stages before synthesis, so simulation errors reach the video. To address these limitations, we present PhysMend, a training-free framework for verification-guided program refinement. PhysMend describes the target event as an editable physical program of grounded objects, events, and required relations, paired with tunable execution parameters. Our key insight is to verify and repair the event on its simulated rollout and on the depth video that conditions the generator (depth control) rather than on generated videos, enabling quantitative violation measurement, targeted editing, and fast re-verification before synthesis. A hybrid verifier evaluates semantic event consistency, physical plausibility, solver stability, and visibility in the depth control. VLM judgments are confined to event semantics and cannot override failed physical measurements, which limits the impact of VLM hallucinations. Each failed check localizes the program fields eligible for repair, and its measured violation scales a bounded update of their parameters. Only accepted depth control reaches the generator, which runs once per input. Experiments on the commonly used benchmarks Physics-IQ, PhyGenBench, and VBench-2.0 demonstrate substantial gains across different generator backbones; for example, PhysMend raises the overall Physics-IQ score of Wan-VACE from 27.33 to 39.40.

TLDR: PhysMend introduces a training-free, verification-guided framework that refines physical programs based on simulation and depth control before video synthesis, ensuring physical consistency and minimizing generator hallucinations.

TLDR: PhysMend 提出了一种无需训练的验证引导式框架，通过在视频生成前对物理程序进行仿真和深度控制图的验证与修正，有效提升了视频生成的物理一致性并减少了视觉模型的幻觉问题。

Residual Guidance for Real-Time Interactive Video Generation

Real-time interactive video generation allows users to change the prompt during generation to steer the evolving video. However, existing streaming models, which generate videos chunk by chunk, often keep following the previous prompt after a switch. We trace this failure to context inertia, where the previous chunk acts as a strong prior that suppresses the new prompt. Furthermore, we find that this inertia persists even when the cache retains only the previous chunk, yet discarding the context breaks visual continuity, which limits approaches that edit the cache. Rather than editing the cache, we propose Residual Guidance (RG), a simple, training-free method that counteracts context inertia with a correction measured outside the cache. Specifically, RG denoises the re-noised previous chunk once under the new prompt without the cache and adds the resulting residual to the early denoising steps of the next chunk, where its content is largely decided and the new prompt is most suppressed. Across settings ranging from a single switch to long multi-switch generation, RG substantially improves prompt switching capability on existing streaming models, including those with dedicated switching mechanisms, while largely retaining visual quality. As it requires no training or per-model tuning and adds only one forward pass per chunk, RG can be easily integrated into existing real-time video generation pipelines.

TLDR: Residual Guidance (RG) is a training-free method that improves prompt-switching in real-time streaming video generation by injecting a correction residual to overcome context inertia without sacrificing visual continuity.

TLDR: Residual Guidance (RG) 是一种无需训练的方法，通过引入修正残差来克服实时流式视频生成中的上下文惯性，从而在不破坏视觉连贯性的前提下显著改善 prompt 切换效果。

Qwen-Image-HD: Autoregressive Diffusion for High Definition Image Generation

Fine-tuning a generator on a complete high-resolution canvas requires long image sequences and substantial computation, yet local regions can still exhibit unnatural repetitive stripes when training resources are limited. We introduce Qwen-Image-HD, which adapts Qwen-Image 2.0 to high-resolution synthesis through local training and blockwise autoregressive generation. Each prediction covers a target tile at 1K scale, conditioned on high-resolution upper and left neighbors and a compressed historical thumbnail. These conditions provide adjacent fine-scale structures and an overview of the scene generated so far. To integrate these complementary cues, we map both forms of history and the target to a shared canvas coordinate system to express their spatial relationships and scale differences. On Qwen-Image-Bench at 4K, Qwen-Image-HD achieves stronger overall evaluation performance than direct 4K generation with FLUX-3-Image-Klein. Compared with full-image 4K supervised fine-tuning of the same backbone, it achieves better generation quality with lower training compute and higher inference efficiency, while exhibiting fewer unnatural repetitive stripes in original-resolution comparisons. We further introduce a Wavefront-AutoRegressive inference schedule that generates tiles on the same anti-diagonal concurrently across GPUs to reduce single-image latency.

TLDR: Qwen-Image-HD introduces a blockwise autoregressive generation framework for high-resolution (up to 4K) image synthesis, using local training and a Wavefront-AutoRegressive inference schedule to improve efficiency and visual quality.

TLDR: Qwen-Image-HD 提出了一种用于高分辨率（最高4K）图像生成的块级自回归框架，通过局部训练和波前自回归推理调度，在降低计算成本的同时提升了生成质量与推理效率。

TokenMatte: Unlocking T2V Diffusion Priors for Pixel-Native Video Matting

High-resolution video matting requires accurate recovery of continuous alpha values, including fine hair, soft boundaries, and motion blur, yet dense alpha supervision remains scarce. Pretrained text-to-video diffusion transformers (DiTs) offer rich spatio-temporal priors, whereas existing generative video matting approaches typically route alpha predictions through RGB-trained VAE latents, which can attenuate fine details and introduce additional decoding overhead. We present TokenMatte, which adapts a pretrained video DiT into a single-pass, auxiliary-free RGB-to-alpha predictor with a pixel-native output interface. At its core is a single shared linear readout that maps each spatio-temporal token directly to its corresponding ($t \times p \times p$) block of alpha pixels, predicting spatial details and temporal variations within the same local video block. Tiling these blocks produces the full-resolution alpha sequence without a convolutional decoder or learned upsampling. The predicted alpha is supervised directly in pixel space, while the ground-truth alpha never enters the VAE, eliminating target-side alpha encoding and output-side VAE decoding. Leveraging the pretrained video prior, TokenMatte is trained using only existing public matting datasets, without additional data curation or auxiliary inputs at inference. Compared with a matched latent-output baseline, our pixel-native interface improves matting accuracy while achieving a 1.51$\times$ inference speedup. TokenMatte establishes a new auxiliary-free state of the art on VideoMatte, YouTubeMatte, and V-HIM60.

TLDR: TokenMatte introduces an auxiliary-free, pixel-native matting approach that leverages pretrained video DiTs by mapping spatio-temporal tokens directly to alpha pixel blocks, bypassing VAE bottlenecks.

TLDR: TokenMatte 提出了一种无需辅助模块的像素级视频抠图方法，通过直接将预训练视频 DiT 的时空 Token 映射为像素块，绕过了 VAE 编码/解码对细节的损失，提升了抠图质量与推理速度。

Sol$^{3}$: Accelerating Video Diffusion for Free via Hierarchical Sparse Attention Routing

Sol-Attn (Sparsifying online attention) combines sparse exact computation with low-cost representative approximation to accelerate training-free inference in diffusion video models. However, intra-block attention can focus on only a few tokens. While preserving these key interactions exactly, the block-level decision incurs the cost of evaluating many low-response members. We propose Sol$^{3}$, a training-free hierarchical sparse attention method that further refines how exact computations are allocated. First, we introduce Gaussian-guided hierarchical routing, which determines the scope of exact computation step by step, from blocks to tokens, using statistical thresholds at each level. Only a subset of members within Mixed blocks that require refinement is selected for exact computation, while representative terms approximate the remaining members. We further design heterogeneous routing statistics that combine block-center correlations with variations in Key and Value within blocks to identify blocks that are difficult to approximate. Among these Mixed blocks, we uniformly compare raw token relevance to select members requiring exact computation. Experiments on video generation, audio-video co-generation, and world simulation tasks demonstrate that Sol$^{3}$ achieves 86% attention sparsity and delivers a $3.07\times$ attention speedup while preserving quality. Our code is available at https://github.com/FofGofx/sol3.

TLDR: Sol³ introduces a hierarchical sparse attention routing mechanism that achieves 3.07x speedup in training-free video diffusion models by dynamically selecting key tokens for exact computation.

TLDR: Sol³ 提出了一种分层稀疏注意力路由机制，通过在块和 token 级别动态筛选关键计算，在无需训练的情况下实现了视频扩散模型 3.07 倍的推理加速。

Déjà-VAE: Training-Free Consistent Editing with Pseudo-Invertible Encoders and Latent Back-Projection

Consistency is essential to generative workflows: repeated processing should preserve information beyond the changes explicitly requested. In latent diffusion models, this requires stability both when moving between latent and pixel spaces and when applying successive edits. Autoencoding errors and unintended changes from the editing model can accumulate across these operations, progressively altering image structure, texture, and color. We introduce Déjà-VAE, a framework that combines exact autoencoding consistency with calibrated editing stabilization, without training or fine-tuning. Its autoencoding component converts a pretrained variational autoencoder into a surjective pseudo-invertible neural network, with a fixed gain balancing deviations from the pretrained encoder and decoder. The modified decoder $D'$ is an explicit nonlinear right inverse of the encoder $E'$, satisfying $E'(D'(z))=z$ by construction. Consequently, $D'$ is a reflexive generalized inverse: latent round trips preserve their input, up to numerical precision, and repeated image reconstruction is idempotent. To suppress editing drift, adaptive wavelet back-projection finds the closest latent to the editor's output in $\ell_2$, subject to matching the previous latent on wavelet coefficients whose changes fall below calibrated thresholds. This correction is applied once per turn, with no additional network evaluations after calibration. Together, these components support workflows that remain in latent space or interleave generation with pixel-space operations and constraints. Tests across models and editing benchmarks show less drift than stock pipelines and dedicated consistency methods.

TLDR: Déjà-VAE introduces a training-free framework that ensures exact autoencoding consistency and reduces editing drift in latent diffusion models using pseudo-invertible encoders and adaptive wavelet back-projection.

TLDR: Déjà-VAE 提出了一种无需训练的框架，通过伪可逆编码器和自适应小波反投影，在潜在扩散模型中实现了精确的自动编码一致性并减少了编辑漂移。

OmniPersist: Persistent Dynamic World Modeling via Coupled Panoramic Evolution

Action-conditioned world models play a vital role in robotics and autonomous driving, yet existing video diffusion models struggle to maintain dynamic object persistence. When the camera turns away and returns, out-of-view moving entities fail to evolve plausibly in latent space, often mutating or vanishing entirely due to bounded perspective views. To resolve this issue, we propose OmniPersist, a unified framework that couples perspective video generation with panoramic evolution within a single-stage diffusion model, without relying on auxiliary models or initial panoramic inputs. To maintain efficiency, we model the panoramic stream at a lower frame rate, bridging both views across space and time via Geometric Anchoring (GeoAnch) for spatial correspondence and Temporal Compensation (TemComp) for temporal aggregation. OmniPersist is agnostic to camera-control architectures, supporting both latent-level and attention-level conditioning. Extensive experiments on a newly constructed dynamic benchmark across image-to-video and video continuation tasks demonstrate consistent gains in visual quality, camera controllability, revisit consistency, and dynamic persistence.

TLDR: OmniPersist is a novel unified diffusion framework that couples perspective video generation with panoramic evolution to ensure dynamic object persistence and consistent revisits without auxiliary models.

TLDR: OmniPersist 提出了一个统一的扩散框架，将透视视频生成与全景演变相结合，在无需辅助模型的情况下实现了动态对象持久性和空间 revisit 的一致性。

MAGPIE: Agentic Multi-Reference Image Generation via Executable Scene Construction

Multi-reference image generation requires a model to preserve the appearance of selected subjects while arranging them according to an instruction. Despite recent progress in reference-based generation and compositional control, reliably organizing selected subjects into the intended scene remains challenging. Although existing methods provide various forms of compositional guidance, the planned relationships are not always explicitly instantiated for inspection and refinement before image synthesis. To address this limitation, we introduce MAGPIE, which represents the intended composition as a simplified 3D scene defined by an executable Blender program. A coding agent links each scene instance to its reference, checks the geometry and rendered view against the instruction, and revises the program to correct discrepancies. The final proxy rendering serves as a visual plan for the image generator. Using MAGPIE-Instruct-3K, we train the generator to follow this plan while drawing each subject's appearance from its reference. MAGPIE improves both Qwen and FLUX on DreamOmni2 and SconeEval. With FLUX, it improves overall success on DreamOmni2 by 28.21 percentage points over the original backbone and achieves the highest overall score among the models evaluated on SconeEval.

TLDR: MAGPIE introduces an agentic workflow that uses executable Blender programs to construct 3D scene compositions as visual plans, significantly improving subject consistency and spatial control in multi-reference image generation.

TLDR: MAGPIE 提出了一种基于代理的工作流，通过可执行的 Blender 程序构建 3D 场景作为视觉规划，从而显著提升了多参考图像生成中的主体一致性和空间布局控制能力。

A Mesh to Remember: Persistent Appearance for Interactive Video Worlds

Video world models synthesize photorealistic observations in response to user actions but struggle to remain consistent over long interactions. Coding agents and text-to-3D models instead generate explicit 3D worlds whose geometry and state persist by construction, but whose appearance falls far short of photorealism. Recent methods combine the strengths of both by rendering such worlds into coarse proxies that guide a video model. However, since these proxies only specify where things are, the renderer must invent what they look like as the world is explored, and anything it forgets is reinvented when a surface reappears. Our key insight is that a mesh identifies which surface each pixel shows, across frames and views, even as objects move, allowing it to serve as an address for what the renderer generates. We propose M2R (Mesh to Remember), a few-step causal video renderer that stores its own features on the surfaces they depict, in each object's frame, and retrieves them by rasterizing the mesh whenever those surfaces come back into view. Appearance thus travels with objects wherever they move, persists across revisits, and is shared by every agent that observes it, while memory grows with the surfaces seen instead of the number of frames. Trained only on synthetic data, M2R transfers zero-shot to real indoor scans and to worlds authored by coding agents. It outperforms state-of-the-art methods in consistency across static, dynamic, and multi-agent benchmarks, more than doubling object permanence and nearly doubling cross-agent consistency, while rendering 720p video in real time (40 FPS).

TLDR: M2R addresses the inconsistency in video world models by storing appearance features directly on 3D mesh surfaces, enabling persistent, photorealistic, and real-time video generation that stays consistent across views and time.

PVWander: 3D Memory-guided Panoramic Video Wandering with Path-Facing Canonicalization

Long-horizon panoramic video wandering aims to continuously synthesize scene-consistent $360^\circ$ observations along user-specified camera trajectories from an initial equirectangular projection (ERP) panorama. Existing panoramic video models have achieved promising results in camera-controlled generation, while recent 3D-guided approaches further provide geometric guidance for improving scene consistency. However, stable long-horizon wandering remains challenging due to diverse motion--orientation relationships and imperfect memory guidance induced by accumulated generation artifacts and depth inaccuracies. To address these challenges, we propose \textbf{PVWander}, a 3D-memory-guided framework that accumulates generated observations into an online point-cloud memory and reprojects it to guide subsequent generation, with two complementary designs: \textit{Path-Facing canonicalization} for motion--orientation alignment and \textit{Timestep-aware gating modulation} for adaptive memory guidance. \textit{Path-Facing canonicalization} exploits the horizontal cyclic symmetry of ERP panoramas to uniformly align each clip's ERP orientation with its principal translation direction, yielding more consistent, forward-like relative motions while preserving the prescribed camera path. \textit{Timestep-aware gating modulation} exploits the coarse-to-fine nature of diffusion denoising to adaptively regulate reprojected memory guidance through timestep-dependent channel-wise gates, preserving its structural benefits while mitigating visual artifacts from imperfect projections. Extensive experiments demonstrate that PVWander achieves improved long-horizon generation quality, while ablations validate the effectiveness of both designs.

TLDR: PVWander introduces a 3D-memory-guided framework for long-horizon panoramic video generation, utilizing Path-Facing canonicalization and Timestep-aware gating to ensure scene consistency and mitigate accumulated artifacts.

Video Models are Excellent Motion Models

Video foundation models have emerged as powerful generators of dynamic visual content, exhibiting remarkable capabilities in synthesizing complex actions and interactions. We argue that these models have also become excellent motion generators, with motion priors that are richer and more general than those learned by dedicated motion models. Their advantage extends beyond visual realism and is manifested in two important aspects: motion naturalness, including coherent body coordination, physical plausibility, and realistic interactions with objects and the environment; and motion scope, encompassing a much broader range of activities and semantic coherence, particularly uncommon, complex, and compound actions. To investigate this position, we introduce a common evaluation framework that compares video and dedicated motion models through three complementary settings: text-only motion generation, reconstruction of video-generated motion from sparse keyframes, and video-based enrichment of motion-model outputs. By converting all outputs into a shared 3D skeletal representation, we evaluate motion naturalness, semantic alignment, action coverage, physical motion characteristics, and fidelity to motion constraints. Our results suggest that video foundation models can generate more natural and semantically meaningful movements, particularly for complex and compound actions. Moreover, we investigate motion-conditioned generation through loose skeletal control, finding that dedicated motion models do not consistently improve video-generated motion, while video models can enrich skeletal animations using sparse motion guidance. Our findings suggest a shift in the role of specialized motion models, from generating complete animations to guiding and controlling the richer motion capabilities already embedded in video foundation models.

TLDR: This paper argues that video foundation models possess superior, more generalizable motion priors compared to dedicated motion models, effectively repositioning the latter as tools for control and guidance rather than primary generators.

Every Edit Its Own Budget: Per-Instruction Token Freezing for Training-Free Acceleration of Multi-Edit Image Editing

Accelerating instruction-based image editing when one instruction requests several edits is challenging. Many training-free accelerators for flow-matching image editors skip tokens whose prediction has stopped changing, but decide this with a single threshold for the whole image. With several edits, such a threshold, tuned for speed, freezes small and slowly settling edits first, and requested edits disappear. We propose TIDE (Training-free Instruction-aware Drift-based Editing), which gives every instruction part its own compute budget. First, TIDE finds the region of each part in the editor's own attention by unbalanced optimal transport, without masks or external models. Second, it freezes a token once its drift is small relative to its own part and moves it along the straight path to its endpoint estimate. Third, a sparse engine computes only the remaining tokens. We prove that part-agnostic rules cannot guarantee per-part error budgets, characterize the optimal per-part threshold policy up to one tie class per part, and derive when our rule attains it. On FLUX.2 Klein, one global threshold breaks at least one edit's budget in 89% of MICE-Bench images, and TIDE drops up to 77% fewer edits at matched or higher speed (48% on MICE-Bench), a gap human raters confirm. On the many-step Klein Base, TIDE is the fastest compared method on six benchmarks (4.81-4.89x) and needs 5.3x fewer TFLOPs than vanilla, the largest saving we measure. With the same settings, it is also the fastest compared method on Qwen-Image-Edit-2511, a second model family (3.46-3.52x), and drops the fewest edits on all six benchmarks. Code will be made publicly available.

TLDR: TIDE is a training-free acceleration method for multi-edit image generation that uses instruction-aware, per-part token freezing via optimal transport to maintain quality while significantly reducing computational costs.

LRQ-Cache: Compressing Feature Caches in Video Diffusion via Low-Rank Approximation and Quantization

Video diffusion transformers (DiTs) achieve high-fidelity generation, but inference is expensive because every denoising step evaluates a large transformer over all spatiotemporal tokens of the video. Temporal caching accelerates them by reusing intermediate features across denoising steps. However, retaining dense feature tensors introduces a persistent storage cost that grows with video length, resolution, and cache coverage, reaching 109-346 GiB per video for the predictive caches we evaluate. Such states exceed the memory of a single GPU, so native caches must either be offloaded to the CPU, which slows generation, or be sharded across several GPUs, e.g., with sequence parallelism, so that the cache rather than the compute sets the number of GPUs. We instead shrink the cached state itself, exploiting its redundancy: a few channel patterns shared across tokens capture much of the energy of each cached feature. Based on this observation, we propose LRQ-Cache, a training-free method that combines low-rank approximation and quantization for feature-cache compression. LRQ-Cache represents each cached feature with a small set of factors, quantizes them before storage, and reconstructs the feature at reuse time, leaving cache mechanisms unchanged. Across three caching mechanisms on HunyuanVideo and Wan2.1, LRQ-Cache reduces the persistent state of predictive caches by 42-71x, to 2.3-4.9 GiB per video. VBench stays within 0.25 pp of the native cache for TeaCache, and the larger drops of the Wan2.1 predictive caches, up to 3.5 pp, shrink at higher ranks. As a result, predictive caches that exceed the memory of a single 80 GB H100 GPU fit on it with LRQ-Cache, making generation 2.7-5.9x faster than with CPU-offloaded native caches.

TLDR: LRQ-Cache is a training-free compression method for video diffusion transformers that uses low-rank approximation and quantization to reduce memory overhead of predictive caches by 42-71x, enabling high-performance generation on single GPUs.
