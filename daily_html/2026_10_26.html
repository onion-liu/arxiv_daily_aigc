EditDistill: Is It Possible to Guide Video Editing with Image Editing?

Recent advances in image editing have enabled fine-grained and highly controllable visual modifications. However, achieving similar levels of controllability in video editing remains an open challenge, particularly for precise local edits, dynamic temporal effects, and consistent lighting across frames.In this paper, we propose EditDistill, a framework that distills image-level visual transformations from edits to guide video editing.Specifically, given a source video and an edit prompt, we first generate an edited version of the initial video frame using an off-the-shelf image editing model. We then distill the visual transformation of this original-edited image pair into a compact representation termed an edit descriptor.This descriptor is injected into a video diffusion model through our lightweight fusion module, which steers the denoising process toward the desired edit. To align the video trajectory with the demonstrated image edit while preserving motion and layout, we train with edit-descriptor alignment and DINO structural consistency losses, and derive an equivalent inference-time guidance formulation under flow matching.Extensive experiments demonstrate that our method achieves strong performance on standard benchmarks and produces high-quality video editing results across diverse scenarios. For more details and videos, please refer to the anonymous webpage: https://yu-shaonian.github.io/EditDistill-Web/.

TLDR: EditDistill leverages high-quality image editing models to guide video editing by distilling visual transformations into an 'edit descriptor' that steers video diffusion models.

TLDR: EditDistill 提出了一种将高质量图像编辑模型的能力迁移至视频编辑的框架，通过将图像编辑过程蒸馏为“编辑描述符”来引导视频扩散模型的生成。

Timing Matters Twice: When to Query and When to Skip in Flow-Matching On-Policy Distillation

On-policy distillation (OPD) enables flow-matching models to acquire complementary capabilities from specialized teachers through supervision on student-generated trajectories. However, different evaluation dimensions, such as aesthetics and OCR accuracy, and different tasks, such as image generation and editing, emphasize different aspects of the denoising process, suggesting that their effective supervision may occur at different stages. Existing methods typically select supervision states from predefined timestep distributions, which may overlook these phase-dependent differences and incur unnecessary computational overhead. This motivates two critical timing decisions: when to adaptively query teachers for effective supervision based on the evolving generative process, and when to skip the remaining rollout to avoid redundant computation. In this paper, we introduce PhaseOPD, a phase-aware query-and-skip framework that identifies generative phases online from revisions of predicted-clean latents, using existing student velocity predictions without additional model evaluations. For each task-assigned teacher, PhaseOPD selects a supervision state based on its phase preference and skips the unused rollout suffix once all query decisions are finalized, preserving the single-query velocity-distillation objective. On SD3.5-M, PhaseOPD executes only 5.56 of 16 rollout positions per local batch, reducing training time by 41.3% relative to DanceOPD while improving Aesthetic, GenEval, and OCR scores.

TLDR: PhaseOPD is a phase-aware framework for flow-matching on-policy distillation that optimizes training efficiency by adaptively querying teachers and skipping redundant denoising steps.

TLDR: PhaseOPD 是一种用于流匹配策略蒸馏的相位感知框架，通过动态决定教师查询时机并跳过冗余步骤，显著提升了训练效率与生成质量。

Freeze What Is Settled, Cache What Is Smooth: Spatial Freezing Keeps Step Caching in Its Low-Error Regime

Instruction-based image editors built on diffusion transformers run tens of denoising steps. Training-free acceleration either reuses features across steps (step caching) or stops computing the regions an edit leaves untouched (region freezing), and the two have been combined before. We ask how a high speed-up target should be split between them. On Qwen-Image-2.1, freezing does more than remove tokens: it lowers the cost of every step, so the step cache can reach the same overall speed-up with a conservative threshold that keeps its error small. A per-step cost model fitted on development runs predicts held-out latency with a median error of 4.0% and reproduces the speed ceilings of the region methods we re-implemented (2.73$\times$ predicted vs. 2.70$\times$ measured for RegionE). Driving frozen tokens to the model's one-step $x_0$ estimate beats returning them to the reference image on every development sample, and in 90% of measured cases the composed error stays below the sum of the two individual errors. The resulting method, FreezePlan, keeps five full steps and gives the rest of the budget to TaylorSeer. On a pre-registered test split it runs 5.05$\times$ faster end to end at 29.8 dB PSNR to the original output, with no significant drop in a VLM judge score; TaylorSeer at 5.02$\times$ reaches 23.5 dB. The curve extends to 5.99$\times$ at 28.0 dB. Swapping in the region selectors of prior methods under our schedule lowers median PSNR by only 0.5--0.6 dB, which places the gain in the schedule and budget split rather than in the selector.

TLDR: FreezePlan optimizes diffusion transformer image editing by intelligently balancing region-based freezing and step-based caching, achieving significant speedups (up to 5.99x) while maintaining high visual fidelity.

TLDR: FreezePlan 通过智能平衡基于区域的冻结（region freezing）和基于步骤的缓存（step caching），优化了扩散 Transformer 图像编辑的性能，在保持高视觉质量的同时实现了显著的加速（最高 5.99 倍）。

Learning the Autoregressive: Direct Score-Difference Estimation with Amortized Future Credit for Video Distillation

Few-step distillation for autoregressive (AR) video generation commonly first initializes an AR student and then refines it with distribution matching distillation (DMD). This refinement process faces two challenges. First, although initialization already yields a reasonably capable student, DMD obtains the refinement direction indirectly by estimating the full student score before forming the teacher-student correction. Second, detached-history training prevents the generator from assigning credit to a current chunk for its influence on subsequent chunks. We introduce Leta, a few-step distillation framework that addresses these challenges with ScoreDelta and FutureCredit. ScoreDelta directly estimates the teacher-student correction from denoising residuals, while FutureCredit learns an auxiliary cross-chunk update signal from vector-Jacobian products (VJPs) of next-chunk teacher residuals. Leta combines these signals into a joint teacher-guided update field in a single forward pass during generator updates. Teacher evaluations and credit VJPs are used only for estimator training. For ScoreDelta, we establish population equivalence to score-difference estimation and a conditional approximation advantage under limited capacity in a fixed-feature linear model. In our controlled experiments, Leta improves over DMD by 0.9 points on VBench and 0.66 on VideoAlign. This advantage persists at a higher generator update frequency (a generator-to-estimator update ratio of G:E=1:1), where Leta improves over DMD by 1.6 on VBench and 0.35 on VideoAlign. Project page: https://scdzx.github.io/Leta/.

TLDR: Leta is a few-step distillation framework for autoregressive video generation that introduces direct score-difference estimation (ScoreDelta) and cross-chunk credit assignment (FutureCredit) to improve efficiency and temporal consistency.

TLDR: Leta 是一种用于自回归视频生成的少步蒸馏框架，通过引入直接分数差估计（ScoreDelta）和跨分块信用分配（FutureCredit）机制，显著提升了蒸馏效率和视频生成质量。

TrackTok: Object-Centric Video Tokenization with Semantically Persistent Tokens

We introduce TrackTok, an efficient video tokenization framework that produces object-centric tokens, i.e., tokens that consistently encode the same semantic objects across time, even as it moves, deforms, or undergoes partial occlusion. Conventional patch-based or existing holistic tokenizers produce token identities often tied to fixed image locations. In contrast, our approach learns temporally persistent object-centric representations that bind visual features across frames into stable semantic units. This enables substantially more compact, continuous latent video representations, reducing the number of tokens required for downstream generative modeling with state-of-the-art flow models, while preserving the semantic structure needed for high-quality synthesis. Our tokenizer offers a favorable quality-efficiency trade-off. TrackTok achieves comparable class-conditional generative FVD on UCF-101 dataset at 8 times fewer latent tokens and better latency, and outperforms existing 1D tokenization baselines on frame prediction task on Kinetics-600 across low token budgets. These results suggest that object-aligned and temporally persistent tokenization is a promising direction for scalable video generation, where reducing latent token counts is critical for efficient modeling. Our code for this work is available at https://github.com/phamleyennhi/tracktok.

TLDR: TrackTok introduces an object-centric video tokenization framework that maintains temporal persistence of semantic objects, enabling high-quality video generation with significantly fewer latent tokens.

TLDR: TrackTok 提出了一种以对象为中心的视频分词框架，通过保持语义对象在时间维度上的持久性，实现了在大幅降低潜在 token 数量的同时进行高质量视频生成。

PhysCreator: Creating Physically-Attributed 3D Assets at Will

Significant advancements in 3D asset creation have enabled high-fidelity 3D generation from images. However, most existing representations remain inherently static, lacking the ability to model physically plausible behaviors under dynamic interactions. We present \textbf{PhysCreator}, a unified framework that directly generates simulation-ready 3D representations with spatially varying physical properties from images. Unlike prior approaches that treat physical attributes as simple post-hoc predictions or rely on expensive per-instance optimization, we reformulate physical property estimation within a unified 3D representation learning framework. We introduce a voxel-aligned latent space by lifting image observations into a spatially structured 3D representation, in which geometry, appearance, and physical properties are jointly modeled and inferred in a feed-forward manner. To enable scalable learning without densely annotated physical ground truth, we leverage vision-language distillation to transfer material-aware semantic priors from powerful vision-language models into spatially grounded voxel-level supervision. Experiments demonstrate that our method achieves superior visual quality, physical plausibility, and generalization compared to prior approaches.

TLDR: PhysCreator is a framework that generates simulation-ready 3D assets with spatially varying physical properties from images using a voxel-aligned latent space and vision-language distillation.

TLDR: PhysCreator 是一个能够从图像生成具有空间物理属性、可直接用于模拟的 3D 资产的框架，通过体素对齐潜空间和视觉-语言蒸馏实现。

CrossPair: Unlocking Generalist Visual Transformation Learning in Image Editors

Language models switch tasks from demonstrations, but vision still often relies on separate systems for restoration, editing, and stylization. A before--after example offers a shared instruction only if the transferable change can be separated from the scene that demonstrates it. We introduce CrossPair, which adapts a pretrained multimodal image editor to transfer diverse transformations from one exemplar pair. Exemplar-before, exemplar-after, and query images take fixed roles in its native VLM and VAE pathways: the VLM extracts operation semantics, while the VAE retains fine appearance and structure. A training-only, change-aware DINOv3 objective aligns feature-change directions across content-distinct scenes, guiding the output to follow the demonstrated relation without an inference-time encoder. We construct Transform-360K by grouping filtered pairs from conditional representations, restoration, editing, virtual try-on, and stylization under shared transformations. On Relation252K-derived seen and held-out transformations, \method improves target agreement and edit-direction similarity over released systems. Ablations show that the semantic and visual exemplar routes are complementary and that DINO relation alignment further strengthens transfer; Transform-360K training also improves aggregate held-out-operation scores.

TLDR: CrossPair is a framework that enables image editors to perform diverse visual transformations (restoration, editing, stylization) from a single exemplar pair by decoupling operation semantics via a VLM and structure via a VAE, guided by a new alignment objective.

TLDR: CrossPair 提出了一种通用的图像编辑学习框架，通过 VLM 提取操作语义并将 VAE 用于保留结构与细节，仅需一对示例图即可实现跨场景的图像变换（如修复、编辑、风格化）。

TopX: Text-to-Motion for Unseen Skeletons from the Skeleton File Alone

Text-to-motion models generate for the skeletons they were trained on, while a new character arrives as a skeleton file and nothing else. We study text-to-motion for skeletons that no training clip has used, with no clips of the character, no template, no retargeting and no fine-tuning. We present TopX, a 73M-parameter flow-matching transformer that generates joint rotations and a root trajectory for any kinematic tree from its tree, rest pose and joint names. Motion is normalised by the rest pose and the size of the skeleton, and the rest pose enters as a world-frame descriptor that does not depend on how the rig's author oriented each joint, with rest conventions randomised in training. Attention is factorised over the joints and the frames of a motion and carries a spatial--temporal encoding designed for skeleton graphs of any topology: queries and keys are rotated across joints by a heat-kernel coordinate of the skeleton graph, which is defined for every tree and does not change with the order of its joints or the eigenbasis of its Laplacian, and across frames by a rotary encoding of frame order. To measure what this buys, we build SkelShift: four motion corpora under one codec, 143,334 captioned clips on skeletons of 4 to 142 joints, from which 4,232 clips on 2,043 skeletons are held out by clip, rig, tree class or species and organised along topology, joint count, body plan, species and rig source, and scored by motion quality: Fréchet distances on public kinetic features and on kinematic descriptors, foot skating and ground contact. Against UniMate trained on the same clips, on test rigs without a training clip, TopX lowers the descriptor FID from 5.73 to 4.58 when UniMate's guidance is tuned for the kinetic FID, and the kinetic FID from 1.48 to 0.55 when it is tuned for the descriptor FID, also on motions no training skeleton performs; fewer of its frames sink below the ground.

TLDR: TopX is a flow-matching transformer that enables zero-shot text-to-motion generation for arbitrary, unseen skeleton topologies without requiring fine-tuning or retargeting, utilizing graph-invariant heat-kernel encodings.

TLDR: TopX 是一种基于流匹配 Transformer 的文本驱动动作生成模型，通过热核坐标编码实现了对任意未见骨骼结构的零样本生成，无需微调或重定向。

StaX-Net: Stackelberg Interleaved Asymmetric Network for Cross-Modality Medical Image Translation

Cross-modality medical image translation is challenged by anatomically inconsistent synthesis that can compromise clinical diagnosis. We propose StaX-Net, a Stackelberg interleaved asymmetric network that incorporates a partially coupled encoder-discriminator structure, and a 2D feature aggregation and spatial transformation (FAST) layer that distills target-supervised priors under the learning using privileged information paradigm. The shared blocks between the generator and discriminator enhance architectural feature alignment. To stabilize the leader-follower training, a Stackelberg-inspired asymmetric optimization scheme employs explicit shared-parameter freezing and asymmetric learning rates. The FAST layer distills target-domain priors during training and applies a source-only transform at inference. This constrains the synthesis process through target-informed feature representations relative to standard generative architectures. The code is available on GitHub for public use.

TLDR: StaX-Net introduces a Stackelberg-inspired asymmetric network for medical image translation, utilizing a FAST layer to integrate privileged information and stabilize training via a leader-follower optimization strategy.

TLDR: StaX-Net 提出了一种受斯塔克伯格博弈启发的非对称网络，利用 FAST 层整合特权信息，并通过主从式优化策略来增强医学图像翻译的稳定性和解剖一致性。
