Semantically Aligned KV Cache Compression for Efficient Agentic Autoregressive Video Generation

Agentic autoregressive video generation combines semantic planning, sequential generation, and visual verification for controllable synthesis. Growing visual histories enlarge the key-value (KV) cache, increasing memory use and attention costs across candidate rollouts. Existing compression methods primarily optimize individual generators without the planner's semantic constraints, risking low priority for states supporting requested attributes or actions. We introduce SemanticKV, a training-free method for semantically aligned KV cache compression. To our knowledge, SemanticKV is the first KV cache compression method for agentic autoregressive video generation that explicitly uses planner-extracted semantic constraints to guide retention. Text cross-attention identifies constraint-related video positions, whose self-attention queries are collected into separate groups. Attention weights are averaged within each group and maximized across groups, giving each cached entry a high score when strongly used by any represented constraint. A single top-k selection retains high-scoring entries under the memory budget, leaving their keys, values, and positions unchanged. For fixed query groups and equal entry costs, this selection minimizes an additive upper bound on worst-group discarded attention. The method operates independently within each candidate without retraining or additional video generations. We evaluate four AR video backbones on three benchmarks for 5-, 30-, and 60-second generation. On Causal Forcing at a compression ratio of 0.7, SemanticKV obtains a VBench Total Score of 84.10, compared with 84.05 for the strongest compressed baseline and 83.99 for Full KV. It achieves a 1.22x speedup over Full KV and reduces KV-cache memory from 6.04 GB to 1.81 GB, a 3.35x reduction.

TLDR: SemanticKV is a training-free KV cache compression method for agentic autoregressive video generation that leverages planner-extracted semantic constraints to selectively retain high-priority cache entries.

GenRM-Flow: Generators are Process-aware Reward Models in Flow Matching

Aligning video generation models with human preference depends critically on the quality of the reward model that scores generated videos. Current video reward models, however, source their reward signal from a model's representational rather than generative capacity: they either repurpose vision-language models as pixel-space scorers, or train auxiliary scoring heads on top of frozen generator features. The generator's actual ability to predict velocities, denoise latents, and produce videos plays no role in scoring its own generations. We argue that this overlooks a structural property of offline preference learning. Any preference optimization objective that approximates an offline preference dataset mathematically targets a strictly defined KL-constrained optimal policy. By isolating the reward term from this analytical solution and mapping the exact generation log-likelihood to its flow-matching surrogate, the empirical reward emerges deterministically as the relative difference in continuous-time velocity-prediction errors. We formalize this equivalence into GenRM-Flow, a framework where a preference-tuned video generator inherently operates as a process-aware reward model directly in the noisy latent space. We further show theoretically that distinct fine-tuning objectives, whether contrastive like DPO and IPO or non-contrastive like RWR, are alternative optimization pathways toward the same optimal policy, each inducing a reward readout from the generator's velocity-prediction errors. Furthermore, the extracted process reward seamlessly substitutes external VLM scorers in FlowAWR. Operating purely on native latent outputs without auxiliary reward networks, this generative reward drives measurable improvements in downstream video generation.

TLDR: GenRM-Flow leverages a generative model's own velocity-prediction errors as a reward signal, effectively turning the video generator into its own reward model and eliminating the need for auxiliary VLM scorers.

TLDR: GenRM-Flow 通过利用视频生成模型自身的轨迹预测误差作为奖励信号，使生成器本身兼任奖励模型，从而消除了对额外视觉语言模型（VLM）评分器的依赖。

DynOLAT: OLAT Video Prediction for Controllable Dynamic Full-Body Relighting

Accurate lighting response is essential for realistic dynamic human relighting, because shadows, highlights, and color changes should follow the target illumination in both direction and relative strength. Current learning-based relighting methods can generate visually plausible videos, but they often exhibit imprecise lighting responses: shadows and highlights may drift away from the target direction, and the output brightness may not faithfully follow the relative intensities in the target environment, making the result closer to appearance harmonization than controllable relighting. To address this issue, we propose DynOLAT, a generalizable OLAT video prediction framework for dynamic full-body humans. Instead of directly synthesizing a single relit appearance, our method predicts temporally consistent One-Light-At-a-Time (OLAT) basis videos from flat-lit inputs, and then linearly combines the predicted bases according to the target environment map. This OLAT-based formulation makes the directional response explicit in the predicted bases, while the relative lighting strength is determined by the linear weights derived from the environment map. We further introduce a dynamic capture pipeline and the first large-scale dynamic full-body OLAT dataset, comprising over 24 million frames from 224 identity-outfit combinations, where different outfits of the same person define separate combinations. Experiments show that DynOLAT responds more faithfully to variations in target lighting direction and relative intensity than the tested baselines, enabling controllable video relighting while preserving fine appearance details and temporal coherence. The dataset and processing tools will be publicly released to foster future research.

TLDR: DynOLAT addresses inaccurate lighting response in human relighting by predicting temporally consistent OLAT basis videos, enabling physically controllable full-body relighting and providing a massive new dynamic dataset.

TLDR: DynOLAT通过预测时间一致的OLAT基础视频，解决了现有动态人体光照重绘中光影不准确的问题，实现了可控的全身光照重绘，并发布了大规模动态数据集。

CrossLiDAR: Controllable Multi-Sensor LiDAR Sequence Generation

High-fidelity LiDAR simulation remains a bottleneck for scalable development and validation of autonomous systems. Existing generators can synthesize realistic scans, but they are typically constrained to a single sensor configuration, offer limited control over structure and geometry, and drift in long-horizon 4D generation. We present CrossLiDAR, a unified transformer-based framework capable of generating controllable and spatio-temporally consistent multi-sensor LiDAR sequences. Our approach leverages a rectified flow objective with a Multi-Modal Diffusion Transformer (MM-DiT) backbone, within a compact latent space of range images. To support multiple LiDAR sensors, an Inter-sensor Module enables interaction across sensors, while a ControlNet-style branch provides structural conditioning (e.g., BEV priors or 3D bounding boxes). For modeling the temporal consistency, CrossLiDAR generates short $N$-frame sequences and extends them autoregressively to improve long-horizon coherence. Our model significantly outperforms state-of-the-art baselines, achieving an average improvement of 29.56\% in JSD and 15.36\% in MMD evaluated on nuScenes and the Waymo Dataset. CrossLiDAR bridges the gap to modern autonomous-driving simulation by enabling calibration-consistent multi-sensor LiDAR rollouts over long horizons for controllable, scenario-based testing.

TLDR: CrossLiDAR is a transformer-based generative framework that uses rectified flow and MM-DiT to produce spatio-temporally consistent, multi-sensor LiDAR sequences with structural control.

TLDR: CrossLiDAR 是一个基于 Transformer 的生成式框架，利用 rectified flow 和 MM-DiT 实现具有结构可控性且时空一致的多传感器 LiDAR 序列生成。

MySign: A High-Fidelity Motion-Capture Dataset for 3D Sign Generation in Bahasa Isyarat Malaysia

Progress in 3D sign generation is constrained by the scarcity of high-fidelity 3D sign language datasets, a limitation that is particularly severe for under-resourced languages. We introduce MySign, the first motion-capture dataset for isolated signs in Bahasa Isyarat Malaysia (BIM). Its 5,000 samples cover 1,000 lexical items (glosses) and were produced by five Deaf native signers. Prior datasets that rely on SMPL-X parameters regressed from video provide pseudo-labels, which are often affected by depth ambiguity, inaccurate finger poses, and temporal jitter. MySign instead provides ground-truth SMPL-X sequences retargeted from recordings made with a motion-capture setup tailored for sign language; these sequences offer higher-fidelity motion, particularly in fine-grained hand and finger articulation. A controlled comparison on the same glosses and signers indicates that training on these sequences improves generation over training on such pseudo-labels. We establish MySign as a challenging benchmark for gloss-conditioned 3D sign generation: state-of-the-art models produce visually plausible outputs but fall short of the ground truth, most notably in fine-grained articulation, revealing persistent gaps in modeling articulated sign motion. In contrast, recognition baselines consistently achieve high lexical accuracy, indicating that the dataset reliably supports supervised learning. The dataset is available at https://huggingface.co/datasets/mysigner/MySign

TLDR: MySign is a novel, high-fidelity motion-capture dataset for Bahasa Isyarat Malaysia (BIM) containing 5,000 isolated signs, addressing the limitations of existing pseudo-labeled SMPL-X data by providing accurate ground-truth motion for fine-grained articulation.

TLDR: MySign 是一个高质量的马来西亚手语 (BIM) 动作捕捉数据集，包含 5,000 个孤立的手语样本，通过提供精准的真实动作捕捉数据（而非伪标签），解决了现有研究在精细手指动作建模上的局限性。

Does Pose Encoding Matter? Analyzing Geometric Representations for Discrete Motion Vocabulary Learning

Compressing continuous human motion into a finite vocabulary of discrete tokens is now the first stage of many motion understanding and generation pipelines. Vector quantization partitions a latent space into Voronoi cells around a finite codebook, but the geometry of that latent space is inherited from the input pose encoding, and it is unclear which encoding admits an efficient discretization. We study four encodings that place skeletal motion in different geometric spaces: Gaussian joint heatmaps on the pixel lattice, which we show to be a morphological dilation of the joint point set by a quadratic structuring function; root-relative joint positions in Euclidean space; local joint rotations on $\SO$ through a continuous 6D embedding; and a redundant full kinematic encoding. For each, we train VQ-VAE tokenizers whose codebooks lie on the unit hypersphere, varying codebook size, temporal stride, and code dimension. Beyond reconstruction, we measure codebook utilization and the effective vocabulary size, i.e., the number of equiprobable spherical Voronoi cells with the same entropy as the observed token distribution. Rotation and position encodings keep converting additional codewords into effective vocabulary, whereas the full kinematic encoding saturates at a few hundred effective cells and heatmaps at fewer than sixty. The smallest code dimension gives the highest utilization for three of the four encodings, and the effect of temporal stride depends on the encoding. Encodings of the same pose family can thus yield effective vocabularies differing by an order of magnitude, a difference that reconstruction loss alone does not reveal, which points to input geometry as a first-order design choice in motion tokenization.

TLDR: The paper investigates how different geometric input encodings affect the efficiency of discrete motion tokenization, revealing that choice of encoding significantly impacts effective vocabulary size and latent space discretization.

SoftPAT3D: Physics-Augmented Text-to-3D Scene Generation with Deformables

We present SoftPAT3D, a physics-augmented framework for text-to-3D scene generation in which deformable objects, such as cloth, stuffed toys and ropes, are generated as physically valid, simulation-ready bodies and placed in intersection-free contact with the furniture that supports them, with one another and with themselves. Existing physics-augmented pipelines simulate rigid bodies only and cannot accommodate a generated deformable object, which text-to-3D generation returns as a geometrically invalid mesh, sometimes self-intersecting, that has no rest shape, no material model and no admissible initial state. These are the inputs a deformable simulation requires, and we construct each from information already in the pipeline. The rest shape comes from how the object is manufactured rather than from the generated physically inconsistent deformed configuration, and a material library assigns each deformable object its initial elastic parameters, which can then be optimized. The occluded support is regenerated and converted into a watertight collision mesh, each object is released from an optimizable placement above the supporting part that the prompt specifies, and the complete scene is driven to static equilibrium under one unified contact model, with completion verified by geometric checks and a vision-language model. When a scene fails the prompt, a grid search over placements corrects it, and the adjoint of a differentiable simulation of the same contact energy reaches the targets the grid does not, places several bodies jointly and recovers and optimizes material parameters. Multi-body scenes equilibrate in a single simulation, and the pipeline also runs from the text prompt alone. On all sixty-five prompts, SoftPAT3D produces no undesired interpenetration of any kind, whereas every other system does, and its scenes directly support scene editing and robotic manipulation.

TLDR: SoftPAT3D is a physics-augmented framework that transforms invalid text-to-3D deformable meshes into simulation-ready, collision-free, and physically stable assets through automated material assignment and differentiable simulation.
