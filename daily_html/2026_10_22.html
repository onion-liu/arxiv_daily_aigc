Attn-Forcing: Learning Full-Context Attention Proxies for Streaming Video Generation

Increasing the attention context length significantly improves streaming video generation quality, while incurring prohibitive memory and computational costs as the context grows. Existing methods attempt to capture this contextual information through attention selection or compression, but remain limited by bounded representations and lack explicit attention-level supervision to retain historical contributions to current queries. To address this, we introduce Attn-Forcing, a novel framework that preserves full-context attention contributions for streaming video generation under bounded memory and computation. We achieve this by introducing attention proxies that continuously approximate the attention responses induced by the historical context. Specifically, we implement the proxies as learnable visual latents and employ a Proxy Update Module to incorporate visual context from historical latents. Moreover, we propose an Attention Matching Loss to explicitly constrain the attention behavior of the learned proxies using full-context attention supervision. Extensive qualitative and quantitative experiments demonstrate that Attn-Forcing consistently improves streaming video generation quality and achieves state-of-the-art performance over existing baseline methods.

TLDR: Attn-Forcing addresses long-context limitations in streaming video generation by using learnable visual latent proxies and attention-level supervision to approximate full-context contributions under constant memory constraints.

TLDR: Attn-Forcing 提出了一种通过可学习的视觉潜在代理（proxies）和注意力匹配损失来近似全上下文的方法，从而在保持有限计算资源的同时提升流式视频生成的质量。

RealMe: Immersive Volumetric Video from Minimal Captures

This paper addresses the problem of creating immersive volumetric videos from sparse and low-resolution capture resources. Conventional dense multi-view reconstruction methods can produce high-quality volumetric videos, but their reliance on costly capture setups makes them impractical in real-world scenarios. Recent approaches use generative models to relax capture requirements, but still suffer from limited resolution and 4D inconsistency. To overcome these constraints, we propose RealMe, a unified framework built around a high-resolution video diffusion model that enhances limited captures into high-resolution (2K), 4D-consistent videos for volumetric video reconstruction. Specifically, RealMe constructs auxiliary data from capture videos and encodes them into latents along with layout-aware positional embeddings. The video diffusion model adapts factorized spatio-temporal attention to the multi-view capture layout, denoises these latents across viewpoints and time, and decodes them into enhanced videos to refine the 4D representation. This adaptation helps the model capture temporal dynamics and multi-view relationships, leading to more 4D-consistent generation. To reduce the data gap between generated videos and real captures, we apply a latent frequency loss that provides frequency-domain supervision in latent space. Extensive experiments on the public DNA-Rendering benchmark and our self-captured RealVV dataset demonstrate that RealMe is able to create immersive volumetric videos and outperforms state-of-the-art methods in visual fidelity and 4D consistency.

TLDR: RealMe is a unified framework that uses high-resolution video diffusion models to reconstruct 4D-consistent, 2K volumetric videos from sparse, low-resolution capture sources.

TLDR: RealMe 是一个统一的框架，利用高分辨率视频扩散模型将稀疏、低分辨率的原始捕捉数据重构为具有 4D 一致性的 2K 体积视频。

Looking Ahead: Modeling Future Evolution in Autoregressive Video Generation

Autoregressive video diffusion models generate videos chunk by chunk, yet producing plausible continuations does not guarantee that they capture how scenes evolve. Under teacher forcing, strong temporal redundancy may allow local visual continuity to serve as a shortcut for current-chunk denoising, reducing the pressure to model visual evolution. We introduce Future Chunk Prediction (FCP), an auxiliary objective jointly optimized with teacher-forced denoising that predicts temporally separated chunks without access to the intervening visual context. Drawing supervision directly from training videos, FCP requires no additional pretrained visual encoder and leaves autoregressive inference unchanged. Our method achieves superior physical plausibility over standard teacher-forced autoregressive training with a lower overall training compute budget, highlighting the effectiveness and efficiency of future-chunk supervision for modeling visual evolution. Intriguingly, our visualizations suggest that FCP fosters a more structured representation space, which may be valuable for building autoregressive world models.

TLDR: The paper introduces Future Chunk Prediction (FCP), an auxiliary objective for autoregressive video diffusion models that forces the network to predict distant video frames, effectively overcoming the 'shortcut' problem inherent in teacher-forced training.

TLDR: 该论文提出了未来块预测（FCP）作为自回归视频扩散模型的辅助目标，通过要求模型预测远距离的视频片段，有效解决了教师强制（teacher forcing）训练中存在的捷径学习问题。

CausalAlign: Causal Alignment of Video Generation Models for Consistent Ego-Motion

Diffusion-based video generation models (VGMs) can produce visually plausible videos, yet their camera ego-motion often drifts over long horizons. Camera ego-motion determines how the viewpoint changes between frames and is essential for maintaining consistent geometry and trajectories. To address this problem, we propose CausalAlign, a causal alignment framework with two components. First, we diagnose how precise ego motion is represented, used, and retained within VGMs using linear probes and causal interventions, revealing a rise-and-fall pattern during denoising. Second, we strengthen ego-motion state in VGM by aligning the representations at the identified layers to a 3D foundation model through delta-token space. This space encodes the changes between consecutive frames, allowing the alignment to focus on ego-motion dynamics rather than static scene content. With Wan2.2 fine-tuned on DL3DV and RealEstate10K, CausalAlign can largely improve motion accuracy, geometric consistency, and temporal smoothness, while preserving visual quality. For example, it improves geometric consistency by more than 20% relative to the base model. Moreover, with the fine-tuned VGM, CausalAlign improves the success rate of world action models (WAMs) by 10 points on mobile manipulation tasks of RoboCasa365 benchmark. Overall, CausalAlign improves ego-motion consistency in diffusion-based VGMs and yields noticeable gains in embodied models.

TLDR: CausalAlign introduces a framework to improve ego-motion consistency in video generation models by diagnosing motion representation through causal interventions and aligning latent states with 3D foundation models.

TLDR: CausalAlign 提出了一种通过因果干预诊断模型内部运动表征，并利用 3D 基础模型对齐潜空间，从而提升视频生成模型中自我运动（ego-motion）一致性的框架。

JointLight: Human-Centric Video Relighting with Asynchronous RGB-Irradiance Joint Modeling

Understanding and modeling light transport in human-centric scenes is a longstanding challenge. While recent progress in diffusion models has boosted research in relighting, precise illumination control still lags, primarily due to the scarcity of diverse, high-quality, precisely labeled video relighting datasets. Existing end-to-end relighting approaches model light transport implicitly through neural networks, which substantially complicates controllability. In contrast, some works resort to path-traced irradiance cues as an explicit blueprint for light transport. However, such constructed irradiance cues are prone to artifacts and hinder generative quality when directly used as control signals. With the key observation that noise corruption removes high-frequency artifacts and preserves low-frequency light transport, we introduce an asynchronous joint-modeling paradigm that facilitates fine-grained illumination control. During two-stage training, we force the model to jointly denoise RGB and ground-truth irradiance frames with separate timesteps scheduling. A domain matching strategy is applied to the second stage to bridge the domain gap between irradiance cues and ground-truth irradiance maps. At inference time, we introduce an asynchronous hijacked generation pipeline that facilitates flexible illumination control. For training data, we develop a multi-illumination high-quality synthetic video dataset with paired irradiance maps using a powerful game engine. Trained exclusively on this dataset, our model exhibits robust generalization to real- world scenarios and outperforms prior approaches in both lighting fidelity and generative quality. The code and dataset will be publicly released.

TLDR: JointLight proposes an asynchronous joint-modeling paradigm for video relighting that uses separate diffusion timesteps for RGB and irradiance cues, enabling precise illumination control and better generative quality.

TLDR: JointLight 提出了一种用于视频重打光的异步联合建模范式，通过对 RGB 和辐照度线索采用分离的时间步调度进行去噪，实现了精确的照明控制和高质量的生成效果。

Spatially Gated Demographic Steering for Debiasing Multi-Person Text-to-Image Generation

Text-to-Image models reproduce societal stereotypes, and existing mitigation applies one demographic correction to the whole scene. In multi-person scene generation this creates a trade-off: global steering improves aggregate balance only by homogenizing the people within an image. Steering-based methods like Embedding Arithmetic drive the intra-image diversity below the unsteered baseline. Text- and latent-projection approaches shift the aggregate skew with only marginal gains in intra-image diversity. We present Spatially Gated Steering (SGS), which steers the demographics of each person independently by extracting per-person masks from the Diffusion Transformer's (DiT) internal attention maps at a critical denoising timestep and applying a distinct demographic vector within each mask for the remaining steps. SGS needs no fine-tuning, inversion or layout specifications, and uses a lightweight segmentor over frozen DiT attention features to localize subjects at early timesteps. On skewed occupational prompts, SGS reaches high intra-image racial and gender diversity while keeping aggregate balance competitive and image quality near baseline, a result we validate on two architecturally distinct diffusion transformers, FLUX.1-Dev and SD3.5-Large. Codebase and model weights are available at https://github.com/cvims/spatially-gated-steering

TLDR: Spatially Gated Steering (SGS) enables fine-grained demographic control in multi-person text-to-image generation by leveraging DiT internal attention maps to apply localized debiasing without requiring model fine-tuning.

TLDR: Spatially Gated Steering (SGS) 通过利用 DiT 内部注意力图实现对多人物文生图场景的局部去偏，无需模型微调即可实现精细的人口统计学特征控制。

CineEvolve: An Agentic Self-Evolution Framework for Cinematic Multi-Shot Video Generation

Cinematic multi-shot video generation requires both narrative planning and iterative refinement. Existing methods either lack narrative reasoning or rely on feed-forward workflows without policy updates. In this paper, we propose CineEvolve, an agentic self-evolution framework for cinematic multi-shot video generation that unifies a Director and a Critic within a single vision-language policy. Emulating real-world filmmaking that couples iterative direction with critical feedback, we train CineEvolve via alternating reinforcement learning, where the Director surfaces flaws to sharpen the Critic, and the updated Critic guides the Director's targeted revisions. To stabilize co-evolution, we introduce Evolutionary GRPO (E-GRPO), leveraging historical anchoring and revision safeguarding to mitigate advantage inflation. Additionally, we contribute CineTraj, a 40K cinematic trajectory dataset spanning creation, critique, and revision. Extensive experiments demonstrate that CineEvolve outperforms existing approaches. Our code will be available at https://github.com/JingZhe-8/CineEvolve.

TLDR: CineEvolve is an agentic self-evolution framework for cinematic multi-shot video generation that uses an alternating reinforcement learning mechanism between a Director and a Critic to enable iterative narrative planning and refinement.

TLDR: CineEvolve 是一种用于电影级多镜头视频生成的智能体自演化框架，通过“导演”与“评论家”之间的交替强化学习实现叙事规划与迭代优化。

Unlocking Spatial Grounding in Flow-Matching Models via SNR Tuning

Despite the success of foundation models in image synthesis, achieving fine-grained spatial control remains a significant challenge, typically requiring auxiliary structural adapters or complex inference-time steering. While modern vision-language (VLM) text encoders possess the latent capacity to represent spatial coordinates, generative models often fail to leverage this information. We demonstrate that the signal-to-noise ratio (SNR) distribution is a critical determinant of spatial grounding in flow-matching models. Using a novel synthetic benchmark of geometric primitives, we isolate structural mechanics from textural complexity inherent in natural imagery and introduce a rigorous color-aware layout metric to quantify spatial fidelity. We show that training on a centered SNR distribution results in poor layout adherence (25.9%). Instead, we identify high-noise regimes as a "critical learning phase" for spatial grounding. Shifting the training focus toward these stages increases layout adherence to 80.8%, effectively reaching the 83.6% reconstruction ceiling of the underlying variational autoencoder. We find that the necessity for this shift scales with resolution and aspect ratio variance, and that the conservative noise schedules employed by models like FLUX.1 are insufficient for precise layout adherence. Finally, we show that these findings translate to real-world imagery via the COCO dataset (1.0 vs. 57.3 Average Precision), where state-of-the-art layout adherence is unlocked through distribution tuning alone, without architectural modifications.

TLDR: The paper identifies that flow-matching models struggle with spatial grounding due to poor SNR scheduling; by shifting training focus to high-noise regimes, the authors achieve state-of-the-art layout adherence without architectural changes.

TLDR: 该论文指出流匹配模型在空间对齐能力上的不足源于信噪比（SNR）调度问题；通过将训练重点转移至高噪声阶段，在不改变模型架构的情况下大幅提升了布局遵循能力。

Sparsity Should Be ALLOTted by Output: Training-Free Allocation of Block-Sparse Attention for Video Diffusion Transformers

Full 3D attention is costly in video Diffusion Transformers. Block-sparse attention reduces this cost by computing only selected attention blocks. However, existing methods either overlook the potential to optimize sparsity allocation or rely on heuristic rules or local metrics to guide it. In this work, we observe that sparsifying attention in different layers and at different denoising steps affects final-video fidelity to substantially different degrees. Yet the identities of the most sensitive layer-step pairs remain nearly unchanged across inputs. Building on these findings, we propose Allocating Layer-step Latency by Output Tests (ALLOT), a training-free sparsity allocation method. Treating each layer-step pair as a cell, ALLOT estimates each cell's contribution to final-video error through (i) cell probing and (ii) trajectory calibration, then jointly optimizes cell densities under a measured-latency budget. It preserves existing selection strategies and kernels, replacing only the allocation component. At equal or shorter wall-clock time, ALLOT outperforms every compared allocation strategy with XAttention, with an 11.8 dB PSNR gain on HunyuanVideo. Extensive experiments across four additional published pipelines on Wan2.1, Wan2.2, and HunyuanVideo show that ALLOT outperforms or ties the default in all nine settings.

TLDR: ALLOT is a training-free optimization method that improves video diffusion transformer efficiency by intelligently allocating block-sparse attention budgets based on sensitivity analysis of layer-step pairs.

TLDR: ALLOT 是一种无需训练的稀疏性分配方法，通过分析不同层和去噪步骤的敏感度，智能分配视频扩散 Transformer 中的块稀疏注意力预算，从而在提升效率的同时保持视频质量。

CCG Diffusion: Combinatory Categorial Grammar-Driven Compositional and Syntactically Disambiguated Text-to-Image Generation

Text-to-image generation models do not explicitly receive the syntactic structure of the prompt, i.e., which word modifies which and where each phrase attaches. Consequently, they often struggle to bind attributes to the correct objects, to depict spatial relations and counts correctly, and to render syntactically ambiguous prompts in the intended interpretation. Existing syntax-based control methods mainly address attribute binding and have limited ability to control spatial relations and counts or to specify the interpretation of syntactically ambiguous prompts. They rely on phrase-structure or dependency grammars and require additional rules or constraints on the tree structure to extract modification relations between words or to specify a syntactic interpretation. We propose CCG Diffusion, a training-free method that controls image generation using Combinatory Categorial Grammar (CCG). In CCG, the category of each word carries rich syntactic information: it specifies what the word combines with and what phrase it forms. Modification relations between words can therefore be read directly from a CCG parse. Moreover, prepositional phrase (PP) attachment ambiguity, a representative syntactic ambiguity, can be resolved simply by fixing the preposition's category. CCG Diffusion applies the same two CCG-based mechanisms, prompt rewriting and attention guidance, both to controlling attribute binding, spatial relations, and counts and to resolving PP ambiguity. On two architecturally different backbones, Stable Diffusion and Sana, CCG Diffusion achieves the highest scores among the compared methods in attribute binding and 2D spatial relations and significantly outperforms all of them in PP ambiguity resolution.

TLDR: CCG Diffusion is a training-free method that leverages Combinatory Categorial Grammar (CCG) to improve text-to-image generation by explicitly modeling syntactic structures, thereby enhancing attribute binding, spatial relation handling, and resolving PP-attachment ambiguity.

TLDR: CCG Diffusion 是一种无需训练的方法，利用组合范畴语法（CCG）显式建模文本提示的句法结构，从而提升了文生图模型在属性绑定、空间关系处理及歧义短语消解方面的准确性。

LiveAct: Towards Real-Time Multi-Agent World Models with Heterogeneous Action Interfaces

Interactive video world models predict how an environment evolves under user actions, but most existing systems assume one controlled agent and one task-specific action interface. We study multi-agent video world modeling, where one model generates a joint visual future for multiple controlled agents while interpreting heterogeneous controls ranging from binary game buttons to continuous robot commands. This task presents two complementary challenges. First, every agent-indexed action stream must control its intended visual entity independently. Second, the model must adapt the meaning of each control variable across different tasks, where the same tensor dimension may denote an attack, steering command, or robot command. We introduce LiveAct, centered on a Heterogeneous Action Interface (HAI) whose two components mirror this decomposition. To support independent control, Agent-Aware Spatial Modulation uses language-driven spatial gates and agent-specific attention to bind each action stream to its intended visual agent. To accommodate heterogeneous controls, Schema-Guided Action Adaptation describes each control variable in language and modulates its semantic token with the synchronized numerical value. We further transfer the complete interface to a blockwise causal generator through teacher forcing and rollout-aware distribution matching, enabling efficient streaming generation while retaining agent-specific control and action semantics. We instantiate the formulation on six multi-agent sources spanning games and multi-robot manipulation, and evaluate visual quality, action following, agent attribution, and latency. Across games and robot manipulation, LiveAct reaches 0.898 and 0.942 agent-attribution accuracy without agent locations or identity crops, and the causal generator attains 17.70 FPS at $512\times 448$.

TLDR: LiveAct is a multi-agent video world model that enables independent control of multiple entities using a Heterogeneous Action Interface (HAI), supporting diverse input types and real-time inference.

TLDR: LiveAct 是一个多智能体视频世界模型，通过异构动作接口（HAI）实现了对多个实体的独立控制，支持多样的动作输入类型并具备实时生成能力。

AdvFidelity: Unifying Preference Alignment and Few-Step Image Generation within Reinforcement Learning

Practical image generation requires both human preference alignment and few-step efficiency, yet combining reinforcement learning (RL) with distillation is often multi-stage and may erode RL gains We observe that few-step RL progressively improves structural coherence, even with specialized rewards, but leaves low-level artifacts that these rewards may fail to penalize. To address this gap, we propose AdvFidelity, which integrates artifact-sensitive fidelity supervision into RL through an online adversarial reward. This enables joint reward alignment and high-fidelity few-step generation within a single RL stage, without distribution matching distillation (DMD) or other explicit distillation objectives. We train a discriminator to rank high-quality references (positives) above content-matched reconstructions (negatives) produced by the current generator. Reconstructing moderately noised references largely preserves style and scene layout while exposing low-level degradations. This encourages the discriminator to assess artifacts rather than content differences. With negatives reconstructed online, alternating updates adapt fidelity supervision to the generator's evolving failure modes. References can come from existing target-domain data or full-step model samples. Built on LeapAlign, AdvFidelity backpropagates gradients from both rewards through the few-step sampling trajectories used at inference. Across two model backbones and three task settings, AdvFidelity matches full-step LeapAlign in alignment, task performance, and overall generation quality and remains highly competitive with other evaluated full-step RL methods, using only 4 or 8 steps without CFG.

TLDR: AdvFidelity integrates artifact-sensitive adversarial supervision into reinforcement learning to enable high-fidelity, few-step image generation without the need for complex multi-stage distillation.

TLDR: AdvFidelity 通过将对伪影敏感的对抗监督集成到强化学习中，实现了无需复杂多阶段蒸馏的高保真少步图像生成。

T-DIG: Text-Conditioned Diversity for Image Generation

Text-to-image (T2I) platforms typically return several images per prompt so users can explore different visual interpretations and choose one that matches their intent. However, T2I models are usually optimized for single-image quality which comes at a cost of multi-image diversity, limiting this exploration. Diversity is also contextual: one user wants variations in background, while another wants them in artistic style, and the same user may later want variations in clothing. The generated images should differ along whatever axis the user cares about. In this paper, we introduce T-DIG, a text-conditioned diversity framework for image generation and editing. T-DIG is an iterative framework comprising multiple modules that lets users type prompts, inspect default generations, request variations using natural language, and continue refining outputs until they're satisfied. At its core, T-DIG uses an LLM policy that produces K axis-conditioned prompts or edit instructions for a frozen generator or editor. We train this policy with GRPO using a text-conditioned diversity reward and alignment penalties. Because the image generator remains frozen, T-DIG applies to open-weight, distilled, and proprietary models. We also release the first dataset for text-conditioned prompt diversity in image generation, with the training and validation splits built from human-written axes and a test set drawn from real user interactions with T-DIG. T-DIG far exceeds baselines in improving text-conditioned diversity, while preserving base-prompt alignment across a variety of metrics and human judgements. We show that user-directed diversity exploration can be added to a wide range of image generation models, open and closed, without the need to access their weights. Website: https://image-study.org/t-dig/

TLDR: T-DIG introduces an iterative framework that uses a text-conditioned LLM policy and reinforcement learning (GRPO) to steer image generators toward specific user-defined axes of diversity without requiring model weight fine-tuning.

TLDR: T-DIG 提出了一种迭代式框架，利用文本条件大模型策略和强化学习（GRPO），在无需微调模型权重的情况下，引导图像生成器实现用户自定义维度的多样性。

Pair-Free Coarse-to-Real Video Generation via Real-to-Coarse Image Supervision

Coarse-to-real (C2R) video generation converts coarse animation, simulation, or game-rendered videos into photorealistic videos while preserving scene layout, camera motion, and dynamics. Training such models requires aligned (coarse, realistic) video pairs, which are expensive to create and confine prior systems to narrow synthetic domains. We exploit a simple asymmetry: paired videos are hard to obtain, but paired real-to-coarse images are easy to construct with existing editing and 3D reconstruction tools. Using these image pairs, we train the DINO-R2C model to transfer realistic images to coarse images in DINO feature space, and apply it frame-wise to ordinary real videos to synthesize pseudo-coarse videos. The C2R# model then learns to generate each real video from its pseudo-coarse counterpart, free from needing (coarse, realistic) video pairs. At inference, the DINO-R2C model is discarded and actual coarse videos drive the C2R# model directly. Thus, our pair-free training scales with any real-video corpora rather than with paired synthetic videos. We will release the model and project webpage at https://gonzalognogales.github.io/coarse2real-sharp/.

TLDR: The paper introduces DINO-R2C and C2R#, a framework for coarse-to-real video generation that eliminates the need for expensive paired video datasets by leveraging image-based pseudo-coarse supervision.

DualHeads: Reward-tilted few-step diffusion made simple

Distilled diffusion models generate high-quality images in a few sampling steps, but aligning them with task requirements and human preferences is difficult. Methods developed for multi-step diffusion models rely on differentiable rewards, or on score fields and stochastic trajectories that distilled generators may lack. We ask whether a few-step generator can be adapted using only external reward evaluations and reference samples, and show that two binary classifiers suffice. Our method, DualHeads, implements them as lightweight heads on a frozen diffusion backbone. The reward head learns which samples of the same prompt score higher, the reference head separates reference images from generated ones, and the generator maximizes a weighted sum of their logits. When the heads are optimal, this objective is KL-regularized maximization of the learned reward, and the ratio of head weights sets the regularization strength. Empirically, with both rule-based and model-based rewards, a few-step DualHeads generator reaches task rewards close to those of 40-step reinforcement learning methods while preserving image quality, at no extra inference cost. We hope that this simple and elegant dual-head formulation will help advance few-step reward-tilted generation.
