Learning-to-Memorize: Dynamic Context Management for Long-Horizon Autoregressive Video Generation

Autoregressive video generation has achieved impressive results on short clips, yet generating long videos remains challenging due to error accumulation over extended horizons, where each predicted frame depends on potentially imperfect previous frames. Existing approaches typically rely on static context management strategies, such as sliding-window caches that discard early frames or fixed sink frames that permanently preserve initial content. These designs either lose critical long-range context or anchor generation to outdated information, which can lead to degraded fidelity, motion stagnation, and reduced diversity. In this paper, we propose Learning to Memorize (L2M), a learned context memory management framework that dynamically retains, evicts, and evolves historical frames according to predicted importance. The framework consists of an importance prediction router that estimates the relevance of each historical frame, degradation-aware training that improves robustness to noisy or corrupted context, and a dynamic memory initialization and evolution mechanism that updates long-term memory using exponential moving average (EMA) importance scores. This allows high-quality frames to replace stale anchors and enables memory to adapt naturally to scene evolution. Trained with a teacher-student distillation objective and sparsity regularization, L2M makes efficient use of a fixed KV-cache budget while preserving the most informative contextual information. Extensive experiments demonstrate that L2M improves long-horizon video generation quality and temporal consistency over existing baselines, providing a learned strategy for context memory management in long autoregressive video generation.

TLDR: L2M is a learned context management framework for autoregressive video generation that dynamically retains and updates historical frames using an importance router to mitigate error accumulation over long horizons.

TLDR: L2M 是一种用于自回归视频生成的学习型上下文管理框架，通过重要性路由机制动态保留和更新历史帧，从而缓解长视频生成中的误差累积问题。

Z-Cache: Accelerating Diffusion Transformers via Self-Reflection

Diffusion transformers have become the most powerful models for visual generation, but still suffer from massive computation costs. To solve this problem, feature caching has been proposed to cache the features of diffusion models in the previous computation steps and then reuse them in the following caching steps, which brings significant acceleration but also degradation in generation quality. To address this problem, this paper proposes Z-cache as a feature caching method that can maintain high-quality generation through self-reflection. Concretely, we observe that the error from feature caching tends to be sharply reduced after each full computation. Based on this observation, Z-Cache is designed to first predict the features in the future caching steps and then perform a full computation. After that, Z-Cache returns to the caching steps and re-predicts them based on the previous and the current computation steps, which brings correction in features. Experiments demonstrate that with Z-Cache, diffusion transformers achieve comparable generation quality to the original model but with faster inference speed, for instance, 6.22X acceleration on FLUX-dev for text-to-image generation. Our code is released on GitHub.

TLDR: Z-Cache is a novel feature caching strategy for diffusion transformers that uses self-reflection to iteratively correct cached features, achieving 6.22x speedup on FLUX-dev without compromising quality.

TLDR: Z-Cache 是一种用于扩散 Transformer 的特征缓存策略，通过自反射机制纠正缓存误差，在不损失图像质量的前提下，在 FLUX-dev 模型上实现了 6.22 倍的推理加速。

Restoring Semantic Fidelity for Training-Free High-Resolution Image Generation in Diffusion Transformers

Diffusion Transformer (DiT)-based text-to-image models have recently achieved remarkable success in image synthesis. However, when directly applied beyond their training resolution, these models often suffer from severe semantic degradation, where meaningful semantic content disappears. Although existing training-free high-resolution generation methods can mitigate this issue, the inherent semantic degradation at high resolutions remains largely unresolved, leading to degraded fine-grained details. Furthermore, widely used structural guidance methods lack modeling of the structure and details in the intermediate trajectory, which degrades the quality of the generated results. In this paper, we propose a training-free framework for high-resolution image generation to address these challenges. First, we propose a text-visual scale-decoupled attention (TVSDA) module, which restores the model's ability to generate semantically meaningful content at high resolutions by preserving the global receptive field of text tokens while aligning the receptive field of image tokens with the training resolution. Second, we propose a structure-detail guidance (SDG) strategy that explicitly models structural information and fine details through scale-wise and temporal-wise guidance, overcoming the limitations of conventional low-resolution guidance. Extensive experiments demonstrate that our method significantly improves both structural coherence and detail fidelity over existing training-free DiT-based high-resolution generation approaches. The code is available at https://github.com/ljjcoder/Restoring-Semantic-Fidelity.

TLDR: This paper introduces a training-free framework for high-resolution image generation in Diffusion Transformers (DiTs) by using a Text-Visual Scale-Decoupled Attention (TVSDA) module and a Structure-Detail Guidance (SDG) strategy to prevent semantic degradation and improve detail fidelity.

dKV Forcing: Separating Generation and Memory Representations through a Differentiable KV Cache

A causal video generator produces two key--value (KV) states for every segment: the KV it uses while denoising that segment, and the KV it writes into the cache for later segments to read. In a pretrained generator the two already differ substantially, later segments attend to them differently, and replacing the written KV with the generation KV degrades the continuation. We propose dKV Forcing (dKV denotes differentiable KV) to learn a dedicated memory representation from its future use: a writer-specific low-rank residual on the shared backbone separates memory writing from generation and is trained through the future reads of what it writes. Learning a write from its future use needs gradients through the cache, which the mutable cache in common use cannot provide. dKV Forcing replaces the training-time cache by persistent differentiable pages with local rematerialization and, in one temporal traversal, computes exactly the gradient that a two-pass reconstruction of the same objective computes. One traversal computes the full memory gradient at $7\%$ generator-update overhead over detached-write training, with $28\%$ less resident memory and $20.8\%$ shorter training cycles than the two-pass reconstruction. Over Self Gradient Forcing (SGF), the same objective trained with the same traversal, writer adaptation raises VBench-Long aesthetic quality by $6.6$ points at 60\,s and $8.5$ points at 240\,s with the highest subject and background consistency, and rollout dropout of the residual keeps most of this gain while restoring the motion of training without write gradients.

TLDR: dKV Forcing introduces a method to decouple generation and memory representations in video models by using a differentiable, low-rank residual cache that optimizes memory writing based on future read requirements.

TLDR: dKV Forcing 提出了一种通过可微低秩残差缓存来解耦视频生成模型中生成过程与记忆表示的方法，旨在通过对未来读取需求进行反向传播来优化记忆写入过程。

NatureFusion: Rethinking Infrared and Visible Image Fusion as Natural Complementary Enhancement

In the absence of ground truth, most existing infrared and visible image fusion methods pursue fidelity to the source images and directly superimpose two physically distinct modalities. As a result, salient infrared targets appear as bright patches of thermal radiation pasted onto the scene. These patches obscure the original color and texture of the targets, so the fused images look unnatural to human observers and deviate from the data distribution learned by vision models pretrained on visible images. Therefore, we redefine infrared and visible image fusion as natural complementary enhancement rather than source fidelity, and propose NatureFusion, which exploits cross-modal complementary cues to infer a clear and natural visible-style image from low-quality visible and infrared image pairs. Specifically, to address the lack of ground truth, we employ a generative foundation model as the teacher to directly generate visually natural fused images for supervision. Furthermore, to fully distill the teacher's generative capability while reducing computational cost, we fine-tune a pretrained generative model with conditional flow matching through a light-weight fusion adapter. Last but not least, to further improve downstream performance without sacrificing natural appearance, we freeze the fusion adapter and introduce a detector-supervised task adapter that strengthens the complementary contribution of infrared. Extensive comparisons with seven multi-modal fusion and seven single-modality enhancement methods demonstrate that NatureFusion generates visually natural fused images with the best perceptual quality. Detection and segmentation models pretrained on visible images achieve substantially higher accuracy on our fused images than on those of any competing method.

TLDR: NatureFusion reframes infrared and visible image fusion as a natural complementary enhancement task, using a generative foundation model as a teacher and conditional flow matching to synthesize high-quality, vision-model-friendly images.

TLDR: NatureFusion将红外与可见光图像融合重新定义为一种自然互补增强任务，利用生成式基础模型作为教师模型，并通过条件流匹配技术合成高质量且适配视觉模型的融合图像。

GSExplorer: Interactive World Generation with a Persistent 3D Gaussian World State

Interactive world generation requires both accurate local control and stable scene consistency over long rollouts. Existing methods improve local generation through camera or geometry conditioning and extend historical context with memory mechanisms. However, long-horizon exploration remains challenging as target-view guidance becomes incomplete and inconsistencies accumulate across repeated scene updates. We present \textbf{GSExplorer}, a generation--reconstruction framework built around a persistent 3D Gaussian world state. At each step, the current 3DGS scene provides multimodal rendering conditions for a condition-driven video generator, while camera geometry is injected through a separate Relative Ray Encoding branch. Generated videos are then fed back to a SLAM-based reconstruction backend, where inconsistency filtering prevents conflicting content from corrupting the scene and cross-chunk bundle adjustment improves spatial alignment across generation steps. We further design a data-construction pipeline that reproduces realistic 3DGS degradation during scene extrapolation, yielding 293K training pairs of degraded multimodal renderings and real target videos. GSExplorer achieves the best overall performance among the compared methods on both single-step and three-step WorldScore evaluation, with strong camera control and consistency across both settings, including a Camera Control score of 99.07 in single-step extrapolation. On 481-frame leave-and-return rollouts, it also shows strong revisit consistency, demonstrating the value of maintaining a reliable 3D world state for continued exploration.

TLDR: GSExplorer is a 3D-aware video generation framework that maintains a persistent 3D Gaussian world state to ensure long-horizon consistency and accurate camera control in interactive world generation.

TLDR: GSExplorer 是一个利用持久化 3D 高斯世界状态的视频生成框架，通过 3D 感知机制解决了交互式生成中长期一致性和相机控制的挑战。

IMPACT: Image-Conditioned Motion with Physics-Aware Collision and Trajectory Guidance for Video Generation

Visually realistic video generation does not guarantee physically consistent motion. Generated objects may exhibit implausible collision responses or rebounds without energy dissipation, while even physically valid motion predictions may not be faithfully realized during synthesis. We propose \textbf{IMPACT}, a physics-state-driven image-to-video framework that addresses these challenges by coupling explicit dynamics prediction with complementary sparse--dense physical guidance. A learnably calibrated Physics State Generator unifies continuous free-flight dynamics with discrete collision responses, linking impact timing, post-impact velocity, rebound attenuation, and eventual settling within a coherent physical-state sequence. To translate this evolution into effective generative constraints, IMPACT derives sparse trajectories and dense region-level motion priors from the same physical rollout. Sparse trajectories constrain global object displacement, while dense priors guide diffusion sampling to encourage coherent object-region motion and suppress unwanted background changes. Their shared physical basis coordinates global and local motion guidance to reduce prediction--generation mismatch. Experiments on 100 controlled sphere--plane collision scenes spanning different material combinations and visual environments demonstrate improved physical plausibility over representative general-purpose, physics-grounded, and motion-controllable baselines. The project repository is available at https://weiweiwiewei.github.io/impact

TLDR: IMPACT is a physics-aware image-to-video framework that improves physical consistency in generated videos by coupling explicit dynamics prediction with sparse trajectory and dense motion guidance.

TLDR: IMPACT 提出了一种物理感知图像生成视频框架，通过结合显式动力学预测与稀疏轨迹及稠密运动引导，有效解决了视频生成中物理规律一致性差的问题。

Biological Congruence: Evaluating Microscopy Generation Across Representations

Generative models increasingly produce realistic microscopy images, yet conventional evaluation primarily characterizes visual fidelity and distributional coverage rather than the biological information represented by synthetic data. We introduce biological congruence as an evaluation principle describing whether biologically relevant information forms a coherent description across representations and remains consistent with its empirical biological context. To study this principle, we develop a text-conditioned diffusion framework that jointly generates microscopy images and instance masks, exposing the same generated biological state through conditioning, visual appearance, and instance structure. Across prompt--mask, prompt--image, image--mask, and population-level analyses, we find strongly feature-dependent behavior: density and confluency are consistently preserved, morphology exhibits recurrent disagreement, while nearest-neighbor distance reveals that strong image--mask agreement can coexist with weaker conditioning and population fidelity. Stress testing increasingly unsupported phenotype combinations further shows that target controllability can increase while untargeted phenotypes reorganize and the complete generated state moves toward configurations better supported by real data. These findings demonstrate that generative quality, controllability, cross-representation agreement, and population fidelity capture complementary properties, motivating biological congruence as a relational perspective for evaluating scientific generative models.

TLDR: The paper introduces 'biological congruence' as a new evaluation framework for generative microscopy models, shifting the focus from mere visual fidelity to the consistency of biological information across text, images, and instance masks.

LUMEN: Geometry-Guided Diffusion Model for Spatially-Varying HDR Illumination Estimation

Estimating scene illumination from images is challenging as lighting is inherently spatially varying, depending on geometry, occlusion, and light sources. Existing methods incorporate geometric cues by deriving local environment maps or probe-based representations, but largely rely on regression or parametric models, resulting in over-smoothed lighting and limited realism. We revisit spatially-varying illumination estimation from a generative perspective and introduce LUMEN, a geometry-conditioned diffusion framework for synthesizing high-dynamic-range (HDR) environment maps at arbitrary spatial locations from single or multi-view images. Specifically, we reconstruct a coarse 3D scene from input images and render partial environment maps at arbitrary locations to encode visibility and spatial structure. These signals are fused with multi-view image features and fed into a diffusion transformer to generate full HDR environment maps. To train the model, we build a synthetic dataset of 700 indoor scenes with dense spatial supervision, including multi-view RGB images, depth, and HDR illumination at multiple sampled locations. This dataset enables the model to learn spatially consistent lighting distributions across diverse scenes. Extensive experiments demonstrate that LUMEN significantly improves visual fidelity and spatial coherence compared to prior approaches.

TLDR: LUMEN is a geometry-guided diffusion model that synthesizes spatially-varying HDR environment maps for complex indoor scenes by leveraging coarse 3D reconstructions as conditioning signals.

TLDR: LUMEN 是一款几何引导的扩散模型，通过利用粗糙的三维重建作为条件信号，为复杂的室内场景合成空间可变的 HDR 环境贴图。

MedNet and U$^2$-ViT: A Nested Vision Transformer Diffusion Framework for Diverse Medical Images Generation

The scarcity of diverse, high-quality medical images remains a significant bottleneck in advancing AI-driven medical diagnostics and treatment planning. Traditional datasets often suffer from class imbalances, limited disease coverage, and insufficient variations in imaging conditions - challenges that hinder the development of robust, generalizable models. To address this, we introduce MedNet, a large-scale, multi-modal text-to-image dataset spanning 61 disease categories with 160 classes, covering both humans and animals. We built this with images from trusted public repositories, ensuring data diversity, quality, and ethical compliance. Additionally, we propose U$^2$-ViT, a novel diffusion-based text-to-image generation architecture arranging Vision Transformers (ViTs) in a nested U-Net structure to enhance the generation of diverse, and high-fidelity medical images. Through extensive experiments, we demonstrate that our model achieves the best Fr'echet Inception Distance (FID) score compared to state-of-the-art models. The source code is available at https://github.com/LakshmikarPolamreddy/MedNet-U2-ViT.

TLDR: The paper introduces MedNet, a large-scale, multi-modal medical image dataset, and U²-ViT, a nested Vision Transformer diffusion architecture that achieves state-of-the-art FID scores for high-fidelity medical image generation.

Recoverability-Aware Evaluation of Diffusion Video Inpainting and Object Removal

Video inpainting for object removal is scored over the whole hole, although the background behind the object is visible in other frames of the shot for some hole pixels and in no frame for others. A whole-hole score therefore mixes copying with generation and cannot show which of the two a remover does well. To separate the two, we propose recoverability-stratified evaluation, in which a point tracker run on the clean video labels each hole pixel by whether and how far away it is observed, so that fidelity and temporal stability can be reported per stratum against a copy reference. To reuse the observed content, we also propose evidence-guided sampling (EGS), which blends tracked colors into the clean-latent prediction of a video diffusion remover without training. On DAVIS, the perceptual advantage of three diffusion removers over flow propagation shrinks where the content is visible a few frames away (LPIPS difference-in-differences +0.046 to +0.056 against E2FGVI-HQ), and real paired captures show the same pattern for two of them. Their DAVIS hole warping error is two to four times that of ProPainter, and copying along clean-video tracks lowers their recoverable LPIPS by a quarter to two fifths. EGS closes 43% and 56% of the gap to this copy reference on held-out DAVIS when its colors come from clean-video tracks. With colors tracked on the masked input, a pre-registered confirmation finds no gain on MiniMax-Remover and a loss on ROSE. Diffusion removers thus leave observed content unused on DAVIS, and training-free reuse does not yet hold with tracked evidence.

TLDR: The paper introduces 'recoverability-stratified evaluation' to distinguish between copying and generation in video inpainting and proposes Evidence-Guided Sampling (EGS) to improve diffusion-based object removal by better leveraging existing video content.

TLDR: 本文提出了“可恢复性分层评估”以区分视频修复中的内容复制与生成，并提出了证据引导采样（EGS），旨在通过更有效地利用视频中的已知内容来改进基于扩散模型的视频对象移除效果。
