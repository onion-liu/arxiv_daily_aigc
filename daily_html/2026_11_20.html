Plan4D: Generative Plannable 4D Worlds

Most world models for future prediction prioritize aestetic quality of generated videos, but offer only weak physical grounding and limited geometric consistency over time, due to the lack of explicit 4D modeling. In this paper, we introduce plan4d, a possible next milestone towards building a generative plannable 4D world model that predicts future RGB videos together with dense 4D geometry, including depth, cameras, point clouds, and scene flow from an observed scene context and text-specified actions. Since training data with paired actions and dense 4D annotations is inherently scarce, plan4d leverages the complementary priors of foundational video generators and 3D reconstructors. Specifically, we propose a model-stitching architecture that couples Depth Anything 3 with LongCat-Video and augments it with an auxiliary DPT head for scene-flow prediction. The resulting model allows us to plan and generate complex, long-horizon, and fine-grained actions in a dynamic 3D scenes, while substantially improving both visual fidelity and geometric accuracy of the generated future videos. Extensive experiments on both synthetic and real-world datasets demonstrate that plan4d achieves state-of-the-art performance in generative 4D modeling of environmental reactions. Project Page: https://fogradio.github.io/plan4d-project

TLDR: Plan4D is a generative world model that improves 4D consistency in video prediction by fusing foundational video generators with 3D reconstructors to produce synchronized RGB video, depth, and scene flow.

TLDR: Plan4D 是一种生成式世界模型，通过融合基础视频生成器与 3D 重建器，在预测未来 RGB 视频的同时输出深度和场景流，从而实现了更高质量的 4D 物理一致性。

Asuka-FM: A System-Architectural Co-Design Framework for Efficient Text-to-Image Pretraining on Consumer Hardware

Frontier text-to-image (T2I) foundation models predominantly rely on multi-billion parameter Diffusion Transformers (DiTs) pretrained across massive compute clusters, creating an accessibility barrier for academic laboratories. When training DiTs from scratch on single consumer GPUs (e.g., an NVIDIA RTX 4090 with 24\,GB VRAM), practitioners encounter three primary bottlenecks: quadratic attention sequence costs, Just-In-Time (JIT) compilation graph breaks induced by variable aspect-ratio bucketing, and an autoencoder throughput wall where frozen Variational Autoencoder (VAE) operations dominate step latency. In this work, we introduce \textbf{Asuka-FM}, an end-to-end system and architectural framework demonstrating that competitive dual-stream DiTs ($\sim$206M parameters) can be pretrained from scratch on a single consumer GPU in $\sim$160 GPU hours for under \$55. Asuka-FM integrates four core designs: (i) an AdaLN-free dual-stream DiT backbone reducing parameter count; (ii) structured sequence sparsification via Sparse-Dense Residual Fusion (SPRINT) to cut attention FLOPs by over 50\%; (iii) continuous aspect-normalized Rotary Position Embeddings (RoPE) coupled with shifted square crop training, guaranteeing static tensor shapes while unlocking zero-shot virtual camera control; and (iv) a pixel adaptation phase utilizing a lightweight (10M parameter) Patch Detailer Head operating under velocity regression ($v$-prediction). Evaluated on open-domain benchmarks without benchmark-specific fine-tuning, Asuka-FM achieves GenEval 0.588 / DPG 78.96 scores at 256px and GenEval 0.609 / DPG 78.51 scores at 512px, setting a new Pareto-optimal efficiency standard for democratic foundation modeling.

TLDR: Asuka-FM introduces a system-architectural framework that enables efficient, scratch pretraining of dual-stream Diffusion Transformers on consumer-grade hardware by addressing attention, compilation, and VAE-throughput bottlenecks.

TLDR: Asuka-FM 提出了一种系统架构协同设计框架，通过解决注意力机制、编译优化及 VAE 吞吐量等瓶颈，实现了在消费级显卡上低成本完成扩散 Transformer 模型的从零预训练。

ViDecide: Teaching Video Diffusion Models to Reason and Plan Step by Step

Current leading video generation models can synthesize highly realistic videos, while still frequently violating physical and logical constraints in multi-step reasoning and planning, even after training with large-scale reasoning data or existing diffusion RL methods. We attribute these failures to the conventional video training paradigm, which treats a solution as a fixed-length clip rather than a chain of decisions. We present ViDecide, a decision-centric framework that trains video diffusion models to predict state transitions causally and decide when to stop, with a decision-focused training schedule. We further propose Corrective Fine-tuning (CFT), which corrects failed decisions by mapping their noise to the model's own successful continuations, achieving stable improvement without policy-gradient optimization. Across abstract games, planning tasks, and a new benchmark of multi-step robot planning, ViDecide achieves an average strict success rate of 83.8%, far above 23.3% for standard fine-tuning and 67.8% for the strongest prior method, and generalizes to larger puzzles and unseen layouts. Our framework therefore unlocks greater potential for video generation models as reliable reasoners and planners in the visual world. Project page: https://kevin-thu.github.io/ViDecide.

TLDR: ViDecide reframes video generation as a causal, decision-centric sequential process, introducing Corrective Fine-tuning to significantly improve reasoning and planning capabilities in diffusion models.

TLDR: ViDecide 将视频生成重构为因果决策序列过程，并引入纠错微调（CFT）机制，显著提升了扩散模型在复杂推理与规划任务中的表现。

Online Temporal Rotations for 2 Bit KV-Cache Quantization in Video Diffusion Transformers

Video diffusion transformers reuse key--value (KV) caches across denoising steps, which quickly becomes a bottleneck for long video generation. We find that quantizing temporally distant cache vectors leads to a large range and high quantization error, but that nearby video tokens in the KV-cache share a persistent pattern per channel. These patterns also vary across videos, motivating rotations that are adapted to each video's unique cache. We propose Temporal Rotation Quantization (TRQ), a method that rotates frames in the cache that are adjacent in time before quantization. For each block of temporally adjacent cache vectors, TRQ fits an orthogonal transform that maps the block's per-channel means to a vector that is close to a constant across channels. To keep the quantization format lightweight, we parameterize this rotation as a tree of $2 \times 2$ planar rotations, each stored with only two bits, followed by a fixed Hadamard transform shared across all blocks. The rotations are fit without gradients or changes to model weights, and we prove that a simple greedy fit is globally optimal for our 'mean-flattening' objective. The transforms are fit online for each new video, and we release fused Triton kernels to make this process efficient at inference. At 2-bit precision and matched compression, TRQ improves VBench subject consistency over the state-of-the-art method QVG from 90.38 to 92.02 on LongCat-Video-13B and from 86.33 to 87.61 on HY-WorldPlay-8B, as well as improving other metrics.

TLDR: TRQ is a novel 2-bit KV-cache quantization method for video diffusion transformers that uses online, lightweight orthogonal transforms to reduce quantization error in temporally adjacent video tokens.

TLDR: TRQ 是一种针对视频扩散 Transformer 的 2-bit KV-Cache 量化方法，通过在线、轻量级的正交变换来减少时间相邻视频 Token 的量化误差。

SpecWM: Turning Verification into Refinement for Streaming World Models

Recently, video world models have advanced interactive generation, with large models offering higher quality and smaller models enabling faster inference. Existing speculative methods combine their strengths through fast draft generation and selective target regeneration guided by rewards on decoded frames. However, verification covers only the external scorer's evaluation dimensions and adds decoding and scoring costs. Moreover, these scores provide no correction, requiring costly target regeneration for rejected drafts. To address these limitations, we propose Speculative World Model (SpecWM), a unified, training-free inference framework for streaming world models. It verifies draft chunks using the target's latent velocity field. Verification also yields a reusable correction; two thresholds select Accept, Refine, or Reject. Specifically, one-step refinement reuses it to improve perceptual quality, while three-step refinement strengthens camera following and trajectory consistency. These findings motivate transition-aware refinement. On WBench Navigation, SpecWM improves visual quality at 1.63$\times$ speedup over the target and raises NavScore from the draft's 75.99 to 85.63, achieving the best quality-speed-control trade-off. Additionally, our LongSpecBench shows sustained quality and control over long, multi-turn rollouts, while experiments on Hy-Nomas validate SpecWM across backbones and denoising budgets.

TLDR: SpecWM is a training-free inference framework for video world models that replaces traditional verification with latent velocity-based refinement, improving both generation speed and trajectory consistency.

TLDR: SpecWM 是一种用于视频世界模型的免训练推理框架，通过基于潜在速度场的细化取代了传统的验证过程，在提升生成速度的同时增强了轨迹一致性。

FlexVSR: Continuous-Scale Super-Resolution for Streaming Video Generation

Real-time streaming video generation has gained rapid momentum in live content creation and interactive media. Deploying such systems in practice requires flexible scaling factors to accommodate diverse source resolutions, target displays, and computational budgets. However, extending fixed-scale generative video super-resolution (VSR) to varying target scales poses two challenges: downsampling from a larger fixed scale incurs redundant cost, while re-optimizing the diffusion transformer (DiT) is costly and alters the shared generative prior. To address both, we propose FlexVSR, a decoupled framework for continuous-scale streaming generative VSR with supervision at only a few scales. Our key insight is that naively resampling fixed-scale latents onto a new target grid induces scale-dependent input mismatch, which can be effectively addressed through parameter-efficient, scale-aware adaptation of the latent interface without updating the DiT. To this end, FlexVSR introduces ReLSA (Refined Latent Scale Adaptation), a lightweight latent adapter that constructs and refines target-grid latents before they enter the DiT, enabling a shared frozen VSR prior to generalize across continuous scales with only 0.274M trainable parameters and an adaptation cost of 7.3 H100 GPU-hours, while introducing no additional temporal dependencies and preserving the native causal streaming protocol. Extensive experiments demonstrate competitive or superior reconstruction and temporal quality, lower latency than most native-scale streaming baselines and offline methods, and effective generalization to unseen continuous scales, filling the continuous-scale gap in real-time streaming generative VSR.

TLDR: FlexVSR introduces a parameter-efficient latent adaptation framework that enables continuous-scale super-resolution in streaming video generation without retraining the frozen generative DiT model.

TLDR: FlexVSR 提出了一种参数高效的潜在空间适配框架，通过引入轻量级重采样与适配机制，实现了在流式视频生成中无需重新训练核心 DiT 模型即可进行任意比例的超分辨率处理。

StreamEgo: Streaming Exocentric-to-Egocentric Video Generation

Exocentric-to-egocentric (Exo2Ego) video generation transforms third-person recordings into first-person viewpoints, unlocking immersive AR/VR experiences and scalable demonstration synthesis for embodied robotics. Extending it to a streaming setting is expected to further enable live applications. However, existing methods suffer from degraded manipulation regions, overfitting and confinement to fixed-length clips with prohibitive latency. To address these limitations, we present StreamEgo, a real-time framework for unbounded, generalizable Exo2Ego video generation. To resolve intricate manipulation dynamics while supporting the streaming pipeline, a streaming dual-branch prior provides pixel-aligned guidance for translating appearance and skeletal kinematics. We also introduce a region-aware loss curriculum to improve generalization and stabilize multi-objective diffusion training. Furthermore, we distill the model into a chunk-wise autoregressive model, augmented with a region-aware corruption mechanism and a first-chunk curriculum, enabling high-quality, generalizable real-time streaming Exo2Ego generation at 24.3 FPS on a single H100 GPU.

TLDR: StreamEgo is a real-time streaming framework for Exo2Ego video generation, utilizing a dual-branch prior and chunk-wise autoregressive distillation to achieve 24.3 FPS performance for immersive AR/VR and robotics applications.

MultiBinder: Preventing Multi-Subject Identity Leakage in Personalized Video Generation

Personalized video generation faces a significant scalability challenge: as the number of custom subjects increases, existing methods suffer from severe identity leakage and semantic entanglement, resulting in videos with blended attributes and incoherent narratives. We identify that this stems from a lack of explicit binding between textual entity phrases and their corresponding visual patterns prior to fusion in diffusion models. To address this, we propose MultiBinder, a novel framework built on a ``bind-then-narrate'' paradigm. Our approach introduces a two-stage conditioning process before video diffusion. First, Cross-modal Entity Binding devises an entity-aware Q-Former to extract identity-centric features from each subject's reference image and tightly align them with its unique entity phrase, yielding disentangled ``identity tokens.'' Second, a Multimodal Large Language Model (MLLM) acts as a ``narrative director'' in the Narrative-Aware Subject Arrangement stage, capturing the action of each subject from the text prompt and synthesizes contextualized subject features that encapsulate ``who is doing what'' and preserve role-specific distinctions. These features are then injected into a video diffusion model via dedicated conditioning pathways, ensuring precise attribute allocation. Extensive experiments on both general and newly curated multi-subject benchmarks show that MultiBinder significantly outperforms existing methods in multi-subject identity fidelity.

TLDR: MultiBinder introduces a 'bind-then-narrate' framework to mitigate identity leakage and semantic entanglement in multi-subject video generation by aligning visual identity tokens with specific entity phrases using an entity-aware Q-Former and an MLLM-based narrative director.

TLDR: MultiBinder 提出了一种“先绑定，后叙事”的框架，通过实体感知 Q-Former 和基于多模态大模型的叙事导演，有效解决了多主体视频生成中存在的身份泄露和语义纠缠问题。

HiUni: Harmonizing Understanding and Generation in Shared Visual Space

Unified multimodal models aim to support visual understanding and generation within a single framework. However, prevailing approaches often separate their visual representations or backbone computation to accommodate the different requirements of these two tasks, which limits the degree of unification. In this paper, we present HiUni, which revisits this design choice and explores whether a pretrained vision language model can be directly extended into a unified model with extensive parameter sharing. Specifically, HiUni reuses the same language-aligned ViT and dense Transformer backbone for both capabilities, while confining task-specific computation to the output side. Yet such a design introduces two key challenges. Generation in pixel space is difficult to optimize, and semantic abstraction in pretrained ViT discards fine spatial details required for synthesis. To address these challenges, HiUni introduces a \emph{Pixel Diffusion Head} tailored to direct pixel-space optimization and a lightweight \emph{HiGate} pathway that provides fine-grained spatial details near the output stage. Extensive experiments show that HiUni achieves a similar average understanding score to the pretrained VLM while attaining competitive performance on image generation and editing. Controlled comparisons and analyses further demonstrate that a single dense backbone can effectively support both capabilities without task-specific backbone computation, highlighting a simpler path toward unified multimodal modeling.

TLDR: HiUni introduces a unified multimodal framework that achieves both visual understanding and pixel-space generation using a single shared ViT and dense Transformer backbone, overcoming limitations through a Pixel Diffusion Head and a lightweight spatial detail pathway.

TLDR: HiUni提出了一种统一的多模态框架，利用单一共享的ViT和Transformer骨干网络同时实现视觉理解与像素空间生成，并通过Pixel Diffusion Head和轻量级空间细节通路解决了现有方法中的性能瓶颈。

Frequency-Synchronized Boundary Coupling for Training-Free Multi-Prompt Long Video Generation

Text-to-video generation has made remarkable progress with diffusion models and transformer-based video architectures, yet generating coherent long videos from evolving textual descriptions remains challenging. Training long-video models from scratch requires substantial computation and large-scale multi-event data, while training-free multi-prompt generation must activate new prompt semantics without breaking temporal continuity. We identify a frequency-agnostic boundary coupling problem in existing transition strategies: weak coupling causes abrupt changes, flicker, and background jumps, whereas overly strong or coarse coupling may over-preserve source semantics and produce ghosting or duplicated subjects around prompt boundaries. To address this problem, we propose FreqSync, a training-free framework that formulates prompt transitions as frequency-selective boundary coupling. FreqSync combines Frequency-Synchronized Source-Conditioned Attention (FS-SCA) with Residual-Clipped Spectral Stitching (RCSS), enabling adjacent segments to share stable low- and mid-frequency structure and motion cues while keeping high-frequency prompt-specific details target-dominant under explicit residual constraints. Extensive experiments show that FreqSync achieves state-of-the-art transition smoothness and prompt alignment while maintaining strong perceptual video quality, demonstrating its effectiveness for coherent multi-prompt long video generation.

TLDR: FreqSync is a training-free framework for coherent long video generation that uses frequency-selective coupling (FS-SCA and RCSS) to maintain structural stability while seamlessly transitioning between prompt-specific high-frequency details.

TLDR: FreqSync 是一种无需训练的视频生成框架，通过频率选择性耦合（FS-SCA 和 RCSS）技术，在保持结构稳定性的同时实现多提示词下视频片段的平滑过渡。

Streaming-AAA: Streaming AAA Game Video Generation at High Resolution, High Frame Rate, and Low Latency

Controllable streaming video generation offers a promising route toward interactive game simulation, yet existing methods remain far from practical gameplay. They often fail to jointly achieve high visual fidelity, high frame rates, and low interaction latency, resulting in poor responsiveness and degraded user experience. We present Streaming-AAA, a new paradigm for controllable infinite generation of AAA-quality game videos. Our system generates 1080p gameplay videos at 43 FPS with 198 ms end-to-end latency interaction on merely NVIDIA A800 GPUs, while supporting long-horizon play without severe temporal degradation or collapse. Streaming-AAA is built upon three key designs. First, we redesign the VAE architecture for efficient high-resolution decoding, enabling real-time synthesis at 1080p. Second, we pretrain the diffusion model using diffusion forcing and significantly extend the temporal training horizon, allowing the model to capture long-term dependencies and maintain persistent memory. Third, we perform post-training distillation and memory compression to improve inference efficiency while retaining visual quality and temporal consistency. Experiments show that Streaming-AAA substantially improves visual fidelity, frame rate, latency, and long-term stability over prior controllable video generation approaches, enabling a significantly more playable experience for interactive AAA game video generation.

TLDR: Streaming-AAA introduces an efficient generative framework for 1080p, high-frame-rate, and low-latency interactive game video generation using optimized VAEs, diffusion forcing, and distillation.

Multi-View Transformer with Geometry-Guided Multi-Modal ControlNet for 3D Virtual Try-On

Editing a 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) capture of a person so that they wear a new garment requires every rendered view to agree on the same garment details. Existing 3D virtual try-on (VTON) pipelines, however, edit training views largely independently and fuse them with hand-designed heuristics such as manually chosen reference views and fixed attention-blending weights. The resulting inconsistencies are baked into the 3D representation and surface as texture drift, blurred prints, and implausible geometry in novel views. We present GS-VTON-MVT, a three-stage framework that combines implicit and explicit 3D reasoning for consistent 3DGS try-on. (i) A multi-view inpainting transformer jointly edits a set of training views with full cross-view self-attention, so that cross-view correspondence is learned rather than imposed. (ii) A geometry-guided multi-modal ControlNet conditions the diffusion editor on the garment image together with depth and surface normals rendered from the current 3DGS state, supplying explicit structure where visual evidence is weakest. (iii) A pose-adaptive persona-aware attention replaces the fixed blending weight between current-view and reference-view features with weights derived from camera-pose proximity. On the 3D-VTONBench benchmark, GS-VTON-MVT improves FID, KID, LPIPS-based cross-view consistency, and user preference over general-purpose 3D editors and prior 3D try-on methods.

TLDR: GS-VTON-MVT introduces a three-stage framework for consistent 3D virtual try-on using a multi-view transformer and geometry-guided ControlNet to solve cross-view inconsistency in 3D Gaussian Splatting edits.

TLDR: GS-VTON-MVT 提出了一种结合多视图 Transformer 和几何引导 ControlNet 的三阶段框架，旨在解决 3D 高斯泼溅 (3DGS) 虚拟试穿中跨视图一致性的问题。

TrajCal: Trajectory-Calibrated Time Schedules for Few-Step Flow Sampling

Flow matching enables high-quality image and video generation, but its iterative sampling process requires repeated evaluations of large neural networks. Existing few-step methods can distort the output and reduce its agreement with a many-step reference. We propose TrajCal, a training-free calibration framework for few-step sampling. Based on scatter search, a well-established metaheuristic for difficult optimization problems, TrajCal starts with diverse seed schedules, deforms and combines these candidates, and selects one fixed schedule by final output fidelity for reuse on new inputs. For both image and video generation, TrajCal achieves higher reference fidelity than all compared baselines at low evaluation budgets while maintaining competitive visual quality. The search is effective even with randomly generated seeds, while trajectory-based seeds further improve fidelity. For cross-style generalization, photography-calibrated schedules also improve fidelity on unseen image styles.

TLDR: TrajCal is a training-free calibration framework that optimizes time schedules for few-step flow matching using metaheuristic scatter search, significantly improving reference fidelity in image and video generation.

ContextRenderer: Dynamic-Context Video Generation for Interactive Game Worlds

Interactive game world models separate executable world evolution from visual generation, using video models to render engine-provided scene representations into detailed observations. During sustained gameplay, however, existing generative renderers struggle to follow changing conditions while preserving visual continuity across diverse proxies and target styles. We therefore introduce ContextRenderer, an autoregressive video diffusion framework designed for both dynamic conditioning and cross-proxy generalization. It couples Dynamic-Context Learning (DCL) for chunk-level interaction supervision with Recurrent History Compression (RHC) to follow condition updates while preserving visual continuity within a bounded token budget. WorldPrism, our interaction-centric dataset constructed from agent-built worlds, AAA gameplay, and open-source videos, supplies the corresponding renderings and chunk-level annotations for these learning tasks. Experiments on GWM-Bench and Solar-Bench show improved reconstruction and visual quality, while qualitative results demonstrate minute-long interactive rendering under changing prompts and subject references. LIFT: Lifting Language-Controlled Image Fusion to Free-Viewpoint 3D Scenes

Multimodal image fusion traditionally generates a single fused image from paired observations at a specific viewpoint, limiting comprehensive scene perception and adaptability to diverse perceptual objectives. To overcome these limitations, we present LIFT, a novel framework for our proposed language-controlled 3D image fusion (L3D-IF) paradigm that extends multimodal image fusion from 2D images to free-viewpoint 3D scenes, enabling language-guided fusion tailored to diverse perceptual objectives. First, LIFT formulates Semantic Modality Binding (SMB), which interprets language instructions to identify semantic targets and their associated modality preferences and grounds these targets in corresponding regions of a shared Gaussian scene, establishing persistent semantic-to-spatial correspondences for consistent fusion control across viewpoints. Second, Language-Conditioned Gaussian Appearance (LCGA) is developed to achieve scene-wide multimodal appearance modeling by combining adaptive multimodal blending with language- and viewpoint-conditioned appearance refinement, enabling spatially adaptive realization of diverse fusion appearances while preserving source fidelity at pure-modality endpoints. Finally, we construct a Multi-View Language-Supervision (MVLS) dataset that associates language instructions and calibrated viewpoints with modulated fused images, providing effective supervision for learning instruction-dependent fusion from finite multi-view observations.
