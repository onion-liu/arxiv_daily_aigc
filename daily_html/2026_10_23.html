Reading, Writing, Sharing, and Interacting with the World through Video

Video foundation models have advanced rapidly in understanding and generating visual sequences, while video world models seek to capture the underlying dynamics of the world they depict. Despite their different objectives, both concern the relationship between video observations and an evolving world. This survey adopts world modeling as a unifying lens for examining these developments. Viewing an observed video as a partial, lossy, and temporally ordered projection of a latent temporal world, we organize the literature around four complementary operations: reading infers world properties from observed video; writing synthesizes video from specified or learned world conditions; sharing integrates understanding and generation through common modeling mechanisms; and interacting predicts and updates world states under actions and evolving observations. This perspective distinguishes temporal coherence in observed or generated sequences from consistency in the underlying world state. Across tasks, representations, models, datasets, and benchmarks, we trace a shift in emphasis from clip-level recognition and perceptual fidelity toward persistent state, controllable evolution, physical and causal validity, and evaluation grounded in interaction and downstream decisions, and we distill the open challenges that follow.

TLDR: This paper presents a unified framework for video foundation and world models by categorizing literature into four operations—reading, writing, sharing, and interacting—shifting the focus from perceptual fidelity to persistent world state modeling.

TLDR: 本文通过“阅读、写作、共享和交互”四个维度，将视频基础模型与世界模型统一在同一个理论框架下，强调了从单纯的感知保真度向持久物理状态建模的范式转移。

UniVOE: A Removal-Driven Framework for Interaction-Aware Universal Video Object Editing

Video object removal and injection are reciprocal editing operations between object-present and object-absent video states, yet they differ substantially in training difficulty. Removal can be effectively bootstrapped from video inpainting and further adapted to erase object-related interaction effects. Injection, however, requires large-scale paired supervision to model object appearance, dynamics, and scene-dependent effects, while perfectly aligned real-world pairs are difficult to collect and synthetic data lacks sufficient realism and diversity. To address this challenge, we present UniVOE, a removal-driven framework for training both video object removal and injection models. We first train an effect-aware remover by transferring video inpainting priors and finetuning on object-present/object-absent pairs. The trained remover converts real object-present videos into object-absent counterparts, creating large-scale paired supervision for injection. An injection model is trained on these pairs in the reverse direction to recover objects together with their dynamics and interaction effects. Moreover, since pixel-level reconstruction alone cannot adequately capture perceptual realism, we introduce a reward-based post-training method that aligns injection results with human preferences using a proprietary reward model. Experiments against strong open-source and commercial systems demonstrate that UniVOE achieves state-of-the-art performance on both object removal and injection, particularly in challenging interaction-effect cases.

TLDR: UniVOE is a framework that treats video object removal and injection as reciprocal tasks, using a self-supervised, removal-driven data generation approach to solve the scarcity of paired training data for complex video editing.

TLDR: UniVOE提出了一种移除驱动的视频编辑框架，通过将物体移除和注入视为互逆任务，利用移除模型自动生成训练数据，解决了视频编辑中缺乏配对数据的问题。

WorldForge V2: Eliciting Camera-Trajectory Alignment in Video Diffusion Models

Video diffusion models have learned rich 3D/4D priors, making them promising for scene generation and video re-rendering. However, their use in spatial tasks is limited by imprecise camera control, spatial and temporal inconsistencies, and entangled camera motion with scene dynamics. Existing approaches, such as task-specific fine-tuning and post-process warping, often introduce visual artifacts, generalize poorly, or incur high computational costs. We propose WorldForge V2 to achieve accurate camera control while preserving the generative capabilities of pretrained video models. We first develop a training-free guidance framework using warped references to iteratively refine generation along a target camera path. Optical-flow analysis identifies motion-related latent channels for selective guidance, helping separate motion control from appearance. A dual-path strategy further compares guided and unguided predictions to correct artifacts from imperfect references. Together, these components guide viewpoint changes while preserving scene appearance and object motion. To reduce refinement costs, v-cache approximates the correction using the preceding denoising prediction, avoiding an additional model evaluation. We further extend motion localization from latent channels to network blocks and introduce Trajectory LoRA, which concentrates adaptation on blocks most responsive to trajectory guidance. A two-stage training strategy teaches these adapters to denoise the states encountered during guided generation, using limited supervision effectively. This enables reliable camera control with very little training data, with a single training scene already supporting generalization to unseen scenes. Quantitative and qualitative evaluations across multiple datasets show that our method achieves state-of-the-art generation quality and trajectory accuracy in camera-controlled video generation.

TLDR: WorldForge V2 introduces a guidance-based framework and Trajectory LoRA to achieve precise camera control in video diffusion models while maintaining visual fidelity and scene dynamics.

TLDR: WorldForge V2 提出了一种基于引导的框架和轨迹微调方法（Trajectory LoRA），在实现视频扩散模型精确相机控制的同时，有效保持了画面质量和场景动态。

Squaring-the-Sphere: Distortion-Free and Seamless Panorama Generation via CubeAtlas

Generating 360-degree panoramas requires both local perspective fidelity and global spherical continuity. Existing representations face a trade-off: equirectangular projection (ERP) enables seamless panorama modeling but suffers from severe distortion and a domain gap to pre-trained image generators, whereas cubemaps preserve perspective geometry but often produce inconsistent faces and visible seams. We present Squaring-the-Sphere, a panoramic image generation framework built on a novel representation CubeAtlas, which unfolds all cubemap faces into a single compact canvas. By arranging orientation-aligned, rotated polar regions alongside the equatorial faces, this representation preserves the perspective geometry of cubemaps while organizing the cross-face relationships required for seamless panorama assembly. It further enables a pretrained diffusion transformer to model the complete panorama jointly in a single pass, without altering the backbone architecture or positional encoding. While CubeAtlas has a token count comparable to ERP, its structured layout makes many dense attention interactions unnecessary. We therefore introduce a layout-aware sparse attention mask that preserves the interactions required for panorama continuity while removing geometrically irrelevant token pairs. As a result, CubeAtlas achieves better quality than ERP modeling with lower attention computation. Furthermore, we introduce a training-free super-resolution technique and a fusion scheme that enhance fine details in distortion-free perspective space before achieving the final ERP panorama. Experiments on various benchmarks demonstrate that our method improves geometric fidelity, cross-view consistency, and high-resolution visual quality over previous methods.

TLDR: Squaring-the-Sphere introduces CubeAtlas, a novel representation that flattens cubemap faces into a single canvas to enable high-fidelity, seamless 360-degree panorama generation using standard diffusion transformers with efficient sparse attention.

TLDR: Squaring-the-Sphere 提出了一种名为 CubeAtlas 的新型表示方法，将立方体贴图面展开为单一画布，利用标准扩散模型实现高质量、无缝的 360 度全景图像生成，并引入稀疏注意力机制提升计算效率。

AnyGameRenderer: Entity-Consistent Generative Rendering Across Games and an Entity-Level Benchmark

AI coding agents can now build small playable games in minutes, but the results usually look like programmer art: gray blockouts, colored geometric primitives, and flat semantic blobs. We present AnyGameRenderer, a video-to-video generative renderer that turns such footage into high-quality visuals with characters, motion detail, and effects. Throughout, it keeps the layout, camera, and every entity's position and timing intact. Our AnyGameRenderer generalizes across a wide range of genres that agents build, such as tower defense, auto-battlers, top-down survivors, turn-based battles, kart racing, and crowd runners. AnyGameRenderer takes plain video as its game-state input, with no complex representations such as engine G-buffers, per-object class labels, or low-poly proxies. Gray blockouts, colored primitives, semantic maps, and wireframes all work as input. It also preserves entity correspondence: group counts stay correct, and entities appear and disappear on the right frame. We implement AnyGameRenderer by LoRA fine-tuning MiniMax-H3 ref2va, a 33B reference-video-conditioned video generation model. Training uses $\sim$10k paired clips from three sources: (i) multi-stream rendering data from 38 agent-made games exported from GEAR, an AI game-creation tool; (ii) two-stream rendering data from Unreal Engine, with programmatically controlled crowds, count changes, and entity scales; and (iii) real gameplay recordings, with pairs reconstructed via 3D-box detection. To evaluate the entity correspondence, we propose an entity-level benchmark of 59 held-out cases with 753 entities, scored against ground-truth boxes. Compared with the base model, AnyGameRenderer better preserves entity correspondence: temporal match rises from 50.9\% to 62.9\%, missed count changes fall from 34.7\% to 21.3\%, and fewer entities are hallucinated or left unrendered.

TLDR: AnyGameRenderer is a video-to-video generative framework that transforms primitive, AI-generated game prototypes into high-quality, entity-consistent visuals without requiring complex engine-level data.

TLDR: AnyGameRenderer 提出了一种视频到视频的生成框架，能够将 AI 生成的简单游戏原型转化为高质量且保持实体一致性的视觉画面，且无需复杂的引擎底层数据。

Making MLLM Latents Matter: Factor-Aware Predictive Learning for Cinematic Video Generation

Cinematic video generation requires both temporal inference from limited inputs (i.e., sparse rather than frame-by-frame guidance) and factor-aware control of character, scene, action, and camera. Multimodal large language models (MLLMs) offer useful priors for both capabilities, but their representations are not automatically actionable for video generation: a Video Diffusion Transformer (DiT) may bypass them through native conditioning pathways, and using them does not ensure either factor-aware control or generation-compatible temporal prediction. We propose Factor-Aware Predictive Learning (FAPL), which establishes effective MLLM latent use as a shared prerequisite and then targets the two capabilities with complementary learning signals. First, latent-only generator grounding trains the DiT with a frozen MLLM and connector, strengthening the DiT's use of MLLM latents for generation. Second, for factor-aware control, paired videos specify a target-factor change and non-target preservation, and factor-span replacement trains the DiT to realize this relationship in its output. Third, for temporal inference, text-only and text-plus-complete-video observations share a video target, and the trained DiT is then frozen while the MLLM and connector are optimized through it. This final stage trains limited-input representations for generation while retaining complete-observation reconstruction as a complementary training mode. Extensive experiments demonstrate our consistently better performance on three cinematic video generation tasks (i.e., sparse-to-video, factor-aware editing, and multi-shot generation) over baselines and validate FAPL's contributions to MLLM feature use, temporal control, and factor coordination.

TLDR: FAPL is a framework that integrates MLLM latents into Video DiTs for cinematic video generation, utilizing a three-stage training approach to improve factor-aware control and temporal prediction.

TLDR: FAPL 是一种将 MLLM 潜在空间融入视频扩散模型（DiT）的框架，通过三阶段训练策略显著提升了视频生成的因素可控性与时间预测能力。

HimmBO: High-Dimensional Interactive Model-Merging Bayesian Optimization for Image Generation

Fine-tuning has become a common way to customize text-to-image models, leading to community collections of over 100K lightweight adapters. Adapters sharing a base model can be merged with continuous coefficients. This turns a large collection into a high-dimensional continuous design space, which recent work explores interactively using preferential Bayesian optimization (PBO). However, existing approaches are limited to a few dozen preselected adapters and require expensive image generation in every interaction round. We introduce HimmBO, a two-stage method that scales preference-driven adapter merging to much larger collections while reducing image-generation cost. Its screening stage recovers the active adapter set through level-set estimation over pre-generated images, requiring no image generation. The refinement stage then optimizes merge coefficients in the recovered low-dimensional subspace. Both stages use a style-aware kernel, allowing preference information to transfer between visually similar adapters. In simulated-user image-matching experiments, HimmBO achieves higher final similarity than all comparisons while reaching a given similarity with under one tenth of the image generations required by prior work. Its performance remains stable as the collection grows to 1080 adapters, while competing methods degrade substantially.

TLDR: HimmBO is a two-stage Bayesian optimization framework that efficiently merges large-scale text-to-image adapters by using a pre-generation screening stage and style-aware kernels to drastically reduce computation.

TLDR: HimmBO 提出了一种两阶段贝叶斯优化框架，通过预生成筛选阶段和风格感知核函数，实现了大规模文生图适配器的高效融合，显著降低了计算成本。

Direct Forcing: A Single-Stage Training Recipe for Few-Step Autoregressive Text-to-Video Generation

Few-step autoregressive video generation enables low-latency streaming, yet prevailing training pipelines separate causal adaptation from distribution matching. Recent representation-based text-to-video methods retain this separation by starting from ODE-initialized checkpoints, even when the target distribution is defined directly by reference videos. We introduce Direct Forcing, a single-stage framework that uses reference videos both as paired examples of how a history continues and as a target population for self-generated rollouts. Causal Continuation Alignment learns paired spatiotemporal correspondence under reference histories, while Rollout Distribution Matching uses maximum mean discrepancy to align the representation distributions of self-generated and reference videos. Together, these complementary signals allow a pretrained video model to learn causal continuation and the target video distribution in a single training stage. Dynamic Gradient Balancing adapts the relative gradient contributions of continuation alignment and distribution matching during training. Starting from bidirectional Wan2.1-1.3B, Direct Forcing learns a four-step autoregressive generator in 250 updates without a separate initialization stage or online diffusion-score supervision, achieving 84.63 VBench Total and 86.01 Quality. Ablation studies further demonstrate the benefits of dynamic gradient balancing and reveal distinct representation preferences: deeper features benefit continuation alignment, whereas intermediate features yield better performance for rollout distribution matching.

TLDR: Direct Forcing is a single-stage training framework for few-step autoregressive video generation that simplifies pipelines by simultaneously learning causal continuation and distribution matching using reference videos.

TLDR: Direct Forcing 是一种用于少步自回归视频生成的单阶段训练框架，它通过利用参考视频同时学习因果连续性和分布匹配，简化了训练流程。

CAVC: Convergence-Adaptive Audio-Visual Cache for Modality-Asymmetric Diffusion Generation

Diffusion-based audio-visual generation methods typically perform joint denoising of audio and video latents throughout the entire sampling trajectory, repeatedly executing modality-specific denoising branches and cross-modal attention at every timestep. However, we observe that such uniform joint computation is not always necessary. The key is a modality-asymmetric recoverability: audio-side drift introduced by mid-stage caching can be substantially reduced by only a few terminal joint denoising steps, whereas video-side degradation caused by caching leads to persistent loss of motion and temporal details. This asymmetry suggests that inference acceleration should selectively cache recoverable audio-side computation while preserving full video refinement. Motivated by this observation, we propose \textbf{Convergence-Adaptive Audio-Visual Cache (CAVC)}, a training-free modality-asymmetric caching framework. CAVC adopts a sandwich-structured computation allocation: after detecting that audio denoiser outputs enter a cache-tolerant regime, it caches and reuses the audio denoiser output to bypass audio denoising and cross-modal attention in the middle stage, while the video branch continues independent spatio-temporal refinement; a few joint steps are then restored at the end to reduce accumulated cross-modal drift and improve synchronization. Experiments across multiple public benchmarks demonstrate that CAVC achieves 1.10--1.35x wall-clock acceleration, improves several visual dynamics and temporal statistics, and maintains competitive audio fidelity and audio-visual alignment.

TLDR: CAVC is a training-free acceleration framework for audio-visual diffusion models that exploits modality-asymmetric recoverability by selectively caching audio denoising steps while maintaining full video refinement.

TLDR: CAVC 是一个针对音视频扩散模型的免训练加速框架，通过利用模态间的不对称可恢复性，选择性地缓存音频去噪步骤，同时保持完整的视频细化过程以提升生成效率。

One Evaluation Is Enough: Inversion-Free Image Editing with Trajectory Correction

Real-image editing commonly relies on inversion and iterative sampling, while efficient one-step editors often stabilize a large update by estimating and averaging editing fields at multiple noise levels. We ask a stricter question: is a single temporal field evaluation sufficient for reliable inversion-free editing? Our answer rests on a previously underused duality in a variance-preserving (VP) one-step model: one source-target query at a shared noisy state simultaneously determines a local angular semantic tangent and a clean endpoint displacement. We integrate the tangent over the analytically known remaining VP angle to obtain a nominal one-step transport, and use the endpoint displacement to correct its long-horizon extrapolation through a constrained projection. This transport-first, correction-second construction yields Endpoint-Calibrated Transport (ECT), a closed-form, training-free editor requiring only one noise level, two text conditions packed into one denoiser batch, and one latent update. ECT requires no image inversion, trajectory integration, second temporal observation, or iterative optimization, and can be directly applied to pretrained epsilon-prediction VP one-step generators. Across the reported benchmark settings, ECT delivers stable gains in editing quality within an extremely short editing time.

TLDR: Endpoint-Calibrated Transport (ECT) enables high-quality, inversion-free image editing in a single step by using a novel geometric approach that combines semantic tangent integration with endpoint displacement correction.

TLDR: Endpoint-Calibrated Transport (ECT) 通过结合语义切线积分与端点位移校正的几何方法，实现了仅需单步推理的高质量免反演图像编辑。

DeXplicit: Making Physical State Explicit in Dexterous Manipulation World Models

Before acting on an object, a robot needs to anticipate how its hand motion will change the physical world and what the resulting scene will look like. Existing approaches typically entangle action-conditioned dynamics, camera motion, and visual synthesis within a video generation model, or represent dynamics as point trajectories without explicit object structure. We introduce DeXplicit, a dexterous manipulation world model that instead predicts how the world evolves explicitly in metric 3D before rendering its visual consequences. Given an initial scene and a future hand trajectory, a lightweight 3D dynamics network predicts object-centric motion and deformation, providing structured and geometrically grounded future world states. These predictions then serve as a strong scaffold for video generation: appearance is transported through the predicted 3D motion and projected into future views, allowing the renderer to focus on completing uncertain and newly visible content rather than implicitly learning world dynamics from pixels. This explicit-3D-first decomposition also enables efficient causal generation, requiring only two denoising steps per block while conditioning on generated history. Across HOT3D, OakInk2, and PhysTwin, DeXplicit reduces moved-point error by 27-40% relative to PointWorld and improves object fidelity IoU+ by 47% on HOT3D under a shared renderer. Our design with two-step causal generation achieves a 57x speedup from our 14B bi-directional configuration in video synthesis.

TLDR: DeXplicit is a world model for dexterous manipulation that separates 3D physical dynamics prediction from visual rendering, using explicit 3D motion as a scaffold to improve video generation efficiency and physical accuracy.

TLDR: DeXplicit 是一种用于灵巧操作的世界模型，它将 3D 物理动力学预测与视觉渲染分离，利用显式的 3D 运动作为骨架来提升视频生成的效率和物理准确性。

PhyEvolve: Multi-Agent State-Evolution Planning for Physics-Aware Video Generation

Recent advances in text-to-video generation have substantially improved visual fidelity, yet existing models still struggle to understand the physical evolution implicit in user prompts, making it difficult to generate videos with continuous physical state evolution. Here, we propose PhyEvolve, a multi-agent framework that enhances natural-language prompts through structured state-evolution planning. PhyEvolve consists of three agents: a Director, a Physical Evolver, and a Composer. Given a user prompt and an optional first frame, the Director grounds the scene and disentangles physical events from observation changes. The Physical Evolver uses scene evidence to identify physical mechanisms then constructs causally linked state transitions that unfold throughout the video, whether visible or not. The Composer utilizes these transitions to enhance the original prompts. Without retraining or model modification, PhyEvolve works directly with existing generators through prompt replacement. Extensive experiments on four major benchmarks show that PhyEvolve achieves state-of-the-art performance, outperforming the cutting-edge prior agentic baseline by 7.9 percentage points on average. These results demonstrate that explicit causal state-evolution planning improves physical consistency and state continuity in generated videos through natural-language prompt enhancement.

TLDR: PhyEvolve is a multi-agent framework that improves video generation consistency by using structured causal state-evolution planning to enhance prompts without requiring model retraining.

TLDR: PhyEvolve 是一个多智能体框架，通过结构化的物理状态演进规划来增强提示词，从而在无需重新训练模型的情况下提升视频生成的物理连贯性。

ParaLin++: Accelerating Parallel Diffusion Integrator via Intrinsic Partially Linear Structure

Training-free few-step samplers are often described as nearly quality-lossless under conventional FID/CLIP-style evaluation. It remains unclear whether that conclusion transfers to more realistic and complex generation-quality benchmarks. We first systematize the quality metrics used to evaluate training-free sampling acceleration for diffusion and flow-matching models. We find that representative training-free step-shortening methods often remain nearly lossless under FID/CLIP-class metrics, yet can suffer substantial quality degradation when evaluated with semantic-composition, dense-instruction, and long-text-rendering benchmarks such as GenEval, DPG-Bench, and LongTextBench. Parallel sampling is a notable exception: by retaining the full-step discretization and removing only the solver's temporal dependency, it can preserve near-lossless quality while accelerating sampling. Building on this observation, we propose \textit{ParaLin++}, a step-parity parallel sampler that preserves the full-step discretization while accelerating the solver iterations. Experiments across Stable Diffusion-v2, LSUN Church, FLUX.1-dev, and Ming-Flash-Omni 2.0 show that ParaLin++ preserves the full-step Euler quality profile at $2.6\times$--$4.8\times$, $1.68\times$ on FLUX, and $1.21\times$ on Ming. Project page: https://paralinplus.github.io/.

TLDR: ParaLin++ introduces a parallel sampling method for diffusion models that accelerates generation while maintaining high-fidelity quality by preserving the full-step discretization of the solver, overcoming limitations of existing training-free few-step samplers.

Pos4CHD: A Positive-Free Mask-Guided Counterfactual Synthesis Framework for Fetal Congenital Heart Defect Analysis

The scarcity of positive cases remains a major bottleneck in developing automated diagnostic systems for fetal Congenital Heart Defects~(CHDs), particularly for rare subtypes. Although normal cases are accessible with effort, positive cases are hard to collect due to low incidence rates and privacy concerns, causing severe class imbalance or absence of positive training samples. Existing works resort to synthesizing positives with conditional generative models, but they are task-specific and require positive training samples, hindering application for fetal CHDs. To this end, we propose a positive-free mask-guided counterfactual generation framework for fetal echocardiography, namely Pos4CHD, which enables realistic, high-quality synthesis of pseudo-positive samples from semantic masks, with only normal samples at hand. Technically, we first develop FE-Gen, a mask-guided generative model tailored for fetal heart ultrasound synthesis that mitigates histogram drift and enhances image-mask spatial alignment. Building on FE-Gen, Pos4CHD employs a mask-to-image generation paradigm for CHD synthesis. It first manipulates semantic masks to simulate fetal-heart structural anomalies and then renders anatomically consistent images. Extensive experiments on in-house datasets, together with expert evaluations, demonstrate the visual fidelity~($-12.97$ FID) and anatomical consistency~($+3.96\%$ DSC) of synthesized images. MoRE: Motion-Regularized Encoding for Articulated 3D Generation

Generating an articulated object from a single image requires recovering both its appearance and the mechanism governing its parts. A compact encoding of joint attributes does not by itself ensure that reconstruction preserves the resulting motion. We present MoRE, Motion-Regularized Encoding for articulated 3D generation. MoRE represents coordinate-aligned joint lines with axis classification and two axis-orthogonal Plücker moment coefficients, removing the arbitrary choice of an origin along the axis. During representation learning, decoded joint hypotheses are used to move corresponding three-dimensional anchors, and their motions are compared with those of annotated joints.
