Multics: Generative Multi-Player World Model with Hierarchical Communication

Multi-player interactive world models aim to simulate shared environments from multiple players' perspectives, but achieving robust generation while scaling to more players remains challenging. Existing approaches that rely on fully-connected communication (e.g., Solaris) incur quadratic attention costs as the number of players grows, while centralized approaches (e.g., $γ$-World) suffer from interference between unrelated events in a shared global representation. To address these challenges, we present Multics, a multi-player video generation framework with hierarchical communication that structures information exchange across player, group, and global levels. This hierarchy introduces group tokens into self-attention as intermediate group-level representations that aggregate locally relevant player information. These tokens connect to global tokens for cross-group communication, while the hierarchical topology limits direct mixing between unrelated local events. Group-token parameters are shared across group slots, allowing the same communication module to accommodate varying numbers of players without introducing new group-specific parameters. We further propose budget-constrained dynamic grouping based on recent camera trajectories, which adaptively groups relevant players while limiting unnecessary communication. Experiments demonstrate that Multics achieves state-of-the-art performance in 4- and 6-player settings, with improved cross-player consistency, player-specific action and camera controllability, and interference robustness. Moreover, Multics generalizes from 4-player training to 6-, 8-, and 10-player inference without retraining, while reducing self-attention computation compared with fully-connected communication.

TLDR: Multics is a hierarchical multi-player video generation framework that replaces quadratic-cost fully-connected attention with group-based tokens, enabling scalable, consistent, and controllable world modeling across varying player counts.

TLDR: Multics 提出了一种分层通信的多人视频生成框架，通过引入组令牌（group tokens）替代二次复杂度的全连接注意力机制，实现了在不同玩家数量下的可扩展、一致且可控的世界模型生成。

Scaling Unified Multimodal Models to Long-Horizon Interleaved Generation

Unified multimodal models (UMMs) provide an interface for interleaved text-image generation, where generated visual states can directly inform subsequent reasoning and synthesis. However, existing systems are still primarily demonstrated on short trajectories or simplified visual content. We develop a large UMM natively trained on interleaved multimodal data, with strong capabilities in image synthesis, text rendering, complex composition, and multimodal planning, and study how to extend these native capabilities to substantially longer autoregressive trajectories. The main challenge is that generated images continuously accumulate in context: irrelevant or imperfect visual states interfere with future synthesis, while previously established characters and scenes must still be recovered after long intervals. We introduce a visual-memory framework that maintains persistent visual references, selectively retrieves scene-relevant historical states while preserving the complete semantic history, and uses few-step self-rollout to adapt the model to imperfect generated histories. We further introduce LongMIG-Bench for evaluating long-horizon text-to-multi-image and image-to-multi-image generation. Our model achieves high-fidelity long-horizon interleaved generation with strong character and scene consistency, demonstrating how visual memory can scale native interleaved generation to substantially longer trajectories.

TLDR: This paper introduces a visual-memory framework and the LongMIG-Bench to enable consistent, long-horizon interleaved text and image generation in unified multimodal models.

MaskSense: Confronting the Visual Exploration Trap in Masked Image Generation

Reinforcement learning (RL) has shown strong potential for aligning generative models with human intent, but adapting RL to masked generative models (MGMs) remains largely underexplored. Existing approaches that directly adapt Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) to MGMs leave a foundational conflict unaddressed: MGMs' exploitative decoding nature is inherently at odds with the exploratory diversity that GRPO presupposes. In this work, we reveal a structural decoding bias termed the visual exploration trap: confidence-based sampling trades the exploration of diverse subject realizations for the greedy resolution of low-uncertainty background regions, causing premature collapse of the generative exploration space and starving GRPO of the rollout diversity it depends on. To this end, we propose MaskSense, a novel RL framework for MGMs that confronts the visual exploration trap at both the sampling and optimization stages. Specifically, we introduce a semantic anchored routing sampling mechanism that leverages semantic priors to preserve high-entropy subject token exploration while amplifying intra-group reward variance through exploratory-exploitative routing to yield more discriminative advantage estimates. Furthermore, we design a global entropy transition anchoring strategy to identify the most consequential decoding steps and concentrate gradient updates on them. Extensive experiments on multiple text-to-image benchmarks demonstrate that MaskSense substantially improves upon the base model, with GenEval accuracy improving from 54% to 86% and HPS from 28.89 to 36.39, achieving state-of-the-art performance.

TLDR: MaskSense addresses the 'visual exploration trap' in masked generative models by introducing semantic-aware sampling and gradient-focused optimization to enhance RL-based alignment and diversity.

TLDR: MaskSense 提出了一种针对掩码生成模型（MGM）的强化学习框架，通过语义引导的采样与梯度锚定策略，解决了模型在对齐过程中因过度关注低不确定性区域而导致的探索空间塌陷问题。

Barycentric Guidance: Turning Foundation Image Editors into Continuous Affective Controllers

Emotions are expressed on a continuum and are naturally described in the valence-arousal (VA) space. Existing VA-based image editors are typically training-based and domain-specific, limiting them to VA regions seen during training and making them brittle across datasets and domains. Meanwhile, despite producing high-quality edits, state-of-the-art foundation image editors are not continuous affective controllers: non-linear emotion representations in text encoders makes prompt interpolation poorly calibrated, so accurate and continuous VA control remains unreliable. To fill this gap, we introduce Barycentric Guidance, a training-free framework that turns pretrained foundation image editors into continuous affective controllers over VA, requiring no finetuning, extra data, or model changes. Our method maps any target VA point to simplex-based guidance weights, enabling VA controllability in three aspects: (i) accurate targeting of VA states, (ii) broad coverage across the VA plane, and (iii) continuous control. Across three face datasets, we improve VA target accuracy by 25-29%, expand VA-plane coverage by 28-50%, and increase expression diversity by 29-49%, over the strongest baseline. Unlike prior domain-specific methods, our training-free approach exploits the foundation model's inherent versatility, enabling generalization beyond human faces to animals, artworks, and complex scenes within a single framework. Code is available at https://github.com/harveymannering/BarycentricGuidance.

TLDR: Barycentric Guidance is a training-free framework that enables continuous affective control (valence-arousal) in foundation image editors by mapping target emotions to simplex-based guidance weights.

TLDR: Barycentric Guidance 是一种无需训练的框架，通过将目标情绪映射为基于单纯形的引导权重，使基础图像编辑模型能够实现连续的情感（效价-唤醒度）控制。

InfoVis: Unified Slide Image Generation and Editing

Generating and editing presentation slides requires models to combine accurate text rendering, instruction following, visual grounding, aesthetic design, and precise editing within one canvas. Existing work covers only subsets of these capabilities and lacks large-scale slide-native supervision and diagnostic evaluation. We formulate four slide-centric tasks and build a scalable data engine with over 13M continual-pretraining records and 1.9M supervised fine-tuning records. We also introduce VisSlideBench, a benchmark of 1,527 manually screened instances that covers all four tasks with task-specific, multidimensional evaluation. Its automatic rankings agree with blinded, dimension-wise human rankings. Using these resources, we train InfoVis, a unified 9B slide generation and editing model. On VisSlideBench, InfoVis leads every evaluated open-source model on all eleven metrics and scores 7.94 overall against 7.99 for Nano-Banana-2. Starting from FLUX.2-klein-base-9B, slide-specific post-training adds 1.6 points overall, with the largest generation gains in content faithfulness; the remaining gap to proprietary models is smallest in content faithfulness and largest in aesthetic quality. Built on InfoVis, our document-to-slide workflow achieves the second-highest Aesthetics score on the external SlidesGen-Bench. We will release the model, code, data, and benchmark.

TLDR: InfoVis is a unified 9B model for presentation slide generation and editing, trained on a massive new dataset and evaluated via a novel benchmark, VisSlideBench, demonstrating state-of-the-art performance among open-source models.

TLDR: InfoVis 是一个统一的 9B 参数演示文稿生成与编辑模型，该研究构建了大规模幻灯片专用数据集和基准测试 VisSlideBench，在开放模型中表现优异。

Image Tokenizers Should Be Trained Between Images

Latent diffusion models generate images as codes of an image tokenizer, whose decoder turns them into the pixel image. Recent tokenizers improve how well these codes reconstruct an image and how easily a generator learns them. Yet a generator does not reproduce the codes of training images, and when a generated code lands outside what the decoder expects, the decoded image contains imperfections. Current methods make the decoder more robust to generated codes by adding noise during training. However, generated codes lie between the training codes and resemble their mixture more than one of them plus noise. To improve generated images, decoders should therefore be trained on codes in the space between training samples. We propose InterpTok, which fine-tunes MAETok to decode mixtures of image codes into natural images. Since no target image exists between two training samples, a discriminator judges whether each decoded mixture looks as natural as its two sources. A mixing ratio loss requires the decoded mixture to keep the mixing ratio of the two codes. Together, they prevent collapse to a trivial solution and yield a sharp and natural composition of both images. InterpTok keeps MAETok's reconstruction quality and improves interpolation quality more than threefold, the best of all tokenizers in a recent benchmark. Beyond interpolation, InterpTok improves the generation FID of a matched diffusion transformer from 11.61 to 8.48 without guidance, and even reaches 8.11 at an eighth of the sampling steps, where the generator on MAETok's codes worsens. The generator trained on our tokenizer also reaches the final result of the one trained on MAETok at half the training steps. Our findings show that the space between image codes can and should be explicitly supervised rather than ignored.

TLDR: InterpTok introduces a novel tokenizer training strategy that explicitly supervises the interpolation space between latent codes, significantly improving generative model fidelity and sampling efficiency.

TLDR: InterpTok 提出了一种在图像编解码器训练中显式监督潜在空间插值的方法，显著提升了生成模型的图像质量和采样效率。

REEL: Learning to Retrieve, Evaluate, and Evolve Visual Memory for Any Long-Horizon Multi-Shot Video Generation

Memory-based long-video generation aims to combine user control with long-range visual consistency. We organize this process into three functional components: a planner, an adapter, and a generator. Representative multi-shot systems couple memory to generator-specific interfaces or use predefined agent workflows, rather than independently training a memory policy for cross-shot objectives. We introduce REEL, a general-purpose visual-memory planner. Through a unified tool interface, REEL learns to retrieve references, create missing visual evidence, and update persistent memory. Backend-specific adapters translate its evidence plans into supported conditioning inputs, allowing the same policy to guide different black-box video generators. To train REEL, we construct two high-quality datasets, REEL-SFT-4k and REEL-RL-1k, covering diverse interaction patterns and prompt types. Training proceeds in three stages: supervised fine-tuning, single-shot Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO), and multi-shot GRPO. Multi-shot GRPO carries policy-generated memory across consecutive shots, so later outcomes guide earlier reference selection and memory updates. REEL-Bench tests identity preservation and intended state changes under text-only requests and timed reference-image uploads. On JoyAI-Echo~1.5 and MiniMax-H3, REEL improves cross-shot consistency on ST-Bench by 15.1\% and 20.7\%, and Core-100 on our proposed REEL-Bench by 2.41 and 8.32 points, respectively, over the no-memory baseline.

TLDR: REEL is a modular memory-based planner that enhances long-horizon video generation by decoupling memory management from specific generators, utilizing GRPO to optimize cross-shot consistency.

TLDR: REEL 提出了一种基于记忆的通用视频生成规划器，通过将记忆管理与特定生成器解耦，并利用 GRPO 优化多镜头间的视觉一致性，显著提升了长视频生成的表现。

CineRecon: Benchmarking Frontier Models for Agentic Cinematic Reconstruction

Reconstructing reference videos as editable dynamic 3D scenes is an important path toward controllable video generation for filmmaking: these scenes provide modifiable, renderable visual proxies that enable explicit control over scene layout, subject actions, and camera behavior. To evaluate agents' ability to reconstruct editable dynamic 3D scenes from dynamic reference videos, we introduce CineRecon, a benchmark comprising 50 reference videos and 360 annotated evaluation clips. The reference videos span film, real-world footage, and games. Given a complete video, an agent uses tools to construct, animate, render, and refine its reconstruction. We evaluate this task under three representation settings: Proxy, Simplified, and Detailed. These settings increase geometric and appearance requirements while retaining the same requirements for spatial relations, camera behavior, and subject dynamics. This design tests whether agents can reproduce the events in a video when detailed appearance is unnecessary, and how performance changes as representation requirements increase. For evaluation, we construct checklists that associate observable properties and events with specific entities and times. A vision-language judge compares timestamp-aligned reference and rendered frames across 11 categories, grouped into camera, static, and dynamic consistency dimensions. We evaluate 12 models using their corresponding agent harnesses and find failures across all three dimensions. Subject motions and interactions remain difficult to reproduce even under the Proxy setting.

TLDR: CineRecon is a new benchmark designed to evaluate AI agents on their ability to reconstruct dynamic 3D scenes from reference videos across varying levels of geometric and appearance complexity.

TLDR: CineRecon 是一个旨在评估 AI 智能体将动态参考视频重建为可编辑 3D 场景能力的基准测试，涵盖了从代理到详细表现的多种复杂维度。

Heuristics are Not Enough: Calibrated and Corrected Caching for Video Diffusion Acceleration

Feature caching has become a mainstream training-free strategy for accelerating video diffusion models by reusing intermediate outputs across denoising steps. However, existing adaptive methods rely on heuristic difference measures misaligned with actual generation quality, employ a single global threshold that ignores heterogeneous phase dynamics, and never validate their budget estimates against true approximation error, allowing systematic bias to propagate uncorrected. We propose \textbf{AlignCache}, a closed-loop caching framework that directly aligns scheduling decisions with their true influence on the final generated video through three tightly coupled components: (1) phase-adaptive budget routing that partitions the denoising trajectory into structurally distinct phases, each governed by an independent threshold and a staleness-aware guard; (2) closed-loop threshold calibration that exploits counterfactual error measurement at the classifier-free guided output level to adjust decision boundaries online via an EMA feedback loop; and (3) momentum-augmented draft synthesis combining direction-magnitude gated residual extrapolation with anchor-guided unconditional correction. By transforming scheduling from a static risk accumulator into a feedback-driven, self-correcting controller, AlignCache consistently achieves superior acceleration-quality trade-offs across state-of-the-art video generation models. Our code is available at https://github.com/jiangjyjy/AlignCache.

TLDR: AlignCache is a closed-loop caching framework that replaces static heuristic-based video diffusion acceleration with a feedback-driven controller, dynamically calibrating thresholds to minimize approximation error.

TLDR: AlignCache 提出了一种闭环缓存框架，通过引入反馈机制动态校准缓存阈值，替代了传统的基于启发式规则的视频扩散模型加速方法，有效降低了生成误差。

Self-Verifier Capture in Unified Visual Generators: Diagnosing and Repairing Closed-Loop Self-Reward

Image-space reward requires decoding and a separate judge; unified visual generators can instead reuse their understanding pathway for decode-free, self-supplied reward. Optimizing against this verifier closes the self-reward loop; shared trainable weights let one update move generation and scoring even with the reward gradient stopped. We call the resulting failure self-verifier capture: self-reward rises while independently measured quality falls. Matched semantic/denoising by shared/frozen cells on Scale-RAE isolate two routes: the policy exploits on-manifold blind spots of a frozen semantic judge, while shared updates additionally rewrite the judge. A frozen judge also becomes stale, and repeated self-refresh yields progressively weaker recoveries. The diagnosis yields a two-part repair: locking the warmed reward read blocks rewriting, while sparse, parameter-disjoint external anchors counter residual exploitation. Unanchored rollouts retain semantic reward across multiple latent noise levels, providing dense latent reward that terminal image-space judging cannot supply, while a drift-triggered controller allocates anchors to high-drift rollouts and propagates their calibration within each group. At matched budgets, the controller improves on fixed-dose anchoring, increasingly so as coverage tightens. Both the unanchored failure and the repair transfer to the higher-performing, architecturally distinct Show-o2. Across both bases, the complete method significantly outperforms a construct-matched every-rollout judge on GenEval and DPG-Bench while externally scoring only 1/16 of rollouts. At 1/32 coverage, it scores higher in all four comparisons and remains jointly non-inferior.

TLDR: This paper identifies 'self-verifier capture' in unified visual generators—where self-reward leads to quality degradation—and proposes a dual-repair mechanism involving locked reward blocks and parameter-disjoint external anchors to maintain alignment and efficiency.

TLDR: 本文探讨了统一视觉生成模型中“自验证器捕获”现象（即自奖励导致质量下降），并提出通过锁定奖励模块及引入参数分离的外部锚点进行修复，从而在极高效率下实现模型对齐。

MiMaAlign: Hierarchical Micro-Macro Alignment for Physically Plausible Video Generation

Text-to-video (T2V) diffusion models have made remarkable progress in generating high-fidelity visuals, yet their outputs still often exhibit physically implausible phenomena, including discontinuous dynamics and implausible event interactions. Existing representation alignment methods mainly transfer generic video features or short-range motion cues, treating physical plausibility as a single-level representation problem. To address this issue, we propose MiMaAlign, a hierarchical micro-macro alignment framework that transfers complementary dynamics-oriented and event-oriented knowledge into different abstraction depths of a pretrained video diffusion model. Specifically, micro-level continuous dynamics alignment imposes constraints by aligning diffusion features with a continuous-time motion representation learned by a Neural Ordinary Differential Equation (Neural ODE), while macro-level event-commonsense alignment provides representations of object interactions and event semantics through an instruction-prompted vision-language model (VLM). With training-only auxiliary objectives, we align the complementary supervision from the two teachers with shallower and deeper generator features, respectively, enabling the generator to internalize the corresponding physical priors. Empirical evaluations show that, on CogVideoX-2B, MiMaAlign achieves a relative improvement of up to 21.0% in the Physical Commonsense (PC) score on the VideoPhy benchmark over the baseline, and also delivers substantial gains on the highly challenging VideoPhy2 benchmark, while maintaining general video quality on VBench and being supported by blind user comparisons.

TLDR: MiMaAlign introduces a hierarchical framework that aligns micro-level continuous dynamics (via Neural ODEs) and macro-level event semantics (via VLMs) to improve the physical plausibility of T2V diffusion models.

TLDR: MiMaAlign 提出了一种分层对齐框架，通过结合微观层面的连续动态（利用神经常微分方程）和宏观层面的事件语义（利用视觉语言模型），显著提升了文生视频模型的物理合理性。

Tikhonov-Stabilized Bezier Representation Forecasting for Training-free Diffusion Acceleration

Diffusion models, particularly Diffusion Transformers, achieve strong image and video generation quality but remain expensive at inference time due to repeated denoiser evaluations. Feature caching offers a deployment-friendly acceleration strategy by reusing or predicting intermediate representations without retraining the generator or modifying the sampler. However, existing cache-then-forecast methods often rely on Taylor-style finite-difference extrapolation, which can become unstable over longer cache intervals, and typically predict local module outputs whose errors may accumulate through subsequent denoiser blocks. We propose \textbf{BeziCast}, a training-free diffusion acceleration framework that forecasts output-proximal denoising representations using low-order B'ezier trajectories. BeziCast estimates B'ezier control points via Tikhonov-stabilized fitting, providing a smooth, capacity-controlled temporal parameterization that avoids explicit high-order derivative estimation and decouples trajectory capacity from cache-query density. To moderate aggressive extrapolation, we further derive equivalent forecasting weights and introduce a convex-hull-inspired guardrail that detects high-risk predictions and softly pulls them toward a conservative simplex-projected estimate. Extensive evaluations across advanced image and video diffusion models demonstrate the effectiveness of BeziCast. In particular, BeziCast accelerates FLUX.1 by up to (4.79\times) and HunyuanVideo by up to (4.11\times), while preserving substantially better generation quality than competing acceleration baselines.

TLDR: BeziCast is a training-free diffusion acceleration framework that uses Tikhonov-stabilized Bezier trajectories and convex-hull guardrails to forecast intermediate representations, significantly speeding up FLUX.1 and HunyuanVideo.

TLDR: BeziCast 是一种无需训练的扩散模型加速框架，通过 Tikhonov 正则化的贝塞尔曲线轨迹预测中间特征，并引入凸包防护机制，显著提升了 FLUX.1 和 HunyuanVideo 的推理速度。

Learning $ω$-Powered Conditional Distributions with Importance-Weighted Flow Matching

Classifier-Free Guidance (CFG) with guidance scale $γ$ is commonly motivated by the $ω$-powered conditional distribution obtained by setting $ω=γ$, but CFG does not, in general, sample from this distribution exactly. We study whether the powered target can instead be learned explicitly. We introduce Importance-Weighted Conditional Flow Matching (IW-CFM), which reweights conditional flow-matching examples using density ratios obtained from frozen conditional and unconditional flow models. With exact normalized weights, the resulting objective has the same gradient as flow matching on the powered target; in practice, we use self-normalized estimation and a regularized empirical approximation for high-dimensional data. On synthetic distributions, IW-CFM recovers analytical powered targets for which numerically matched CFG, $γ=ω$, exhibits a visible mismatch. On ImageNet, an amortized lightweight corrector achieves better alignment than fixed- and matched-guidance CFG with the corresponding weighted and filtered reference distributions. We additionally present an exploratory CLIP analysis of powered-density rankings on real ImageNet data. Aggregate shifts across selected prompt-specific contrasts coexist with heterogeneous classwise responses, including nonmonotonic trajectories. These results provide a practical route for studying and approximating model-defined powered conditional distributions.

TLDR: The paper introduces Importance-Weighted Conditional Flow Matching (IW-CFM) to explicitly learn $\omega$-powered conditional distributions, addressing the limitations of Classifier-Free Guidance in accurately sampling from these distributions.

SyncReward: Human-Aligned Synchronization Reward for Audio-Visual Generation

Recent years have witnessed rapid progress in joint audio--visual generation. However, audio--visual synchronization remains a challenge, as generated speech and sound often temporally misalign with visual events. A key bottleneck is the lack of human-annotated data, reliable evaluation benchmarks, and synchronization-aware evaluation models for generated audio--visual outputs. To address these limitations, we conduct a comprehensive study spanning data, benchmarking, and reward modeling. We first introduce SyncReward-Data-80K, a dataset containing 80K professional synchronization ratings for clips from eight audio--visual generators. A taxonomy-guided construction pipeline covers four sound-event families: Speech, Onset, Instrument, and Ambient. We further construct \textbf{SyncReward-Bench}, a held-out benchmark of 1K clips, each independently rated by multiple professionals. Using this benchmark, we systematically evaluate specialized synchronization metrics and omni-modal models, revealing their substantial misalignment with human judgments. Finally, we develop SyncReward, a reward model that combines semantic--temporal contrastive pretraining on real clips with human supervision on generated clips. As an evaluation metric, SyncReward achieves the strongest agreement with human ratings among the evaluated methods. As a reward, it improves synchronization through inference-time best-of-$N$ selection and reinforcement learning based post-training. We will release the data, benchmark, model, and code at https://github.com/WeChatCV/SyncReward.

TLDR: SyncReward addresses audio-visual misalignment by introducing a large-scale human-annotated dataset, a comprehensive benchmark, and a reward model that improves synchronization in generative models through RL and best-of-N selection.

TLDR: SyncReward 通过引入大规模人工标注数据集、基准测试以及一个专用的奖励模型，解决了音视频生成中长期存在的同步问题，并通过强化学习提升了生成质量。

RAR: Rate-Adaptive Rollout for Time-Consistent Interactive World Models

Video-based interactive world models aim to enable real-time, user-controllable environments. Yet most existing systems are trained and rolled out at a fixed temporal rate, limiting both runtime-rate adaptation and physical-horizon coverage -- a limitation we term temporal-rate coupling.
