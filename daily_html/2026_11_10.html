Plant Orbiter: 3D Plant Reconstruction and Generation with Video Diffusion Orbit Cameras and 3D Gaussian Splatting

Modern digital phenotyping pipelines require the precise measurement of a range of 3D morphological plant traits. The accurate, high throughput acquisition of these traits helps breeders and researchers accelerate the development of new varieties, techniques and products. Recent advances in 3D technologies have enabled a rapid transition from 2D to 3D pipelines, but high throughput acquisition of digital 3D twins of plants remains a significant challenge due to complex morphology, thin structures and occlusions. In this paper we present Plant Orbiter, a new approach to generating 3D plants that leverages recent advances in video diffusion to produce high quality 3D Gaussian Splats of plants from a single image. We show that our method more accurately recreates plant morphology than other state-of-the-art methods, achieving the highest 3D F-score against all baselines, while being more practical for high throughput pipelines due to easier alignment and reduced cost of camera placement. Combined with a 2D diffusion model, we show that our method also achieves state-of-the-art results in the generation of 3D plant models from a text prompt.

TLDR: Plant Orbiter is a novel framework that utilizes video diffusion models and 3D Gaussian Splatting to reconstruct high-fidelity 3D plant models from single images or text prompts, effectively handling complex morphologies and occlusions.

TLDR: Plant Orbiter 提出了一种利用视频扩散模型和 3D 高斯溅射技术，从单张图像或文本提示中生成高精度 3D 植物模型的方法，有效解决了植物复杂形态和遮挡带来的重建难题。

WanderBench: Can World Models Explore Dynamic Worlds from Video?

Video world models conditioned on an input video make it possible to explore real, recorded dynamic scenes, enabling re-shooting of footage, free-viewpoint replay, and real-to-sim environments for embodied agents. Such uses require the generated rollout to stay faithful to the recorded world, yet existing benchmarks start from a single image or a text prompt, and video-conditioned generation is evaluated mainly on perceptual quality and camera following. These evaluations do not directly assess whether exploration preserves subject identity, event progression, and scene consistency upon revisiting a location. We introduce WanderBench, a benchmark for exploring dynamic worlds from video. It comprises 745 single-shot source videos of real and AI-generated content in three difficulty tiers (3-25 s), paired with camera routes given as keyboard actions, and evaluates six dimensions with 24 human-validated sub-metrics. Benchmarking eight models, we find that as clips grow longer, models keep the static scene and visual quality but lose camera control and the source dynamics, and that none keeps revisited places consistent. We further propose WanderLand, a training-free framework that conditions each frame of a frozen model on the same-instant source and, on revisits, on its own earlier static content. WanderLand improves five models on every dimension, by up to 8.3 points in source-dynamics fidelity.

TLDR: WanderBench introduces a comprehensive benchmark for evaluating video world models' ability to maintain spatial and temporal consistency during interactive exploration, alongside a training-free framework, WanderLand, that significantly improves consistency and control.

TLDR: WanderBench 引入了一个用于评估视频世界模型在交互式探索中保持时空一致性的基准，并提出了训练自由的框架 WanderLand，显著提升了模型在场景一致性和相机控制方面的表现。

LongSCOP: Semantically Consistent Long Video Outpainting

Video out-painting is an important problem, yet existing research focuses primarily on short clips, leaving long video out-painting largely underexplored. Extending video out-painting to long videos poses substantial challenges. A natural solution is iterative out-painting, i.e., generating videos chunk by chunk, but this introduces two major issues: generation instability or even failure caused by inter-chunk inconsistencies and error accumulation, and semantic drift over long temporal horizons. In this paper, we propose LongSCOP, short for Semantically Consistent long Video Out-Painting. First, we introduce a training recipe with three components for smooth and stable long-horizon generation: self-forcing training, latent calibration, and future references. Beyond generation stability, we recognize that the rich temporal context of long videos provides valuable references for maintaining semantic consistency. We therefore develop a VLM-based agent that automatically selects the most informative reference frames from the entire video to maintain consistent subjects, scenes, and backgrounds throughout long-horizon generation. We also construct a high-quality training dataset of 62K video clips. To facilitate research on this underexplored problem, we introduce LVO-Bench, a benchmark curated by human experts to assess long-horizon stability and semantic consistency. Extensive experiments demonstrate that our method outperforms existing approaches in both generation stability and semantic coherence.

TLDR: LongSCOP addresses long-duration video outpainting by combining a stable training recipe with a VLM-based agent that leverages global temporal context to ensure semantic consistency.

TLDR: LongSCOP 通过结合稳定的训练策略与基于 VLM 的智能体，利用全局时序上下文信息，有效解决了长视频补全中的语义一致性与生成稳定性难题。

PhysOPSD: Code as Privilege for Physically Plausible Video Generation

While modern video generative models achieve remarkable visual fidelity, they remain noticeably brittle when generating physically plausible dynamics and complex interactions. Targeted post-training is crucial, yet supervised fine-tuning suffers from offline state mismatch, and reinforcement learning is hindered by sparse, ill-defined physical reward functions. On-Policy (Self-)Distillation (OPSD) reconciles on-policy rollouts with dense corrective feedback, shifting the core challenge to designing effective privileged context. However, conventional modalities fall short: text prompts are inherently declarative, whereas real reference videos heavily entangle physical laws with visual appearance without parametric control. To this end, we propose PhysOPSD, introducing executable code as privileged physical context for video generation. Unlike static visual observations, executable code instantiates an editable physical world with intrinsic abstraction and controllability. Powered by PhysOPSD-Engine, we translate real-world physical scenarios into verifiable simulation programs. PhysOPSD then incorporates (i) dual-level physical privilege to bridge the information gap via an intermediate bottleneck, alongside (ii) controlled physical variations under shared rollout noise to expose causal dynamic shifts. Extensive experiments demonstrate that PhysOPSD substantially improves physical plausibility across diverse backbones, outperforming SFT and RL baselines with only $3$K samples, while preserving general video generation quality.

TLDR: PhysOPSD improves physical plausibility in video generation by using executable code as a privileged, controllable context, effectively addressing the limitations of text-based and visual-only guidance.

TLDR: PhysOPSD 通过引入可执行代码作为特权物理上下文来增强视频生成的物理真实感，有效解决了传统文本或视频引导在物理控制上的局限性。

MPEG-Diffusion: Mixed-Resolution Video Generation

Video diffusion transformers (DiTs) generate realistic videos, but their heavy computational cost limits broader real-world applications. Mixed-resolution generation reduces this cost by generating the visually important region at high resolution (HR) and the rest at low resolution (LR), yet existing methods suffer a severe quality drop on videos and require knowing the HR region in advance. We show that these failures are not inherent to mixed resolution, and propose $\textbf{MPEG-Diffusion}$ to address their three root causes: (1) dual-resolution attention to mitigate the curse of mixed resolution, where each query reads the K/V of its own resolution, (2) a mixed-resolution decoder to blend the HR and LR latents before decoding and resolve distortion, and (3) a self-selected HR region using the DiT's knowledge to find the visually important region without requiring it in advance. Processing only $30\%$ of full-resolution tokens, MPEG-Diffusion speeds up denoising by $4.8\times$ on Wan2.1 14B ($3.8\times$ on 1.3B), outperforming prior methods on nearly every metric. In the user study, we demonstrate the high quality of MPEG-Diffusion, with raters preferring it over prior mixed-resolution methods in over $90\%$ of pairs and rating it close to full resolution.

TLDR: MPEG-Diffusion introduces a novel mixed-resolution strategy for video diffusion transformers that intelligently selects high-resolution regions and optimizes attention and decoding, achieving significant speedups without sacrificing visual quality.

TLDR: MPEG-Diffusion 提出了一种针对视频扩散模型的混合分辨率生成策略，通过自适应选择高分辨率区域及优化注意力机制与解码过程，在大幅提升推理速度的同时保持了高质量生成效果。

Direct Trajectory Optimization for Inference-Time Alignment of Text-to-Image Diffusion Models

Alignment of text-to-image diffusion models with reward functions is a key challenge in controllable generation. Existing approaches typically rely on optimizing model inputs or fine-tuning denoising networks, which requires computationally expensive backpropagation through large denoising models. In this work, we propose Direct Trajectory Optimization (DTO), a training-free framework that performs alignment by directly optimizing the sampling trajectory of diffusion models. Specifically, DTO treats intermediate denoising scores and sampled noise as the optimization variables, eliminating the need for gradients through the denoising network. To mitigate the out-of-distribution problem, we introduce Reward-Optimized Score Guidance, which interpolates between the optimized scores and the pretrained model predictions to stabilize sampling, while providing a provable theoretical guarantee of reward improvement under constrained distributions. Empirically, DTO improves alignment performance across three diffusion models on two benchmarks, outperforming existing inference-time alignment methods while maintaining high efficiency. These results suggest that trajectory-level optimization is a promising direction for efficient and effective diffusion model alignment.

TLDR: Direct Trajectory Optimization (DTO) is a training-free framework that aligns text-to-image diffusion models by optimizing sampling trajectories instead of model weights, bypassing expensive backpropagation.

TLDR: Direct Trajectory Optimization (DTO) 提出了一种无需训练的框架，通过直接优化扩散模型的采样轨迹来实现对齐，避免了昂贵的反向传播过程。

QCC-WM: Linking Macro-State Dynamics and Visual Generation via Quotient Causal Construction

A key challenge in action-conditioned world modeling is to construct macro-states from high-dimensional observations that support recursive prediction under actions, while keeping their predicted evolution consistent with visual changes in generated observations. We propose QCC-WM, a visual world model based on Quotient-based Causal Construction. QCC-WM uses action responses to guide macro-variable construction for recursive action-conditioned prediction. A shared reference quotient measurement constrains the consistency between predicted states and observed changes, allowing macro-level predictions to provide structured constraints for future visual generation. Using translational and rotational actions and their predicted responses, the model aggregates local visual features into macro-nodes with spatial correspondence through causal abstraction, and recursively predicts node content and motion under a shared reference measurement. Macro-level predictions guide future observation generation, while predicted motion transports historical appearance information. Node-level errors between generated observations and macro-level targets are then mapped back to the corresponding spatial regions for correction, improving consistency between visual generation and macro-level predictions. On the US-VLA dataset, QCC-WM achieves an ROI-MSE of 0.01861 for four-step prediction. Compared with the best baseline at each prediction horizon, it reduces LPIPS by 48.59%, 41.68%, and 30.23% for four-step prediction and eight- and sixteen-step recursive extrapolation, respectively. Ablation studies and mechanism analyses show that motion-guided reference transport mainly improves pixel reconstruction, while node-level error feedback further improves perceptual quality and consistency between generated observations and macro-level predictions.

TLDR: QCC-WM introduces a world model using Quotient-based Causal Construction to map high-dimensional observations into structured macro-states, significantly improving consistency and perceptual quality in long-term video prediction.

TLDR: QCC-WM 提出了一种基于商空间因果构建的世界模型，将高维观测映射为结构化宏观状态，显著提升了视频预测的连贯性与感知质量。

Four-Step Text-Bearing Image Generation via Distillation with Local Response Regularization and Teacher-Guided Training

Generating text-bearing images requires coherent scenes and accurate characters. Large text-to-image models improve both capabilities through repeated denoising, at substantial sampling cost. Few-step distillation reduces this cost but requires the student to organize scenes and refine glyphs within a short trajectory. We present a four-step distillation framework combining local response regularization with teacher-guided text training. Built on distribution matching, the framework distills the GLM-Image DiT while retaining its autoregressive visual prior, glyph encoder, and VAE. A path-length penalty promotes consistent local responses of the conditional denoising field. Progressive training combines synthetic strings with teacher-generated imagery to preserve character accuracy and scene-integrated typography. The student achieves GenEval Overall 0.84 and CVTG-2K word accuracy 0.5045, compared with 0.81 and 0.1690 for four-step TDM. A coefficient study favors $10^{-5}$, and Qwen-Image experiments show additional gains from response regularization and text supervision. Single-image inference takes 1.742 s, compared with 5.818 s for the teacher, yielding a $3.34\times$ speedup at the same 40 GB GPU memory requirement.

TLDR: This paper introduces a four-step distillation framework for text-bearing image generation that uses local response regularization and teacher-guided training to significantly improve text rendering accuracy and inference speed.

Leveraging Complementary Event Frame and Voxel Representations for RGB-Guided Multimodal Pre-training

In this paper, we propose CM3AE 2.0, a unified RGB Frame and Event-Voxel/-Frame pre-training framework. By jointly learning multimodal spatiotemporal representations and exploiting latent cross-modal dependencies through cross-modal generation, the framework enhances multimodal information interaction and collaborative modeling capabilities. Specifically, we first propose an Event-aware Spatiotemporal Masked Reconstruction (ESMR) strategy to learn unified multimodal spatiotemporal representations. It leverages event responses to guide masking toward dynamically salient regions. Through spatiotemporal masked reconstruction, it learns spatial structures and their temporal variations from continuous RGB-Event observations. In addition, it incorporates multimodal fusion reconstruction and cross-modal contrastive learning to facilitate the modeling of cross-modal complementary information and the learning of shared representations. Building upon this, we further propose a Hierarchical Cross-modal Generation (HCG) strategy to learn latent cross-modal dependencies through visual and language generation. At the visual level, Event-conditioned RGB generation is performed using a pre-trained diffusion model to capture visual associations between the two modalities. At the language level, video-level text description generation based on joint RGB-Event representations establishes connections between multimodal visual features and linguistic semantics, further enriching the learning of cross-modal dependencies. Extensive experiments on four diverse downstream tasks demonstrate that CM3AE 2.0 consistently improves performance, validating the effectiveness and generalization capability of the proposed pre-training framework.

TLDR: CM3AE 2.0 is a multimodal pre-training framework that integrates RGB frames and event voxels via spatiotemporal masked reconstruction and hierarchical cross-modal generation to enhance representation learning for visual and linguistic tasks.

How Many Candidates Does a One-Step World Model Need? A Random-Codebook Account of Nearest-of-m Training on Frozen DINO Tokens

One-step stochastic world models avoid the mode averaging of deterministic feature predictors without the tens of network evaluations per step that diffusion and flow matching require. The simplest such objective is nearest-of-m matching (conditional implicit maximum likelihood estimation, IMLE): draw m candidate futures and pull only the one closest to the observed outcome. Recent work conjectures that a small m suffices for feature-space world models and that a strictly proper energy score should replace the hard minimum. We test both claims with one-step world models on frozen DINOv2/v3 patch tokens, using benchmarks whose futures can be enumerated. Under mode-like uncertainty, nearest-of-m with m=16 covers the enumerated futures better than 50-step flow matching (at about 1/30 of its latency), one-step flow generators and an energy-score generator at matched parameter counts, and its rollouts stay on the token manifold where flow rollouts drift. One quantity governs the choice of m: the nearest-of-m objective is the expected distortion of a random codebook of m draws from the generator. This view yields three predictions, recorded before the corresponding runs: (i) memorization of the realized outcome in contexts that never repeat, whose strength is governed by the codebook's miss distortion I(m) rather than its miss probability, and which an energy-score generator does not escape by raising m; (ii) a flattening of mode probabilities with m, matching a known optimum within 0.01; and (iii) under-dispersion on continuous high-dimensional outcomes, which bounds the method on real robot video. Per-step matching on self-rollouts cuts long-horizon error by a third on a stochastic Push-T variant; an ablation separates its manifold and coverage benefits. Planning success does not improve despite accurate risk estimates, a negative result we report with its diagnosis.

TLDR: The paper investigates the efficiency of 'nearest-of-m' (IMLE) training for one-step world models on DINO tokens, demonstrating that it outperforms flow matching in latency and manifold adherence while providing a theoretical framework based on random codebook distortion.

TLDR: 本文探讨了基于 DINO Token 的一步世界模型中“nearest-of-m”(IMLE) 训练策略的有效性，证明其在推理延迟和流形保持方面优于流匹配方法，并提出了基于随机码本失真的理论框架。

LO-World: Unified BEV Latent Modeling for Joint 4D Occupancy and LiDAR Forecasting

LiDAR point clouds and 3D semantic occupancy represent two distinct modalities capturing fine-grained metric geometry and dense volumetric semantics, respectively, holding natural potential for mutual complementarity. However, existing world models typically forecast these modalities independently or cascade them heuristically, missing the opportunity for reciprocal cross-modal regularization. To address this, we propose \textbf{LO-World}, a unified 4D world model that forecasts both modalities within a shared BEV latent space. This single-stream formulation enables dense semantics and metric geometry to mutually reinforce each other, while eliminating multi-pipeline redundancy. Specifically, LO-World couples an efficient hybrid range-voxel autoencoder with a flow-matching generator that caches historical context to reduce iterative sampling latency. Extensive experiments demonstrate that LO-World outperforms prior state-of-the-art baselines, improving occupancy forecasting IoU by 10.9% and reducing LiDAR Chamfer Distance by 30.1%. Meanwhile, it sustains a real-time throughput exceeding $25$~FPS on a single RTX 4090. Our code is available at https://github.com/lzzzzzm/LO-World.

TLDR: LO-World is a unified 4D world model that generates LiDAR point clouds and 3D occupancy maps within a shared BEV latent space, using flow-matching to achieve high-performance, real-time multimodal forecasting.
