ManimCode: Learning Visual Self-Correction for Educational Animation

Manim supports precise, programmatic video generation, but a successfully rendered educational animation can still have visual defects, missing explanations, or incorrect content. Rendering does not directly identify the code changes needed to correct these visual failures, so feedback-driven revision is needed. Existing agentic frameworks use iterative rendering and revision but typically rely on large models, often proprietary. Existing Manim datasets primarily contain final programs and often lack quality verification and the trajectories of feedback and revisions needed to learn this refinement process. We introduce ManimData and ManimCode, a data and training framework for feedback-driven educational animation generation. We construct ManimData through a render-guided pipeline that iteratively generates, executes, reviews, and revises Manim programs with retrieved API documentation. Its 24,034 render-verified, quality-filtered records preserve intermediate programs, execution errors, visual critiques, and corrective revisions. We use role-conditioned supervised fine-tuning on these trajectories to train ManimCode, a single 4B model, to write, review, and revise animation programs. To improve correction of errors that differ from those in the collected trajectories, we then train the Reviser with reinforcement learning on self-generated states, using rendering outcomes and reference-free quality feedback. ManimCode achieves 96.6% and 97.0% render success on ManimData-test and ManimBench, compared with 52.6% and 82.0% for the untrained 4B model under the same inference setup, while also improving video quality scores from 2.48 to 2.94 and from 3.00 to 3.44. Integrating ManimCode into two long-form educational video generation systems increases completed videos from 19/117 to 107/117 and from 53/239 to 239/239, alongside improvements in educational effectiveness and visual quality.

TLDR: ManimCode is a framework that uses a render-guided data pipeline and reinforcement learning to train a compact 4B model for self-correcting the generation of educational animations.

TLDR: ManimCode 是一个通过渲染引导的数据流水线和强化学习训练的框架，旨在让 4B 小参数模型能够自动修正教育类动画代码，提升视频生成的成功率与质量。

FewStepRL: Fine-tuning Few-step Diffusion Models with Reinforcement Learning

Reinforcement learning (RL) has become a standard post-training method for diffusion models, but most RL objectives roll out the current policy at every iteration, and with a many-step sampler these rollouts typically dominate the cost of training. Few-step models cut this cost, yet they model an average velocity over an interval, whereas existing RL objectives are built for an instantaneous velocity with a many-step sampler. We adapt RL objectives to few-step models systematically in three phases, namely a transformation, a decomposition and an adaptation. The transformation to $x$-space expresses each objective we consider through a common policy prediction, at the clean end by default and at an explicitly marked level for transition or flow-map objectives, so that objectives stated over densities, velocities or rewards become comparable. The decomposition writes the gradient of each of these objectives as the sum of a self-regression toward a reference and a reward augmentation, so that a method reduces, at the gradient level, to a choice of anchors and weights. The adaptation computes each anchor from a few-step model at the end of its transport or along its tangent, with the instantaneous velocity recovered from the network itself or from an auxiliary network. This yields few-step versions of FlowGRPO, AWM, DiffusionNFT, DiffusionOPSD and ReFL in one modular codebase. In our experiments, reward-gradient-based methods are the most effective when the reward is differentiable and its gradient is affordable; otherwise DiffusionNFT-style methods generally reach high rewards in relatively few iterations despite a sometimes higher per-iteration cost. Extra inference steps show no systematic aggregate benefit in our experiments, although selected outputs visibly improve with two to four times as many sampling steps as in training.

TLDR: FewStepRL provides a unified framework to adapt existing Reinforcement Learning objectives for few-step diffusion models by decomposing gradients into self-regression and reward augmentation components.

TLDR: FewStepRL 提供了一个统一的框架，通过将梯度分解为自回归和奖励增强两部分，系统地将现有的强化学习目标函数适配到少步扩散模型中。

Bri3D: Bridging the Object-Scene Gap in Single-Image 3D Generation via Scene-Anchor

Generating compositional 3D scenes from a single image requires recovering detailed object geometry and coherent spatial arrangements. Existing object-to-scene methods, including SAM3D and MIDI, extend object generation through image-context reasoning or inter-instance modeling. Coordinating objects remains challenging with limited scene-level supervision. Directly extending object-centric generators to entire scenes can degrade geometry and fail to preserve instance scale, orientation, and pose. We propose Bri3D, a framework bridging scene-level structural reasoning and object-level generative priors through instance-aware voxel scene anchors. Bri3D predicts a color-coded scene scaffold encoding coarse object geometry and spatial arrangements. Each instance is extracted as a scene anchor and refined into a pose-preserving, prior-compatible voxel support, enabling a frozen object generator to synthesize detailed textured geometry in the anchor-defined spatial reference. This coarse-to-fine design separates scene organization from object-scale detail synthesis, focusing scene-level training on spatial organization and anchor refinement while reusing pretrained object priors. Experiments demonstrate improvements in scene layout, object geometry, visual quality, and generalization. Bri3D predicts scene layouts efficiently; full scene generation is faster than SceneGen and SAM3D.

TLDR: Bri3D introduces a coarse-to-fine framework that uses 'scene anchors' to bridge the gap between scene-level spatial organization and object-level generative priors, enabling high-quality, efficient 3D scene generation from single images.

TLDR: Bri3D 提出了一种“场景锚点”框架，通过从粗到细的策略连接场景级空间布局与对象级生成先验，实现了高效且高质量的单图 3D 场景生成。

WAM-Sim0.1: World Anchored Memory Meets Online RL for Consistent and Realistic Driving Simulation

Autoregressive driving world models systematically suffer from structural drift, identity drift, and quality decay over long rollout horizons due to transient conditioning signals that lack persistent state. We present WAM-Sim, a driving world model anchored by a persistent hierarchical memory and optimized via online flow-matching RL. WAM-Sim structures persistent context across three temporal and semantic scales: pose-aligned structural tokens encoding persistent road topology, read-only scene tokens supplying geolocated background appearance, and write-once instance tokens preserving individual traffic participant identities across occlusions and chunk boundaries. To restore visual sharpness and physical plausibility degraded by multi-scale conditioning, we introduce an online post-training framework that optimizes self-generated rollouts via flow-matching RL guided by hierarchy-aligned visual rewards and a vision-language model physical judge. The invariant memory empirically confines policy optimization within the anchored context, helping suppress semantic collapse and reward hacking, while online RL systematically enforces physical commonsense and fine-grained visual fidelity. On the nuScenes benchmark, WAM-Sim achieves a superior generation fidelity (FID=3.47, FVD=25.72) and strong downstream perception performance (24.16 mAP, 71.23 road mIoU). On minute-scale rollouts of self-collected data, WAM-Sim substantially mitigates structural and identity drift, preserving high scene similarity and subject identity coherence across extended horizons. Project page: https://fudan-generative-vision.github.io/WAM-Sim.

TLDR: WAM-Sim introduces a persistent hierarchical memory system and online flow-matching RL to solve structural and identity drift in autoregressive driving world models.

TLDR: WAM-Sim 引入了一种持久的分层记忆系统和在线流匹配强化学习方法，解决了自回归驾驶世界模型中常见的结构和身份漂移问题。

StitchEdit: Stitching Depth Priors into Image Editors via Per-Layer Gradient Probing

Text-guided image editing with diffusion models struggles with spatial edits (e.g. manipulating objects, changing viewpoints, manipulating occlusions) that depend on the 3D structure understanding of a scene. Marigold and follow-up work demonstrate that a pretrained diffusion backbone already harbors strong geometric priors that can be activated for depth estimation, yet how to redirect these priors toward editing remains unresolved. Through per-layer gradient probing, we discover that the editing and depth objectives are compatible in early transformer layers but actively conflict in late layers, explaining why naive depth-conditioned editing either ignores geometry or degrades visual quality. We present StitchEdit, which first activates the backbone's geometric capability via a lightweight depth-denoising adapter, then selectively fine-tunes this adapter for editing using a probe-guided per-layer learning rate schedule that preserves depth knowledge where it helps and prunes it where it conflicts. On the proposed StitchEditBench, a benchmark of 150 geometry-dependent edits spanning spatial transformation, occlusion manipulation, and viewpoint change, StitchEdit consistently surpasses both instruction-based and depth-conditioned baselines on perceptual quality and geometric fidelity.

TLDR: StitchEdit introduces a per-layer gradient probing technique to align diffusion models' internal geometric priors with text-guided spatial image editing, enabling improved viewpoint and occlusion manipulation.

TLDR: StitchEdit 提出了一种基于层级梯度探测（per-layer gradient probing）的技术，旨在将扩散模型内部的几何先验与文本引导的图像编辑相结合，从而提升对空间结构、遮挡和视角变化的编辑能力。

PlayGen2: Learning Real-Time Playable Worlds with Explicit 3D State

Building a playable world requires more than generating plausible video: it demands responsive control, persistent spatial structure, and coherent interactions with dynamic entities. Using generated frames as the sole recurrent world representation ties these requirements to view-dependent appearance synthesis, making it difficult to maintain long-term spatial and temporal consistency in generation and interaction. We present PlayGen2, a real-time interactive world model that separates learned 3D state evolution from neural rendering. PlayGen2 maintains a persistent world memory comprising static geometry represented by a volumetric occupancy grid and identity-indexed 3D states for the player and other entities. Within a fixed-size local region, its world-state model jointly learns action-conditioned entity dynamics, geometry completion, and entity discovery, committing updates to a persistent world memory. A separate neural renderer synthesizes first-person observations from depth and semantic projections of the world state, without feeding generated images back into state prediction. Using Doom as a testbed, we demonstrate that the model successfully learns world dynamics including geometry-aware player motion and player--NPC interactions. The framework is computationally efficient, enabling the complete simulation and rendering pipeline to achieve approximately 160ms backend latency on a MacBook Pro with an M4 Pro chip. Beyond single-player interaction, we demonstrate zero-shot deployment on unseen Doom maps, multiplayer interaction through the composition of single-player models, and programmable gameplay through rule-based state interventions. These capabilities demonstrate the transferability and extensibility of the learned game-world model. Code and pretrained model weights are publicly available.

TLDR: PlayGen2 introduces a decoupled architecture that separates 3D state evolution from neural rendering to achieve real-time, persistent, and interactive world generation, overcoming the limitations of purely video-based world models.

TLDR: PlayGen2 提出了一种将 3D 状态演化与神经渲染解耦的架构，实现了实时、持久且可交互的世界生成，克服了纯视频生成模型在空间和时间一致性上的局限性。

Adapters as Data: The Latent Space of LoRAs

LoRA adapters are usually treated as finished artifacts rather than as a data modality. Prior work has shown that LoRA adapters populate a low-dimensional manifold that a linear PCA basis already organizes well enough to support different operations such as sampling, editing, and inversion. We ask what a learned, nonlinear latent adds beyond this. We train a transformer autoencoder on the same population of LoRA identity adapters, mapping each into a latent space and compare every operation against the identical operation applied directly to the raw adapter weights. The latent preserves the capabilities already available in weight space, sampling diverse identities and editing individual attributes. Beyond preservation, we show that the latent is far more robust to displacement than weight space, degrading into distorted but still valid faces rather than texture. Fixed-dimensional weight-space representations cannot natively host adapters of more than one rank, so we extend our sequential autoencoder to hold mixed-rank adapters within one shared latent. This gives a path toward treating adapter weights of varying architecture as a single, general modality rather than one tied to a fixed rank, and points to a learned, nonlinear latent as the mechanism that could carry this generalization further, toward heterogeneous populations of adapters.

TLDR: The paper introduces an autoencoder-based latent space for LoRA adapters, treating them as a unified data modality to enable robust editing, sampling, and the integration of mixed-rank adapters.

TLDR: 该论文提出了一种基于自动编码器的LoRA适配器潜在空间，将适配器视为一种统一的数据模态，从而实现了鲁棒的编辑、采样以及混合秩适配器的集成。

CoGenRec: A Unified Generative Framework for Complementary Vision Reconstruction

Capturing scenes with extreme motion and high dynamic range is fundamentally constrained by trade-offs among temporal resolution, spatial detail, signal-to-noise ratio, and dynamic range under practical bandwidth, power, and quantum-efficiency limits. Conventional frame-based cameras therefore suffer from temporal undersampling, exposure-induced motion blur, and sensor saturation. The Complementary Vision Sensor (CVS) addresses these limitations by decomposing visual information across two complementary pathways, a spatially dense, temporally sparse, long-exposure RGB pathway and a temporally dense, spatially sparse, short-exposure patiotemporal difference pathway. Their complementary measurements provide appearance, structural, and motion cues across a wide dynamic range that enable reconstruction beyond conventional imaging. We present CoGenRec, a complementarity-driven generative reconstruction framework that unifies video deblurring, high-temporal-resolution frame interpolation, and HDR fusion. CoGenRec encodes complementary measurements into multi-scale spatiotemporal features using exposure-aware appearance modeling and bidirectional temporal propagation. These features constrain a video diffusion model and are injected into the VAE decoder to recover fine structures lost during latent-space compression, combining the scene fidelity of physical measurements with the appearance recovery capabilities of learned video priors. On SportsSloMo-CVS, CoGenRec improves PSNR over the strongest general-purpose baseline by 4.40 dB for deblurring, 3.89 dB for frame interpolation, and 5.04 dB for HDR fusion, respectively. It also achieves strong perceptual quality on 129 unseen scenes captured with real CVS. These results establish a unified approach to advance high-quality reconstruction under challenging motion and illumination conditions. Project page: https://cogenrec.github.io/.

TLDR: CoGenRec is a unified generative framework that leverages complementary RGB and spatiotemporal difference pathways, constrained by a video diffusion model, to reconstruct high-fidelity HDR video from challenging conditions.

TLDR: CoGenRec 是一个统一的生成式重建框架，通过结合高分辨率 RGB 图像与时空差分信号，利用视频扩散模型实现对极端运动和 HDR 场景的高质量重建。

Generator-Aligned Reward Modeling with Target-Aware Bounded Optimization for Visual Generation

Post-training aims to enhance desired attributes in visual generative models while preserving their general generation capabilities. Reward-guided post-training faces two challenges: real training data may mismatch the generator's output distribution, and attribute supervision may be confounded by unrelated content. Unbounded reward maximization can also cause overoptimization and degrade generation quality. We propose a framework combining Generator-Aligned Reward Modeling (GARM) and Target-Aware Bounded Optimization (TABO). GARM mitigates distribution mismatch through generator-support matching and reduces content confounding using content-matched real contrasts, yielding generator-relevant, attribute-focused reward supervision. TABO maps the learned reward to a smooth, upper-bounded function calibrated from real target-positive examples. This transformation is independent of the optimization algorithm and supports both direct reward-gradient and policy-based optimization. We further introduce Target-Aware GRPO (TA-GRPO), an adaptation of Flow-GRPO that combines fixed stage-wise advantage scaling, a generator-to-target curriculum, and timestep-balanced credit assignment for flow-based video optimization. Experiments show that GARM improves target-attribute expression while maintaining competitive generation quality. TABO better balances attribute enhancement and visual quality than unconstrained reward maximization, while TA-GRPO improves target enhancement over vanilla Flow-GRPO and reduces unintended attribute leakage on neutral prompts. These results highlight the importance of generator-aligned reward supervision and controlled, data-calibrated optimization for targeted visual generation.

TLDR: This paper introduces a framework combining Generator-Aligned Reward Modeling (GARM) and Target-Aware Bounded Optimization (TABO) to improve attribute control in visual generation while preventing overoptimization and content degradation.

TLDR: 本文提出了一个结合生成器对齐奖励建模（GARM）和目标感知边界优化（TABO）的框架，旨在提升视觉生成中的属性控制能力，同时有效避免过优化和内容质量下降。

Guidance Is a Field, Not a Scalar: Interval-Integrated Classifier-Free Guidance for One-Step Video Generation

One-step generators built on MeanFlow synthesize a video in a single function evaluation, yet inherit a rigid treatment of classifier-free guidance (CFG): the guidance scale is frozen during training or, at best, exposed as a single scalar. This conflicts with mounting evidence that the optimal guidance is not a constant but a field that varies with the noise level, with spatial content, and, for video, with frame position. Every existing remedy presupposes a multi-step sampler along which guidance can be re-scheduled--an assumption one-step models violate by construction. We resolve this tension through a single observation: because the MeanFlow target is an integral over a noise interval, an arbitrary guidance field can be absorbed into the target itself and compiled into one evaluation. We formalize this as the interval-integrated CFG identity, prove that the compiled guidance carries no schedule-discretization error and can thus match or exceed any finite multi-step sampler, and instantiate it as AGAVE, which equips the average-velocity field with a learnable temporal schedule, a free motion-saliency spatial modulation, and a frame-position asymmetry. Adding fewer than ten scalars at zero inference cost, AGAVE surpasses all one-step baselines on VBench, UCF-101 and MSR-VTT while matching multi-step guided sampling. Project repository: https://github.com/YMlinfeng/AGAVE.

TLDR: AGAVE introduces an 'interval-integrated' CFG method for one-step video generation, allowing guidance to be treated as a flexible field rather than a static scalar without increasing inference costs.

TLDR: AGAVE 提出了一种用于单步视频生成的“区间积分”无分类器引导（CFG）方法，将引导处理为灵活的场而非静态标量，且无需增加推理成本。

Training-Free Predictive Spatio-Temporal Token Pruning for Few-Step Autoregressive Video Diffusion

Few-step autoregressive (AR) video diffusion models generate videos chunk by chunk and have made efficient video generation practical. However, further accelerating these models toward real-time generation remains challenging, as few-step distillation leaves little cross-step redundancy for existing training-free caching methods to exploit. We revisit this regime and identify two token-level redundancy patterns: cross-frame redundancy from static content shared across adjacent frames, and cross-step redundancy from tokens that converge early during denoising. Crucially, we find that early clean estimates along the model's own denoising trajectory provide an effective online signal for locating both patterns, requiring no retraining, auxiliary predictor, or reference video. Based on these findings, we propose PreSTP (Predictive Spatio-Temporal Token Pruning), a training-free framework that constructs a unified token-wise redundancy mask from cross-frame similarity and cross-step convergence. Tokens marked as redundant skip subsequent computation under a consistent last-computed-value restoration rule, and a content-adaptive mask reuse strategy further reduces dense probing cost. On 5-second generation, PreSTP achieves 1.52x-3.20x speedups and a better efficiency-fidelity trade-off than coarse-grained caching baselines such as TeaCache and FlowCache. On 30-second generation, it reaches up to 1.96x speedup while improving VBench-Long and suppressing long-horizon drift. Notably, it continues to reduce inference cost even in the extreme one-step regime, where cross-step redundancy vanishes entirely and coarse step-wise caching is no longer applicable.

TLDR: PreSTP is a training-free token pruning framework for autoregressive video diffusion that exploits cross-frame and cross-step redundancy to achieve significant speedups without retraining.

TLDR: PreSTP 是一种用于自回归视频扩散模型的免训练令牌剪枝框架，通过利用跨帧和跨步冗余，在无需重训练的情况下显著提升了推理速度。

Compact Motion Embeddings for Feed-forward Unified Human Animation

Animating a reference image with a driver's motion requires a motion representation that captures face, hand, and body articulation but excludes the driver's appearance. Most body animation methods take motion from external pose estimators and render it with many denoising steps; compact learned motion representations mostly cover the face. We present FUHA, a real-time-capable feed-forward transformer that learns a compact motion embedding of the whole human and uses it for face, upper-body, and full-body animation. A Q-Former compresses the driver's features from a frozen visual encoder into this embedding. To disentangle motion from appearance, a novel contrastive loss pulls it toward the motion embedding of the driver's rendered keypoint image and away from that of the same person in another pose. At inference time, FUHA needs no external pose estimators, keypoints, or parametric body models. As a retrieval key, the motion embedding matches body pose better than the learned global embeddings of ten vision encoders. Its same-person retrieval rate is 0.1%, against 9.5% for its own DINOv3 backbone. On our upper-body benchmark, FUHA outperforms all baselines on every self-reenactment metric and on cross-reenactment identity similarity. It is competitive on face and full-body and runs at up to 38.9 FPS at 512x512 on a single RTX 4090.

TLDR: FUHA is a real-time, feed-forward transformer that learns a compact motion embedding for unified full-body, upper-body, and facial animation without requiring external pose estimation or parametric models.

TLDR: FUHA 提出了一种基于 Transformer 的实时前馈动画框架，通过学习紧凑的全身动作嵌入，无需外部姿态估计或参数化模型即可实现面部、上半身及全身动画生成。

WeLM-Image: A Unified Image Generation Model towards Visual Intelligence

We present \textbf{WeLM-Image}, a unified image foundation model that integrates multimodal understanding into text-to-image generation, instruction-guided editing, and multi-reference synthesis. Designed for single-GPU deployment in WeChat's large-scale consumer applications, the model processes text, reference images, and target latents as one interleaved sequence. A Mixture-of-Transformers (MoT) backbone combines shared attention with independent understanding and generation parameters to limit optimization interference. Equipping each path with Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) modules provides sparse capacity, with 31.7B stored parameters and approximately 5.4B generation parameters activated per target latent token. A generation-oriented data engine combines a knowledge-grounded visual vocabulary with large-scale visual clustering to balance semantic coverage and editing tasks, while training-time evaluation guides iterative data refinement. Bilingual annotations and verified editing supervision support progressive training up to native 1.5K resolution. We optimize generation and editing separately with rubric- and checklist-based rewards, then consolidate the specialized policies through noise-aware on-policy distillation. In the reported overall comparisons, the 1536-resolution model ranks first among the evaluated open-source models on PRISM-Bench, Qwen-Image-Bench, and UnicBench. MeanFlow-based few-step distillation yields WeLM-Image-Turbo, which generates a $1024\times1024$ image in 4.98\,s on a single NVIDIA H20 GPU using eight guidance-free steps.

TLDR: WeLM-Image is a unified 31.7B parameter multimodal model using a Mixture-of-Transformers (MoT) and Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) architecture to balance text-to-image generation, instruction-based editing, and multi-reference synthesis on single-GPU deployments.

TLDR: WeLM-Image 是一个统一的多模态大模型，利用混合专家架构 (MoE) 和混合 Transformer (MoT) 实现单 GPU 部署，在文本生成图像、指令编辑及多参考图像合成方面表现出色。

ArtiVidRM: Verifiable Artifact-as-Rubric Reward Modeling for Explicit Quality Articulation in Video Generation

Reinforcement learning has become an effective approach for improving video generation, but its success depends heavily on reliable reward models. Existing methods either map videos directly to opaque scalar scores or generate free-form rationales that are not explicitly optimized as the basis of the final judgment. We propose ArtiVidRM, a verifiable, artifact-as-rubrics video reward model that follows an observe-then-score paradigm. It first identifies visible generation artifacts and organizes them into a structured artifact list, then uses these artifacts as explicit rubrics for quality assessment. This intermediate representation grounds each score in concrete video-level evidence, making the reward more interpretable and informative for optimization. We further introduce a multi-reward reinforcement learning strategy to jointly optimize artifact prediction and quality assessment. The reward decomposes into complementary objectives that evaluate artifact prediction quality, including semantic coverage and prediction efficiency, together with a pairwise preference objective that supervises quality ranking through score differences. By jointly optimizing the intermediate artifact representation and the downstream preference prediction, the model learns to produce informative artifact lists while improving the accuracy of video quality assessment. Experiments show that ArtiVidRM achieves state-of-the-art performance on video preference benchmarks, provides effective signals for video diffusion optimization, and enables artifact-guided refinement of closed-source video generators.

TLDR: ArtiVidRM introduces an 'observe-then-score' reward model for video generation that uses structured artifact identification as explicit rubrics to improve the interpretability and performance of preference-based RL optimization.

TLDR: ArtiVidRM 提出了一种用于视频生成的“先观察后评分”奖励模型，通过将检测到的生成伪影作为显式评估标准，提高了强化学习优化过程的可解释性和效果。
