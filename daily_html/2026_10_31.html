Latent Reasoning in Continuous Space for Unified Multimodal Models

Unified Multimodal Models (UMMs) achieve remarkable success on diverse multimodal understanding and generation tasks, yet they still struggle to effectively leverage language-based chain-of-thought reasoning for image generation. As a result, existing reasoning-augmented UMMs often fail to inherit the test-time scaling and fine-grained controllability observed in large language models. We attribute this failure to a fundamental modality mismatch: reasoning is performed in a discrete token space, whereas image generation operates in a continuous space. To bridge this gap, we introduce a Latent Reasoning framework in Continuous space (LARC), which alleviates the token-level bottleneck by reasoning over continuous hidden states. Specifically, the training framework consists of two stages: (i) curriculum supervised fine-tuning (SFT), and (ii) information-gain reinforcement learning (RL). First, the curriculum SFT stage gradually converts token-level reasoning into latent reasoning. Subsequently, the information-gain RL stage self-evolves latent reasoning without ground truth supervision. Experiments across diverse text-to-image generation benchmarks show that LARC consistently improves generation quality, prompt following, and compositional fidelity. Notably, LARC achieves state-of-the-art performance on GenEval with a 7%p gain over BAGEL and demonstrates clear test-time scaling behavior, outperforming token-based generation under both parallel and sequential test-time scaling strategies.

TLDR: LARC addresses the modality mismatch between discrete token-based reasoning and continuous image generation by introducing a latent space reasoning framework that enables test-time scaling and improved compositional fidelity in UMMs.

A Better Critic Matters for Distribution Matching Distillation

Distribution Matching Distillation (DMD) trains a few-step student using the difference between a teacher's score and an online critic's estimate of the student distribution's score. We investigate what the critic needs, in capacity and in information, to provide a reliable training signal. On the capacity side, controlled experiments across seven SiT model sizes on ImageNet show that increasing teacher size while keeping a small critic fixed can degrade student quality and cause training collapse. Scaling the critic alongside the teacher, a strategy we call joint scaling, prevents collapse in our experiments and allows the student to benefit from larger teachers. On the information side, rather than forcing the critic to model a step-agnostic mixture of student outputs, we introduce a step-aware critic (STAC) that conditions on the student's generation step to estimate step-specific scores. Together, joint scaling and STAC consistently improve few-step distillation across class-conditional ImageNet generation, text-to-image generation with SDXL and FLUX.2, and text-to-video generation with Wan2.1.

TLDR: This paper improves Distribution Matching Distillation (DMD) by introducing 'joint scaling' of the critic and a 'step-aware' critic (STAC), significantly enhancing few-step generation performance across image and video models.

Learning Clean Generative Processes from Noisy Measurements

We study how to learn a generative model of clean images using only noisy observations. Diffusion processes and their generalizations to non-Gaussian noise require posterior predictions at every noise level. We first show that it is possible to learn these predictions from independent noisy views using Noise2Noise regression, but only at or above the noise level of the training data. To learn the predictions below that level, needed to generate clean images, we impose consistency: a prediction should equal its expectation after continuing the sampling process. We derive conditions under which consistency uniquely determines the continuation to the clean image distribution and verify them for Poisson, binomial, Gamma, and negative-binomial measurements. Experiments on CIFAR-10, photon-counting microscopy, and synthetic aperture radar show that the required posterior predictions can be learned from noisy views alone and that the consistency relation repairs the continuation beyond the noise level of the data, which severely deteriorates without consistency.

TLDR: This paper introduces a method for learning clean generative models directly from noisy data by leveraging Noise2Noise regression and a novel consistency constraint that enables extrapolation beyond the noise level of the training observations.

TLDR: 本文提出了一种仅利用噪声观测数据学习清晰图像生成模型的方法，通过结合 Noise2Noise 回归和一种新颖的一致性约束，实现了从噪声数据向无噪声生成过程的推断。

Any Forcing: Any-Step Generality and One-Step Stability for Autoregressive Video Generation

Autoregressive video generation enables efficient inference through streaming prediction. However, existing methods are typically restricted to fixed sampling steps, limiting the flexibility to balance generation quality and efficiency, while one-step generation suffers from degraded visual quality and error accumulation over long sequences, leading to content drift and generation collapse. To address these issues, we propose Any Forcing, a unified framework for achieving Any-Step Generality and One-Step Stability. Specifically, we introduce a unified adversarial discriminator that jointly performs real-distribution alignment, inter-step consistency alignment, and first-frame quality enhancement, enabling flexible sampling while preserving one-step generation quality. Furthermore, we propose a training-free adaptive long-term memory selection mechanism that selects historical states based on global semantic relevance and local value contribution, while remapping their temporal positions to preserve temporal relationships within the memory cache, thereby enabling stable one-step long-video generation. Experiments demonstrate that Any Forcing achieves high-quality and stable one-step autoregressive video generation, achieving 20.9 FPS and 24.7 FPS for short and long video generation on a single H100 GPU, respectively.

TLDR: Any Forcing is a unified framework for autoregressive video generation that enables flexible sampling steps and stable one-step generation via an adversarial discriminator and an adaptive long-term memory mechanism.

TLDR: Any Forcing 是一个统一的自回归视频生成框架，通过引入对抗性判别器和自适应长期记忆机制，实现了任意步数的灵活性及稳定的一步生成效果。

GeoFlip: Benchmarking Geometry-Sensitive Physics in Video Generation

Video generation models synthesize realistic motion, but can they respond correctly to changes in initial geometry? We introduce GeoFlip, a video generation benchmark centered on physical outcome flips induced by geometry. With shared physical settings and textual prompts held fixed, GeoFlip applies controlled interventions to support extent, object placement, and mechanism configuration, constructing paired scenes with contrasting reference events, such as remaining stable versus falling or tipping. These pairs test whether models translate geometric differences in the first frame into correct physical evolution. The benchmark comprises 482 scene pairs and 964 reference videos across six physical categories, with reference outcomes established through procedural scene construction, physics simulation, and numerical validation and verification of physical events. We evaluate 16 models for image to video generation along three dimensions: event correctness, structural integrity, and similarity to reference videos. We examine evaluation consistency with independent vision language judges. On the 442 conditions shared by all models, the highest event correctness averaged across tasks is 27.98%, revealing substantial limitations in physical generation conditioned on geometry. Model strengths vary across physical mechanisms, and the three evaluation dimensions capture distinct capabilities. Typical failures include ignoring geometric changes, generating incorrect object motion, bending rigid objects, and introducing additional components. Rank correlations between judges reach 0.906 for event correctness and 0.929 for structural integrity. By combining controlled geometric interventions, verifiable physical references, and multidimensional evaluation, GeoFlip assesses progress from visual realism toward physical consistency conditioned on geometry.

TLDR: GeoFlip is a new benchmark designed to evaluate how well video generation models maintain physical consistency when geometric conditions in the initial frame are altered.

TLDR: GeoFlip 是一个旨在评估视频生成模型在初始帧几何条件改变时，能否保持物理一致性的基准测试。

MoETok: Sparse Expert Routing for 1D Visual Tokenizers

Modern visual tokenizers aim to preserve fine-grained visual details while learning high-level semantic representations for downstream generation and understanding. Query-based 1D visual tokenizers encode images into flexible latent sequences, yet how individual learnable queries gather and encode image information remains poorly understood. We reveal that these queries spontaneously develop complementary roles under semantic supervision: a small set of Content queries captures high-level semantic information, while a larger set of Detail queries preserves fine-grained visual details for reconstruction. To adapt token processing to these heterogeneous roles, we introduce MoETok, a 1D visual tokenizer that routes tokens through sparsely activated experts. Jointly optimized with reconstruction and semantic objectives, the router learns role-specialized processing paths adapted to each token's representation. Across model scales and token budgets, MoETok consistently improves reconstruction, semantic representation, and autoregressive generation over dense tokenizers under matched supervision and active computation. On ImageNet, it combines strong reconstruction and semantic representation with state-of-the-art class-conditional generation within the 1D tokenizer family. We further validate MoETok at scale in a unified autoregressive model jointly trained for understanding and generation with a next-token cross-entropy objective. The resulting model combines competitive visual understanding with state-of-the-art generation among unified discrete models, matching or surpassing diffusion-based models such as BAGEL and Qwen-Image on benchmarks including DPG-Bench (87.61), GenEval2 (40.04 GM), and WISE (0.49).

TLDR: MoETok introduces a sparse expert routing mechanism for 1D visual tokenizers, dynamically assigning processing paths to semantic 'Content' queries and reconstruction-focused 'Detail' queries to improve generative and understanding performance.

TLDR: MoETok 提出了一种用于一维视觉分词器的稀疏专家路由机制，通过动态分配处理路径来区分语义查询和细节重建查询，从而提升了视觉生成与理解任务的性能。

Scout-Refine-Correct: Teaching Flow Models to Refine Their Own Samples

The quality of image generation models trained with diffusion or flow matching is largely fixed by the training budget: at inference, the only available lever is the number of sampling steps, and additional steps improve quality only up to a point. We present a training recipe, Scout-Refine-Correct (SRC), that instead gives a flow-matching generator a genuine inference-time refinement capability. At each sampling step the model takes a scout rollout -- $K$ steps ahead along its own trajectory -- extrapolates a full-sample estimate, and feeds that estimate back to itself as a self-conditioning signal before taking the real step. This can be repeated for $R$ refinement rounds. Two correction losses, applied during training at states the model's own rollouts actually reach rather than only on the ground-truth path, teach the model to use this signal to reach new samples instead of collapsing to a suboptimal one. On ImageNet-256, SRC improves LightningDiT-B from FID~14.5 to FID~7.26 at NFE~550, matching a privileged best-of-$N$ baseline that uses an oracle FID verifier to choose among samples. SRC uses no external verifier, no reward signal, and, after ablation, almost no extra parameters over the base architecture. Combining SRC with best-of-$N$ improves the result further, to FID~3.81 at NFE~4400, showing that the two axes are complementary. We ablate the main design choices -- the architectural pathway for the scout signal, the scout horizon, each correction loss, and the training schedule to demonstrate key working components of training.

TLDR: Scout-Refine-Correct (SRC) is a training framework for flow-matching models that enables iterative inference-time refinement by teaching the model to use self-generated 'scout' trajectories to improve sample quality without external verifiers.

TLDR: Scout-Refine-Correct (SRC) 是一种流匹配模型训练框架，通过引导模型利用自身生成的轨迹进行推理时修正，从而在无需外部验证器的情况下显著提升图像生成质量。

From Evaluation to Evolution: Benchmarking Image Editing and Evolving Image Models

Real-world image editing requires coordinating multiple visual transformations with content preservation and reference constraints, yet aggregate scores can readily obscure unmet local requirements. Effective evaluation must therefore identify local failures and translate them into priorities for subsequent model improvement. We introduce WeGenEditBench, comprising 2,740 evaluation instances and 148 task types across single-image, text-intensive, and multi-reference editing. Pre-generated checklists shared across models decompose each request into atomic requirements; item-level deductions and visual evidence are established first and then summarized into scores for edit execution, preservation, visual quality, and task-specific correctness. To support the experience-acquisition component of recursive self-improvement (RSI), we develop WeGenEvoStudio, a unified multi-agent harness for RSI-oriented learning that integrates model-conditioned failure diagnosis, retrieval-grounded data construction, supervised adaptation, and re-evaluation. Among 15 evaluated configurations, GPT-Image-2 leads single-image editing with an Overall score of 4.076, while Seedream-5.0-Pro leads text-intensive editing at 4.291. Evolution experiments across four generation and editing benchmarks demonstrate substantial targeted gains: on their report-selected Top-5 single-image targets, FLUX.2 [dev] improves Overall from 1.083 to 3.517, while Qwen-Image-Edit-2511 gains 13.2%. Together, our benchmark and harness provide a foundation for developing more reliable image models through interpretable evaluation and feedback-driven learning.

TLDR: The paper introduces WeGenEditBench for fine-grained image editing evaluation and WeGenEvoStudio, a multi-agent framework designed to drive recursive self-improvement in image models via failure diagnosis and iterative adaptation.

RADI3N: GeometRic ImAge EDiting via ParametrIc 3D CoNtrol

Recent diffusion-based methods have substantially advanced image editing, yet parametric 3D control at the part level remains challenging. Existing approaches either operate on whole objects with manual rigging, or revert to 2D handles at the part level. They also struggle to model appearance and texture under large transformations, leading to unrealistic outputs in geometry-intensive edits. To address these limitations, we introduce RADI3N, a framework for semantically-grounded part-aware object manipulation with parametric 3D control. Our CS-ARAP deformation module suppresses rotation-field discontinuities at part boundaries via neighborhood covariance smoothing, producing structurally consistent deformations under complex part-level edits. We further introduce an inversion-free selective texture transfer mechanism that suppresses high-frequency positional biases and scales up low-frequency semantic cues within cross-image attention. This eliminates strict spatial bias and supports semantic matching, allowing source appearance to follow the edited part onto its new geometry. Experiments demonstrate that RADI3N achieves state-of-the-art performance in geometric fidelity, viewpoint consistency, and controllability.

TLDR: RADI3N is a diffusion-based image editing framework that enables part-aware, parametric 3D control through a novel CS-ARAP deformation module and an inversion-free selective texture transfer mechanism.

SketchDirector: Complex Scene Sketching via Agentic Layout Reasoning and Detail Refinement

Existing sketch generation methods often rely on image guidance and are largely limited to individual objects or simplified scenes, leaving text-guided complex scene sketching underexplored. We present SketchDirector, an agentic framework that mimics the human sketching workflow for automatic multi-concept scene sketching. Given a scene-level text prompt, SketchDirector first employs a large language model~(LLM) to decompose the scene into multiple draft assets, where each is represented by a set of Bézier curves. Then, an agentic reasoning loop iteratively plans and executes their spatial arrangement, guided by self-evolving memory. Finally, a detail refinement module leverages diffusion priors to enhance sketch quality while preserving geometric structure. To support future research and applications in multi-concept scene sketch generation, we further introduce the \textit{MCSketch8K} dataset, comprising editable vector assets, compositional layouts, and object-level manipulation annotations. Extensive experiments show that SketchDirector outperforms state-of-the-art baselines in semantic and compositional quality while effectively preserving overall contour organization during refinement. MATS: A Multi-Agent Framework for Controllable and Reflective Tumor Synthesis

Synthesizing realistic tumors on medical images helps address data scarcity for clinical AI. Existing diffusion-based methods, however, lack fine-grained attribute-level controllability, closed-loop quality verification, and awareness of which morphological patterns are underrepresented in the target distribution. We present MATS, a multi-agent framework for text-controllable, cross-organ tumor synthesis. Given a target disease, MATS first profiles its morphological distribution over a fine-grained attribute space covering tumor geometry and appearance, and constructs a generation plan that amplifies underrepresented subtypes while keeping common ones dominant. It then localizes the target organ, proposes anatomically valid placement, and executes two-stage diffusion to produce the tumor mask and render texture, each conditioned on the corresponding attributes. A closed-loop reflective mechanism verifies attribute alignment at three checkpoints throughout synthesis and provides targeted feedback to guide re-generation when misalignment is detected. Trained exclusively on a single-source pancreatic neuroendocrine tumor dataset, MATS consistently improves downstream segmentation both in-domain and across LiTS, KiTS, and MSD-Pancreas benchmarks, whereas most prior generative augmentation methods fall below real-only training.

TLDR: MATS is a multi-agent framework that uses attribute-level profiling, closed-loop reflective verification, and two-stage diffusion to synthesize controllable and anatomically plausible tumors, outperforming existing generative augmentation methods.

TLDR: MATS 是一个多智能体框架，利用属性级分析、闭环反射式验证和两阶段扩散模型，实现了可控且解剖学合理的肿瘤合成，在多项基准测试中优于现有的生成式增强方法。

TADP: Dual-Branch Temporally Adaptive Differential Privacy for Expression-Aware Video Anonymization

We present TADP, a temporally adaptive differential privacy (DP) framework for inference-time privacy-preserving video generation under fixed-length clip-replacement adjacency. TADP uses a dual-branch design to extract identity and expression representations, clips and privatizes both branches with temporally adaptive Gaussian mechanisms whose noise scales depend only on preceding privatized releases within the same clip and are bounded below by history-uniform calibrated minima, and accounts for their joint release via adaptive Rényi Differential Privacy (RDP) composition. The clip-level RDP guarantee is converted to $(\varepsilon,δ)$-differential privacy, while soft quantization and subsequent temporal processing are applied only after privatization as DP post-processing. Experiments on facial expression datasets show reduced identity leakage under evaluated attacks, usable perceptual quality and temporal stability, and high facial expression recognition utility relative to the evaluated privacy-preserving baselines. TADP formally satisfies clip-level DP and empirically provides a favorable privacy--utility trade-off under the evaluated threat models and datasets.

TLDR: TADP is a dual-branch, temporally adaptive differential privacy framework designed to preserve facial expressions in video anonymization while maintaining identity privacy through clip-level Gaussian noise mechanisms.

TLDR: TADP 是一种用于视频去隐私化的双分支、时间自适应差分隐私框架，通过基于剪辑的差分隐私机制，在保护身份隐私的同时保留面部表情。

Coarse3D: Controllable and Faithful Sparse Structure Generation for Image-to-3D

Coarse-to-fine 3D-native generation relies heavily on the first-stage sparse structure, which determines the global orientation and overall geometry for subsequent refinement. However, existing coarse generators suffer from two key limitations: their orientation is difficult to control due to ambiguous semantic canonicalization, and their structures are often inconsistent with input images due to weak 2D-3D grounding. We propose Coarse3D, a sparse-structure generation model that produces orientation-controllable and structurally faithful voxel geometry from arbitrary unposed images. Coarse3D introduces three designs: a first-frame anchored canonical space that aligns generation with the first input view while preserving object-centric normalization; a dual-tower DiT that couples generation with auxiliary feed-forward reconstruction to learn spatially grounded features; and spatial-prior guided attention that regularizes cross-attention with pixel-to-voxel correspondence. Coarse3D can directly replace the coarse stage of existing pipelines. Experiments show that it substantially improves sparse structure quality and consistently enhances final 3D generation, achieving state-of-the-art performance.

TLDR: Coarse3D introduces a controllable and faithful sparse structure generation model for Image-to-3D, utilizing anchored canonical space and spatial-prior guided attention to solve common orientation and grounding issues.

VoxLution: Evolving Voxels for High-Fidelity 3D Scene Generation

High-resolution 3D indoor scene generation is crucial for immersive applications such as virtual reality, spatial computing, and robotics simulation. We introduce VoxLution, a coarse-to-fine transformer-based approach that generates high-fidelity indoor scenes with rich details, overcoming the overly smooth geometry typical of existing generative models. First, we generate a low-resolution spatial layout; then, based on this structural information, we progressively refine the scene geometry locally in chunks. Key to our approach is formulating 3D super-resolution as a sequence modeling problem over multi-resolution sparse voxel grids. To achieve this, we introduce a training-free, resolution-invariant tokenization scheme that compactly encodes the geometric information of eight octants into a single decimal number. We formulate the transformer sequence to include multiple resolutions, allowing the coarse layout to guide the generation of fine local details. To ensure coherence across local chunks, we incorporate adjacent chunk boundary information directly into the transformer sequencing. VoxLution achieves scene generation at a finer voxel resolution than existing baselines, demonstrating state-of-the-art performance on standard benchmarks with 59.6% lower FPD and 51.1% lower FID on 3D-FRONT, and 70.5% lower FPD and 73.8% lower FID on Scannet++.

TLDR: VoxLution is a coarse-to-fine transformer model that generates high-fidelity 3D indoor scenes by treating 3D super-resolution as a sequence modeling task with a novel, resolution-invariant tokenization scheme.
