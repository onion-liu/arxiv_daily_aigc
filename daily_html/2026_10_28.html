Know, then Draw: A New Learning Paradigm for Visual Generation

Visual generation requires both high-level semantic knowledge and fine-grained pixel details. We propose a learning paradigm that assigns these roles to autoregressive pretraining for knowledge acquisition and diffusion post-training for high-fidelity rendering. Within this framework, we study the objective, representation, and scaling of knowledge acquisition and rendering. Controlled comparisons identify autoregression as the stronger pretraining objective for downstream rendering, improving both learning efficiency and final generation quality. We further find that AR-only generation quality does not necessarily correlate with final performance; semantic quality matters more. This motivates a purely semantic tokenizer trained with language supervision alone, without an image decoder or reconstruction loss. We establish compute-optimal scaling laws for knowledge acquisition and rendering, and find that autoregressive pretraining lowers the fitted asymptotic rendering loss compared with pixel diffusion models trained from scratch. Guided by the laws, we train a 3B-parameter text-to-image model with only 6,600 H100 GPU-hours, achieving 86 on GenEval and 87 on DPG-Bench and validating the proposed paradigm at scale.

TLDR: The paper introduces a two-stage learning paradigm, 'Know, then Draw,' utilizing autoregressive pretraining for semantic knowledge acquisition followed by diffusion for high-fidelity rendering, achieving efficient scaling and strong generation benchmarks.

TLDR: 该论文提出了一种“先认知，后绘制”（Know, then Draw）的学习范式，通过自回归预训练进行语义知识获取，再由扩散模型进行高保真渲染，在实现高效扩展的同时取得了优秀的生成效果。

ReferStream: Interactive Subject Injection for Streaming Video Generation

Injecting new subjects naturally during streaming video generation requires integrating newly supplied visual references into the existing video while preserving established subjects and the scene. Encoding references independently of the current scene provides little guidance for this integration, while keeping their features in persistent memory can interfere with established context. We propose ReferStream, a framework that requires no additional training and combines reference construction using the current scene with transient cache injection. Joint Latent places reference latents in target regions of the latest scene latent, allowing reference features to be extracted in context. Cache Restoration uses these features to condition the injection chunk, then restores the overwritten global cache entries while retaining the newly generated history. This procedure incorporates new visual conditions without retraining the generator or revising emitted frames. We further introduce ReferStream Bench, a new benchmark with scheduled reference arrivals, evolving instructions, and explicit annotations of subject presence for InShot and MultiShot generation. Evaluation follows the event schedule and measures both the fidelity of new subjects and the preservation of established subjects, alongside video quality, text adherence, and count accuracy. Experiments on ReferStream Bench show higher scores for text adherence, subject similarity, and count accuracy than the compared baselines, including offline methods given all references in advance. Project page: https://xushuolin.github.io/ReferStream/.

TLDR: ReferStream is a training-free framework for streaming video generation that enables dynamic subject injection by using joint latent construction and transient cache restoration, outperforming existing offline methods.

TLDR: ReferStream 是一个无需训练的流式视频生成框架，通过联合潜在空间构建和瞬态缓存恢复技术，实现了视频流中动态的主体注入，且表现优于现有的离线方法。

GroundControl: Satellite-Conditioned Multi-View Diffusion for Reconstruction-Free Novel View Synthesis

Multi-view or video diffusion models have recently emerged as state-of-the-art approaches for novel-view synthesis from sparse image collections. While they can produce high quality images, they tend to hallucinate unrealistic spatial layouts when synthesizing views outside the input views (scene extrapolation). We propose to address this challenge for outdoor scenes by using satellite images as an additional information source to condition the multi-view generative model. We introduce a new satellite-conditioned multi-view diffusion model that combines conditioning from a satellite image and street-level input images when generating novel-views. Our cross-attention based conditioning allows us to retain pretrained multi-view diffusion priors to generate seamless, realistic, novel views from a handful of source views, while also adhering to the scene layout as seen from satellite images. Our model does not use 3D scene reconstruction, and only relies on posed street and satellite views. Our experiments on outdoor datasets show that our model is better than both satellite-conditioned or street-view conditioned baselines on novel-view synthesis, especially on challenging cases where target views have very little overlap with source views. Project page: https://nianticspatial.github.io/groundcontrol

TLDR: GroundControl is a novel multi-view diffusion model that integrates satellite imagery as conditioning to improve the spatial consistency of novel-view synthesis for outdoor scenes without requiring 3D reconstruction.

TLDR: GroundControl 提出了一种结合卫星图像作为条件的新型多视图扩散模型，旨在无需 3D 重建的情况下，提升室外场景视角合成的空间一致性。

ALADDIN: Adversarial Latent Diffusion Distillation for One-Step Identity Swapping

Face swapping transfers a source identity onto a target image while preserving the target's pose, expression, hair, and lighting. The most realistic current methods are diffusion-based, but they need many network evaluations per image. GAN-based methods are fast, yet trained from scratch they must learn realism from the adversarial signal alone and produce visibly weaker results. We present ALADDIN (Adversarial Latent Diffusion Distillation for One-Step Identity Swapping), a one-step face swapper obtained by initialising the generator from a pre-trained multi-step flow-matching model and training it adversarially under identity and target-preservation constraints. We expose a controllable trade-off between identity transfer and target-attribute preservation by training a student model with triplet pseudo-label supervision. We additionally identify a bias in the standard evaluation protocol, which favours methods trained against the same identity encoder used to score them, and re-evaluate all baselines with an independent one. ALADDIN is state-of-the-art among one-step methods in realism and target preservation at comparable identity transfer, and matches the best multi-step model with 28 times fewer network evaluations and an order of magnitude fewer training pairs.

TLDR: ALADDIN achieves high-fidelity, one-step face swapping by distilling a pre-trained flow-matching model using adversarial training, offering SOTA performance with significantly reduced computational cost.

TLDR: ALADDIN 通过对预训练流匹配模型进行对抗蒸馏，实现了高质量的单步人脸替换，在大幅降低计算成本的同时达到了同类方法的最优水平。

GameVerse: A Minute-Scale Gameplay Dataset for Long-Horizon Interactive World Modeling

Recent advances in interactive world models have made it possible to generate controllable, dynamic environments from action inputs. Yet current models struggle to maintain coherence beyond roughly one minute of rollout, a limitation rooted not in model capacity but in the scarcity of long-horizon training data. Existing gameplay video datasets typically consist of clips under ten seconds, with captions that describe whole clips in coarse and undifferentiated text, which is insufficient for controllable long-horizon generation. We address this with GameVerse, a minute-scale gameplay video dataset purpose-built for long-horizon interactive world modeling. GameVerse contains 69.7K clips spanning 1,655 hours and 349.6 million frames across diverse first- and third-person gameplay, including 1,358 hours of continuous two-minute sequences, an order of magnitude longer than prior datasets. Clips pass through a unified pipeline of scene-consistent segmentation, quality filtering, camera pose estimation, and event-based caption annotation. Each clip is paired with a temporally stable camera trajectory that remains coherent across minutes, together with captions that separate static scene context from dynamic foreground events and align to event boundaries rather than fixed time windows. Comprehensive analysis shows that GameVerse surpasses existing public datasets in scale, diversity, and annotation quality. Experiments further demonstrate that training on GameVerse substantially extends the temporal horizon of interactive world models.

TLDR: GameVerse is a large-scale, minute-long gameplay dataset designed to overcome the temporal incoherence of current interactive world models by providing high-quality, temporally stable, and event-annotated long-horizon data.

TLDR: GameVerse 是一个大规模、分钟级的游戏数据集，旨在通过提供高质量、时间上稳定且带有事件标注的长程数据，解决当前交互式世界模型在长序列生成中一致性不足的问题。

A First-Step Bias Restores Lost Diversity in Diffusion Model Distillation

Distribution-matching distillation enables high-quality image generation in only a few denoising steps. However, distilled student models often map different initial noise samples to similar outputs under the same prompt. To locate where diversity is lost, we compare the teacher and student along the sampling trajectory. The largest measured reduction in the response to different initial noises occurs during the first, high-noise step. When we replace the first step with the teacher's corresponding trajectory and leave the final three student steps unchanged, most of the output diversity returns. This suggests that the first step is a key point for restoring diversity. Motivated by this finding, we introduce \emph{ReSeed}, a post-distillation method that adds a seed-response bias adapter only to the first student step and aligns its velocity with the teacher's first-interval secant velocity. All student weights and later denoising steps remain unchanged. We evaluate ReSeed on three text-to-image models (SD3-Medium, SD3.5-Medium, and FLUX.1-dev) and one text-to-video model (Wan2.1-T2V-1.3B). For a distilled 4-step FLUX.1-dev model, \emph{ReSeed} requires only $0.00196\%$ additional trainable parameters relative to the base model and is trained on 8,000 prompt--noise pairs, while keeping the distilled student fully frozen and requiring no target images, perceptual losses, or adversarial losses. It improves DINO diversity by $48.3\%$ while maintaining competitive visual quality. These results show that substantial diversity can be recovered through a lightweight, data-efficient tuning of only the first denoising step, without finetuning the full student model.

TLDR: ReSeed is a lightweight post-distillation technique that restores image and video generation diversity by applying a small, trainable bias adapter exclusively to the first denoising step of pre-trained student models.

TLDR: ReSeed 是一种轻量级的后蒸馏技术，通过仅对扩散模型第一步去噪过程添加一个小型的可训练偏置适配器，有效恢复了图像和视频生成的多样性。

Visual Autoregressive Generation with Next Token Imagination

The space of possible visual content is enormous, and arguably unbounded. Any training set, however large, therefore covers only a tiny fraction of it. Yet generative models are typically trained to fit only the images they are given. In visual autoregressive (AR) generation, for example, each token is predicted from the preceding ground-truth (GT) tokens and supervised solely by the GT next token. Confining learning to observed data in this way limits how much of the visual distribution a model can capture. In this work, we hypothesize that letting a model imagine beyond its training data, by exploring plausible visual realizations it has never observed, can ease this limitation. We use visual AR generation as a proof of concept, since its fixed, discrete token space makes imagination easier to steer and constrain. We propose Next Token Imagination (NTI), a method that allows the model to explore beyond the GT next token, subject to a carefully designed criterion that keeps this imagination bounded by the underlying visual distribution. Extensive experiments on ImageNet show that NTI substantially and consistently improves the fidelity of generated images, approaching state-of-the-art diffusion and AR models while using a smaller model and lower training cost.

TLDR: The paper introduces 'Next Token Imagination' (NTI), a novel autoregressive generation technique that allows models to explore plausible, unobserved visual tokens during training to improve generation fidelity and efficiency.

TLDR: 本文提出了一种名为“Next Token Imagination”（NTI）的自回归生成方法，通过在训练过程中引导模型探索未观测到的合理视觉令牌，从而提升生成图像的质量与训练效率。

Hy-Nomas: Towards Free-form Interactive World Generation

Interactive world generation requires models to follow successive commands while preserving relevant history and action consequences. We present Hy-Nomas, a video world model with an 8B diffusion backbone that supports free-form text instructions and continuous camera control across multiple interaction turns. It generates variable-length segments conditioned on current inputs and cached history. Our central design aligns structured video captions with action-aware causal attention: descriptive context remains accessible across segments, while action instructions are restricted to their intended temporal windows. A vision-language Prompt Enhancer (PE) translates user requests and multimodal interaction histories into structured conditions for the next segment. A multi-stage curriculum progressively introduces causal generation, text interaction, camera control, and training on model-generated histories, followed by diffusion distillation. In human evaluation, Hy-Nomas shows superior performance over existing world models. We release model weights, code and benchmarks, together with a lightweight adaptation workflow, to support reproducible research on interactive world generation.

TLDR: Hy-Nomas is an 8B diffusion-based video world model that supports free-form text instructions and continuous camera control by using structured causal attention to manage long-term history and temporal action instructions.

TLDR: Hy-Nomas 是一个基于 8B 扩散模型的视频世界模型，通过结构化因果注意力机制管理长期历史和时序动作指令，从而实现自由文本指令交互与连续相机控制。

Look Only When Needed: Certificate-Guided Visual Routing for Efficient Diffusion VLMs

Diffusion vision-language models (DVLMs) process their entire input at every denoising step. Since visual tokens make up 89-97% of this input on the benchmarks we study, most of the decoding computation is spent repeatedly processing the same image. Visual token pruning methods partially relieve this burden by removing part of the visual tokens, but they lose the removed information and still process the remaining image at every step. To measure how often the image is actually needed, we compare LLaDA-V's predictions with and without the image at every decoding step and find that removing the image changes a token being unmasked at only 22.8% of steps on average and at as few as 7.9%. We therefore propose LOWN, a decoding method that decides at every step whether to process the image and how many tokens to unmask. LOWN predicts the masked tokens without the image and processes the image only when this prediction disagrees with the last image-conditioned prediction. Agreement certifies the earlier prediction for reuse, and the number of certified tokens sets how many are unmasked in that step. We further observe that the need for the image differs across benchmarks, so LOWN's adaptive decision is needed rather than a fixed schedule. Without training or a tuned threshold, LOWN retains 99.6% of LLaDA-V's score on ten benchmarks at 35.3% of the decoding FLOPs on average. LOWN also generalizes across LLaDA-based DVLMs, composes orthogonally with visual token pruning, and runs under key-value caching for further efficiency gains. Code and project page are available at https://hyeon9698.github.io/LOWN/

TLDR: LOWN is an efficient decoding method for Diffusion VLMs that dynamically decides when to process visual input based on prediction agreement, significantly reducing FLOPs without requiring retraining.

TLDR: LOWN 是一种针对扩散视觉语言模型的动态推理方法，通过判断模型是否需要视觉输入来减少计算冗余，在不进行重训练的情况下显著降低了浮点运算量并保持了生成性能。

Haste Makes Waste? Towards Safe Few-Step Text-to-Image Models

While few-step distilled text-to-image models have gained traction due to their fast generation times, the speed-up implications on their unsafe generation rates remain unknown. We uncover an inherent tension between speed and safety: few-step models are often more unsafe than their teachers, and reducing the number of inference steps consistently raises unsafe generation rates. We further find that acceleration makes unsafe concepts harder to erase in few-step models, with existing concept-erasure methods markedly less effective than in teachers. We offer a mechanistic diagnosis for why erasure methods fail on few-step models, finding that the fragility of few-step models' generation process and the way generation guidance is usually distilled into those models may hinder established concept-erasure methods. To address the speed-safety trade-off, we propose DICE (Distillation-Integrated Concept Erasure), a post-distillation erasure method tailored for few-step models. DICE aligns both its latent construction and its generation-preservation loss with the distillation method that produced the model, keeping erasure faithful to few-step models. DICE demonstrates strong results in our evaluation. For example, on FLUX Schnell, DICE reduces unsafe-generation rate to 0.046 (vs. at least 0.161 for prior erasure methods we test), while mostly preserving benign generation quality.

TLDR: The paper identifies an inherent trade-off between inference speed and safety in distilled text-to-image models and introduces DICE, a tailored concept-erasure method that maintains safety in few-step models.

TLDR: 本文揭示了蒸馏后的少步文生图模型在加速的同时会引发安全性下降的问题，并提出了DICE方法，通过针对性的概念擦除技术有效解决了这一速度与安全性的权衡。

Image Modeling: From Handcrafted Priors to Foundation Image Models

This paper unifies six decades of image modeling, evolving from handcrafted mathematical tractable priors to solve inverse problems in imaging to data-driven deep foundation image models for both image generation and solving inverse problems, under a common Bayesian framework that formulates image modeling as an implicit or explicit probabilistic characterization of a data manifold. Every image restoration or generative task fundamentally seeks to answer the same question: What makes an image look like a real image? In the Bayesian framework, this question is encapsulated by the prior probability density p(x), or its more optimization-friendly counterpart, the log-prior log p(x). While early works in solving inverse problems, such as Tikhonov, Total Variation (TV), and Non-local priors relied on explicit handcrafted definitions of log prior to model local smoothness, modern techniques, such as Plug-and-Play (PnP) priors and Denoising Diffusion models, are rooted in algorithmic uncoupling and implicit manifold learning that apply to both inverse problems and conditional image generation. By grounding imaging inverse problems and conditional image generation under a single mathematical engine: the gradient of the log-prior, or the Stein Score Function, we bridge the gap between signal processing and machine learning communities, turning what seems like parallel developments into a single, evolution of image modeling from handcrafted analytical models to models learned from big data, demonstrating that the pursuit of the denoising score is the common thread connecting variational optimization (in MAP estimation) to posterior sampling (in modern foundation image models).

TLDR: This paper provides a unified Bayesian framework for image modeling, connecting six decades of research—from handcrafted priors to modern diffusion models—through the lens of the Stein Score Function.

TLDR: 本文提出了一个统一的贝叶斯框架，通过Stein得分函数将过去六十年的图像建模研究（从手工先验到现代扩散模型）有机结合在一起。

FrameForge: Harnessing Priors of Video Foundation Models for Unified Image Generation

Unified conditional image generation requires a single model to preserve different aspects of a visual input while producing a target image that follows textual guidance. Pretrained video models provide a promising foundation by encoding visual transformations across time, yet existing approaches typically assign the condition and target to fixed temporal positions. We observe that condition-target relationships vary substantially across generation tasks, while video models naturally encode visual transformations at different temporal scales. This motivates us to model conditional generation through input-adaptive virtual temporal relations. We propose FrameForge, which captures two complementary aspects of such relations. Mixture of Temporal Experts (MoTE) models how far the target lies from the condition by dynamically selecting temporal RoPE experts with different virtual intervals. Chain of Temporal Reasoning (CoTR) models how the condition is transformed into the target through a latent intermediate state, with a reasoning-bottleneck attention mask encouraging information propagation through this pathway. Experiments on DreamBench, COCO2017, and GEdit-Bench show that FrameForge achieves superior performance in subject-driven generation, canny-to-image generation, and image editing among the compared methods.

TLDR: FrameForge leverages pretrained video models for unified image generation by introducing input-adaptive temporal relations via Mixture of Temporal Experts (MoTE) and Chain of Temporal Reasoning (CoTR).

TLDR: FrameForge 利用预训练视频模型进行统一图像生成，通过“时间专家混合”（MoTE）和“时间推理链”（CoTR）引入了输入自适应的时间关系建模方法。

Nemotron-Labs-Diffusion-Uni: Elastic Self-Refining Unified Diffusion LLM with Cross-Paradigm On-Policy Distillation

We propose Nemotron-Labs-Diffusion-Uni (NLD-Uni), a unified discrete diffusion large language model (DLLM) for visual understanding and generation tasks. Compared with prior unified diffusion models, NLD-Uni introduces two major innovations. First, we propose a unified cross-paradigm on-policy distillation (OPD) framework that successfully transfers the visual understanding ability of autoregressive VLMs and visual generation ability of continuous flow models, to a unified DLLM. Second, NLD-Uni incorporates a novel progressive self-refining diffusion process that gradually corrupts each token across multiple steps during training, as opposed to adopting the conventional masked diffusion formulation that directly corrupts a clean image token to an uninformative mask token. At inference, this design allows NLD-Uni to make intermediate commit to a token, enabling progressive self-refinement for better visual quality. Through extensive experiments, we show that NLD-Uni achieves state-of-the-art performance among unified DLLMs on diverse tasks including visual question-answering, multi-modal reasoning, object grounding, text-to-image generation, and image editing.

TLDR: NLD-Uni is a unified discrete diffusion LLM that leverages cross-paradigm on-policy distillation and a progressive self-refining diffusion process to bridge visual understanding and high-quality generation.

TLDR: NLD-Uni 是一种统一的离散扩散大语言模型，通过跨范式策略蒸馏和渐进式自精炼扩散过程，实现了视觉理解与高质量生成能力的统一。

LociArt: A Unified Paradigm for Free-Style Local Video Stylization

Video stylization typically transforms entire scenes, while local artistic editing requires precise region--style association, spatial control, and temporal consistency. We introduce LociArt, a unified paradigm for free-style local video stylization that explicitly specifies where to stylize and what style to apply while preserving non-target content. At its core, GRNStyle builds on Generative Refinement Networks (GRN) and introduces a polar style-aware mask that embeds style identities as equal-norm directions within selected regions. Complementary representation--geometry conditioning combines encoded mask features with raw-mask spatial support, integrating spatiotemporal context with geometric control. To provide paired supervision, we develop an automated pipeline combining global stylization, video object removal, matting, and compositing, and construct the LociArt-100K Dataset with 100K local stylization pairs. We further establish the LociArt Benchmark, comprising 80 source videos and 84 target masks covering 168 foreground/background stylization cases, with a Style Prototype Similarity (SPS) evaluation scheme for regional style quality and complementary measures of non-target preservation and temporal consistency. Experiments show that GRNStyle outperforms existing methods in regional style quality and spatial control while maintaining strong temporal consistency. Qualitative examples further illustrate joint stylization of multiple regions with the same or different learned styles, despite training only on single-region, single-style pairs. Together, these components provide a unified foundation for learning and evaluating controllable local video stylization. Project page: https://higherhu.github.io/lociart/.

TLDR: LociArt is a unified framework for precise, local video stylization that uses polar style-aware masks and a geometry-conditioned refinement network to achieve high-quality regional artistic editing while preserving background integrity.

SpeeDiff: Scalable Pixel-Anchored End-to-End Latent Diffusion Model

We present Scalable Pixel-anchored End-to-end Diffusion (SpeeDiff), a latent diffusion method that jointly trains the VAE and the diffusion model from scratch. In principle, joint training allows the diffusion loss gradient to directly guide the VAE encoder, encouraging the formation of a generation-friendly latent space and potentially yielding faster convergence than the conventional two-stage approach with a pretrained frozen VAE. However, a naive end-to-end implementation severely degrades performance, as unrestricted backpropagation of the diffusion loss leads to latent space collapse. Our main technical contribution is a simple yet effective Tweedie Pixel Reconstruction (TPR) loss, which provides additional pixel-level feedback by decoding a predicted clean latent from an intermediate noisy state using Tweedie's formula, thereby alleviating collapse. Furthermore, our method enables jointly scaling a fully transformer-based architecture and enhances representation alignment within the end-to-end framework. Our SpeeDiff-XL model achieves over 140x and 61x faster training compared to Vanilla SiT and REPA, respectively, while attaining an FID of 1.50 without guidance on ImageNet 256x256 generation. With a more efficient 32x compressed VAE, our model further reaches an FID of 1.53 without guidance on ImageNet 512x512 generation.

TLDR: SpeeDiff is an end-to-end latent diffusion model that jointly trains the VAE and transformer-based diffusion backbone using a novel Tweedie Pixel Reconstruction (TPR) loss to prevent latent collapse and achieve superior training efficiency.
