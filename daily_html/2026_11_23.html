Mass Forcing: On-Policy Mass-Aware Distillation for Autoregressive Video Generation

Distribution Matching Distillation (DMD) has emerged as a promising paradigm for real-time video synthesis, distilling pretrained diffusion models into few-step autoregressive generators. However, its reverse-KL objective is mode-seeking and can collapse the student's conditional video distribution onto a narrow subset of modes, leading to reduced sample diversity, diminished temporal dynamics, and color oversaturation. Existing methods mitigate this issue mainly via coverage-oriented student initialization, but we observe that such coverage is transient: across three initializations, the fraction of dynamic videos drops sharply during subsequent DMD training, indicating that the mode-seeking objective, not the initialization, drives this degradation. We propose Mass Forcing, a probability-mass-aware framework that moves on-policy DMD supervision from whole videos to individual states. Each update branches several continuations from a shared student-generated prefix, making the student's probability mass at that state measurable against the teacher. First, Mass Forcing estimates relative teacher-to-student density ratios among these continuations through latent-space score integration and uses them to rebalance conditional DMD updates, amplifying rollouts underrepresented relative to the teacher. Second, since mismatched mode probabilities do not necessarily induce strong local score corrections, it aligns generated rollout sets with a reweighted reference distribution defined by the same mass estimates, providing cross-rollout supervision. Experiments on MovieGen and VBench show that Mass Forcing improves video fidelity and sustains temporal dynamics throughout training, achieving the highest Total scores among compared methods and a Dynamic Degree of 83% after 1,000 generator updates, versus 12-32% for the baselines, with no additional inference-time overhead.

TLDR: Mass Forcing introduces an on-policy, probability-mass-aware distillation framework that prevents mode collapse in autoregressive video generation by rebalancing conditional updates based on teacher-to-student density ratios.

TLDR: Mass Forcing 提出了一种基于概率质量感知的自回归视频生成蒸馏框架，通过利用教师模型与学生模型的密度比重构条件更新，有效解决了视频生成中的模态崩塌和动态缺失问题。

Align the Pair, Not the Sample: Representation Alignment for Faster and Better One-Step Generato

Representation alignment markedly accelerates diffusion training, but one-step generators raise a question that diffusion does not: \emph{which training path should receive the loss?} A one-step generator sees two kinds of inputs. Corrupted real images come with their clean counterpart, which provides an instance-paired target. Samples generated from pure noise have no corresponding real image, yet this is where the final model operates. We study this question on Priming, which trains one-step generators from scratch by reconstructing corrupted images and then matching feature distributions with optimal transport. Aligning the generative path, the natural choice, collapses recall ($0.45\!\to\!0.15$--$0.25$) in all 17 configurations we test, including five target constructions and loss weights down to $0.05$. Aligning the paired reconstruction path with a frozen DINOv2 encoder instead helps in every setting we test. A short analysis explains this asymmetry: when the target carries no instance information about the input, the optimal aligned feature is constant within each class, so the loss pulls samples toward class prototypes. A control confirms this: aligning the paired path to a random same-class image instead of the input's own image removes most of the gain. Inside the generator, paired-path alignment creates an interface block through which post-training routes noise, and the image semantics carried through it predict sample quality across 16 models. The resulting recipe, \method, accelerates \emph{both} training stages on ImageNet $256\!\times\!256$: it matches the 160-epoch baseline with $2\times$ fewer priming epochs, surpasses its final quality with $4\times$ fewer post-training updates, and lowers FID to 1.30 (B) and 1.155 (L) at the standard budget. A joint single-stage variant trains native one-step generators and reaches FID 1.22 with 115M parameters.

TLDR: This paper addresses training instability in one-step generative models by identifying that aligning generative paths leads to class-prototype collapse, and proposes a solution that aligns paired reconstruction paths instead, achieving state-of-the-art efficiency and performance.

Seeing Through Preferences: Recovering Hidden Local Preference Neighborhoods for Text-to-Image Alignment

Aligning text-to-image (T2I) diffusion models with human preferences typically relies on large-scale pairwise annotations, which are expensive to obtain and provide only sparse supervision. Moreover, not all preference comparisons contribute equally to learning: comparisons between semantically distant images often reveal little about the fine-grained perceptual factors underlying human judgments. We argue that informative supervision can arise from local preference neighborhoods surrounding human-preferred images, where semantically aligned alternatives expose meaningful perceptual and structural differences. Motivated by this observation, we propose PrefLens, a framework that recovers local preference neighborhoods from sparse human feedback. Specifically, PrefLens generates controlled perceptual variations around preferred images that largely retain semantic identity and selects compact sets of informative neighbors in a reward-defined preference space induced by pretrained reward models. By transforming each human preference annotation into a structured neighborhood of informative comparisons, PrefLens enriches supervision without requiring additional human labeling. Our experiments and diagnostic evaluations show that the recovered neighborhoods capture human-relevant preference characteristics and provide effective supervision for preference alignment across diverse settings.

TLDR: PrefLens is a framework that enhances T2I model alignment by synthesizing informative local preference neighborhoods from sparse human feedback, enriching supervision without additional manual labeling.

HorizonFlow: Few-Step Video Distillation in 600 Iterations

Distribution matching distillation (DMD) enables fast video generation by distilling a multi-step teacher into a few-step student. However, DMD-based methods often suffer from unnatural motion and costly training. We trace these limitations to the DMD gradient, which jointly optimizes appearance, layout, and motion without distinguishing their learning dynamics. Its static component, which carries appearance and layout, has a much higher signal-to-noise ratio than its motion component. As a result, per-frame quality converges within a few hundred iterations, whereas motion lags far behind. Beyond being noisy, the gradient also lacks two signals. (1) Video VAEs encode several frames into one latent frame (four in Wan2.1), and the gradient provides little guidance for spreading motion over these frames, causing periodic stutter. (2) DMD does not constrain the final sampling step to preserve the layout set by earlier steps, so objects merge and drift once the video moves. We propose HorizonFlow, which decouples the correction of motion from that of layout. It keeps the DMD loss and only shifts its regression target, with one training-only correction in the motion component and one in the orthogonal layout component. Chunk Evenness addresses (1) by spreading the motion of each latent chunk evenly over its frames, and Semantic Commitment addresses (2) by anchoring the coarse layout to the teacher's completion of the student's last step. In only 600 iterations, HorizonFlow distills Wan2.1-T2V-1.3B into a 4-step model using 8.4k training samples, 4.6-304$\times$ fewer than prior work. On VBench, HorizonFlow outperforms all released few-step Wan2.1-T2V-1.3B models. The same recipe transfers to Cosmos-Predict2.5-2B, where our student outperforms NVIDIA's distilled model. Code: https://github.com/XIANGLONGGUO/HorizonFlow-repo

TLDR: HorizonFlow introduces a decoupled distillation strategy for video generation that separates motion and layout learning, enabling efficient 4-step model training in just 600 iterations.

TLDR: HorizonFlow 提出了一种视频生成模型的解耦蒸馏策略，通过分离运动与布局的学习，仅需 600 次迭代即可实现高效的 4 步模型训练。

MSBench: Benchmarking Script-Controlled Multi-Shot Text-to-Video Generation

Text-to-video (T2V) models can now generate visually plausible short clips, but multi-shot generation requires a different set of skills: composing multiple shots with intended cut timing, transition style, camera language, and cross-shot continuity. How far this multi-shot output can be controlled from text remains unmeasured: existing benchmarks either evaluate single shots or, when they provide shot-level scripts, typically check whether the requested shots appear but not when they change or how they are joined. We introduce MSBench, a benchmark for controllable text-driven multi-shot video generation built around structured Scene-Entity-Shot mini-scripts that specify each shot's time span, camera state, content, and outgoing transition. MSBench defines a four-dimensional hybrid evaluation framework spanning Visual Quality, Dynamic Quality, Semantic Alignment, and Cross-Shot Consistency with 11 sub-metrics. Experiments on 14 commercial and open-source systems show that visual quality does little to separate the systems, whereas multi-shot control does: many models cut mainly on scene changes and render requested gradual transitions as hard cuts. Human evaluations further validate MSBench, with its system ranking reaching a Spearman correlation of 0.87 with human preferences. The project page is available at https://totalhuang.github.io/MSBench-page/.

TLDR: MSBench is a new benchmark designed to evaluate text-to-video models on multi-shot generation, focusing on temporal control, transitions, and cross-shot consistency beyond simple single-shot quality.

TLDR: MSBench 是一个评估多镜头文本生成视频能力的基准测试，旨在解决现有模型在镜头切换、转场效果和跨镜头连贯性控制方面缺乏定量评价的问题。

PrimiTok: Learning to Diversify and Allocate Primitives for Visual Tokenization

Unifying visual understanding and generation requires representations that preserve both semantic information and fine-grained image details. Continuous tokenizers trained primarily for semantic alignment may omit fine-grained reconstruction details, whereas discrete tokenizers face information loss from codebook quantization. Conventional multi-codebook designs assign every token the same number of codewords regardless of local content. We instead view codewords as reusable visual primitives whose allocation and composition adapt to each token. We introduce PrimiTok, a compositional visual tokenizer that represents each visual token through adaptive allocation and weighted composition of learned codebook primitives. PrimiTok integrates two complementary components: Diverse Quantitative Primitives (DQP), which encourages diversity among sub-codebook centroids to reduce redundancy, and a Dynamic Primitive Allocator (DPA), which selects primitives according to token complexity. Embedding quantization error supervises how many primitives are selected from each sub-codebook, and DPA aggregates them with input-dependent weights. Joint reconstruction and image--text contrastive learning encourage the resulting representations to retain visual fidelity and semantic alignment. Experiments demonstrate improved reconstruction and semantic alignment over SOTA baselines, with further evaluations on multimodal understanding and visual generation.

TLDR: PrimiTok introduces a compositional visual tokenizer that replaces fixed-codebook approaches with adaptive, weighted primitives, enhancing both reconstruction fidelity and semantic alignment for multimodal tasks.

TLDR: PrimiTok 提出了一种组合式视觉分词器，通过自适应分配和加权组合视觉基元（primitives）替代了传统的固定码本方案，从而在视觉重建质量与语义对齐能力上取得了双重提升。

Robust Concept Erasure in Text-to-Image Models via Simulating Brain Functional Localization

Concept erasure in text-to-image models aims to prevent a target concept from appearing in generated images while preserving general image generation capability. However, erased concepts can still be reactivated by attacks. Prior attacks mainly search text or image input spaces, leaving the initial latent space underexplored. To address this gap, we introduce IVO (Initial latent Variable Optimization), a new concept reactivation attack that changes the starting point of image generation by optimizing the initial latent. Comprehensive experiments across 8 erasure methods show that IVO reactivates erased concepts more effectively than the attack baselines, revealing a blind spot in erasure robustness evaluation. This motivates us to localize model components associated with the target concept and then treat them as intervention sites for more robust concept erasure. We propose CeFlune (Concept Erasure via Functional Localization of Unit Neurons), which adapts brain functional localization to text-to-image models. CeFlune defines linear modules as Unit Neurons and localizes concept-responsive ones by comparing their response trajectories in the model's task and resting states. It then updates the localized Unit Neurons to constrain their responses and the erased model's predictions. Across U-Net diffusion and flow-matching transformer architectures, CeFlune achieves a favorable balance between concept erasure and general image generation capability in comparisons with up to 15 baseline methods. Under IVO and 4 other evaluated attacks, CeFlune also shows lower concept reactivation.

TLDR: The paper introduces a novel attack method (IVO) for concept reactivation in text-to-image models and proposes CeFlune, a robust erasure technique inspired by brain functional localization to improve model safety.

TLDR: 本文提出了一种针对文生图模型概念遗忘的攻击方法（IVO），并基于大脑功能定位启发设计了CeFlune算法，旨在提高模型概念遗忘的鲁棒性。

PhyCoVE: Benchmarking Physical Counterfactual Video Editing across Diverse Material Dynamics

Physical counterfactual video editing turns a source video and a language-specified physical edit at a given execution frame into a counterfactual video that shows how the scene would evolve after the edit. Existing evaluations with counterfactual target videos mainly cover object removal or specific rigid-body motions, although real scenes also involve elastic deformation, fluid flow, and interactions among rigid bodies, elastic solids, and fluids. We introduce PhyCoVE, a benchmark of 533 cases on 110 source videos that covers rigid, elastic, and fluid dynamics in five interaction patterns, with edits that remove an object, change a physical quantity, or insert a new object. For the simulated cases, a language model proposes scene designs, coding agents build them with solvers suited to each material and physically based rendering and then simulate each edit from the source scene state, and humans check every stage. For the real cases, we record continuously from one camera position before and after each edit. We also propose counterfactual fidelity, a metric that applies across materials and their interactions and measures how much of the physical change from the source video to the target video a generated video reproduces. We evaluate seven open-source and commercial video editing models and find that counterfactual fidelity ranks them nearly as human preference does, but even the best model reproduces only about one seventh of the physical change. The models change how a scene looks but rarely how it evolves, whereas a physical edit requires understanding the dynamics that it changes. Physical counterfactual video editing thus remains far from solved, and PhyCoVE provides a foundation for developing and evaluating methods for it.

TLDR: PhyCoVE introduces a new benchmark for physical counterfactual video editing, covering rigid, elastic, and fluid dynamics to assess how well generative models understand and simulate physical scene evolution.

TLDR: PhyCoVE 引入了一个物理反事实视频编辑基准，涵盖了刚体、弹性体和流体动力学，旨在评估生成模型对物理场景演化过程的理解与模拟能力。

Supply the Global, Supervise the Local: Combining Representation Conditioning and Alignment in Diffusion Transformers

A pretrained image representation helps train a diffusion transformer in several roles, including as an alignment target, which the model's hidden states learn to match, as in representation alignment (REPA), and as a condition, an extra input describing the image to be generated. Combined, they would give the model an input and a target, but whether they conflict is unknown. To test this, we let the condition carry only part of REPA's target: we split the representation of each image patch into an image-level (global) part, shared by all patches of the image, and a within-image (local) part, the patch's difference from the image-level part, and use one token carrying the image-level part as the condition. Then, the two roles overlap on the image-level part, where they could conflict, while only REPA supervises the within-image part, where we can see whether the token helps REPA. We find that the token and REPA together teach both the image-level part and the within-image part, the main source of REPA's FID improvement, far more than the sum of what each teaches alone, so they cooperate rather than conflict. These findings give one design rule for combining REPA with an image-level condition, supply the global, supervise the local, which we implement as Global-Condition REPA (GC-REPA): REPA with the token as an extra input; the token carries no information beyond REPA's target and is drawn at sampling from a separately trained token prior. GC-REPA greatly accelerates convergence: on ImageNet 256*256, SiT-XL/2 + GC-REPA reaches REPA's 4M- and SiT-XL/2's 7M-iteration FIDs within 200K and 100K iterations (20 and 70 fewer).

TLDR: The paper introduces GC-REPA, a method that improves Diffusion Transformers by combining global-level conditioning and local-level representation alignment, leading to significantly faster convergence and better performance.

TLDR: 该论文提出了 GC-REPA 方法，通过结合全局条件控制和局部表征对齐，显著提升了扩散 Transformer 的训练收敛速度和图像生成质量。

Single-Transformer Latent Diffusion

Latent generative models typically rely on three separate networks: an encoder that tokenizes pixels, a generator that models the latent distribution, and a decoder that maps latents back to pixels. UNITE recently merged the first two into a single generative encoder trained in one stage, but its decoder remains a separate network. We ask whether this last network can be folded in too, so that one diffusion transformer encodes, generates, and decodes. Simply letting the shared backbone decode does not help. What makes it work is to cast all three modes as position-aligned sequence maps over one fully shared backbone, distinguished only by a mode token and the timestep. The resulting model, UNIFIED, is simple: one network trained from scratch in a single stage with only reconstruction and flow-matching losses, without adversarial losses or pretrained encoders (e.g., DINO). On ImageNet-1K 256x256 at matched parameters, UNIFIED improves gFID over UNITE at three sizes (~60M, ~115M, and ~215M) and reaches 2.08 with 215M parameters, the lowest among compared models under 250M parameters. On text-to-image generation, UNIFIED-B (218M) reaches a GenEval score of 0.87 at 512x512, exceeding single-stage pixel diffusion models with around one billion parameters. Full unification of latent diffusion is thus not merely feasible but beneficial.

TLDR: The paper introduces UNIFIED, a single-backbone Transformer that integrates encoding, generation, and decoding into one model using position-aligned sequence maps, achieving state-of-the-art performance for its parameter size without external pre-trained components.

TLDR: 本文提出了 UNIFIED 模型，通过将编码、生成和解码任务统一在一个单一的 Transformer 主干网络中，利用位置对齐的序列映射实现端到端训练，在同等参数量下取得了卓越的生成性能。

Demystifying Classifier-Free Guidance for Auto-Regressive Image Generation

Classifier-free guidance (CFG) has been widely adopted in autoregressive (AR) models for high-quality image generation. Despite its strong empirical performance, its mechanism in AR models remains unclear. This paper demystifies the mechanism of CFG through both empirical and theoretical studies. By examining the top-ranked semantics of different components in CFG, we show that texture information is primarily encoded in the difference between the conditional and unconditional logits, whereas both logits share previous-token repetition as their leading semantics. This strong repetition semantics obscures the desired texture information, causing greedy decoding from the conditional logits alone to produce nearly pure-color images. Through a training-dynamics analysis of shallow transformers, we prove that this shared repetition semantics does not arise from limitations or failures of pretraining, but instead originates from the texture sparsity of images. We further show that CFG improves generation quality by rectifying this repetition bias during inference. Motivated by the shared semantics between conditional and unconditional logits, we propose Attention Weight Reuse (AttnReuse), which reuses intermediate attention computations from the conditional-logit forward pass to accelerate the unconditional-logit computation. AttnReuse reduces about $25\%$ of attention computation with little performance degradation across different models.

TLDR: This paper investigates why Classifier-Free Guidance (CFG) succeeds in autoregressive image generation, identifying that it corrects a shared 'repetition bias' caused by texture sparsity, and proposes an efficient 'AttnReuse' method to accelerate inference.

TLDR: 该论文深入探讨了分类器自由引导（CFG）在自回归图像生成中的作用机制，指出其通过纠正纹理稀疏性带来的重复偏差来提升质量，并提出了能够降低约25%注意力计算开销的AttnReuse加速方法。

Seeing vs. Knowing: Counterfactual Supervision and Correction for 3D-Aware Object Editing

Rotating, translating, or rescaling an object in a photograph requires revealing unseen surfaces, preserving visible appearance, and updating shadows and reflections. Reconstructed 3D assets, rendered at the target pose as a 3D state, provide explicit geometric guidance, but their imperfect textures may conflict with the photograph, while standard paired training does not explicitly teach the editor which source to trust. To address this, we propose counterfactual state supervision, which constructs controlled photo--state conflicts through hidden-surface repainting, hidden geometry with re-rendered shadows and reflections, and visible-surface texture replacement. This teaches the editor to follow the 3D state for unseen content while preserving photograph-visible appearance. However, learning this distinction does not ensure that it is maintained during sampling. We therefore introduce symmetric counterfactual correction, which uses state-side and photo-side twins, which share the photograph and the state respectively, to construct directed latent perturbations toward condition-inconsistent targets and train recovery toward the target specified by the current condition. Both strategies operate only during training, requiring no architectural changes or additional inference steps. Experiments on OBJect and SpatialEdit-Bench demonstrate strong spatial editing performance and content preservation, while controlled ablations show improved source selection and sustained responsiveness to conditioning during sampling.

TLDR: This paper introduces counterfactual supervision and symmetric correction to train 3D-aware image editors to balance geometric guidance from 3D assets with original visual fidelity, enabling better consistency during object manipulation.

Ego-HMB: Human Motion Bridging from Egocentric Images via A Motion Bridge Diffusion Model

We introduce Egocentric Human Motion Bridging, a task that generates a coherent 3D human motion sequence from two endpoint egocentric images. Unlike conventional motion in-betweening, which requires structured 3D endpoint poses, this setting uses raw first-person observations available from wearable devices in VR/AR and robotics. It presents challenges due to viewpoint variations, partial body visibility, and temporal ambiguity under cross-modal endpoint constraints. To address these challenges, we propose Ego-HMB, a unified diffusion-based framework that learns endpoint pose estimation and temporally consistent motion infilling within a single model through two training stages. First, Egocentric-to-Canonical Pretraining uses a hierarchical curriculum with progressively increasing temporal spans to strengthen endpoint pose priors. Second, Motion Bridge Diffusion Training incorporates endpoint and physics awareness, diffusing from neighboring poses to promote temporal continuity while preserving global endpoint constraints. Extensive experiments compare our approach with one-stage methods that directly generate motion from two egocentric images and two-stage pipelines that combine endpoint pose estimation with motion interpolation. Ego-HMB consistently outperforms these baselines in endpoint accuracy and motion generation quality. Code is available at https://github.com/gaogehan/Ego-HMB.

TLDR: Ego-HMB is a diffusion-based framework that generates coherent 3D human motion sequences from two raw egocentric endpoint images, bridging the gap between VR/AR observations and motion synthesis.

MIMORPHEUS: Rethinking Cross-Modal Supervision for Human Pose Estimation from Radar Signals

Deep learning has shown promise for estimating human pose with multiple-input multiple-output (MIMO) radar through walls. Building paired radar and pose datasets demands considerable time and manual effort, while large visual pose datasets provide no corresponding radar echoes. Physical simulation can supply synthetic signals, but requires expertise in scattering models and can leave a gap between simulated and real data. To address this challenge, we introduce MIMORPHEUS, a framework that generates radar echoes from 3D human poses through conditional diffusion. To prepare echo and pose representations, MIMO-VAE compresses the echoes according to their channel and fast time sample structure, while SkeleCoder learns pose features through contrastive alignment with echo features. These pose features then guide diffusion in the MIMO-VAE latent space, and the VAE decoder reconstructs echoes from the generated latents. Once trained, the generator augments radar data by producing different echoes for a recorded pose and supplies the radar modality for pose annotations in existing visual datasets. In data augmentation experiments, our method reduces MPJPE by 6.8%-11.2% across four radar pose estimators. Transfer experiments with three public pose datasets also show that visual pose annotations can supply useful radar training data. Code will be made publicly available on GitHub.

TLDR: MIMORPHEUS is a diffusion-based framework that enables cross-modal synthesis by mapping 3D human poses to MIMO radar signals, effectively bridging the gap between visual pose data and radar-based estimation.

Training-Free 3D Editing via Feature-Divergence Localization and Trajectory Correction

Modifying 3D assets requires enacting targeted structural transformations while strictly preserving the geometric identity of unedited regions. While recent probability flow models enable training-free editing, bounding these modifications within a high-dimensional latent space presents severe mathematical challenges. Current inversion-free trajectories rely on global velocity updates, which often cause spatial bleed into preserved structures and induce trajectory under-commitment, causing edits to stall mid-path. To resolve these dual failures, we introduce FocusFlow, a segmentation-free framework that structures the integration trajectory into two disjoint phases. During early coarse structure formation, FocusFlow applies Feature-Divergence Localization (FDL) to spatially gate the velocity field using intrinsic cross-attention. Once coarse structure has formed, FocusFlow applies Reference Generation Guidance (RGG), a trajectory-correction term that drives convergence to the target shape by pulling the trajectory toward an explicit clean-latent target. Evaluated on the joint Google Scanned Objects and PartObjaverse-Tiny benchmarks, FocusFlow obtains the best preservation scores and the largest edit magnitude among compared methods, suggesting that it can reduce the traditional preservation-modification trade-off. By providing stable, localized geometric control without manual spatial annotations, this phase-scheduled approach lowers technical barriers to the modification of synthetic 3D media.

TLDR: FocusFlow introduces a training-free 3D editing framework that uses feature-divergence localization and trajectory correction to enable targeted structural edits while preserving geometric integrity.

T2ISpec: Benchmarking Speculative Decoding for Text-to-Image Generation

Text-to-image (T2I) generators produce detailed images but remain slow at inference. Speculative decoding can reduce this cost, yet its behavior across the rendering requirements of T2I prompts remains insufficiently understood. We introduce T2ISpec, a benchmark of 1,400 prompts balanced across seven rendering topics and two difficulty levels. It evaluates autoregressive and diffusion/flow accelerators by latency, work performed by the target model, and image quality. Our study reveals three key findings: (1) aggregate results hide large differences between topics, (2) alignment and preference scores can disagree with prompt constraint satisfaction, and (3) large reductions in target work can yield limited latency gains. Motivated by these findings, we propose TopicRoute, a plugin that combines prompt routing with speculative and cache controls without retraining the generator. Across six model--method pairs, it achieves a geometric mean speedup of 1.0308x over the native methods, and the speedup carries over to external prompts with the same settings, while quality gains and losses vary across configurations.

TLDR: The paper introduces T2ISpec, a benchmark for evaluating speculative decoding in text-to-image models, and proposes TopicRoute, a routing plugin that optimizes inference speed based on prompt topics without requiring retraining.

Where Does Personalization Reside in Photo Editing? Evidence from One Photographer's Lightroom History

Personalized photo-editing systems must infer both a photographer's recurring preferences and the intent of a particular shoot, yet these signals are rarely evaluated separately. We study interpretable Lightroom adjustments in a longitudinal, private dataset of 3,193 edited photographs from 41 catalogs by one professional photographer, with holdouts formed at the batch level. We compare absolute and prior-relative targets, image-conditioned predictors, and estimators that receive a small set of edits from the target batch. Residual prediction improves validation error, but its advantage on held-out batches is small and not significant under a batch-clustered bootstrap (0.592 vs 0.582 EV exposure MAE). In a batch-shared protocol, closed-form shrinkage using 4, 8, or 16 same-batch anchors reaches 0.466, 0.457, or 0.453 EV with the best estimator at each k, compared with 0.586-0.592 EV for the prior-only baseline; the paired reductions are significant under a batch-clustered bootstrap. Learned anchor-conditioned heads do not improve on this closed-form estimator, and a negative-trained prior already yields a low cold-image false-edit rate, leaving little benefit for an additional gate. The recommended estimator is closed-form and auditable, and it requires only a handful of same-batch edits. A render-space check against 383 native Lightroom exports reproduces the ranking: at k=8, online EB lowers Delta E00 against the prior by 0.41 (p=0.042), and the photographer's true parameters lower it by 0.78 (p=0.034). An appendix extends the action space from six to thirteen global controls: a same-batch median look lowers rendered chroma toward the export, while a per-photograph residual head again fails to transfer. The study is limited to one photographer and does not claim that slider accuracy measures aesthetic preference.

TLDR: This study analyzes a professional photographer's Lightroom history to isolate recurring stylistic preferences from shoot-specific intent, finding that simple, closed-form shrinkage estimators using a few batch anchors outperform complex learned predictors.
