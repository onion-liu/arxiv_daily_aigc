VibeVideoBench:Evaluating Multimodal Agents on Code-Driven Video Generation

Diffusion video models produce striking clips, but they cannot reliably render an exact formula, a correct chart, a ruleconsistent game or narration synchronized to on-screen events. A different route is emerging: multimodal agents that write code to render every frame and synthesize every sound. We introduce VibeVideoBench, a benchmark for evaluating agents on code-driven video generation. Its 239 self-contained tasks in English and Chinese span 12 categories, from data stories and music-driven pieces to classroom lessons and paper explainers; each asks an agent, running in its native harness inside a sandbox, to deliver a finished MP4 with sound within one hour. Evaluation is hierarchical and evidence-based: program checks of the delivery spec, about 13k task-specific rubric checks traced to the requirements they enforce, and five quality dimensions anchored per task, judged by three VLM judge panels and validated with blind human preferences. Across nine frontier agent systems and 2,151 scored rollouts, the benchmark is far from solved: GPT-6 Astra and Claude Opus 5.5 lead clearly, yet even the best system fails a core requirement on about a quarter of tasks. Whether a video arrives at all matters as much as its quality, and three quarters of the lost videos are final renders started too late, run too slowly or never started. The judges agree with each other and with human preferences on system rankings, but only moderately on individual videos.

TLDR: VibeVideoBench is a new benchmark designed to evaluate multimodal agents on code-driven video generation, addressing the limitations of diffusion models in rendering precise, rule-consistent, and synchronized video content.

TLDR: VibeVideoBench 是一个旨在评估多模态智能体代码驱动视频生成能力的基准测试，旨在解决扩散模型在渲染精确、符合规则和同步视频内容方面的局限性。

VidEdit3D: Flexible 3D Gaussian Editing with Video Diffusion

Recent advancements in text-guided 3D editing, particularly with 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS), have shown promise but still lag behind the flexibility of 2D editing tools. Existing methods face a critical trade-off between preserving multi-view consistency and allowing structural modifications. Image-based editors can also struggle to apply the intended edit to objects viewed from unusual angles. To address these limitations, we introduce VidEdit3D, a framework that reformulates 3D scene editing as a trajectory-based video editing task. By drawing a parallel between camera movement in static 3D scenes and temporal dynamics in video, we leverage the robust spatiotemporal priors of pre-trained Video Diffusion Models (VDMs) to encourage semantic and structural coherence across views. Specifically, we propose Trajectory-Guided Rendering (TGR) to select informative keyframes and construct smooth video clips aligned with VDM input requirements. To propagate edits from an anchor view to these clips, we develop Flow-Aligned Video Editing (FAVE), a training-free framework incorporating an Editing Consistency Guidance (ECG) mechanism. ECG contrasts source and edited image conditions at a shared latent state, using scheduled source-image amplification to strengthen adherence to the edited reference while maintaining original scene context. Finally, we introduce Region-Selective 3DGS Refinement (RS-GS) to strictly confine geometric and appearance updates to target primitives, reducing background attribute leakage. Experiments demonstrate stronger instruction alignment and better multi-view consistency than the baselines.

TLDR: VidEdit3D introduces a framework that reformulates 3D Gaussian Splatting editing as a video editing task, utilizing video diffusion models for superior multi-view consistency and structural modification control.

TLDR: VidEdit3D提出了一种将3D高斯喷洒编辑重构为视频编辑任务的框架，利用视频扩散模型实现了更强的多视图一致性和结构化修改控制。

Triage: Skip, Skim, or Refine Attention Tiles in Video Diffusion Transformers

Attention over tens of thousands of visual tokens dominates the inference of video diffusion transformers (DiTs). Existing methods reduce this cost through sparsity, low precision, or both, but use a single precision for all kept tiles. We observe that the output error introduced by approximation varies widely across tiles. Many tiles can be skipped with little error, others must be computed but gain little beyond low precision, and a few retain substantial error even at low precision. We present Triage, a training-free attention method that assigns each tile one of three approximation tiers: skipping it by replacing it with its block means, skimming it in NVFP4, or refining it with NVFP4 residual correction. A lightweight router predicts each tile's error under each tier from block statistics and moves a tile to a more accurate tier only when the predicted error reduction justifies the extra compute, with a single price setting this trade-off. A fused kernel executes all three tiers in a single pass on the FP4 tensor cores of RTX Blackwell GPUs. Across Wan2.2, HunyuanVideo, and LTX-2.5, Triage accelerates end-to-end generation by up to 2.32x over dense FlashAttention, and in each of five settings it runs faster than every baseline while reaching 0.3-3.0 dB higher PSNR than the highest-PSNR baseline.

TLDR: Triage is a training-free attention optimization method for video diffusion transformers that dynamically assigns compute tiers (skip, skim, or refine) to visual tiles based on predicted error, achieving significant speedups on Blackwell GPUs while maintaining high visual fidelity.

TLDR: Triage 是一种针对视频扩散 Transformer 的免训练注意力优化方法，通过轻量级路由根据预测误差将视觉块分为跳过、粗略计算或精细计算三个层级，在 Blackwell GPU 上实现了显著的推理加速并保持了高图像质量。

OmniShowcase: Omnidirectional Subject-Consistent Video Generation from Multi-View Images

Generating videos of a specific subject from a few reference images remains challenging when the camera reveals surfaces that are not visible in the input. Early subject-to-video (S2V) methods condition on a single reference image and must therefore infer the subject's unseen appearance and geometry as the viewpoint changes. To address this limitation, recent methods extend S2V to multiple reference views, providing more information about the subject from different viewpoints. However, they do not explicitly specify the 3D structure that connects these observations. In addition, existing training data do not guarantee that viewpoint changes preserve the same underlying 3D subject. We present OmniShowcase, built on two components: (i) a fully automated data synthesis pipeline that produces training videos along diverse camera trajectories, with viewpoint variation driven by underlying subject geometry, even spanning full 360-degree orbits; and (ii) a geometry-conditioning method that provides the video model with a turntable of surface-normal maps reconstructed from the reference views without requiring temporal alignment with the generated frames. We construct Showcase-4K, a corpus of 3D-asset-based videos covering twelve camera motions across 88 trajectory configurations with reconstruction-derived surface-normal conditioning, and ShowcaseBench, a held-out evaluation set with the source meshes and camera parameters used to render each video. Experiments show state-of-the-art subject identity and 3D consistency while maintaining competitive video quality and motion. Project page: justin4ai.github.io/OmniShowcase

TLDR: OmniShowcase introduces a framework for subject-consistent 360-degree video generation by leveraging multi-view reference images, a geometry-aware automated data synthesis pipeline, and surface-normal conditioning.

TLDR: OmniShowcase 提出了一种基于多视角参考图像的 360 度全方位主体一致性视频生成框架，通过自动化的数据合成流程和几何法线条件控制来提升生成质量。

SeeRF: Learning to See Beyond Fields of View via Generative Image Registration and Fusion

Due to the inherent discrepancies in imaging viewpoints and field-of-view mismatches among multi-modal sensors, existing fusion methods have long struggled to achieve complete scene-level information integration beyond the limitation in sensor fields of view. Consequently, the generated fusion results often contain modality-specific regions and fusion artifacts, limiting the comprehensive representation of scene structures and semantic information. To address this challenge, we propose a generative registration-fusion framework, termed SeeRF. Specifically, it first introduces a deformation generation strategy based on the diffeomorphic stationary velocity field, combined with visual foundation model-guided feature extraction and a coarse-to-fine progressive multi-modal image registration module, to achieve accurate cross-modal flow estimation and generate valid field-of-view masks. Subsequently, the registration module is tightly coupled with the generative diffusion module for multi-modal completion and fusion, where the cross-modal control encoding module and the control residual feature encoder-decoder mechanism provide effective guidance. By leveraging the powerful prior modeling capability of generative diffusion models, the proposed framework overcomes the limitations imposed by shared fields of view and enables effective completion and natural fusion of non-overlapping field-of-view regions. Extensive experiments on multiple public datasets and real-world scenarios demonstrate that the proposed method achieves superior performance, validating its effectiveness, superiority, and robustness. The code will be available at https://github.com/XunpengYi/SeeRF.

TLDR: SeeRF is a generative registration-fusion framework that leverages diffeomorphic flow estimation and diffusion models to perform cross-modal image registration and out-of-field-of-view completion/fusion.

TLDR: SeeRF 提出了一种生成式配准与融合框架，利用微分同胚流估计和扩散模型实现了跨模态图像配准，以及超越传感器视场（FOV）限制的场景补全与融合。

Beyond Distribution Matching: Self-Supervised Representation Forcing for Few-Step Video Generation

Few-step distillation is essential for deploying modern video diffusion models. Among existing approaches, Distribution Matching Distillation (DMD) has emerged as a highly promising paradigm by achieving strong output-space fidelity without adversarial training, while remaining flexible across various student architectures. We observe, however, that DMD fundamentally relies on an output-space objective, lacking explicit regularization on the model's internal representations. As the distillation step count aggressively shrinks, this output-only supervision becomes insufficient, causing the distilled student to degrade in both fine-grained spatial structures and coherent temporal dynamics. To address this limitation, we introduce Self Flow Forcing (SF$^2$), which incorporates a predictive representation loss into the DMD framework without altering its output objective. By providing the student and an Exponential Moving Average (EMA) teacher with heterogeneous noise levels, we establish an information asymmetry. This formulation forces the student to predict the teacher's internal representations from a more corrupted input, effectively integrating self-supervised representation alignment into distribution matching. Extensive experiments demonstrate that SF$^2$ effectively mitigates temporal flickering and consistently improves both spatial fidelity and motion quality. Furthermore, the proposed mechanism is architecture-agnostic - seamlessly supporting both bidirectional and causal models - and requires no external feature extractors, introducing minimal computational overhead.

TLDR: The paper introduces Self Flow Forcing (SF²), a method that improves few-step video diffusion distillation by aligning internal representations between student and teacher models to enhance temporal and spatial fidelity.

TLDR: 该论文提出了 Self Flow Forcing (SF²) 方法，通过在学生模型和教师模型之间对齐内部表征，增强了少步视频扩散模型蒸馏的时空一致性与细节质量。

Copying As Defense: Protecting Images from Editing By Eliciting Model Copying

Image editors built on diffusion transformers (DiTs), such as Flux Kontext, make it possible to turn a single photograph into a convincing manipulated image, including harmful and non-consensual edits. Proactive defenses aim to prevent such manipulations by adding an imperceptible perturbation before an image is shared. Existing defenses for DiT-based in-context editors make the output attend less to the protected reference image. But this leaves the model free to follow the edit instruction using the text and its own generated tokens, often producing a new, weakly grounded image rather than preventing the edit. We find that the opposite strategy is more effective: instead of leading the model to ignore the reference, we encourage it to copy it. Through controlled attention interventions, we show that concentrating target-token attention on the reference image in only a few early blocks is sufficient to collapse the editing behavior, causing the model to reproduce its input, often as a lossy copy, rather than execute the requested transformation. Based on this insight, we introduce Copying-as-Defense (CaD), which maximizes per-token target-to-reference attention mass in early transformer blocks. Since attention rows are normalized, increasing reference attention suppresses the attention allocated to the text and generated canvas. Restricting the objective to early blocks also lets us truncate the backpropagation path, keeping optimization cheap. Across editors and benchmarks, CaD reduces average edit success from 81.4% on unprotected images to 24.0%, a 70.6% relative reduction, and by a further 44.3% relative to DeContext, the strongest baseline (43.0%). CaD is also roughly 1.8-2.0x faster per optimization step than comparable methods.

TLDR: The paper introduces Copying-as-Defense (CaD), a proactive adversarial defense for DiT-based image editors that forces the model to copy the input image instead of editing it by maximizing attention to the reference image in early layers.

TLDR: 本文提出了“复制作为防御”（CaD）方法，通过在DiT图像编辑模型的早期层中强制增加对输入图像的注意力，促使模型直接复制原图而非执行篡改，从而有效防御恶意图像编辑。

WorldPager: Zero-Stall Tiered Memory for Long-Horizon World Models on a Single Consumer GPU

Interactive world models keep the KV of past chunks as retrieval memory so that revisited places stay consistent. On a single consumer machine (one RTX 4090, 32 GiB of host memory, one NVMe SSD), long sessions with such memory hit two walls. The first is capacity: the KV of Matrix-Game 2.0 exhausts host memory after 45 seconds of video in bf16 and after 2.8 minutes in int4. The second is the model's positional horizon of 4.3 minutes. We show that this memory is capacity-bound rather than bandwidth-bound and that its access set is known before compute, because the camera pose is an input. An analytical model built on these observations predicts spill points to within one chunk and classifies zero versus non-zero stalls correctly on held-out configurations. Guided by it, WorldPager stores int4 KV in a GPU/DRAM/NVMe hierarchy instead of recomputing it, prefetches each chunk's memory from the extrapolated pose, never waits on a miss, and writes back in the background; to our knowledge, it is the first to place the KV memory of an interactive world model on an NVMe tier with zero-stall prefetching. With training-free position handling, it generates 37.5 minutes of video continuously with zero stalls and constant host memory, at about 9.5 frames per second. On a sealed test set, int4 storage shows no measurable quality loss; on long-gap revisits it lowers image quality by about one MUSIQ point.

TLDR: WorldPager introduces a tiered memory management system that enables long-horizon interactive world models to run on consumer GPUs by efficiently offloading and prefetching KV cache to NVMe storage with zero stalls.

TLDR: WorldPager 提出了一种分层内存管理系统，通过将 KV 缓存高效卸载并预取至 NVMe 存储，实现了在消费级 GPU 上进行长时程交互式世界模型生成，且不会产生停顿。

Generative Structure from Motion with Native 3D Diffusion

Recovering an object's 3D structure and camera motion from a few uncalibrated views is a fundamental problem in computer vision. Classical structure from motion methods recover 3D geometry consistent with the input images but cannot recover unseen regions; native generation methods produce plausible 3D objects which are related but not necessarily faithful to the input images. Methods simply concatenating reconstruction and generation allow error to cascade across the two stages, instead of producing a 3D object consistent with both the distribution and the input. We propose GenSfM, which integrates image-based 3D reconstruction with a native 3D diffusion model. In particular, we use a multi-modal diffusion transformer to jointly denoise the shape latent and per-view pose latents under shared self-attention, grounding generated geometry in the observed views while using object-level priors to infer unobserved regions. A compact 3D-native UV-volume tokenization keeps the joint sequence small as views are added, and a pose-aware stage refines geometry and texture by sampling per-voxel image features at the recovered camera poses. On Toys4k and GSO, for $4$ uncalibrated views, GenSfM improves novel-view PSNR by $3.2$-$4.5$ dB and reduces median Chamfer distance by $2.8$-$3.8\times$ over a strong native-3D generation baseline, while recovering input-view poses over $96\%$ Acc@$5^\circ$ without any external pose-estimation backbone, post-hoc optimization, or known intrinsics. Project page: https://dotb1ue.github.io/GenSfm/.

TLDR: GenSfM introduces a multi-modal diffusion transformer that jointly reconstructs 3D geometry and camera poses from sparse, uncalibrated views by integrating 3D-native diffusion with latent structure recovery.

TLDR: GenSfM 提出了一种多模态扩散 Transformer，通过将 3D 原生扩散模型与潜在结构恢复相结合，实现了从少量未标定图像中同时重建 3D 几何结构与相机位姿。

More Memory, Better Videos? A Controlled Study of Visual Memory in Long-Video Generation

Long-video generators increasingly retrieve past visual observations to prevent identity drift. The intuition is compelling: more access to history should make future video more consistent. But an image can be faithful to the past and wrong for the present. Does visual memory always improve the generated story? We investigate this question through VMem-Bench, a two-track diagnostic study. Track-A measures which references are selected from 92 real films (16,548 segments), before generation and without generator confounds. Track-B compares four retrieval policies with no-memory and gold-informed last-seen controls on 30 causally generated stories (2,824 segments) using a shared generator. Retrieval increases entity recall from 0.559 to 0.628-0.693 and improves similarity to past appearances, but decreases precision from 0.977 to 0.915-0.938 and forbidden-entity avoidance from 0.968 to 0.845-0.898. It can also reinforce obsolete appearances after the story changes them. Conversely, high long-gap recall can arise without memory when the prompt itself supplies the appearance; an appearance-stripping analysis exposes this confound. These results distinguish historical fidelity from current-story correctness and show why retrieval relevance or final-video consistency alone does not establish memory utility.

TLDR: This paper investigates the trade-off in long-video generation between historical visual memory and current-story correctness, revealing that while memory improves entity recall, it often leads to precision drops and the reinforcement of obsolete visual appearances.

TLDR: 本文探讨了长视频生成中视觉记忆与当前叙事一致性之间的权衡，研究发现尽管外部检索能提高实体召回率，但往往会导致精度下降，并可能错误地强化已过时的视觉外观。

DiffAuction: An Auction-Driven Framework for Generative Image Advertising

Diffusion models enable multiple advertisers to compete for exposure within a shared generated image. Yet existing approaches are computationally costly when generating images under varying target-share configurations, and it remains unclear how the resulting outcomes should be evaluated and priced. To address these gaps, we propose DiffAuction, a framework integrating controllable generation, click-intent evaluation, and auction design. First, our training-free Residual Share Calibration (RSC) decomposes joint-attention updates into product-specific residuals and rescales them according to target shares. We further introduce Share-Conditioned Prompting (SCP) to adapt the viewpoint to each desired allocation. Second, we develop an evaluator that uses a VLM to extract visual evidence and an LLM to assess Click-Through Intent (CTI), a proxy for click propensity, along four dimensions: Product Prominence, Brand Recognizability, Scene Integration, and Contextual Value Communication. We validate its assessments against judgments from 10 trained annotators on 1000 images. Third, we formulate this advertising setting as an auction over jointly feasible generation configurations. Each configuration has a public expected CTI vector, while advertisers hold configuration-specific private values per unit of CTI that map click intent into commercial value. We adopt a learnable affine maximizer auction (AMA), which jointly selects a shared generation configuration and determines advertiser-specific payments to maximize expected platform revenue. The resulting allocation and payment rules guarantee dominant-strategy incentive compatibility (DSIC) and individual rationality (IR). Experiments show that our generation method strengthens the monotonic relationship between target shares and CTI relative to baselines, while the learned AMA achieves higher platform revenue in the resulting auction setting.

TLDR: DiffAuction introduces a framework that enables multi-advertiser product placement in diffusion-generated images through controllable generation, VLM/LLM-based click-intent evaluation, and a revenue-maximizing auction mechanism.

TLDR: DiffAuction 提出了一种通过受控生成、基于视觉/语言模型的点击意图评估以及收益最大化拍卖机制，在扩散模型生成图像中实现多广告主产品植入的框架。

ToDoPro: Faster and Better Reference-Based Generation: Early Token Reduction via Downsampled Self-Probing

Recent generic reference-based image generation models natively support multiple conditions by concatenating all reference image and text tokens into a joint sequence processed by a single Diffusion Transformer (DiT). However, this versatile paradigm comes at an efficiency cost: accommodating multiple references leads to a token explosion, incurring massive $\mathcal{O}(N^2)$ computational complexity. Most acceleration methods operate inside the DiT, showing limited capability in reducing spatial redundancy and failing to alleviate peak VRAM. While some pre-model token reduction alternatives exist, they inevitably introduce external models. This raises a question: Is it possible to achieve acceleration and memory savings while preserving compatibility with in-model techniques and requiring no additional parameters? In this work, we realize this goal by proposing the early Token reduction via Downsampled self-Probing (ToDoPro). By executing a few denoising steps on spatially $2\times$ downsampled reference images, this cheap probing stage utilizes the DiT model itself to effectively and efficiently extract reference token importance. Using the probed importance, we apply a hierarchical reduction before the main denoising stage: we completely discard low-importance tokens, substitute mid-importance tokens with their downsampled counterparts to cheaply preserve global context, and keep high-importance tokens. Experiments across three base models and two benchmarks demonstrate that our early reduction accelerates inference significantly and mitigates peak GPU memory. Furthermore, we can improve generation quality by safely filtering out noisy, uninformative reference tokens, enabling the model to concentrate its limited attention capacity on genuinely useful tokens. The code will be released at https://github.com/scy639/ToDoPro

TLDR: ToDoPro is a training-free token reduction method for reference-based generation that uses a lightweight downsampled self-probing stage to prune redundant reference tokens, improving inference speed and memory efficiency without needing external models.

TLDR: ToDoPro 是一种针对参考图生成任务的无需训练的 Token 剪枝方法，通过轻量级的下采样自探测阶段筛选冗余参考 Token，在不依赖额外模型的前提下提升了推理速度与显存利用率。

Video World Models Need Predictable Latents

Video world models trained on large-scale robot data promise a safe and scalable approach for learning and evaluating policies that act in the physical world. However, they still struggle with long-horizon rollouts and out-of-distribution generalization, and they remain computationally demanding at inference time. Typically, video world models make predictions in the latent space of a reconstruction-trained autoencoder, which however is not directly optimized for dynamics modeling. On the other hand, world models designed for latent planning usually optimize latents to be predictable, but they forgo accurate prediction of observations in pixel space. In this paper, we show that it is possible to achieve the best of both worlds: a latent geometry optimized to make future prediction as simple as possible without sacrificing accurate prediction of future observations. More precisely, we find that temporal objectives for representation learning, paired with a past-conditioned decoder architecture, lead to consistent improvements in generative video world models at the same training budget. The gains include up to 5x cheaper inference, over 7x faster training, and 15-24% lower pixel and perceptual errors out of distribution and over long horizons. Our world models also agree more closely with real-world policy evaluation when using a vision-language model to judge task success from their pixel-space predictions. Overall, our study indicates that temporal predictability is an important design criterion for the latent space of video world models, and provides concrete guidance on how to incorporate it into the training of autoencoders.

TLDR: The paper introduces a latent space optimization method for video world models that balances predictable dynamics with accurate pixel-space reconstruction, significantly improving training/inference efficiency and long-horizon generation.

FlockWorld: Toward Population-Scale Multi-Agent Video World Models through Artificial Life Simulation

Most action-conditioned video world models remain limited to a single player, while existing multiplayer models support only small, fixed groups of agents. We introduce FlockWorld, an artificial-life environment built on Reynolds's Boids algorithm for studying population-scale multi-agent video world models. Specifically, FlockWorld renders synchronized egocentric crops, assigns distinct colors to preserve camera-agent identity across views, exposes rule-derived accelerations as actions, and lets the number of camera agents be configured independently of the size of the surrounding population. We further introduce FlockDiT, an action-conditioned latent diffusion transformer trained with flow matching on FlockWorld data to jointly generate synchronized egocentric videos for multiple agents. Our results suggest that joint generation benefits agent detection at density-matched settings and that diffusion forcing improves rollout fidelity. Together, FlockWorld and FlockDiT provide a controlled testbed for multi-agent video generation.

TLDR: FlockWorld is a new artificial-life framework for multi-agent video world modeling, paired with FlockDiT, an action-conditioned latent diffusion transformer that generates synchronized, population-scale egocentric videos.
