Guidance as Variance Matching

Guidance repels the prediction of a diffusion model away from a weaker reference prediction. It substantially improves sample quality, but its mechanism remains poorly understood. We first find that, at the scales used in practice, guidance does not improve the prediction of the posterior mean (the model's training target); it makes the prediction worse, while samples improve. Second, posterior-mean predictions have less variance than the data, and we find that guidance restores it. We introduce variance-matched guidance: a closed-form rule that picks the guidance scale by matching the variance of the guided predictions to that of the data. Without tuning, it recovers the hand-picked scales of eleven widely used models across image, video, audio, motion, and world generation; to our knowledge, no previous explanation of guidance predicts them. Applied per noise level, it yields a schedule without tuned hyperparameters that users prefer to hand-crafted ones on SD 3.5 Medium. Third, we offer an interpretation of the mechanism behind guidance: each model makes a random error in predicting the posterior mean, and guidance rescales the difference of these errors into a sample with the posterior's covariance, which improves sampling. This view also suggests two new hypotheses that our experiments support: guidance can work with negative scales, and guiding a model with its own checkpoint needs a larger scale than guiding it with an independently trained model.

TLDR: The paper demystifies diffusion guidance by showing it acts as a variance-matching mechanism rather than improving the posterior mean, providing a parameter-free method to set guidance scales across multiple generative modalities.

Generation as Reverse Encoding

Pretrained visual representations accelerate generative learning by abstracting away appearance and spatial detail. Yet the same abstraction makes the return to pixels far harder, shifting much of the burden onto a larger decoder. We ask why the representation-to-pixel path should be learned independently when the encoder already provides a learned pixel-to-semantics path (the semantic encoding path). We introduce RevFlow, based on the principle of generation as reverse encoding. RevFlow uses intermediate representations of a frozen encoder as successive generative targets, first generating deep semantic representations, then shallow representations that retain appearance and spatial detail, before decoding to pixels. To learn these transitions without training a separate generator for each stage, we develop HyC-DiT, a unified generative model with a standard DiT backbone shared across stages and stage-specific condition injection. We further show that native matching in semantic representation space enables few-step generation. RevFlow accelerates both learning and sampling. On ImageNet $256 \times 256$, RevFlow reaches 1.96 FID at 690M and 1.74 FID at 1.55B after only 5 training epochs. It also achieves 30.25 dB reconstruction PSNR with an 89M decoder. After a short native-matching adaptation phase, RevFlow-XL achieves 2.85 FID using a single sampling step in each stage. Our code repository is at https://github.com/ChengShiest/RevFlow

TLDR: RevFlow proposes a 'generation as reverse encoding' paradigm, leveraging frozen encoder features as hierarchical targets for a unified DiT-based generator to improve training efficiency and sampling speed.

TLDR: RevFlow 提出了一种“生成即反向编码”的范式，通过利用冻结编码器的分层特征作为生成目标，结合统一的 DiT 主干网络，显著提升了生成模型的训练效率和采样速度。

WorldInteract: Controllable Streaming Video Generation for Multi-Material Interactions

Video world models aim to create interactive physical worlds that capture how diverse materials move and interact. Yet material-dependent dynamics remain underexplored in controllable video generation, limiting reliable control in scenes with different materials. We present WorldInteract, a streaming video generation framework that decomposes cross-material interaction into material-dependent behavior generation and contact response computation. External pushes and contact impulses are expressed through a shared velocity-increment interface, allowing material behaviors learned from interaction-free videos to be reused in interacting scenes. A physics controller predicts contacts and computes velocity increments from estimated masses and restitution coefficients, while a causal generator executes these increments using learned material behaviors. This separation enables cross-material interaction without supervised contact videos spanning diverse material pairings and contact configurations. We train the generator on 30k simulated interaction-free videos spanning 22 material classes and introduce Material-Aware RL, using verifiable physical rewards to refine material behavior and control execution. WorldInteract substantially outperforms state-of-the-art methods on Physics-IQ and delivers more reliable push control on real-world images.

TLDR: WorldInteract is a novel streaming video generation framework that decouples material-dependent dynamics from contact physics, enabling controllable cross-material interactions without needing supervised interaction data.

ShowNow: Fine-Grained Speculative Streaming for Responsive World Models

Interactive world models must generate video continuously while responding promptly to user actions. Real-time throughput alone leaves input latency unresolved: coarse generation blocks delay action updates, and generation after input adds work to the response path. We introduce ShowNow, a fine-grained speculative streaming framework that uses computation available during playback to prepare action-conditioned futures before input arrives. Single-latent commitment enables frequent decisions, while configurable preparation covers one to eight actions. We construct a matched multi-future dataset to train alternative continuations from a common state; shared-history tree execution and fused commitment make their preparation efficient. On a single H100, our 5B model sustains 480p generation at 16 fps with eight prepared futures. In ViZDoom, the complete ShowNow deployment reduces median input-to-display latency from 620 to 208 ms relative to the native-block SolarWM-FT deployment. Matched-policy experiments reduce restart empty ticks from 1.56% to 0.09% (94%) and improve on-time responses to action changes. These results establish complementary roles for fine-grained commitment and advance preparation, with measured trade-offs in visual fidelity and long-horizon consistency. ShowNow turns playback-time computation into responsive visual interaction. Project: https://github.com/showlab/ShowNow.

TLDR: ShowNow is a fine-grained speculative streaming framework for interactive world models that leverages playback-time computation to pre-generate action-conditioned video futures, significantly reducing input-to-display latency.

TLDR: ShowNow 是一个针对交互式世界模型的细粒度推测流式传输框架，它利用播放时的计算资源提前生成响应用户操作的视频未来，从而显著降低从输入到显示的延迟。

Neural Rendering Compiler: Generative Video Models as Programmable Renderers

Generative video models are becoming powerful renderers, yet lack the predictable control interfaces of conventional systems such as HTML/CSS engines or Blender. Their capabilities emerge implicitly from training, making it difficult to determine what they can reliably execute. We present Neural Rendering Compiler (NeurCo), which automatically probes frozen video models to construct model-specific capability manuals describing which semantic requirements they can reliably satisfy. NeurCo represents user intent in a model-agnostic intermediate representation of objects, constraints, events, and cameras, then compiles it into model-specific execution strategies. Reliable requirements remain unchanged, while unreliable ones are selectively rewritten or compiled into alternative execution strategies. We introduce VComBench, a benchmark of 380 compositional, multi-requirement tasks spanning text-to-video, image-to-video, and reference-to-video generation. NeurCo improves requirement satisfaction from 46.8% to 52.3% on Wan and from 66.5% to 69.3% on Minimax H3, and achieves further gains when combined with prompt enhancers, including on Seedance 2.5. Our analysis reveals model-specific differences in control capabilities and trade-offs between controllability and photorealism, highlighting capability-guided compilation as a promising approach to programmable generative video rendering.

TLDR: NeurCo is a framework that treats generative video models as programmable renderers by automatically probing their capabilities and compiling user intent into model-specific execution strategies to improve control.

TLDR: NeurCo 是一个将生成式视频模型视为可编程渲染器的框架，通过自动探测模型能力并将用户意图编译为特定的执行策略，从而提升视频生成的控制精度。

Motion Beyond Pixels: Learning Future Visual Representations for Object-Centric Dynamics

Many embodied world models predict future states in latent spaces optimized for RGB reconstruction, which do not explicitly prioritize object-level semantics and geometry relevant to motion generation. We present MotionRep, a visual representation world model that jointly generates future DINO representations and 3D object flow, coupling semantic and geometric information with object-centric dynamics. To make high-dimensional DINO features amenable to generative modeling, we introduce DINO-VAE, which combines temporal and channel compression with latent regularization to produce compact latents while preserving semantic and geometric information. Conditioned on an initial observation, object query points, and a language instruction, a unified diffusion transformer predicts future visual representations and 3D object flow through joint attention. MotionRep employs asymmetric modality-specific noise schedules to keep the visual stream ahead of 3D object flow during joint denoising, allowing progressively refined future visual representations to guide flow generation. Experiments on simulated and real-world manipulation benchmarks demonstrate that MotionRep outperforms pixel-based baselines in 3D object flow accuracy and generalization to unseen scenes, while requiring only three denoising steps for inference. These results support predictive visual representations as an effective future-state space for object-centric dynamics modeling.

TLDR: MotionRep is a world model that predicts future DINO representations and 3D object flow using a compressed DINO-VAE and a unified diffusion transformer, outperforming pixel-based models in object-centric motion generation.

TLDR: MotionRep 是一种通过压缩 DINO-VAE 和统一扩散 Transformer 来预测未来 DINO 表示及 3D 物体流的世界模型，在以物体为中心的运动生成任务中优于传统的基于像素的方法。

Beyond Pose Control: Anatomically Consistent Ultrasound Video Generation

Camera-controlled video generation anticipates how a scene appears from new viewpoints by relying on a prior learned implicitly from large-scale data. Ultrasound poses the same problem, with the probe pose as the viewpoint, but under opposite conditions: anatomy offers an explicit prior that every sonographer relies on, whereas tracked scans are too scarce to learn it implicitly. As a result, pose control alone is not enough, and pose-conditioned ultrasound generators produce implausible anatomy in unscanned regions and inconsistent anatomy on revisits. We go beyond pose control and give the generator the two kinds of knowledge that make up a sonographer's mental model. An anatomical prior, built from a population of subjects, guides generation where nothing has been scanned, and is refined online by every generated chunk towards the scanned subject. A 3D latent memory, designed around the cross-sectional physics of ultrasound, compounds the generated slices into a volume of the patient and reslices it at new poses, keeping revisited anatomy consistent. The fine-grained memory enters the generator at the input, while the coarse anatomy prior guides the layout across transformer blocks. Experiments on thyroid, kidney, and lumbar spine show improved visual fidelity, pose following, and anatomical consistency over pose-conditioned and memory-augmented baselines.

TLDR: The paper introduces a framework for anatomically consistent ultrasound video generation by integrating a population-based anatomical prior and a 3D latent memory, moving beyond simple pose control to handle sparse ultrasound data.

TLDR: 该论文提出了一种超声视频生成框架，通过集成基于人群的解剖先验和3D潜在记忆，解决了超声数据稀缺导致的生成解剖结构不一致问题。

SceneDesigner++: A Unified 9-DoF Pose Control Framework for T2I and T2V Generation

Precise 9-DoF pose control is essential for visual content generation, enabling users to specify object location, size, and orientation in 3D space. However, existing methods typically focus on either text-to-image (T2I) or text-to-video (T2V) generation, with limitations in pose controllability and temporal coherence. We present SceneDesigner++, a unified framework for 9-DoF pose control across both T2I and T2V generation. We construct ObjectPose9D++, a dataset containing high-quality images and videos with accurate pose annotations. Trained on ObjectPose9D++, SceneDesigner++ combines absolute and relative pose encoding strategies to decouple frame-wise spatial grounding from inter-frame motion modeling. Specifically, absolute pose encoding leverages CNOCS map sequences to provide explicit, frame-wise 9-DoF pose guidance. Meanwhile, our relative pose encoding, termed VPRoPE, incorporates relative pose information into rotary position embeddings to explicitly model inter-frame pose transitions, thereby promoting temporally coherent object motion. A learnable orthogonal transformation is further introduced for each attention head, allowing different heads to capture complementary geometric relationships while preserving input feature norms to promote training stability. We further adopt a two-stage training strategy to learn the absolute and relative pose encoding mechanisms separately. Benefiting from this lightweight and decoupled design, SceneDesigner++ also supports integration with an independently trained camera-control module, enabling separate or joint control over object and camera motion. Finally, we introduce T2IVPoseEval, a benchmark for evaluating diverse object poses and motion patterns in both T2I and T2V tasks. Extensive experiments demonstrate that SceneDesigner++ consistently outperforms same-backbone baselines in pose fidelity and motion quality.

TLDR: SceneDesigner++ introduces a unified framework for 9-DoF pose control in T2I and T2V generation using a novel dual-encoding strategy (absolute CNOCS maps and relative VPRoPE) and a new benchmark dataset.

TLDR: SceneDesigner++ 提出了一个用于文生图（T2I）和文生视频（T2V）的统一 9-DoF 姿态控制框架，通过结合绝对和相对姿态编码策略，显著提升了空间定位与时间一致性，并配套了新的评估基准。

ProxyGenBench: Benchmarking Proxy-Guided Video Generation

Recent video generators produce realistic videos, yet controlling how scenes unfold remains challenging. Structural proxies, such as animated 3D blockouts, guide generation by specifying scene layout, motion, camera behavior, and event order. However, these coarse inputs leave details unspecified and may contain artifacts that should not be reproduced. Successful proxy-guided generation must therefore Preserve intended structure, Complete missing content and correct proxy artifacts, and Bind text, image, and audio conditions to the right entities and events. We introduce ProxyGenBench to study these interacting requirements, with 312 evaluation cases from 60 scenes across three scene tasks and controlled variations in proxy fidelity, text detail, and multimodal inputs. We evaluate five video generators using verified, case-specific checklists. Experiments show that proxies improve spatiotemporal control, but greater proxy detail can coincide with poorer completion, while strong structural adherence does not ensure correct binding.

TLDR: ProxyGenBench is a new benchmark designed to evaluate how effectively video generation models utilize structural proxies (like 3D blockouts) to control scene layout and motion while balancing content completion and multimodal binding.

PreserveReward: Reliable Consistency Reward Modeling for Image Editing via Dual-Teacher On-Policy Self-Distillation

Instruction-guided image editing has a wide range of applications, yet existing models may inadvertently alter content that should remain unchanged. Reliable consistency evaluation is therefore essential for assessing content preservation and providing reward signals for Reinforcement Learning (RL). However, existing reward models may overlook subtle visual changes or fail to distinguish necessary modifications from unintended alterations, limiting the reliability of consistency assessment. To address this challenge, we propose PreserveReward, a consistency reward model for image editing that integrates visual On-Policy Self-Distillation (OPSD) to enhance fine-grained visual perception and consistency assessment. PreserveReward learns from reference preservation scores through a regression-based scoring loss and employs two complementary teacher views for self-distillation. The local teacher uses matched crops to identify subtle visual changes, while the joint teacher combines local details with global context to determine whether these changes align with the editing intent. To improve the reliability of distillation supervision, we calibrate teacher score distributions via minimum-KL projection under reference-score error constraints and adaptively select teacher process supervision based on gradient alignment with the scoring objective, mitigating scoring bias and optimization conflicts. Extensive experiments across multiple benchmarks demonstrate that PreserveReward achieves strong alignment with human preference on non-edited content preservation and generalizes well across diverse editing scenarios.

TLDR: PreserveReward introduces a dual-teacher on-policy self-distillation framework for reinforcement learning to improve content preservation in instruction-guided image editing.

Most of the Way There: Turning a Strong Video Prior into an Action-Conditioned World Model

Modern video generators excel at producing visually plausible futures guided by text and image prompts. Yet their emphasis on semantic plausibility does not guarantee fidelity to the physical consequences of specific actions. Action-conditioned world models (ACWMs), in contrast, aim to simulate how the world responds to robot actions, enabling scalable policy evaluation and improvement without costly real-world rollouts. The challenge is not merely to generate a plausible future, but to predict what follows from a specific physical action. As video priors grow stronger, we ask how little adaptation is truly needed to bridge this gap. We introduce Dianjing, a minimal recipe that preserves and unlocks the capabilities of strong video priors through unified physical action conditioning, training concentrated at the high noise levels where motion and layout, and thus the consequences of actions, are decided, and native history conditioning. Built upon MiniMax H3, a 33B video foundation model, Dianjing freezes the pretrained backbone and trains only 0.34% of its parameters using approximately 65 hours of robot video across two embodiments, without additional robot-specific pretraining or camera calibration. Controlled ablations show that where training is allocated across noise levels matters more than how actions enter the network. On WorldOracle, which evaluates predictions against paired real-world executions, Dianjing achieves the best scores on four of the five evaluation tracks and ranks second on AgiBot-Long. Our findings suggest that strong video priors already bring us most of the way to world modeling; lightweight physical action grounding can unlock their potential for real-world prediction.

TLDR: Dianjing demonstrates that strong pre-trained video foundation models can be efficiently transformed into action-conditioned world models with minimal parameter training (0.34%), focusing on noise-level adaptation for physical grounding.

TLDR: Dianjing 研究表明，通过极少量的参数训练（0.34%）并聚焦于噪声水平的调整，可以将强大的预训练视频基础模型高效转化为具备物理动作条件的世界模型。

StateWeaver: Multi-Agent Coordination of Story-State Transitions for Long-Horizon Video Generation

Modern video foundation models generate high-quality short clips, but preserving long-video consistency and correctly depicting event-driven state changes remain challenging. Existing agentic workflows face these challenges because continual planning and repair accumulate context, while reference updates remain limited. We introduce StateWeaver, which combines Active States and Hierarchical Collaboration to connect review-driven visual reference updates with cross-shot state inheritance. Active States translate state discrepancies identified during review into concrete visual asset requirements that guide reference construction, validation, and regeneration, preserving identity and already-correct content while repairing target attributes. Confirmed generation results are linked to the corresponding asset states, story time, and reference versions for verification and reuse by subsequent shots. Hierarchical Collaboration isolates execution contexts by task, clip, and repair round, and uses hierarchical memory to organize this process across multiple sets. Across multiple benchmarks, StateWeaver outperforms existing methods and produces long videos with sustained cross-shot consistency under predefined and autonomously planned storyboards.

TLDR: StateWeaver is a multi-agent framework that maintains long-term visual consistency in video generation by using Active States to track object state transitions and Hierarchical Collaboration to manage memory and cross-shot repairs.

Semantic State Reading for Joint Image-Semantic Diffusion Transformers

Pretrained visual representations improve diffusion-transformer training, yet exploiting both global context and local spatial structure requires deciding which semantic states to model and how to access them. We introduce Semantic State Reading (SSR), which jointly denoises image latents, a global CLS token encoding image-level context, and a compact patch state preserving spatial correspondence. SSR couples this global-local modeling with distinct reading paths: local states are fused with corresponding image tokens at the input, while independent projections inject the current global state into every transformer block. Both semantic scopes thus remain accessible throughout denoising. Ablations show complementary benefits from local-state modeling and repeated global reading, and semantic-trajectory interventions alter image layout under a fixed class label. On ImageNet $256 \times 256$, SiT-XL/2 with SSR reaches or surpasses the reference FIDs of SiT, REPA, and Representation Entanglement for Generation (REG) with $100\times$, $40\times$, and $2\times$ fewer training iterations, respectively. SSR achieves unguided FID 1.78 at 2M iterations and FID 1.34 with classifier-free guidance alone. Combining classifier-free guidance with autoguidance yields FID 1.31 at 1M iterations, achieving the best FID in our main comparison table.

TLDR: Semantic State Reading (SSR) is a novel architecture for diffusion transformers that enhances image synthesis by explicitly modeling and injecting both global semantic context and local spatial states, significantly accelerating training convergence.

TORE: Token-Wise Taylor Order Selection via Decomposed Residual Error Estimation for Diffusion Acceleration

Diffusion models excel at image and video generation, but iterative denoising incurs substantial inference latency. Recent token-adaptive acceleration methods select Taylor prediction orders for the full-model residual using shallow-layer proxy scores. However, candidate orders are evaluated on these proxy features but used to predict the full-model residual, creating a proxy--target mismatch: the proxy-preferred order may not minimize residual prediction error. To address this mismatch, we propose \textbf{TORE} (\textbf{T}aylor \textbf{O}rder selection via \textbf{R}esidual \textbf{E}rror estimation), which selects token-wise Taylor orders based on estimated prediction errors for the full-model residual. Yet direct error evaluation requires future ground-truth residuals, which are unavailable during order selection. TORE therefore estimates prediction error by analyzing how errors arise in Taylor extrapolation. Specifically, it decomposes prediction error into truncation error and finite-difference derivative error, then estimates both contributions from cached residuals to preselect token-wise orders for upcoming skip steps. Extensive experiments show that TORE achieves $4.31\times$ speedup on FLUX.1-dev and $4.18\times$ on Wan2.1 while maintaining high generation quality and fidelity.

TLDR: TORE accelerates diffusion model inference by dynamically selecting token-wise Taylor expansion orders based on a novel decomposition and estimation of prediction errors, bypassing the proxy-target mismatch of previous methods.

VibeVideoAgent: Closing the Perceptual Loop for Code-Driven Video Generation

Code-driven video generation asks a coding agent to write and run rendering programs. This gives exact control over typography, data visualizations, synchronized audio, and multi-shot scenes, and it leaves an editable project behind. A program that exits cleanly can still render overlapping labels, a camera aimed away from its subject, broken object interactions, or narration that drifts out of sync, and none of these raise a runtime error. Feeding rendered frames and audio back into the coding session bloats its long-horizon context, while critiquing only the finished full-resolution render turns early design mistakes into costly rewrites. We introduce VibeVideoAgent, a training-free framework that lets coding agents inspect their drafts throughout production. Multimodal perception runs in short-lived contexts kept apart from the coder and returns compact, grounded Findings. Seven primitives operate on a unified multi-clock representation of assets and drafts: they explore media from coarse to fine, verify quoted requirements with evidence from every required modality, compare a draft with a reference or an earlier revision on separate clocks, and measure stream signals locally. A lightweight staged protocol has the agent test its riskiest decision on a minimal preview first. On all 239 VibeVideoBench tasks, with matched agent runtimes and a fixed perception model, VibeVideoAgent improves holistic audiovisual quality (L3) for Claude Opus 5.5 (4.03 -> 4.43, while L2 rubric compliance rises from 98.87% to 99.35%), GPT-6 Astra (4.03 -> 4.27), and Claude Sonnet 5.5 (3.97 -> 4.10), with the largest gains in audio-visual synchronization (up to +0.56). FoamSurf: A Voronoi-Surfel Representation for Differentiable Rendering

Scenes inside a scattering medium contain solid surfaces, such as the seabed or rocks, and a semi-transparent medium, such as water, fog, or smoke. Recent Gaussian-based methods produce realistic images of scenes in scattering media, but traces of the medium can remain visible in surface-only renders. Neural fields separate surfaces and medium more accurately, but they are slow to train and difficult to edit. Both approaches evaluate the medium only along camera rays, so its effects along reflected and refracted rays are not modeled. These limitations prevent reliable surface recovery, independent editing of surfaces and the medium, and consistent rendering of inserted objects. We present FoamSurf, a hybrid representation that models surfaces with planar Gaussian surfels and the medium with space-filling Voronoi cells. A single differentiable ray tracer composites both along arbitrary rays, while training priors keep them separate without segmentation masks. Across real underwater scenes, synthetic scenes, and dense fog, FoamSurf achieves image quality on par with the state of the art and the most accurate surface-medium separation among the compared methods. The recovered components can be removed, edited, or stylized independently, and the same reconstruction supports inserted mesh objects with attenuation and scattering along both direct and reflected or refracted rays. Together, these capabilities enable scene restoration, editing, and composition within a single reconstructed model. Project page: https://grzegorzwilczynski.com/foamsurf/
