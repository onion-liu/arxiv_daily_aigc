LynnReal-Hand: Unlocking World Interaction with Hand Control

Interactive video world models enable exploration through text and camera controls, yet direct object interaction requires finer control. Hands provide a natural interface, but translating continuous hand movements into coherent visual responses remains challenging because of complex hand-object dynamics and demanding real-time computation. To address these challenges, we introduce LynnReal-Hand, a hand-controlled video world model that unifies precise interaction modeling with efficient streaming generation. We curate 100K interaction clips spanning diverse real and synthetic environments and develop hand-aware supervision to improve fine-grained hand motions and object interactions. Building on this foundation, we learn a causal flow map for few-step generation and introduce region-aware score distillation that combines complementary video and image priors to refine interaction dynamics and visual appearance. To further reduce inference latency, we propose Ultra-Fast-VAE, a compact video decoder that preserves high reconstruction fidelity with substantially lower computational cost. Extensive evaluations on HandBench demonstrate superior hand-following accuracy and visual quality compared with existing approaches. Our full-sequence model achieves an FVD of 181.67 and a MANO Dice score of 93.02, while the three-step causal model maintains a MANO Dice score of 92.49. For real-time interaction, 540p streaming on a single H200 reaches 18.03 FPS with 741ms latency and an optimized peak GPU memory footprint of only 34.89GB. We will release the datasets, model weights, and the full training and inference code to support research on hand-controlled video world models.

TLDR: LynnReal-Hand introduces a hand-controlled video world model utilizing a curated interaction dataset, region-aware score distillation, and an Ultra-Fast-VAE to achieve high-fidelity, real-time interactive video generation.

TLDR: LynnReal-Hand 提出了一种手部控制的视频世界模型，通过构建交互数据集、区域感知蒸馏和高效的 Ultra-Fast-VAE，实现了高保真度的实时交互式视频生成。

MovieRoll: A Systematic Framework for Rolling Out Long-Form Videos

Streaming video generation must sustain quality beyond the training horizon while handling self-generated history and growing cache costs. To address these challenges, we present MovieRoll, a base-to-distilled framework that connects streaming fine-tuning, long-rollout distillation, and long-horizon inference. We first establish a robust multi-step base through Window-wise Teacher Forcing (WinTF), which jointly denoises chunks at progressive noise levels with bidirectional attention within each window and causal access to preceding history. To retain historical context efficiently, hierarchical KV-cache compression (HieraKV) exploits layer-wise attention patterns, retaining full history in shallow layers and sink--recent history in deeper layers. This reduces cache memory by up to 78% while improving generation quality. Building on this base, cumulative long tuning with distribution matching distillation (CuLT-DMD) holds parameters fixed throughout each long self-rollout and averages local gradients before updating. Each update therefore combines supervision across temporal positions while retaining only one local computation graph, enabling efficient long-rollout training of a few-step generator. To extend generation beyond the training horizon, KV recomputation bounds context for both models, while guidance annealing (AnnealCFG) reduces guidance toward the clean endpoint to stabilize MovieRoll-Base. Complementing these modeling advances, FBCA attention and PS-VAE decoding support efficient training and streaming output. On HeliosBench, MovieRoll-Base remains competitive at 30s after training on videos of up to 15s, and MovieRoll-Distilled achieves the highest total scores among the compared methods across single- and multi-event evaluations up to 60s, combining sustained naturalness with active motion.

TLDR: MovieRoll is a comprehensive framework for long-form video generation that utilizes window-wise teacher forcing, hierarchical KV-cache compression, and cumulative distillation to enable high-quality, long-duration streaming video generation beyond training horizons.

TLDR: MovieRoll 是一个用于长视频生成的系统化框架，通过窗口强制教学、分层KV缓存压缩和累积蒸馏技术，实现了超越训练时长的长序列视频生成，并在长时序一致性和计算效率上表现优异。

RAGE: Residual-Adaptive Gating for Flow-Based Image Editing

Recent inversion-free, flow-based image editing methods evolve the source image toward the target via a differential velocity field. We observe that this velocity field does not explicitly account for which regions should be preserved, leading to unintended drift in non-editing regions. To address this, we formulate source fidelity control as a stochastic optimal control problem in which a selective fidelity term on the editing residual is enforced along the editing trajectory. Solving this problem and approximating its solution yields the Residual Gating Principle: each latent component is pulled back toward the source with a gain that decays exponentially with the local residual magnitude, so that small deviations are suppressed while large, semantically meaningful edits pass through unimpeded. The proposed method, RAGE, requires only one lightweight gated interpolation per sampling step, involves no mask estimation, attention maps, retraining, or architecture-specific modifications, and is applied on top of FlowEdit-based editors. On PIE-Bench, RAGE achieves the best overall rank among 17 methods and substantially improves background preservation on both Stable Diffusion 3 and FLUX backbones while maintaining competitive editing quality.

TLDR: RAGE introduces a Residual-Adaptive Gating mechanism for flow-based image editing that preserves background fidelity by suppressing unintended drift without requiring masks or retraining.

TLDR: RAGE 提出了一种针对基于流（flow-based）图像编辑的残差自适应门控机制，通过抑制非编辑区域的漂移，在无需掩码或重训练的情况下显著提升了背景保持效果。

MORE: Merge Once, Route Early for Training-Free Multi-Subject Composition from Large LoRA Pools

Public hubs offer many personalized LoRAs per text-to-image backbone, yet composing two of them in one image remains fragile. In FLUX, keeping a pool of $K=10$ or $20$ subject LoRAs loaded makes their deltas interfere, and even loading only the two requested LoRAs keeps both subjects in fewer than 4% of images: one subject vanishes, the two fuse, or the image splits into a collage. We present MORE (Merge Once, Route Early), a training-free two-stage pipeline for FLUX. Merge once: the whole pool is folded into a single fixed-rank adapter by the subspace-regression router of SSR-Merge, made reliable by ridge, rank and calibration choices, and factored exactly into a shared part and per-subject parts. Route early: during the first 30% of the denoising steps each subject's part is gated to its layout region; a decaying soft attention bias ties every region to its own subject tokens, and a cyclic swap guidance extrapolates away from the prediction with the subjects' regions permuted, which counteracts region homogenization. On held-out seeds, MORE keeps both subjects in 94.2% ($K=10$) and 78.3% ($K=20$) of images, against 50.8% / 35.8% for FreeFuse and 25.0% / 24.2% for LoRAShop, and reaches an ImageReward of 1.32 (best baseline 0.07), while all $K$ subjects stay in one adapter. With layouts inferred automatically it still keeps both subjects in 70.0% / 55.0% of images.

TLDR: MORE is a training-free pipeline for FLUX that enables robust multi-subject composition by merging multiple LoRAs into a single adapter and using spatial routing during denoising to prevent subject interference.

TLDR: MORE 是一个针对 FLUX 模型的无需训练的组合框架，通过将多个 LoRA 合并为单个适配器，并在去噪过程中使用空间路由策略，有效解决了多主体图像生成中的干扰和融合问题。

ScoutCache: Efficient Few-Step Autoregressive Video Diffusion via Conditional Readout and Selective Caching

Few-step autoregressive video diffusion enables efficient long video generation, but inference still incurs substantial computational costs from repeated denoising and KV cache operations. Existing acceleration methods reduce parts of this overhead, while historical KV reading and cache construction remain costly across video chunks. To address these challenges, we propose ScoutCache, a training-free framework that jointly optimizes denoising, historical KV reading, and cache construction. We observe that shallow feature changes provide useful information for estimating clean latent updates, while historical KV dependence varies across Transformer layers and attention heads but remains relatively stable across video chunks. Based on these observations, we develop Conditional Denoising Readout (CDR) to estimate intermediate denoising updates from shallow feature changes and known noise variations. We further introduce Output Risk Guided Historical KV Reading (ORHR) to allocate historical reading budgets based on attention output perturbations, and Content and Coverage Guided Sparse Cache Construction (CCSC) to reduce cache construction costs by selecting informative spatial tokens while maintaining spatial coverage. Extensive experiments on Self-Forcing and LongLive using VBench and MovieGen demonstrate that ScoutCache achieves up to 2.28$\times$ and 1.86$\times$ speedups, respectively, while consistently improving generation quality without modifying pretrained model parameters.

TLDR: ScoutCache is a training-free framework that accelerates autoregressive video diffusion by optimizing denoising, KV reading, and cache construction through conditional readout and selective token strategies.

TLDR: ScoutCache 提出了一种无需训练的框架，通过条件读出和选择性缓存机制，优化了自回归视频扩散模型中的去噪、历史KV读取和缓存构建过程，显著提升了生成效率。

Sparse Attention for Visual Generation: Toward Algorithm-System Co-Design

Rapid advances in image and video generation have enabled high-fidelity synthesis at higher resolutions and longer durations, making the quadratic cost of dense self-attention a major bottleneck as visual token sequences grow. Sparse attention seeks to preserve generation quality at lower computational cost by retaining informative query-key interactions and omitting less consequential ones, with end-to-end gains depending on the cost of constructing sparse support and the efficiency with which the system maps the resulting workload to hardware. We survey sparse attention for visual generation through a 4+1+3 analytical framework that links algorithmic design to system implementation. The framework examines how static and dynamic methods use source information and the evidence derived from it to decide which interactions to retain and at what granularity, while accounting for the roles of reuse in reducing repeated computation and correction in compensating for omitted contributions. We relate these algorithmic choices to their implementation on hardware through the framework's three system dimensions of data organization, execution, and dataflow. We summarize common evaluation metrics and discuss open challenges and research directions, providing researchers and practitioners with a reference for the joint design of sparse attention algorithms and systems.

TLDR: This paper presents a comprehensive 4+1+3 framework for sparse attention in visual generation, bridging algorithmic design with hardware-level system implementation to mitigate the quadratic cost of self-attention.

TLDR: 本文提出了一种针对视觉生成中稀疏注意力的“4+1+3”分析框架，旨在将算法设计与硬件系统实现相结合，以缓解自注意力机制计算复杂度随序列长度呈二次增长的问题。

Can Image Generators Hold a Scene Together When Space Is Broken Apart?

Image generators are good at drawing scenes in which things that belong together appear next to each other. In almost every image they generate, and in almost every benchmark used to evaluate these images, "belonging to the same whole" and "adjacent to each other" go together. Therefore, we cannot know whether the model truly grasps the scene as a whole, or simply makes each part connect with its adjacent parts. We propose GJBench to disentangle the two. It requires the model to draw a scene as a scattered jigsaw puzzle: an image is cut into interlocking pieces, then moved apart and rotated. Thus, the spatial plausibility of the scene exists only in how these pieces collectively point to a whole. The model may have a reference image, a target layout, both, or neither. We separately evaluate three requirements: whether the scene is broken down into a specified number of separate pieces, whether the pieces interlock, and whether the pieces can reconstruct the reference scene. While every open and closed model we evaluated could draw these scenes well as a whole, none could keep them together as scattered puzzle pieces, and the three requirements categorized failures into different patterns. The models draw separate pieces but miscount them in different ways, and unless their outlines are provided, the pieces rarely interlock, and their content does not reconstruct the reference image. We also found that the target layout helps the puzzle pieces fit together, while the reference image improves neither their count nor their fit. These findings show that keeping a scene continuous across space that the canvas no longer shows remains an open problem for image generators.

TLDR: The paper introduces GJBench to test if image generators understand global scene coherence by forcing them to generate spatially scattered, interlocking jigsaw puzzle pieces, revealing that current models struggle to maintain scene integrity when spatial adjacency is disrupted.

TLDR: 该论文提出了GJBench基准测试，通过迫使图像生成模型绘制空间破碎、互锁的拼图碎片，揭示了当前模型在失去空间邻近性时无法保持场景整体连贯性的缺陷。

SciWorldBench: Do Video World Models Really Understand Scientific Experiments?

While the ability of video generation models to accurately depict scientific phenomena has received growing attention, determining whether these models genuinely understand the underlying principles remains a critical challenge. Existing benchmarks typically evaluate video samples in isolation, making it difficult to expose models that simply fit superficial visual priors. In scientific experiments, a small change in one condition produces a markedly different outcome even when the rest of the setup remains fixed. Capitalizing on this sensitivity, we introduce SciWorldBench, a benchmark comprising paired scientific experiments. Each pair varies a single condition in an otherwise fixed setup, yielding two mutually exclusive expected outcomes. Each generation prompt specifies the condition but omits the expected outcome. We evaluate ten text-and-image-to-video (TI2V) models using an independent vision-language model (VLM) that assesses each video without access to the condition text or the label identifying the correct outcome. A pair is scored as correct only when both videos depict their respective scientifically correct outcomes. Across all ten models, we find that the two videos in an experimental pair frequently depict the same outcome even though the conditions require different results. This recurring failure suggests that current video world models struggle to apply the underlying scientific principles reliably during generation. We will publicly release the benchmark dataset and open-source the evaluation code.

TLDR: SciWorldBench introduces a paired-experiment benchmark to test if video generation models truly understand scientific causality, revealing that current state-of-the-art models often rely on superficial visual priors rather than underlying principles.

TLDR: SciWorldBench 提出了一个基于成对实验的基准测试，旨在评估视频生成模型是否真正理解科学因果关系。研究发现，当前主流模型往往依赖视觉表象而非真正的科学原理，难以处理条件变化带来的不同实验结果。

QASK-Edit: Adaptive Sparse Attention Across Queries and Key Sources for Efficient Video Editing

Diffusion Transformers (DiTs) have achieved strong performance in generation based video editing, but suffer from high inference latency due to the long token sequences formed by conditioning inputs and generated videos. Although training-free sparse attention reduces this cost, existing methods typically apply a fixed retention ratio or a single threshold shared across all tokens. In this work, we view video editing as conditional generation and identify a key insight: attention demand varies significantly both across different key sources (e.g., source videos, reference images, audio, text, and generated videos) and across queries within each source. Motivated by this, we propose QASK-Edit (Query-Adaptive Sparsity over Key Sources for Video Editing), a training-free sparse attention framework that allocates computation independently for each query and key source. Specifically, for each query, QASK-Edit considers both the attention mass and approximation risk of each block. It then evaluates candidate routing thresholds against each key source's risk budget, choosing exact computation or approximations to dynamically adjust sparsity for each query. Extensive experiments on three video editing models demonstrate that QASK-Edit achieves up to $1.46\times$ end-to-end speedup over dense attention pipeline while preserving both editing quality and unedited region fidelity. Furthermore, QASK-Edit generalizes directly to Reference-to-Video generation, delivering up to $1.36\times$ speedup while maintaining generation quality.

TLDR: QASK-Edit is a training-free sparse attention framework for video editing that dynamically optimizes computation by evaluating attention demand across diverse key sources and query-specific risk budgets.

TLDR: QASK-Edit 提出了一种无需训练的稀疏注意力框架，通过动态评估不同查询和关键源的注意力需求与风险，实现了视频编辑任务中计算资源的自适应分配。

Counterfactual Feature Steering for Adjacency-Preserving Identity Unlearning

Identity unlearning in text-to-image diffusion models is increasingly important for privacy, safety, and ethical deployment, as it aims to remove the model's ability to generate specific individuals. In this work, we study identity unlearning with \emph{adjacency preservation}, where the goal is to remove a target individual's identity while preserving related non-target identities. We propose Counterfactual Feature Steering (CFS), a method requiring one-time SAE training that performs bounded inference-time concept editing in the latent space of a sparse autoencoder attached to the text encoder. CFS derives a target direction by contrasting forget and retain distributions, then applies a per-token projection proportional to each token's current alignment with this direction, yielding counterfactual representations that suppress the target while limiting collateral damage. To evaluate adjacency preservation, we introduce the Adjacency-Weighted Unlearning Score (AWUS), which measures the tradeoff between target erasure and collateral damage, and extend it to face-specific identity similarity using ArcFace. Across 4,000 prompts derived from Celeb-100, spanning 100 celebrity identities, CFS achieves the strongest evaluated tradeoff between identity erasure of target identities and preservation of adjacent and generic concepts, while remaining stable across intervention strengths where other steering-based methods degrade. Independent ArcFace evaluation and a 10,000-caption COCO study further examine identity and general preservation. Quantitative nudity-suppression benchmarks and qualitative artistic-style examples provide evidence of transfer beyond identities on SD~1.4.

TLDR: The paper introduces Counterfactual Feature Steering (CFS), a method using sparse autoencoders to selectively erase specific identities in diffusion models while preserving related concepts, evaluated via a new metric, AWUS.

TLDR: 本文提出了“反事实特征引导”（CFS）方法，利用稀疏自编码器在扩散模型中实现特定身份的精准擦除，同时通过新的AWUS指标确保保留相关非目标身份特征。

ASCG: Adaptive Spatial Classifier Guidance for Surgical Concept Suppression in Diffusion Models

While text-to-image (T2I) diffusion models achieve remarkable quality, they risk producing harmful content. Existing safety mechanisms often rely on indiscriminate, global interventions that induce unintended semantic drift and degrade image quality. To address this, we propose Adaptive Spatial Classifier Guidance (ASCG), a necessity-driven framework that employs probabilistic monitoring to activate guidance selectively upon harm detection. By leveraging Grad-CAM for localized suppression, ASCG effectively removes harmful concepts while preserving superior semantic fidelity compared to conventional methods. Furthermore, we overcome the limitations of traditional metrics like NudeNet---which fails to assess semantic integrity---by introducing a VLM-based evaluation framework to provide a fine-grained, robust analysis of both safety and context preservation. Our results demonstrate that ASCG not only balances safety and fidelity but also exhibits remarkable plug-and-play synergy with existing frameworks to establish state-of-the-art performance.

TLDR: ASCG is a novel, selective safety framework for T2I diffusion models that uses probabilistic monitoring and Grad-CAM for localized harm suppression, significantly improving semantic fidelity compared to global methods.

LiveView: Real-Time Camera-Controlled Re-filming of Streaming Videos

Real-time camera-controlled video re-filming aims to synthesize target views from a streaming source video under online user-specified camera trajectories. This task poses two key challenges: maintaining synchronization and content consistency across views, and achieving low-latency rendering with long-horizon stability. Existing approaches are predominantly designed for offline full-sequence processing and rely on bidirectional attention or costly depth-based geometric preprocessing, making them unsuitable for low-latency streaming scenarios. To address these limitations, we develop a teacher-to-student framework that distills a high-fidelity bidirectional re-filming teacher into a few-step causal autoregressive student. We introduce Cross-Frame Injection to interleave each incoming source frame with its synchronized target latent, thereby establishing direct cross-view correspondence and improving content consistency. To ensure stable long-horizon generation, we further propose Shared Relative RoPE, which bounds temporal indices within the pretrained range, and Cycle Streaming Tuning, which exposes the student to extended cyclic trajectories during streaming training. These designs enable low-latency online video re-filming with precise camera control and sustained spatiotemporal consistency over long streaming rollouts.

TLDR: LiveView introduces a teacher-student distillation framework with Cross-Frame Injection and Shared Relative RoPE to enable low-latency, real-time camera-controlled video re-filming for streaming content.

TLDR: LiveView 提出了一种教师-学生蒸馏框架，通过跨帧注入（Cross-Frame Injection）和共享相对位置编码（Shared Relative RoPE），实现了低延迟、实时的流媒体视频摄像机控制重拍技术。

LEDGE: Separating Critic and Editor Failures in Text-to-Image Self-Correction

Text-to-image (T2I) self-correction combines a vision-language model (VLM) critic that diagnoses errors and proposes instructions with an image editor that executes them. End-to-end evaluation obscures whether failures arise from incorrect diagnoses or an editor unable to execute valid instructions. We introduce LEDGE (Learning Error Diagnosis in Generation and Editing), a framework that separates these bottlenecks. LEDGE compiles oracle edit instructions from GenEval while holding the generated images and the editor fixed, isolating the editor's correction capability from the critic's diagnostic accuracy. On Stable Diffusion 3.5 Large, oracle instructions raise GenEval from 72.0% to 81.2%, versus 74.1% for the baseline VLM critic. Yet 435 of 644 failed images remain unrepaired, exposing a substantial editor gap. We also use oracle labels from disjoint training prompts to supervise the critic. Oracle supervision raises exact diagnosis from 33.3% (the same VLM with our structured output, untrained) to 70.8%. With the same editor, the resulting critic improves GenEval from 42.2% (uncorrected) to 59.4% on Stable Diffusion 1.5, against 50.9% for the baseline critic, and from 72.0% to 76.0% on Stable Diffusion 3.5 Large, despite training only on Stable Diffusion 1.5. Improvements transfer to unseen object classes, a second editor, and independent T2I-CompBench judges and prompts. A small human evaluation confirms the ranking in estimated net corrections but suggests a smaller gain than detector-based evaluation. LEDGE thus distinguishes two limits of self-correction: knowing what to fix and being able to fix it.

TLDR: LEDGE introduces a diagnostic framework to decouple critic diagnosis from editor execution in T2I self-correction, revealing that current models suffer from both poor diagnostic accuracy and limited editing capabilities.

TLDR: LEDGE 提出了一种诊断框架，将文本到图像（T2I）自修正过程中的“批评者诊断”与“编辑器执行”解耦，揭示了当前模型在诊断准确性和编辑能力方面存在的双重瓶颈。

Trait Collapse in Text-to-Image Generation: Evaluating and Mitigating Personality Inconsistency in Visual Storytelling

In visual storytelling, a believable character must not only look consistent across scenes but also \textit{behave} in ways that reflect their personality. Current text-to-image (T2I) models preserve visual appearance effectively, yet consistently fail to maintain personality-relevant behavior---a failure we term \textit{trait collapse}. We show that conventional metrics (CLIP Score, DINO similarity) are blind to this failure, and that abstract personality descriptors and scene-level emotion labels do not produce consistent improvements over neutral prompts across models. To systematically diagnose trait collapse, we introduce \textbf{ViStoryBench-Personality}, a story-level benchmark combining an expert-validated Visual Cues Lexicon (Fleiss' $κ= 0.757$) with a six-dimensional metric suite covering personality alignment, cross-scene stability, and behavioral coherence. Evaluating seven T2I systems, we confirm a persistent semantic gap between abstract trait labels and visual evidence, and find that visual identity consistency does not imply personality consistency. To bridge this gap, we propose \textbf{Trait-Behavior-Visual (TBV)}, a training-free prompting framework that decomposes personality traits into renderable visual constraints. TBV improves Personality Alignment Score by up to $+22.1\%$, with larger gains in models with stronger language-conditioning capacity.

TLDR: The paper identifies 'trait collapse' as a failure in T2I models to maintain consistent character personalities, introduces the ViStoryBench-Personality benchmark, and proposes a training-free 'Trait-Behavior-Visual' (TBV) prompting framework to improve behavioral coherence.

BlockAlign: Preference Optimization for Trajectory-Distilled Video Generation

Directly aligning trajectory-distilled video generators slashes rollout costs and optimizes the deployed few-step model without teacher fine-tuning or re-distillation. However, conventional flow-matching preference objectives assume single-head local velocity supervision, mismatching the finite-interval, block-average transition interface of trajectory-distilled generators such as Parallel Decoding Distillation (PDD) and Parametric Trajectory Distillation (PTD). Naively supervising only the primary initial-subinterval velocity sub-head rather than the fused block-average velocity at block starts (DirectAlign) induces a severe training--inference mismatch that degrades quality below the unaligned baseline.
