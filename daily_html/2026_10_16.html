Which Tokens to Merge? Diffusion Dynamics for Efficient Image Generation

Token merging has emerged as an effective training-free strategy for accelerating diffusion models by reducing redundant token computation while minimizing generation degradation. A key challenge is determining which tokens can be safely merged and which should be preserved for subsequent computation. Existing method addresses this by preserving prompt-aligned foreground regions, thereby protecting semantically relevant structures. However, this text-relevance criterion overlooks background objects, local details that are not explicitly mentioned in the prompt, leading to degraded structural coherence. In this work, we propose Dynamics-aware Token Merging (DaTMe), a training-free framework that grounds token merging in the underlying diffusion dynamics. Our key insight is that token importance should reflect whether a token remains unresolved during denoising and whether it carries structural information. To this end, DaTMe constructs a dynamics- aware importance map from the Tweedie denoised estimate. The map combines a temporal signal, measuring inter-step changes in the predicted clean image, with a structural signal, capturing local high-frequency content such as boundaries and fine details. Using this map, DaTMe adaptively assigns tokens into merging roles under a given compression ratio: important tokens are preserved as anchors, while tokens that are both temporally stabilized and structurally homogeneous are selected as safe merge candidates. Experiments on PixArt-a and FLUX demonstrate that DaTMe maintains visual fidelity across the entire image, highlighting diffusion dynamics as an effective token-level merging decision in efficient generation. Our code is available at https://github.com/SeungJuCha/DaTMe.

TLDR: DaTMe is a training-free token merging framework for diffusion models that uses diffusion dynamics (temporal change and structural detail) to identify optimal tokens to merge, outperforming text-relevance-only methods.

TLDR: DaTMe 是一种用于扩散模型的免训练 Token 合并框架，通过利用扩散动力学（时间变化和结构细节）来识别可合并的 Token，在保持图像质量的同时显著提高了生成效率。

SoftCache: Adaptive Step Size Caching for Diffusion Transformers

Diffusion and flow models generate a sample over iterative steps, each a full pass through a large network, and that pass dominates generation cost. Training-free caching accelerates them by reusing activations from recent evaluations instead of recomputing them. Dynamic caching methods decide when to refresh by accumulating a cheap probe of the network input into a staleness signal and spending a fresh evaluation once it crosses a threshold. They perform hard caching: the signal is a binary switch between refresh and reuse, and every step, fresh or cached, advances by the prescribed step size. We observe that the signal carries much more than this switch. How far it sits below the threshold, its headroom, predicts the error induced by reuse. We propose SoftCache, which performs soft caching: instead of a binary switch, the headroom sets how far each cached step goes. Where the headroom is large, the sampler jumps past several scheduled nodes, and as a refresh nears, its steps shrink back toward the original step. These adaptive jumps may land between the scheduled nodes, taking the sampler off its fixed grid. SoftCache requires no training and adds no per-backbone settings: on FLUX for images and on HunyuanVideo and Wan2.1 for video, at speedups from $3.5\times$ to $6.9\times$, it is ahead of every baseline on all four fidelity metrics.

TLDR: SoftCache introduces an adaptive step-size caching mechanism for diffusion transformers that dynamically adjusts sampling jumps based on activation staleness, achieving significant speedups without retraining.

TLDR: SoftCache 提出了一种针对扩散Transformer的自适应步长缓存机制，根据激活值的陈旧程度动态调整采样跨度，在无需训练的情况下实现了显著的推理加速。

Perceive, Predict, and Plan: a Unified World Action Model for Egocentric Dexterous Manipulation

We introduce P$^{3}$-WAM, a unified world action model for egocentric dexterous manipulation that learns 3D perception, action-conditioned prediction, and goal-directed planning through three complementary modes: Perceive, which reconstructs articulated hand motion and camera trajectories from video; Predict, which generates visual futures under prescribed actions; and Plan, which jointly generates action trajectories and their anticipated visual consequences conditioned on an initial image, a language instruction, and an optional visual goal. Our model integrates a compact, structured 3D action representation into a pretrained video diffusion transformer through a mixture-of-transformers architecture with shared base weights, modality-specific adaptation, and bidirectional visual-action attention. Randomized modality visibility lets video and 3D actions serve as either conditioning context or prediction targets across the three modes. Experiments show that unified training improves all three capabilities over single-mode training, including gains in planning quality under a vision-language model evaluation. Through unified training, our model outperforms specialized baselines on 3D hand reconstruction and action-conditioned video prediction across four datasets, while producing goal-directed manipulation videos with stronger instruction adherence and action progression than video-generation baselines. Project Page: https://p3-wam.pages.dev/

TLDR: P3-WAM is a unified framework for egocentric dexterous manipulation that integrates 3D perception, action-conditioned prediction, and goal-directed planning within a shared video diffusion transformer architecture.

Speculative Forcing for Efficient Autoregressive Video Generation

Few-step autoregressive video generators enable streaming generation, yet they run every token through the final denoising step, regardless of how much refinement it needs. We find this uniform computation is often unnecessary: many tokens already closely match their eventual full-step outputs in the initial steps, while others require refinement until the end. We introduce Speculative Forcing, a framework that treats intermediate clean estimates as drafts and predicts when individual tokens can be safely accepted early. Once accepted, a token is frozen as its final output and undergoes no further denoising, while its cached keys and values remain available as context for the remaining tokens. We explore two alternative verifier designs: a training-free one based on similarity to clean content and stability across steps, and a learned one that reads the frozen generator's intermediate features to estimate each token's remaining gap to its full-step output. Across short-video, long-video, and world generation, Speculative Forcing preserves the quality of full-step sampling while substantially reducing computation. On LongLive, it matches the four-step generator with ~1.7 denoising steps per token, running over 1.5x faster at 41.7 and 37.8 fps. On Lingbot-World 2.0, where camera motion continually changes the visible content, it maintains quality with ~2.5 denoising steps per token and runs 1.35x faster at 24.5 fps.

TLDR: Speculative Forcing introduces an adaptive early-exit mechanism for autoregressive video generation that freezes tokens once they reach sufficient quality, significantly reducing redundant computation.

TLDR: Speculative Forcing 提出了一种针对自回归视频生成的自适应提前退出机制，通过在Token达到足够质量时将其冻结以避免冗余计算，从而显著提升生成效率。

ACEdit: Stable Multi-Turn Image Editing via Asymmetric Conditioning

Image editing models produce impressive results for individual edits, but their quality often deteriorates over multiple editing turns. Each editing turn introduces small distribution shifts that accumulate over successive edits, creating a gap between the input distributions encountered during the editor's original training and multi-turn inference. We introduce a training scheme that makes multi-turn image editing stable under this accumulating distribution shift. A key is to force the editor to operate on its own outputs from previous editing turns during training, bringing the principle of Self-Forcing to image editing. The main challenge is that self-generated editing trajectories deviate from ground-truth trajectories, making direct supervision unreliable. We address this challenge through a distillation objective that leverages a teacher image editor to provide a reliable training signal when the model operates on its own previous outputs. We introduce an asymmetric conditioning scheme: while the student performs edits sequentially on its own outputs, the teacher remains anchored to the original clean image and receives a compound editing instruction. Experiments show substantially reduced degradation over long multi-turn sequences while maintaining editing quality and instruction adherence.

TLDR: ACEdit addresses the quality degradation in multi-turn image editing by employing a 'Self-Forcing' training scheme with asymmetric conditioning, using a teacher model to distill stable editing signals.

TLDR: ACEdit 通过引入“自我强化”（Self-Forcing）训练方案和非对称条件机制，解决了多轮图像编辑中因分布偏移导致的质量退化问题，利用教师模型提供稳定的蒸馏信号。

AVC-Reward: Event-Centric Audio-Video Consistency Evaluation

As joint audio-video generation models become more capable, they can generate audio-visual scenes exhibiting three forms of complexity: multi-event composition, multi-layer audio mixture, and asymmetric cross-modal relations. However, current evaluations largely reuse metrics from earlier simple and unidirectional tasks. They typically evaluate an audio-video clip as a whole and rely on simple predefined relations, making them unsuitable for these increasingly complex scenes. We propose an event-centric evaluation framework that disentangles audio and visual events at the event level, analyzes each event independently, cross-validates relations across modalities, and aggregates the judgments into a coarse-to-fine, three-level consistency metric. The metric assesses global narrative harmony, event-level temporal synchronization, and fine-grained attribute alignment, providing a general formulation for audio-video consistency evaluation across diverse generation settings and cross-modal relations. We instantiate the metric as AVC-Reward, a trainable audio-video reward model. To evaluate the agreement between automatic judgments and human perception across generation paradigms and relation complexities, we construct AVC-Benchmark, which is primarily built from diverse positive and negative samples generated by various types of audio-video generation models. AVC-Reward achieves state-of-the-art alignment with human judgment among automatic methods, provides interpretable rationales that localize failures, and improves the audio-video consistency of a joint generation model when used as an online reinforcement-learning signal. Project page: https://tsuki0512.github.io/AVC-Reward.github.io/

TLDR: AVC-Reward is an event-centric evaluation framework for joint audio-video generation that disentangles audio-visual events to provide fine-grained consistency metrics, outperforming current holistic evaluation methods.

TLDR: AVC-Reward 是一个针对音视频生成任务的事件中心评估框架，通过将音视频事件解耦，提供细粒度的一致性度量，在评估效果上超越了传统的整体式评估方法。

Not Just a Subject: Capturing Adaptive Scene-Specific Variance for Visual Storytelling

Recent advancements in diffusion models have significantly improved text-to-image generation, enabling their application to visual storytelling, where coherent image sequences are generated from narrative prompts. However, existing methods primarily focus on identity preservation and often fail to adapt to diverse scene variations, resulting in semantic misalignment and structural inflexibility. We propose EOS-Story, a training-free framework that harmonizes identity consistency with scene-specific adaptation across text and vision spaces. In the text space, we decouple the narrative into identity and scene sub-prompts to prevent information dilution, but this leaves the scene without access to appearance cues. To bridge this gap, we introduce Cross-Contextual Bridging, which softly injects a phrase-level appearance residual into the scene's embedding. In the vision space, we find that scale mismatch between identity and scene subjects causes a few identity tokens to be repeatedly transferred, producing structural artifacts. We address this with Adaptive Identity Preservation, which anchors identity features at the scene-specific scale (Identity Anchoring) and integrates them via Identity-Aware Self-Attention. To evaluate performance under varied spatial compositions, we extend the Consistory+ benchmark with diverse framing scenarios (Consistory+_+). Extensive experiments show that EOS-Story achieves competitive or superior performance on both benchmarks.

TLDR: EOS-Story is a training-free framework for visual storytelling that addresses identity-scene misalignment by using Cross-Contextual Bridging for textual semantics and Adaptive Identity Preservation for spatial consistency.

TLDR: EOS-Story 是一个用于视觉叙事的免训练框架，通过跨上下文桥接技术和自适应身份保持机制，有效解决了视觉叙事中身份一致性与场景多样性之间的矛盾。

Velox: Spatial Memory Makes World Models Efficient

Video world models turn actions and camera motion into long visual rollouts, making video a natural interface for interactive simulation. Such interaction requires responsive generation, yet coherent rollouts must retain visual memory of what has already been generated. Chunked video diffusion models usually keep this context in growing KV caches, so later chunks become progressively slower. To address this bottleneck, we introduce Velox, a training-free attention router for region-level context selection using frustum slab correspondences from streaming geometry. Velox represents historical blocks as camera frustum slabs with depth intervals and obtains current-block depth through recency-priority multiwarp. For well-covered regions, frustum slab overlap softly modulates each layer's model-derived block importance. Uncovered regions retain the model-derived route without geometric bias. The fixed token-block granularity matches FlashAttention-style tiled execution and reduces the attention term that grows with rollout length. Frustum slab construction runs asynchronously with denoising on the same H200. At chunk 48, Velox accelerates attention by $4.36\times$ and denoising by $2.43\times$. On the full 158-case WBench navigation split, it remains within 0.15 API-free average points of dense LingBot-Fast-Cam. Please see our project page at https://velox-world.com/velox/ for more details.

TLDR: Velox introduces a training-free attention routing mechanism for video world models that uses geometric frustum slabs to efficiently manage spatial memory, significantly accelerating long-rollout generation without sacrificing performance.

TLDR: Velox 引入了一种无需训练的注意力路由机制，利用几何视锥体分层（frustum slabs）进行空间记忆管理，在视频世界模型中实现了长序列生成的显著加速，同时保持了高质量的生成效果。

Routing Policy Optimization for Online Diffusion Reinforcement Learning

Reinforcement learning can align visual generators with reward signals beyond their pretraining objectives, but existing score-function methods typically introduce stochasticity by perturbing the reverse denoising trajectory, leading to costly rollout-based optimization. We instead ask whether stochastic exploration can be introduced inside the generator while preserving its original sampler. Routing Policy Optimization (RPO) answers this question by turning attention routing into a stochastic exploration action. At a few selected denoising levels, each query block keeps its own block and samples further key blocks in proportion to the generator's native attention mass, or keeps dense attention. The sampled route perturbs the velocity of that transition, and a Gaussian likelihood around the dense velocity scores the perturbation with a score-function objective. RPO further uses a controlled two-rollout estimator: two rollouts share the same prompt, initial latent, routed denoising levels, and dense anchor layers, differing only in their sampled attention routes. Their reward difference provides an advantage signal for the routed transitions. Because the likelihood differentiates the dense velocity itself, the learned updates remain effective after routing is removed. At inference, RPO restores dense attention and retains the original deterministic sampler. Experiments on text-to-image and text-to-video generation show that RPO improves generation quality while achieving nearly 4x higher training efficiency than Flow-GRPO-style methods. Please see our project page at https://velox-world.com/rpo for visual results.

TLDR: Routing Policy Optimization (RPO) improves the training efficiency of diffusion-based alignment by substituting costly trajectory perturbations with stochastic attention routing within the generator, maintaining native sampling at inference.

TLDR: Routing Policy Optimization (RPO) 通过在生成器内部引入随机注意力路由，取代了传统扩散模型对齐中昂贵的轨迹扰动，在提升生成质量的同时实现了显著的训练加速。

Beyond Pairwise Repulsion: Collective Diversity via Determinant Maximization in Diffusion Sampling

Strong guidance in text-to-image (T2I) diffusion models concentrates sampling toward high-density modes of the conditional distribution, collapsing independently sampled noise vectors onto nearly identical visual prototypes. This limits utility for tasks that demand distributional breadth, most critically synthetic data augmentation. Diversity, however, is a collective property of the whole batch: a set can preserve every pairwise distance yet still collapse onto a low-dimensional subspace. Existing sum-of-pairwise noise-optimization and repulsion objectives respond only weakly to this higher-order failure. We propose DetNO (Determinant Noise Optimization), which formulates diverse generation as RKHS volume maximization on the initial noise, maximizing the log-determinant of an RBF Gram matrix over one-step Tweedie predictions. This set-level functional equals the squared batch volume; its repulsion is strongest when the batch is most collapsed and self-attenuates as diversity is achieved. An anchor-based fidelity term keeps the optimized noises near plausible regions, and Lagrangian relaxation gives a two-term objective with a single hyperparameter $λ$ governing the diversity-quality trade-off, without training or sampler modification. Across three backbones (SD1.5, SDXL, SD3), DetNO achieves leading intra-batch diversity in VendiScore and Mean Self-Similarity, and a downstream few-shot classification experiment confirms that broader mode coverage brings statistically significant accuracy gains for synthetic data augmentation in the low-shot regime. Fixing the pipeline and varying only the batch objective, both RBF-based aggregators outperform InfoNCE, and the log-determinant attains the best downstream accuracy on both benchmarks. Its diversity advantage over InfoNCE widens as the batch grows. Code is available at https://github.com/mobled37/DetNO.

TLDR: DetNO enhances diffusion model generation diversity by maximizing the determinant of an RBF Gram matrix on noise inputs, outperforming pairwise repulsion methods for synthetic data augmentation.

TLDR: DetNO通过最大化初始噪声RBF核矩阵的行列式，提升了扩散模型的生成多样性，在合成数据增强任务中表现优于现有的两两排斥方法。

SCOPE: Semantic Bounded Credit Optimization for Prompt Engineering in Text-to-image Generation

Reinforcement learning (RL) enables language models to improve text-to-image (T2I) generation by rewriting prompts for a frozen image generator. Existing methods typically apply a single signal derived from the image to individual subword tokens, although objects, counts, attributes, and relations form coherent semantic spans. We propose SCOPE, an objective based on Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) that aligns update weighting with this structure. The rewriter jointly generates an enhanced prompt and its semantic partition. SCOPE pools shifts in policy log probabilities within each span to derive a shared coefficient for its token losses while retaining a single advantage for each completion. It requires neither separate span rewards nor additional image renders. Across Qwen-Image, SD3, and FLUX.1-dev, SCOPE achieves average relative gains over vanilla GRPO of 9.48% on GeneVal and 7.62% on T2I-CompBench. Ablation studies show that both semantic boundaries and pooling within spans contribute to these gains, supporting semantic structure as a practical inductive bias for prompt engineering with RL.

TLDR: SCOPE introduces a semantic-aware reinforcement learning approach for prompt engineering in T2I models by grouping subword tokens into coherent semantic spans to optimize update weighting, achieving significant performance gains.

TLDR: SCOPE提出了一种用于文本生成图像模型的提示词工程强化学习方法，通过将子词标记分组为语义片段来优化更新权重，在无需额外奖励的情况下显著提升了生成效果。

FeaTok: Feature-Loss Tokenization for Image Generation

Discrete image generators learn the distribution of tokens produced by an image tokenizer. These token representations should be easy for generators to model, not merely enable faithful image reconstruction. Yet these tokenizers are commonly trained to reconstruct pixels, which prioritizes low-level fidelity and may disrupt representation structure useful for generation. Motivated by feature-predictive self-supervised learning, we introduce feature-loss tokenization (FeaTok), which trains discrete tokens by reconstructing pretrained features rather than pixels. Ablations across SigLIP, SigLIP2, DINOv2, and DINOv3 show better generative performance with FeaTok. In class-conditional generation, FeaTok advances the quality-compute Pareto frontier, reducing gFDr6 at 256x256 by up to 29 percent over the strongest discrete baselines and outperforming the strongest unguided continuous baseline (RAEv2) with 38 times less sampling compute. FeaTok also achieves higher human-preference reward-model scores than prior art without reward-based training and surpasses continuous and discrete text-conditional baselines on three benchmarks with only 500 fine-tuning updates after pre-training.

TLDR: FeaTok proposes a feature-loss tokenization approach for image generation that reconstructs pretrained high-level features instead of raw pixels, significantly improving generative performance and efficiency.

TLDR: FeaTok 提出了一种基于特征损失的图像标记化方法，通过重构预训练的高层特征而非原始像素，显著提升了图像生成的质量与计算效率。

Edit4D: Editable 4D Scene Generation with Textual Control

We introduce Edit4D, an editable text-to-4D scene generation framework that constructs 4D scenes from separate background and dynamic character representations. Recent advances in generative models have enabled text-driven dynamic scene synthesis, but editable 4D scene generation remains challenging due to the need to preserve spatial geometry and temporal dynamics. Existing approaches typically generate holistic scene videos, where the background and dynamic characters are coupled, making object-level editing and flexible camera control difficult. Edit4D addresses these limitations through a stage-specific generation pipeline. Given a user instruction, Edit4D first decomposes it into stage-specific prompts for background, dynamic character, and scene-character composition. Using these prompts, we synthesize multi-view scene images with text-to-image and camera-conditioned generative priors and reconstruct a background 3D Gaussian splatting representation. We then generate a character image, animate it with an image-conditioned video prior, reconstruct a 4D mesh, and bind Gaussian primitives to obtain a character representation. Finally, both components are composed through text-guided segmentation and contact-aware optimization. A global scene refinement is then performed to improve harmonization and rendering consistency. By integrating stage-specific components, Edit4D enables controllable camera motion, flexible object-level editing, and the practical construction of editable 4D scenes. Project page: https://chanwo0kim.github.io/Edit4D/

TLDR: Edit4D is a framework for controllable 4D scene generation that decomposes scenes into independent background and dynamic character representations, allowing for flexible editing, camera control, and scene composition.

Decoupled Safety Control: A Safety-Control Algorithm for Training-Free Safety Guidance

Diffusion models achieve strong performance in text-to-image generation, but they can still produce unsafe content. Training-free safety guidance offers a practical inference-time alternative that intervenes during sampling without modifying model weights. However, many existing safeguards still rely on a coupled safety-control chain. In this chain, the same unsafe condition simultaneously specifies what should be avoided, indicates the current unsafe signal, constructs the intervention, and determines when that intervention remains active. This coupling can work when unsafe factors are explicit, but becomes less reliable once harmful and benign semantics are entangled in the current generation state, often leaving residual unsafe content or unnecessarily degrading benign-generation quality or prompt fidelity. We address this problem with Decoupled Safety Control (DSC), a plug-in safety-control algorithm for training-free safety guidance. DSC reformulates training-free safety guidance as an explicit state-dependent control process, so the executed intervention is determined from the current generation state rather than inherited directly from a single unsafe condition. Experiments on standard safety benchmarks show that DSC improves representative latent- and text-space safeguards, achieving stronger unsafe-content suppression while preserving benign-generation quality and maintaining efficient inference.

TLDR: Decoupled Safety Control (DSC) is a training-free inference-time intervention method for diffusion models that decouples safety conditions from the control mechanism to better handle the entanglement of harmful and benign semantics, improving both safety and generation fidelity.

TLDR: Decoupled Safety Control (DSC) 是一种针对扩散模型的无需训练推理时干预算法，它将安全条件与控制机制解耦，从而更好地处理有害与良性语义的纠缠，在提升安全性的同时兼顾生成质量。

Rethinking Classifier-Free Guidance for TI2I Flow Matching

We study Text-and-Image-to-Image (TI2I) flow matching methods for controllable image editing. In a broader generative modeling context, Classifier-Free Guidance (CFG) is established as a key component responsible for high quality outputs. However, we argue that its role in TI2I modeling is poorly understood. Specifically, we make the surprising observation that the CFG mechanism is implicitly harmful for editing in the noisy phase of flow modeling, hindering instruction following and controllability. We propose a simple correction in the early flow steps, enabling a richer alignment-preservation tradeoff at no extra cost. The effectiveness of our approach is demonstrated on both pixel-space and latent TI2I flows.

TLDR: The paper identifies that standard Classifier-Free Guidance (CFG) hinders instruction following during the early, noisy phase of TI2I flow matching and proposes a corrective strategy to improve alignment without additional computational cost.
