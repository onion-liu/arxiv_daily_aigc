YANAMI: A Large-Scale Multimodal Dataset and Benchmark for Multiturn Stateful Interaction

Unified Multimodal Models (UMMs) integrate visual understanding, generation, and editing within a single unified framework. However, existing unified multimodal datasets and benchmarks remain dominated by single-turn tasks. Even in recent multi-turn datasets, many turns can be solved independently without tracking information across turns. Consequently, they provide limited supervision and evaluation for multimodal stateful interaction, which requires models to understand, maintain, update, and faithfully realize evolving dialogue states across turns. To address this gap, we introduce YANAMI, the first large-scale multimodal dialogue dataset and benchmark for stateful interaction, comprising YANAMI-400K and YANAMI-Bench. YANAMI-400K contains 400K multi-turn multimodal dialogues, with more than 2M images and 2.6M dialogue turns. The dialogues span four representative scenarios: Multi-Image Fusion, Image Editing, World Modeling, and Visual Reasoning, and require models to continuously track, preserve, and revise evolving multimodal dialogue states across turns. YANAMI-Bench comprises 400 multi-turn dialogues for systematically evaluating multimodal stateful interaction. It jointly evaluates Dialogue State Understanding and Visual Realization, characterizing the gap between models' ability to understand evolving dialogue states and their ability to faithfully realize them in visual outputs. Experiments show that fine-tuning BAGEL on YANAMI-400K improves image editing, generation, and multimodal stateful interaction by 10.3%, 2.0%, and 13.23%, respectively, while preserving its general visual understanding capabilities. Evaluations on YANAMI-Bench further reveal that existing UMMs still struggle to maintain, update, and visually realize evolving multimodal dialogue states.

TLDR: YANAMI is a large-scale dataset and benchmark designed to evaluate and improve how Unified Multimodal Models maintain and realize evolving states across multi-turn, stateful interactions.

TLDR: YANAMI 是一个大规模多模态数据集和基准，旨在评估和改进统一多模态模型在多轮交互中维持与实现不断演化的多模态状态的能力。

Möbius: A Unified Motion-Language Model Bridging Understanding and Generation

We aim to build a truly unified motion model that can understand, generate, and edit human motion within a single framework. Existing motion UMMs remain only partially unified: motion understanding is handled by a language model, while generation is often delegated to a separate diffusion module operating over learned motion latents. We introduce Möbius, which brings the entire diffusion process into the same multimodal representation space used for motion understanding. Clean motion, noisy motion, and text jointly participate in shared attention, while role-specific parameters process understanding and generation streams. Importantly, Möbius operates directly on continuous frame-level raw motion, requiring neither discrete tokenization nor a pretrained motion VAE. This simple formulation naturally supports motion captioning, text-to-motion generation, temporal completion, and instruction-guided editing within a single model. On HumanML3D, Möbius achieves state-of-the-art performance among unified motion models. Scaling to 1.7B parameters and 1,700 hours of motion data further demonstrates that our framework is both effective and scalable.

TLDR: Möbius is a unified multimodal model that integrates motion understanding and generation within a single diffusion framework, operating directly on raw motion data without discrete tokenization.

TLDR: Möbius 是一个统一的多模态模型，通过单一扩散框架实现了动作理解与生成的融合，且直接在原始动作数据上进行操作，无需离散化标记。

ScoreDial: Precise Control of Visual Attributes in Image Generation via Regressor Guidance from Limited Human Ratings

Requested-value control is stricter than continuous steering: given $c$ on a human-rating scale, the generated image should attain that value from only limited ratings. We present ScoreDial, which fits a differentiable regressor $R$ to 100-479 human-rated images; once $R$ is fixed, a requested value $c$ is defined on its output scale, and learned target conditioning is paired with error-adaptive guidance that repeatedly reduces $|R(\mathbf{x})-c|$ during sampling. Across tire slip resistance, interior visual complexity, food desire-to-eat, and soap translucency, using one set of sampler settings shared across all four, ScoreDial reaches requested values and achieves, on all four attributes, $R$-MAE at most 0.013 over a range of width 0.8 and Spearman $ρ=0.980$, the maximum attainable under our evaluation protocol. Because $R$ also drives sampling, we evaluate agreement with $R$ separately from matched sweeps, an independent auditor fitted to the same ratings on a backbone none of the deployed regressors uses, and blinded human judgments. MPGD-flow also reaches the requested values under $R$, but ScoreDial has larger auditor displacement on every attribute, the largest among all compared methods, and a stronger MPGD-flow setting gives up target agreement without closing this gap. On every attribute, removing target conditioning leaves low $R$-MAE but more than halves auditor displacement, separating precise score correction from attribute movement. Blinded rating studies with 15 raters per attribute show that mean human ratings track $c$ on tire and soap, with linear $R^2=0.665$ and $R^2=0.786$, and in forced-choice comparisons of matched sweeps, raters chose ScoreDial's as the clearer progression in at least 84% of judgments against every baseline.

TLDR: ScoreDial enables precise control of specific visual attributes in image generation using a regressor-guided sampling approach trained on very limited human preference data.

TLDR: ScoreDial 通过基于少量人工标注训练的可微回归器引导采样，实现了对图像生成中特定视觉属性的精确数值控制。

PATTS: Parallel-Aware Test-Time Scaling for Thought-Image Generation in Multimodal Discrete Diffusion LLMs

Test-time scaling (TTS) improves generative models by allocating additional inference-time computation to sampling, search, and verification. Existing image generation approaches, however, typically rank completed images or image-centric intermediate states. We study TTS for parallel multimodal discrete diffusion language models, in which reasoning tokens and image tokens are decoded jointly within a single generation trajectory. We first diagnose a limitation of conventional final-image selection: on GenEval, a CLIP-style image-prompt score is weakly aligned with within-prompt compositional correctness, leaving substantial headroom between verifier selection and oracle selection. We then propose PATTS, a novel parallel-aware TTS framework that searches over intermediate thought-image states using cross-modal verifier signals. PATTS uses a state-dependent reliability weight, derived from model-internal trajectory diagnostics, to modulate the influence of external semantic verification during generation. In the fixed N=32 evaluation on MMaDA-Parallel, the PATTS reaches a GenEval score of 0.844, compared with 0.628 without TTS, 0.787 for Best-of-N, and 0.810 for FKD. These results show that reliability-aware trajectory verification can improve test-time selection for parallel thought-image co-generation under the evaluated search budget. Our code release is at https://github.com/Ringo-Star/PATTS.git

TLDR: PATTS introduces a parallel-aware test-time scaling framework for multimodal discrete diffusion models, utilizing state-dependent reliability weights to verify intermediate thought-image states and improve compositional correctness.

TLDR: PATTS 提出了一种针对多模态离散扩散模型的并行感知测试时扩展（TTS）框架，通过基于状态依赖的可靠性权重验证中间思维-图像状态，从而提升了组合生成的准确性。

Behind Video Generation: Observations and Insights on Multimodal Understanding from Kling

Video generation has progressed from isolated clips to coherent short-form audiovisual sequences, and the bottleneck is shifting from rendering to semantic control. This report summarizes the observations and insights on multimodal understanding gained from successive generations of Kling. We argue that generation quality is the projection of multimodal understanding through the entire data--conditioning--alignment--evaluation chain, and we examine this chain module by module. Kling-MLLM, a shared multimodal foundation model that unifies image, video, audio, synchronized audio--video, and interleaved inputs, is specialized for each downstream module. Because a DiT is trained on far fewer training samples than a language model, we treat captioning as a semantic contract that maximizes description density, and extend it to reference-grounded captioning that makes reference bindings explicit. Prompt enhancement translates user intent into this caption distribution: basic PE is derived from captions of compelling videos and refined with text-level and generator-in-the-loop reinforcement learning, while agentic PE refines the story and retrieves compelling examples. We show that a reliable text encoder comes from perceptual training rather than from stronger language ability, and that candidates can be screened from text alone before end-to-end training. Reward modeling pairs a general reward model for explainable, localized critiques with a decision reward model for fast multidimensional scoring, treating perceptual bandwidth as a first-class design parameter. Finally, we review task-specific automatic evaluation, focusing on protocols that expose scoring shortcuts and help trace failures to their upstream source. We hope these lessons offer practical guidance for building the understanding stack behind controllable video generation.

TLDR: This report details insights from the Kling model development, arguing that high-quality video generation relies on a unified multimodal understanding stack encompassing data, conditioning, alignment, and evaluation.

TLDR: 该报告总结了Kling模型开发过程中的洞察，提出高质量视频生成依赖于涵盖数据、条件控制、对齐及评估的全方位多模态理解技术栈。

Pixel-space video generation with U-JiT

Latent diffusion and flow matching models are ubiquitous in state-of-the-art generative video models. While highly effective, relying on pretrained autoencoders to compress the data into a lower-dimensional latent space also comes with drawbacks. First, training proceeds in two stages: autoencoder pretraining, with compression and reconstruction objectives, followed by latent generative model training, precluding fully end-to-end training. Second, autoencoders also lead to an inherent loss of signal, and the trade-off between reconstruction quality and generative performance in the latent space is still an active area of research. In this paper we explore the viability of generative video models directly in pixel space. We analyze recent approaches in pixel-space generative modeling of images that rely on basic transformer designs, and identify several shortcomings that limit their effectiveness for the high-dimensional pixel-space video generation. To overcome these limitations, we propose a UNet-like transformer architecture along with improvements to the training recipe and sampling strategy. We conduct controlled experiments, training latent and pixel-space models from scratch on Kinetics-600. For the standard frames-to-video task at 128 resolution, our U-JiT model improves generative FVD by 27% over prior state-of-the-art results, and by 14% against the JiT pixel-space baseline. Scaling to higher 256 resolution, we find the improvement over pixel-space and latent-based models grows as we increase the spatiotemporal compression rate up to $64 \times 64 \times 8$. Finally, we find that U-JiT improves per-frame FID by 27% over latent models and 61% over pixel-space models on class-to-video generation, with respectively 9% and 56% reduction in FVD.

TLDR: U-JiT introduces a UNet-like transformer architecture for pixel-space video generation, effectively bypassing the limitations of latent-space models by avoiding pretrained autoencoders and achieving superior generative quality on Kinetics-600.

TLDR: U-JiT 提出了一种用于像素空间视频生成的类 UNet Transformer 架构，通过绕过预训练自编码器克服了潜在空间模型的局限性，并在 Kinetics-600 数据集上取得了显著的生成质量提升。

E2I-Flow: EEG-to-Image Reconstruction via Wavelet-Temporal Cross-Attention and Conditional Flow Matching

Electroencephalography (EEG)-based visual decoding and reconstruction aims to recover visual information from non-invasive brain signals. But there are challenges due to the complex temporal-frequency characteristics of EEG signals and the difficulty of discrete denoising framework in mapping neural activity to meaningful visual representations. Here, we propose E2I-Flow, an EEG-to-image decoding and reconstruction framework that combines a novel EEG encoder for time-frequency representation learning from EEG features, and conditional flow matching for visual decoding. A stationary-wavelet frequency branch and a temporal-mixing branch are integrated through bidirectional cross-attention and adaptive fusion, with the resulting EEG representations aligned to ground-truth image embeddings using contrastive and mean-squared-error loss supervisions. The aligned EEG representation is then used as a condition for a Transformer-based flow-matching model to predict the corresponding visual embedding for image synthesis. Experimental results show strong zero-shot EEG-to-image retrieval and improved visual reconstruction performance, outperforming several state-of-the-art baselines.

TLDR: E2I-Flow introduces a novel EEG-to-image reconstruction framework utilizing wavelet-temporal cross-attention for feature extraction and conditional flow matching for high-fidelity visual synthesis.

TLDR: E2I-Flow 提出了一种脑电图（EEG）转图像重构框架，通过结合小波时序交叉注意力机制与条件流匹配模型，实现了从脑电信号到高质量图像的准确生成。

Hybrid Particle-Voxel Dynamics for Multimodal 3D World Simulation

Predicting how the world evolves in response to robot actions is central to policy learning, evaluation, and planning. However, many action-conditioned world models predict future observations in 2D image or video space without explicitly modeling the underlying 3D scene, making spatial consistency across viewpoints and over long-horizon interactions challenging. We introduce 3D World Simulator, an action-conditioned multimodal world modeling framework that jointly predicts appearance, geometry, and tactile observations from a unified 3D representation. Our approach combines a hybrid particle-voxel autoencoder that compresses point clouds into compact 3D voxel latents with an action-conditioned latent dynamics model that predicts their evolution. The shared 3D representation supports geometrically consistent rendering from arbitrary viewpoints and flexible integration of spatially aligned tactile signals. Our experiments demonstrate interactive, long-horizon, multimodal prediction at 50 Hz. We then show how our model enables scalable data generation for policy training and evaluation using tactile and multi-view visual observations, including wrist-mounted and novel camera views. Furthermore, we scale our method to large datasets containing diverse objects and demonstrate model-based planning with unseen objects and robot configurations. Together, these results highlight the potential of hybrid particle-voxel dynamics as a foundation for multimodal 3D world simulation.

TLDR: The paper introduces a hybrid particle-voxel 3D world simulator that models appearance, geometry, and tactile feedback to achieve consistent, long-horizon multimodal world prediction for robotic planning.

Is Forgetting Unified? Exploring Concept Unlearning in Unified Multimodal Models

Unified multimodal models (UMMs) integrate image generation and visual understanding, but are existing single-pathway unlearning methods sufficient to suppress target concepts across both? In this work, we study cross-pathway concept unlearning. Our goal is to prevent a model from both generating images of a target concept and identifying it in input images, while preserving other capabilities. To this end, we introduce UUBench, a diagnostic benchmark covering 30 target concepts and 60 semantically related neighboring concepts. Experiments on representative UMMs show that the evaluated single-pathway methods achieve limited joint forgetting: generation-side editors strongly suppress target generation but yield limited forgetting in visual understanding, while understanding-side baselines struggle to achieve substantial forgetting even in their own pathway. Their direct composition also yields limited joint forgetting. We therefore propose UniForget, achieving joint forgetting rates of 80.0% on BAGEL-7B-MoT and 92.7% on Janus-Pro-7B, outperforming representative baselines. Further analysis reveals trade-offs between joint forgetting and capability preservation.

TLDR: This paper introduces UUBench to evaluate joint concept unlearning in unified multimodal models, revealing that existing single-pathway methods are insufficient, and proposes UniForget to effectively suppress concepts in both image generation and visual understanding pathways.

TLDR: 本文针对统一多模态模型提出 UUBench 基准，揭示了现有单路径遗忘方法在跨任务（图像生成与视觉理解）上的局限性，并提出了 UniForget 方法以实现针对特定概念的高效联合遗忘。

Efficient and Faithful High-Resolution Image Editing with Sparse Cross-Resolution Denoising

Instruction-based image editors still primarily operate at 1K, whereas practical editing increasingly requires faithful 2K/4K outputs. Dense high-resolution adaptation preserves the editing trajectory but incurs expensive target-source attention; editing at 1K followed by external refinement is cheaper but can separate detail restoration from the editing trajectory. We introduce FLASH, a native high-resolution editor based on sparse cross-resolution denoising. A frozen 1K branch provides a semantic and structural anchor, while a target-resolution branch reconstructs local detail within the same Transformer. An asymmetric topology retains full 1K attention, guides high-resolution queries with spatially aligned low-resolution noisy-target states, and confines high-resolution target-reference interaction to local Halo neighborhoods. Resolution-specific diffusion-state mapping and spatial YaRN adapt the pretrained editor to larger grids. An optional, training-free progressive path replaces early high-resolution denoising with a 1K prefix and completes refinement in the same editor. On VINS-4KEval, the direct model achieves the strongest structural fidelity among the compared methods at both 2K and 4K. A separate 4K forward benchmark with R = 1 records 4.2x faster total forward execution and 25.3x faster attention than dense target-reference attention. Progressive inference reduces end-to-end 4K latency by 30.5%, with a modest quality cost.

TLDR: FLASH is a native high-resolution image editor that uses sparse cross-resolution denoising to balance semantic fidelity and computational efficiency at 2K/4K resolutions.

SphereFlow: Missing Modality Imputation via Geometric Transport on Hypersphere

Missing modality imputation aims to synthesize unobserved modalities from observed ones. Existing methods treat each modality as living on its own latent manifold, forcing the generator to learn a long, unstructured jump between them. We present SphereFlow, which takes a fundamentally different view: by projecting all modalities into a shared hyperspherical latent space via a frozen self-supervised encoder, cross-modal imputation reduces to short-range geometric transport between nearby points on the sphere. Realizing this idea with flow matching, however, exposes two geometric conflicts: the standard Gaussian noise source is catastrophically mismatched with the unit-norm data manifold, and the linear interpolation path departs the sphere surface into a region where the decoder has no training signal. We resolve both with simple, geometry-aware modifications: replacing the noise source with the observed-modality latent so that the velocity field learns only the cross-modal displacement, and training the decoder with a time-weighted loss that extends its domain to the near-surface region traversed by the flow. We prove that this data-as-source formulation reduces the worst-case off-manifold deviation from a quantity that grows with the latent dimension to a small, dimension-independent bound governed by inter-modality similarity. Experiments on BraTS multi-modal MRI imputation and CT-MRI translation demonstrate state-of-the-art generation fidelity across all settings, while downstream brain tumor segmentation with our imputed data closes roughly eighty percent of the gap to the full-modality oracle. Code is available at https://github.com/WTHH031230/SphereFlow.

TLDR: SphereFlow introduces a geometric transport approach for missing modality imputation by mapping modalities onto a hypersphere and utilizing flow matching to perform short-range cross-modal translation, avoiding off-manifold errors.

TLDR: SphereFlow 提出了一种基于超球面几何传输的缺失模态补全方法，通过将多模态映射到统一的超球面并通过流匹配实现模态间的短程转换，有效解决了传统方法中存在的偏离流形问题。

StepX-OPD: Rethinking Multi-Teacher On-Policy Distillation in Diffusion Models

A deployable text-to-image diffusion model must hold several capabilities at once, such as instruction following, text rendering and human preference, and must sample in a few steps. Capabilities and few-step sampling are built in separate stages: a full-step model first consolidates capabilities through multi-teacher on-policy distillation, then serves as a fixed teacher for step distillation. Every full-step model we evaluate scores lower once compressed, and the second stage cannot begin until the first finishes. We introduce StepX-OPD, which merges the two stages: one student acquires every capability while learning to sample in a few steps, so frozen specialists supervise it on the states its own rollout visits rather than on a longer trajectory it never runs. Because a decomposition of the rollout's KL weights velocity mismatch most heavily near the clean end, we also regress the student's velocity on the low-noise part of the rollout. Separately, we find that capability integration makes models write prompt words into images on prompts that never asked for text, and we give a variant that lowers it. We evaluate on SD3.5-Medium and FLUX.1-dev across instruction following, text rendering, image quality and human preference. On both architectures, StepX-OPD attains the highest normalized average score among the four-step methods compared and exceeds the forty-step Flow-OPD model, and on SD3.5-Medium it trains with about 37% fewer GPU-hours than sequential DiffusionOPD followed by step distillation.

TLDR: StepX-OPD proposes a unified framework that simultaneously performs multi-teacher on-policy distillation and step compression for diffusion models, bypassing the traditional two-stage pipeline while achieving superior performance in few-step generation.

TLDR: StepX-OPD 提出了一种统一的框架，将多教师在线策略蒸馏与步数压缩合并为一个阶段，跳过了传统的两阶段流程，在实现少步生成的同时提升了模型性能。

FlowTranslator: Training-Free In-Image Machine Translation with Flow-Based Editing

In-image machine translation (IIMT) replaces text in an image with its translation while preserving the surrounding visual context. Existing task-specific models achieve convincing performance but require massive paired training images before and after translation. As advanced text-to-image (T2I) models have reached a practically usable level of multilingual text generation, we argue that this capability can be transferred to IIMT and present FlowTranslator. It is a training-free IIMT framework that first employs an OCR model and an LLM to generate the translation prompt, which is injected into the T2I-based diffusion model. To optimize the accurate and natural generation of translated text, we introduce two trajectory controls: Region Coupling Release (RCR) partially decorrelates target-branch noise inside the text region early in the trajectory, while Coupling Expansion (CE) amplifies the emerging edit displacement mid-trajectory. Under the EasyOCR read-back protocol, FlowTranslator achieves average PRIM BLEU and COMET scores of 20.6 and 57.2, respectively, substantially outperforming the strong training-based baseline UniTranslator. On the more challenging and realistic whole-image ScenePair photographs translated into Chinese, it reaches 40.1\% exact-match accuracy and surpasses existing editing systems, some of which only address the text-rendering stage rather than the complete IIMT task. These results establish flow-based editing as a strong, fully training-free baseline for IIMT.

TLDR: FlowTranslator is a training-free framework for In-Image Machine Translation that leverages pre-trained T2I diffusion models and innovative trajectory controls (RCR and CE) to achieve state-of-the-art performance without needing task-specific training data.

TLDR: FlowTranslator 是一种无需训练的图像内机器翻译框架，它利用预训练的 T2I 扩散模型及创新的轨迹控制技术（RCR 和 CE），在无需特定任务训练数据的情况下实现了业界领先的性能。

CounterSketch: Physics-Grounded Counterfactual Reasoning for Sketch-to-Video Generation

Counterfactual sketch animation explores how a static sketch evolves under a hypothetical intervention. We present CounterSketch, a physics-grounded framework that transforms natural-language ``What if...?'' queries into physically plausible sketch animations. The framework integrates VLM-guided scene grounding, causal event-chain reasoning, constraint-based trajectory generation, and verification. It models physical transitions to update the scene graph, computes continuous object trajectories, and validates them against physical, geometric, and semantic constraints. The verified trajectories are then projected into object-level motion bounding boxes to guide downstream sketch-video synthesis. Experiments on CounterSketchBench (80 questions) demonstrate that physics-grounded trajectories provide stronger motion guidance than direct VLM predictions.

TLDR: CounterSketch is a physics-grounded framework that translates counterfactual 'What if' queries into plausible sketch-to-video animations by combining VLM-guided reasoning with constraint-based trajectory generation.

VIGOR: Visual Gain Ordering for Hallucination Mitigation in Multimodal Discrete Diffusion Language Models

Multimodal discrete diffusion language models (dLLMs) generate responses through iterative mask prediction and offer a promising alternative to autoregressive large vision-language models.
