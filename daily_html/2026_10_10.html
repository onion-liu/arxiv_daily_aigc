InstantFusion: A Shared Latent Interface for Heterogeneous Image Generators

Diffusion/flow models have emerged as the de facto paradigms for image generation, giving rise to a diverse family of modern generators with increasingly heterogeneous architectures and representations. Despite their remarkable generation capabilities, the cross-model alignment of latent representations and the consistency of their underlying denoising dynamics remain underexplored. In this work, we uncover an intriguing cross-model compatibility: independently trained generators exhibit similar latent spatial organization, while directly handing off intermediate states between unaligned models can still preserve coarse visual content. Motivated by these observations, we propose InstantFusion, a lightweight shared latent interface that connects the denoising trajectories of heterogeneous image generators. Specifically, to enable flexible cross-model state translation, model-specific Latent AutoEncoders (LAEs) are introduced to project intermediate states from different generators into a shared latent space and decode them into the latent space of a target generator. This shared space is constrained by two complementary objectives: aligning intermediate states derived from the same image at matched noise levels and synchronizing their corresponding local denoising velocities. Once trained, InstantFusion allows denoising to seamlessly transition across frozen generators without requiring a dedicated mapping for every model pair. The versatility of InstantFusion is demonstrated across acceleration, multi-preference composition, cross-architecture on-policy distillation, and cross-model composition, achieving competitive performance across these diverse settings.

TLDR: InstantFusion introduces a shared latent interface using model-specific autoencoders to enable seamless transitions and state-sharing between heterogeneous, independently trained diffusion models.

TLDR: InstantFusion 提出了一种基于共享潜在空间的接口，利用特定于模型的自编码器实现了不同架构扩散模型之间的状态转换与协同工作。

Images Talk Back: Guiding MMDiTs by Suppressing Image-to-Text Feedback

Multimodal diffusion transformers (MMDiTs) allow the evolving image to update internal text representations through joint attention. These representations then guide subsequent image updates, raising a question: does this feedback help correct deviations from the prompt, or preserve them? We find that image-to-text (I2T) feedback can preserve the objects and attributes already represented in the evolving image, including errors. Transplanting I2T messages can rescue failures or disrupt successes. At matched intervention strengths, suppressing the influence of I2T feedback improves generation, whereas opposite controls degrade it. These findings motivate Semantic Feedback Guidance (SFG), which suppresses the influence of I2T feedback by guiding away from an auxiliary branch that strengthens this feedback and weakens text-to-image conditioning. The in-place variant, iSFG, directly suppresses I2T feedback and strengthens text-to-image conditioning, requiring no extra model evaluations. Temporal interventions motivate running the SFG branch during the first 20% of image sampling steps, requiring about $1.2\times$ the base NFE without classifier-free guidance (CFG). Experiments across three image-generation backbones show gains in alignment and preference-model scores. On FLUX, adding SFG to CFG raises GenEval from $0.6661$ to $0.7479$; on SD3.5M, iSFG adds $+1.33$ HPSv2.1 over CFG at zero extra NFE. The control also transfers to text-to-video and image-to-video generation, improving VBench scores. Code is available at https://github.com/yyc1215lll-creator/SFG.

TLDR: The paper introduces Semantic Feedback Guidance (SFG), a method that improves MMDiT image and video generation by suppressing image-to-text (I2T) feedback loops that tend to solidify early generation errors.

TLDR: 本文提出了语义反馈引导（SFG）方法，通过抑制多模态扩散模型（MMDiT）中固化早期生成错误的“图生文”反馈机制，显著提升了图像和视频的生成质量。

Neurons++: A Unified Visual-Stream Framework for Image and Video Reconstruction from fMRI with Neural-to-Visual Interpretability

Decoding visual experience from fMRI is important for understanding human perception and developing brain-computer interfaces. Recent generative decoding methods can reconstruct plausible images and videos, but visual plausibility alone does not ensure that the output is faithfully grounded in brain activity. In this work, we propose Neurons++, a visual-stream fMRI reconstruction framework for both static image and dynamic video reconstruction. Compared with our conference version Neurons, Neurons++ extends the paradigm from video-only reconstruction to visual-stream reconstruction for both images and videos, and replaces parallel task conditioning with serial cortex-inspired decoding. The model first uses image-fMRI pretraining to obtain a stable spatial-semantic initialization, and then performs subject-specific fine-tuning for image and video reconstruction separately. The serial design organizes fMRI-derived representations into explicit spatial, semantic, language-level, and temporal cues. Extensive experiments show that Neurons++ achieves state-of-the-art video reconstruction and competitive image reconstruction across standard benchmarks. Beyond reconstruction fidelity, neural-to-visual analyses indicate that Neurons++ exhibits qualitative directional controllability under neural perturbation, that its fMRI encoder read-out is sensitive to stimulus-relevant cortical features, and that it supports ROI-guided semantic modulation. Code and model weights are available at: https://github.com/xmed-lab/NeuronsPlusPlus.

TLDR: Neurons++ is a unified framework for image and video reconstruction from fMRI, utilizing a serial, cortex-inspired architecture that maps brain signals into explicit spatial, semantic, and temporal cues to improve both fidelity and neural interpretability.

TLDR: Neurons++ 是一个用于从 fMRI 重建图像和视频的统一框架，通过采用受大脑皮层启发的串行架构，将脑信号映射为空间、语义和时间线索，从而提升了重建的保真度与神经可解释性。

GLIMPSE: Global-Local Indexing with Multi-Precision Spectral Encoding for Explorable Video World Models

A camera-controlled video world model must keep a scene consistent when the camera returns to it. Memories that render stored features into the target frame can turn depth and pose errors into ghosting or duplicated structure. Positional transport avoids that resampling by relocating a historical patch's address rather than its content, but assigns full positional precision even to inaccurate projections. We present GLIMPSE, a global-local memory interface with band-wise multi-precision coordinates and geometry-conditioned spectral attenuation to reduce sensitivity to inaccurate projected addresses. Local patch memory supplies view-specific appearance, while recurrent global memory provides scene context. Footprint-guided retrieval selects historical patches under a fixed local read budget. Under matched retrieval and read budgets, GLIMPSE improves geometric correspondence and photometric consistency over deterministic positional transport.

TLDR: GLIMPSE introduces a multi-precision memory interface for video world models that mitigates ghosting and geometric inconsistencies by combining local view-specific appearance with geometry-conditioned spectral attenuation.

TLDR: GLIMPSE 提出了一种用于视频世界模型的多精度记忆接口，通过结合局部视图外观与几何条件下的谱衰减，有效解决了重采样带来的重影和几何不一致问题。

What We Talk About When We Talk About Emotion Editing

Emotional image editing aims to change the emotion a scene conveys while preserving its essential content. Because the same scene can support different emotional interpretations, achieving an emotional goal requires visual changes grounded in the source context. Motivated by the Component Process Model, which links emotional responses to event appraisal, we investigate neural representations as a reference for these editing decisions. Using TRIBE v2, an fMRI encoding model, we analyze 400 source-target pairs across eight emotion categories. Hidden states from EmoEdit, EmoEditor, and AffectDelta correspond more strongly to predicted target-image responses in visual regions than in affect-related regions. This imbalance motivates ROI-ReFT, which combines source visual responses as a content reference with a predicted affective response change as an editing direction. Two interacting branches use both conditions to guide a frozen editor throughout generation. Predicted responses of the generated image supervise affective change and constrain visual drift. On a post hoc selected subset of 400 editing requests, ROI-ReFT achieves 91.75% target-emotion accuracy and 83.25% joint success, with the same output both matching the target emotion and reaching source-output DINO-I of at least 0.80. Our code and dataset will be publicly released.

TLDR: The paper introduces ROI-ReFT, a method for emotional image editing that leverages fMRI-based neural representations to guide generation, ensuring targeted emotional changes while preserving content integrity.

TLDR: 该论文提出了一种名为 ROI-ReFT 的情感图像编辑方法，利用基于 fMRI 的神经表征来指导生成过程，在实现目标情感转换的同时保持原始场景的内容一致性。

ELMA: Benchmarking Anaphoric Compositions for Long-Term Text-to-Motion Generation

Text-guided long-term human motion generation is essential for applications such as continuously operating digital avatars. Despite recent progress on short clips, existing models struggle with multi-segment instructions that involve anaphora, where constraints established in earlier segments must be preserved over time. In this paper, we introduce a novel task of long-term motion generation from such anaphoric compositions. To mitigate this, we present ELMA, an episodic long-term motion dataset with dense segment-level annotations that preserve cross-segment dependencies. Built via a fully automated collection and filtering pipeline, ELMA extracts high-quality, continuous 30-second clips from in-the-wild videos, capturing the episodic narratives necessary for long-range state modeling. Leveraging this dataset, we fine-tune state-of-the-art motion generation models to incorporate long-range context. Extensive experiments reveal fundamental shortcomings in current models when handling context-dependent instructions. We demonstrate that ELMA enables significant improvements in both global physical realism and semantic alignment, setting a robust new baseline for long-term motion synthesis.

TLDR: The paper introduces ELMA, a new dataset and benchmark designed to evaluate and improve long-term text-to-motion generation by focusing on cross-segment anaphoric dependencies.

TLDR: 本文提出了ELMA数据集与基准，旨在解决长文本到动作生成中跨片段指代关系的一致性问题，从而提升长时序动作生成的质量。

Stride: Scale-Aware Multi-Agent Evolutionary Refinement for Infographic Generation

Infographic generation challenges text-to-image models because a single image must satisfy numerous visual, textual, and semantic constraints. Iterative agentic refinement with a multimodal verifier is a natural remedy, but existing methods face a scale mismatch: local edits cannot correct global composition errors, while full regeneration may destroy already-correct regions. We introduce Stride, a constraint-grounded multi-agent framework that treats refinement over any frozen generator as a history-aware evolution strategy. Stride separates constraint evaluation - whether each requirement is satisfied - from visual grounding - where and how it is violated. It then routes global revisions to a composer subagent and local corrections to a retoucher subagent. Each subagent conducts internal planning and generates multiple candidates, while Stride accepts a candidate only when it improves the verifier score, reducing regression across iterations. Because stochastic generators and imperfect verifiers make any single step unreliable, it is this combination of scale-aware routing, per-step redundancy, and monotonic acceptance that yields stable improvement across iterations. With fully open-weight generator-verifier systems, Stride outperforms proprietary systems including Nano-Banana-2.0 and Seedream-5.0-Pro on BizGenEval, matches GPT-Image-2.0 and surpasses Seedream-5.0-Pro on IGenBench, and achieves a new state-of-the-art score on WISE, while substantially outperforming existing agentic methods across all evaluated benchmarks.

TLDR: Stride is a multi-agent framework that improves infographic generation by decoupling global and local refinements through scale-aware routing and monotonic evolution, achieving SOTA results across several benchmarks.

TLDR: Stride 是一个多智能体框架，通过比例感知路由和单调进化策略，将全局布局与局部细节修正解耦，显著提升了信息图生成的质量并达到了行业领先水平。

TextVBench: Evaluating Text Rendering and Preservation in Video Generation

Modern video generators can synthesize convincing scenes while rendering a few characters incorrectly, omitting requested text, or changing it across frames. Measuring these failures is harder than applying OCR independently to each frame: when a scene contains multiple text targets and multiple noisy OCR regions, the evaluator must first determine which observation belongs to which intended text. We introduce TextVBench, a benchmark and evaluation framework that formulates visual-text evaluation as a multi-target, multi-instance spatiotemporal correspondence problem. TextVBench contains two complementary tracks: TextVBench-T2V measures the creation and maintenance of prompt-specified text, while TextVBench-Ref2V measures preservation of text from reference images. Both tracks use target-conditioned candidate selection rather than unconstrained VLM transcription, support one target mapping to multiple OCR regions, and separate frame-level spatial fidelity from cross-frame temporal fidelity. A length-weighted harmonic score summarizes these factors, while extra-text diagnostics expose failure modes hidden by a single OCR score. The benchmark is organized around controlled text, layout, carrier, and motion attributes so that aggregate scores can be traced back to specific capability limits. Across the evaluated model runs, MiniMax-H3 reaches a T2V TextVScore of 68.32, while the strongest Ref2V score is 56.51; attribute slices reveal a pronounced drop as the number of simultaneous reference text regions increases.

TLDR: TextVBench provides a novel spatiotemporal evaluation framework for video generation models, focusing on accurate text rendering, preservation, and cross-frame consistency through a multi-target correspondence approach.

TLDR: TextVBench 为视频生成模型提供了一个新的时空评估框架，通过多目标对应方法专门解决文本渲染、保存及跨帧一致性的评估难题。

VideoAWR: Reinforcing Video Flow Models with Decoupled-Timestep Advantage-Weighted Rectification

Regression-based RL for flow models mandates sampling \emph{without} classifier-free guidance (CFG) to align the rectification target with the reference marginal velocity. Extending Advantage-Weighted Rectification (AWR), which dynamically scales this regression target based on reward, to video generation encounters a cold-start pathology: unguided rollouts collapse into low-fidelity regions where intra-group reward gaps remain wide but no longer track perceptual quality, rendering the optimization target arbitrary. Conversely, utilizing guided rollouts to restore fidelity, while keeping the regression target tied to the guided sampling distribution, provably triggers a geometric guidance blow-up under exponential-moving-average (EMA) updates. We propose \textbf{VideoAWR}, which resolves this tension by untying sampling from the optimization anchor. An EMA reference policy generates \emph{guided} rollouts to restore reward separability, while the trainable policy is rectified against the \emph{unguided} conditional anchor. We prove this decoupled objective is mathematically exact: the centered advantage cancels the anchor in expectation, filtering out the reward-neutral guidance offset and retaining only the reward-correlated covariance increment. Consequently, inference runs single-branch and CFG-free, yet inference-time CFG preserves its original semantics. Furthermore, we introduce \emph{Decoupled Timestep Rectification}, supervising each rollout at an independently sampled timestep without bias to improve sample efficiency. Experiments on Wan2.1-1.3B and 14B demonstrate stable optimization and superior visual and motion quality in \emph{both} CFG-free and CFG-enabled regimes. TLDR: VideoAWR introduces a decoupled reinforcement learning framework for video flow models that resolves the trade-off between reward-guided training and CFG-free inference by separating the sampling distribution from the optimization anchor.

Exploring Attention Sinks in Text-to-Image Transformers from Diffusion and Autoregressive Models

Attention sinks are tokens or positions that consistently receive disproportionate attention mass, often beyond what their semantic content would suggest. Despite extensive study in large language models, their behavior and functional relevance in text-to-image (T2I) Transformers remain largely unexplored. To fill this gap and better understand how text-to-image Transformers allocate and route attention, we explore this phenomenon of text-to-image transformers across bidirectional diffusion models and causal autoregressive models. Specifically, we explore whether attention sinks exist, where they emerge, and how they functionally influence generation. Through systematic attention analysis and targeted interventions, we identify that attention sinks emerge in both bidirectional diffusion and autoregressive models but exhibit distinct patterns. The autoregressive model concentrates attention on the first and global-scale tokens, whereas the bidirectional models develop sinks near modality boundaries, within text regions, and at spatial corners. Moreover, these patterns vary across generation stages and are localized to a sparse subset of layers and heads. To assess their functional relevance, we perturb sink-related heads and token positions. It turns out that perturbing sink-ranked heads produces larger average changes than non-sink comparisons, while some sink-position perturbations have limited effects on bidirectional model scores. We further introduce register tokens to redistribute attention sinks and find that several configurations preserve or modestly improve generation performance. Overall, attention sinks are architecture-, inference-stage-, and component-dependent phenomena, providing a basis for mechanistic analysis and principled attention design in text-to-image transformers.

TLDR: This paper investigates the role and emergence of 'attention sinks' in both diffusion and autoregressive text-to-image Transformers, revealing architecture-specific patterns and their functional impact on generation quality.

TLDR: 本文深入研究了扩散模型和自回归文本生成图像Transformer中的“注意力汇聚（Attention Sinks）”现象，揭示了其架构依赖性的分布规律及其对生成质量的功能性影响。

CoVA: Composing VAE Representations for Generative Learning

Representation alignment with pretrained visual encoders improves diffusion transformer training but requires auxiliary encoders and extra training overhead. Recent approaches instead exploit representations from the generative model's own variational autoencoder (VAE). However, reconstruction-oriented VAE features are not optimized for the semantic discrimination and spatial structure needed for generative learning. We introduce \textbf{CoVA} (\textbf{Co}mposed \textbf{VA}E Representations), a lightweight framework that composes VAE features online into stage-wise guidance for flow-matching training. A small latent composer is jointly trained with class supervision, masked latent reconstruction, and horizontal-flip consistency, yielding semantically discriminative, spatially structured dense features and sample-dependent class posteriors. CoVA aligns intermediate SiT features with the composed representations early in training, then removes the increasingly detrimental alignment and reuses detached composer posteriors for sample-dependent soft label conditioning. We further uncover a hidden confound in soft label conditioning: averaging class embeddings attenuates their magnitude, coupling semantic interpolation with conditioning strength. This attenuation alone can substantially improve FID while degrading Recall, revealing a non-semantic source of the apparent gain. CoVA corrects this confound by restoring the hard-label magnitude, isolating posterior-dependent directional semantics. CoVA adds only 8.5M training-only auxiliary parameters (1.3\% of SiT-XL/2) and introduces no inference-time overhead. On ImageNet 256$\times$256, CoVA reduces SiT-XL/2 FID from 17.2 to 7.4 (\textbf{57\%}) in 400K steps. With classifier-free guidance, it achieves an FID of \textbf{2.03} in only 400 epochs, versus 2.06 after 1,400 epochs for vanilla SiT-XL/2.

TLDR: CoVA introduces a lightweight framework to compose VAE features for flow-matching training, significantly accelerating convergence and improving image generation quality while identifying and fixing a hidden confound in soft label conditioning.

TLDR: CoVA 提出了一种通过组合 VAE 特征来辅助流匹配训练的轻量化框架，在显著提升生成质量和收敛速度的同时，纠正了软标签条件生成中的一个隐蔽偏差。

ThinkDrag: Semantic Drag-Based Image Editing with Visual Reasoning

Drag-based image editing provides an intuitive interface for spatially controlled image manipulation by allowing users to specify handle and target points. Existing methods have made substantial progress through optimization-based, one-step, and feed-forward formulations, but they often interpret drag instructions as geometric displacements. This limits their effectiveness when the desired edit depends on interpreting image content and inferring a plausible semantic transformation from sparse point constraints. We introduce ThinkDrag, a unified multimodal framework for drag-based image editing with visual reasoning. ThinkDrag is trained to associate point constraints with meaningful object- and scene-level transformations, and can optionally produce an explicit reasoning trace that interprets the intended edit before image generation. This reasoning trace improves interpretability, particularly for ambiguous drags. Drag Endpoint Markers, motivated by an analysis of cross-image attention, provide an explicit correspondence signal that grounds each drag in image space. To support this framework, we construct a supervised dataset of semantic drag transformations paired with reasoning traces and introduce DragBench++, a benchmark targeting challenging drag-based editing scenarios with reference edit solutions. Experiments show that ThinkDrag achieves state-of-the-art performance, improving generation quality and plausibility while maintaining competitive point-following precision.

TLDR: ThinkDrag is a multimodal framework that enhances drag-based image editing by incorporating visual reasoning, allowing the model to interpret semantic transformations rather than just geometric displacements.

TLDR: ThinkDrag 是一个引入视觉推理的图像编辑框架，它通过理解语义变换而非仅进行几何位移，实现了更自然、准确的拖拽式图像编辑。

PAGE: Parts-Aware Guidance for Co-Speech Gesture Portrait Video Generation

Co-speech gesture video generation aims to synthesize realistic, audio-driven portrait videos from a single reference image and a speech signal. Despite advances in visual quality, diffusion-based approaches still suffer from degraded local details and imperfect audio-visual alignment. We present PAGE (Parts-Aware GuidancE), a general, backbone-agnostic optimization framework for diffusion-based generation that enhances local spatial fidelity and global audio-visual consistency at both the training-objective and denoising-trajectory levels. During training, we propose Parts-Specific Reweighting (PSR), which adaptively increases supervision in semantically important regions such as lips, eyes, and hands, improving visual fidelity in perceptually critical areas. During inference, we introduce Consistency Classifier Guidance (CCG), an audio-conditioned latent classifier that identifies and corrects audio-visual inconsistencies in the generated video. We further design Differential Guidance (DG), an efficient inference strategy that refines facial and body motion separately while avoiding cross-region interference. To enable large-scale evaluation, we construct the Chinese Announcer Speech (CAS) dataset, the first comprehensive Chinese benchmark for co-speech gesture portrait video generation. Compared with the widely used PATS dataset, CAS includes substantially more identities, video clips, and scene diversity. Extensive experiments on both PATS and CAS demonstrate that PAGE achieves state-of-the-art performance in perceptual quality, gesture diversity, and audio-visual alignment. Project page: https://aigc-explorer.github.io/PAGE/.

TLDR: PAGE is a backbone-agnostic framework for co-speech portrait video generation that improves local fidelity and audio-visual consistency via parts-specific reweighting and specialized diffusion guidance, accompanied by a new large-scale Chinese dataset (CAS).

Golden Anchor for Video Generation

Video generation supports digital content creation, with applications in virtual avatars, AI-driven filmmaking, interactive game engines, and world models. State-of-the-art systems achieve controllability primarily through image-to-video (I2V) generation, in which an injected anchor image determines the identity, composition, and style of the resulting video. In modern pipelines, this anchor is synthesized by a text-to-image (T2I) generator. This raises a largely overlooked question: Are images obtained directly from the T2I model truly suitable for current video models? Our analysis shows that anchor aesthetic and quality scores are weakly correlated with downstream video quality. The structure-and-texture score is negatively correlated with I2V video quality in both the visual and motion dimensions. Fine-tuning the T2I model on an image reward accordingly yields anchors with visibly denser structural and character detail: they score higher on image metrics, yet lower the video reward on every I2V model we test and change downstream motion unpredictably. We therefore introduce Golden Anchor Learning (GAL), to our knowledge the first framework for learning golden anchors by optimizing a T2I generator with downstream video feedback. GAL evaluates candidate anchors through the videos they induce under a frozen I2V model and converts video rewards into image-level preferences for DPO fine-tuning of the T2I generator. On all four I2V models, GAL raises the VBench average by $2.97\%$--$8.46\%$ over the untuned generator. The rendered clips also score higher under VideoReward-Overall, and their extracted frames under HPSv3 and UniPercept. GAL yields consistent motion gains across all four I2V models, as measured by a $5.62\%$--$38.24\%$ increase in VBench dynamic degree. Although GAL is trained only on HunyuanVideo-1.5 renders, these gains extend to the other three I2V models.

TLDR: The paper identifies that standard T2I-generated anchors are poorly optimized for I2V video generation and proposes Golden Anchor Learning (GAL), a framework that fine-tunes T2I models using downstream video-level feedback.

TLDR: 该论文指出传统的文生图（T2I）锚点并不直接等同于高质量视频生成的最佳起点，因此提出了“金锚点学习（GAL）”框架，通过视频生成的反馈来优化T2I模型，从而显著提升视频生成质量与动态表现。

Destination-Conditioned Adaptation with Semantic-Geometric Alignment for Generative Inbetweening

Generative inbetweening aims to synthesize temporally coherent intermediate frames between a start frame and a target frame.
