VidDub: Replacement-Aware Distillation for Two-Step Video Dubbing

Video dubbing aims to synthesize lip movements that match target speech while preserving the speaker identity, head pose, and background of a source video. Existing editors are predominantly trained via self-reconstruction under original paired audio, even though the source mouth becomes a conflicting target when driving speech is replaced. We introduce replacement-aware cross-audio supervision: paired-audio samples use full-frame reconstruction, whereas unpaired replacement-audio samples mask out source-mouth reconstruction losses and supervise the mouth solely through audio-visual synchronization while preserving unmasked facial regions and background. Because offline dubbing provides the complete source video and target speech beforehand, we present VidDub, a full-context diffusion editor distilled into two denoising steps (2 NFE). Our replacement-aware teacher first learns accurate lip synchronization without regressing toward conflicting source mouth movements. Matched trajectory regression then transfers the teacher's CFG-guided trajectories to a two-step student, and distribution matching distillation recovers visual quality after step compression. We will release the curated training dataset, VidDub-Bench, fixed paired- and cross-audio evaluation manifests, preprocessing outputs, and unified evaluation code. Across three training seeds, masking out conflicting source-mouth reconstruction improves cross-audio Sync-C by 0.833; the final two-step model combines strong lip synchronization with superior visual quality, identity preservation, and visual speech recognition across four benchmarks.

TLDR: VidDub introduces a replacement-aware distillation framework for video dubbing that solves the 'conflicting mouth' problem in cross-audio synthesis and achieves high-quality lip synchronization in just two diffusion steps.

TaoMate-HD: Progressive-Resolution Autoregressive Audio-Video Generation

Streaming digital humans produce detailed video while continuing speech as new text arrives. High-resolution denoising is costly, and text updates can cause repeated or interrupted speech. We present TaoMate-HD, an autoregressive model for high-resolution audio-video streaming. For each short segment, or chunk, two denoising steps establish coarse video structure at low resolution. A final step refines the video to high resolution, with audio generated throughout the same trajectory. Each stage passes its audiovisual predictions to the same stage of the next chunk as history. Across text updates, the model retains this history to infer what has already been spoken. Continuation training utilizes asymmetric text conditioning, with separate text windows for generation and distillation. Experiments show a 1.6x speedup over direct high-resolution generation with the same model and hardware. Optimized stage-parallel deployment provides a further 1.9x overall speedup over serial progressive generation. Our continuation training reduces character error rate by 11.18 percentage points relative to the baseline. The model also supports hour-long audiovisual generation. Our project page is https://taoliveaigc.github.io/TaoMate-HD.

TLDR: TaoMate-HD is an autoregressive model that enables high-resolution, long-duration audio-video streaming for digital humans through a novel progressive-resolution denoising strategy and history-preserving continuation training.

TLDR: TaoMate-HD 提出了一种基于自回归的流式高分辨率音视频生成模型，通过渐进式分辨率降噪和历史状态保持策略，实现了数字人长时间、高质量的实时交互生成。

TaoMate-FVSR:Audio-Guided Facial Video Restoration

AI-generated videos often depict people within detailed scenes while leaving their small facial regions poorly resolved. Visual super-resolution can sharpen these regions without resolving whether the recovered lips and teeth agree with the spoken audio. We present an audio-guided facial video restoration framework that addresses this ambiguity within a reference-guided video flow model. Source--noise mixing initializes reconstruction from a partially preserved observation, and structured reference injection separates appearance anchors from continuous motion context. Training combines restoration of degraded images and videos with correction of degraded dubbed sources under target audio. Appearance and synchronization supervision allow the model to recover facial detail and support visual dubbing when the audio is replaced. On talking-head restoration benchmarks, our method achieves the best paired perceptual similarity and synchronization scores among the compared restoration methods. Component comparisons examine audio conditioning, the reconstruction formulation, and reference injection. VideoEditor-MoE: Scaling Complex Video Editing with Mixture of Experts

Complex video editing requires understanding complex instructions while jointly fulfilling multiple editing requests. Meeting these demands through dense model scaling substantially increases computational cost. We introduce VideoEditor-MoE, the first open-source mixture-of-experts framework for video editing. Beyond sparse MLP-MoE layers, MoE-Attention expands attention capacity through routed key--value experts while preserving global interactions among video tokens. Initialized from a 5B video editing model, VideoEditor-MoE scales to 14B and 56B parameters, with approximately 8B parameters activated per token in both variants. Comparisons with existing methods and two complementary baselines, Explicit-MoE and MLP-MoE, assess its benefits over explicit expert guidance and MLP-only expert architectures. Further analyses demonstrate that all experts remain active and reveal that learned routing responds more strongly to sample content and diffusion noise levels than to human-defined editing categories. Spatial analysis further indicates sensitivity to object-level semantic features. We also identify decoupled weight decay as a strategy for stabilizing expert scaling.

TLDR: VideoEditor-MoE introduces a sparse Mixture-of-Experts architecture for video editing, utilizing both MLP-MoE and novel routed key-value attention experts to scale performance efficiently while maintaining computational feasibility.

TLDR: VideoEditor-MoE 提出了一种用于视频编辑的稀疏专家混合（MoE）架构，通过引入 MLP-MoE 和路由键值注意力专家，在大幅扩展模型参数量的同时有效控制了计算成本。

Efficient Hybrid Distillation: Synergizing Score and Adversarial Objectives for One-Step Diffusion

Numerous distillation methods have been developed to accelerate diffusion models. Recent research indicates that hybrid strategies---blending different paradigms---yield superior results compared to single-method approaches. However, current works predominantly rely on a simple combination of objectives, leaving the question of how to effectively synergize them largely underexplored. In this paper, we specifically investigate the synergy between Score Distillation and Adversarial Distillation. We reveal that their operating zones are distinct and complementary: Score Distillation is effective in high-noise regimes for capturing the global distribution yet lacks fine-grained supervision in low-noise regions. Conversely, Adversarial Distillation is effective in low-noise regions but is prone to instability and suffers from discriminator failure when applied to high-noise intervals. Leveraging this insight, we propose Efficient Adversarial Score Distillation (EASD), a framework that synergizes these objectives via a regime-adaptive sampling strategy, directing each objective to focus on its effective interval. We further apply this paradigm to DiT-based Flow Matching models. Empirically, our method achieves a promising one-step FID of 1.39 on ImageNet 256$\times$256 using the SiT-XL/2+REPA model. This work provides a concrete guideline for maximizing the potential of hybrid distillation. Our code and model are available at https://github.com/pf0607/EASD .

TLDR: The paper introduces Efficient Adversarial Score Distillation (EASD), a framework for one-step diffusion distillation that uses a regime-adaptive sampling strategy to combine the complementary strengths of score and adversarial distillation.

TLDR: 本文提出了高效对抗分数蒸馏（EASD）框架，通过一种区域自适应采样策略，协同利用分数蒸馏和对抗蒸馏的互补优势，实现了高效的一步扩散模型蒸馏。

One Image Is Enough: Self-Generated Supervision for Personalizing Human Video Diffusion at Test Time

Pose-driven human video diffusion animates a person from a single reference image and a target pose sequence, yet it struggles to preserve identity, rendering the face and garments unfaithfully as the pose departs from it. Personalization is the standard remedy, but its supervision is missing: one image fixes identity at a single pose, while a multi-pose subject video cannot be assumed in practice. Instead, we synthesize the missing supervision. A domain-tuned, pose-conditioned generator produces pseudo-ground-truth frames at selected key poses. An inference-time reference-only sampler steers these candidates toward the subject and keeps the most identity-consistent. A per-subject low-rank adapter then trains on static clips of them, configured from the reference alone, with no subject video, ground truth, or labels. Although lower in fidelity, these synthetic frames supervise more effectively than real ones. Real frames as captured carry motion that erodes the temporal prior, while a static clip conveys appearance alone. On TikTok and UBC Fashion, the adapted model improves the base on every metric, inverts the identity-versus-motion trade-off, and matches or surpasses adaptation on five real frames despite using none. Code and weights are available at https://github.com/SadmanPinon/one-image-is-enough.

TLDR: This paper introduces a self-supervised personalization framework for human video diffusion that leverages a domain-tuned generator to synthesize 'pseudo-ground-truth' frames from a single reference image, enabling identity-preserving animation without the need for multi-pose training videos.

TLDR: 该论文提出了一种用于人物视频扩散模型的自监督个性化框架，利用领域微调的生成器从单张参考图中合成“伪基准真值”帧，实现了在无需多姿态训练视频的情况下保持身份一致性的动画生成。

RouteREPA: Routed Representation Alignment for Just Image Transformers

Representation alignment (REPA) is widely used to accelerate the training of latent-space diffusion transformers (DiTs). However, applying it to pixel-space models such as Just Image Transformers (JiT) is challenging. Our analysis reveals a pronounced representation conflict between discriminative alignment and pixel-space generation. Compared with its latent counterpart, JiT learns representations that are substantially less aligned with DINOv2. Applying REPA to JiT consistently reduces the reconstructability of its intermediate representations, whereas this effect is not observed in Latent JiT. We attribute this conflict to JiT directly reconstructing pixels, which requires its representations to hierarchically encode discriminative semantics and reconstruction-relevant visual information across network depths. To address these challenges, we propose Routed Representation Alignment (RouteREPA), which uses lightweight, representation-dependent routers to adaptively route task-oriented representations for alignment and generation. We further apply routing across multiple network depths to accommodate their depth-dependent representation requirements. Extensive experiments on ImageNet-1K at 256x256 resolution show that RouteREPA consistently improves JiT across model scales and training budgets, outperforming standard REPA. On JiT-H/16, RouteREPA further surpasses PixelREPA using only one-third of its training epochs without classifier-free guidance.

TLDR: RouteREPA addresses the conflict between discriminative representation alignment and pixel-level reconstruction in Image Transformers by introducing adaptive, depth-dependent routing mechanisms.

TLDR: RouteREPA 通过引入自适应的深度感知路由机制，解决了图像 Transformer 在进行判别式表征对齐与像素级重建任务时产生的冲突问题。

Unlocking the Potential of Contrastive Regularization for Diffusion Transformer Training

Representation regularization has emerged as an effective way to improve diffusion transformer training. Yet one of the most canonical regularizers--InfoNCE-style contrastive learning--remains largely underexplored in this setting. We naturally ask a question: can standard contrastive learning serve as an effective regularizer for diffusion transformers? Surprisingly, we find that naive InfoNCE regularization brings only marginal gains. Our analysis reveals that the objective is easily dominated by a shortcut: near-orthogonal noise components in diffusion inputs provide a bypass for enlarging negative-pair distances while weakening positive-pair alignment, especially at high noise levels. To unlock the benefit of contrastive regularization, we propose Clean Representation-ANchored Contrastive (CRAN) regularization. Specifically, CRAN anchors contrastive dispersion with clean-sample representation distances, transferring clean relational semantic structure to noisy diffusion representations to suppress the noise shortcut. Extensive experiments on ImageNet show that CRAN consistently improves diffusion transformer training across model scales and training budgets, outperforming internal feature-alignment and dispersive regularization baselines while matching external DINO-guided REPA. These results re-establish InfoNCE-based contrastive learning as an effective regularizer for diffusion training, once its noise-driven over-dispersion is properly constrained.

TLDR: The paper introduces CRAN regularization, a technique that anchors contrastive learning to clean representations to prevent noise-driven shortcuts in diffusion transformer training, leading to consistent performance improvements.

TLDR: 本文提出了 CRAN 正则化方法，通过将对比学习锚定在干净样本的表示上，解决了扩散模型训练中噪声引起的快捷方式问题，从而显著提升了扩散 Transformer 的性能。

Disen-Forcing: Disentangling Semantic Anchoring from Motion for Autoregressive Video Diffusion

Autoregressive video diffusion models (AR-VDMs) enable real-time, long-horizon video generation but degrade rapidly once the inference horizon outruns the training horizon, due to compounding exposure bias. To stabilize long rollouts, recent methods heuristically adopt frame sink, keeping initial frames as a persistent model context, inspired by attention sinks in LLMs. We revisit this design and find that the mechanism by which initial frames stabilize long-range consistency in AR-VDMs is not an LLM-style attention sink; instead, long-range consistency is anchored through a sparse subset of attention heads, while the majority of heads predominantly capture local temporal dynamics. This observation reveals an over-conditioning pathology in existing frame-sink strategies: by universally exposing every head to the pinned initial-frame context, the model becomes prone to collapsing motion toward the low-dynamic modes of the teacher during model distillation. We propose Disen-Forcing, a distillation-time plug-in for AR-VDMs that disentangles and reinforces this head specialization through disentangled context routing and motion-disentangling regularization. On long-horizon video generation benchmarks, Disen-Forcing simultaneously improves long-range consistency and motion dynamics of base AR-VDMs, a trade-off that sink-based methods fail to balance.

TLDR: Disen-Forcing is a distillation technique for autoregressive video diffusion models that disentangles motion dynamics from semantic anchoring to prevent motion collapse, improving long-horizon consistency without sacrificing temporal dynamics.

TLDR: Disen-Forcing 是一种针对自回归视频生成模型（AR-VDM）的蒸馏插件，通过解耦语义锚定与运动动态，解决了长序列生成中的“运动崩溃”问题，在提升长时一致性的同时保留了丰富的动态细节。

OmniOPD: Stable On-Policy Distillation for Joint Audio-Video Generation

Reinforcement learning (RL) elevates joint audio-video generation, but optimizing heterogeneous, modality-specific audio and video rewards often introduces cross-modal interference. On-policy distillation (OPD) alleviates this issue by transferring the capabilities of specialized audio and video RL teachers into a single student model. However, a significant imbalance in cross-modal token counts leaves audio with sparse supervision, causing unstable distillation. We observe that naively increasing the audio loss weight fails to solve this problem since audio learning relies heavily on visual context, indicating richer visual cues are crucial to offset sparse audio signals. To address this, we propose OmniOPD, a modality-wise on-policy distillation framework designed for balanced and stable audio-video generation. First, we construct privileged visual information using asymmetric noise scheduling, allowing the audio teacher to generate more informative targets from cleaner video context. Second, we introduce grouped multi-noise sampling to expand audio supervision coverage and stabilize training. Third, we introduce timestep-wise importance weighting based on teacher-student transition divergence, preserving reliable supervision while down-weighting unreliable learning signals, to stabilize fluctuations in audio learning. Evaluated on JavisBench and VBench demonstrate OmniOPD preserves audio quality while significantly enhancing video generation, achieving effective joint distillation.

TLDR: OmniOPD is an on-policy distillation framework that resolves cross-modal instability in joint audio-video generation by using asymmetric noise scheduling, grouped multi-noise sampling, and adaptive importance weighting.

TLDR: OmniOPD 是一种用于联合音视频生成的在策略蒸馏框架，通过引入非对称噪声调度、分组多噪声采样和自适应权重调整，解决了音频与视频模态间蒸馏的不稳定性问题。

History Forcing: Distilling History-Dependent Continuations for Long-Horizon Video World Models

Few-step autoregressive video diffusion enables efficient generation, yet maintaining consistency over long rollouts remains challenging, especially when subjects or scenes reappear after going out of sight. Despite progress in fidelity and rollout robustness, existing distillation methods do not fully exploit richer historical information to constrain the student's current prediction. To address this gap, we introduce \textbf{History Forcing}, a unified on-policy distillation framework that strengthens historical supervision for few-step autoregressive video generators. The framework uses the residual between teacher predictions with and without informative historical context to decouple this supervision. Such a historical context can be obtained using earlier generated chunks with less accumulated drift or a fixed-view teacher rollout that keeps the scene visible while the student looks away. The residual is then weighted and added to the score or velocity prediction used for distillation, forming a corrected target. By aligning the student with this corrected target through distillation, History Forcing incorporates long-range historical constraints into autoregressive generation. Our method achieves state-of-the-art performance among 1.3B models, with a VBench quality score of 85.07 for 15s video generation and a VBench-Long aggregate score of 79.82 for 60s generation. For out-of-sight world modeling, evaluations further demonstrate more plausible scene evolution and more consistent states when the camera returns, with Evolution Quality improving from 2.35 to 2.70 on PlayWorld and Object Consistency from 58.43 to 62.18 on MemoBench.

TLDR: History Forcing is a novel on-policy distillation framework for video generation that improves long-horizon consistency by incorporating historical context via a residual-based correction target.

TLDR: History Forcing 是一种用于视频生成的策略蒸馏框架，通过引入基于残差的修正目标，将历史上下文信息整合到模型中，从而提升了长视频生成的时序一致性。

MoviEdit: Long-form Multi-Shot Video Editing Made Easy

Long-form multi-shot video editing requires an intended edit to remain coherent across abrupt changes in scene, viewpoint, and subject appearance, while processing substantially longer visual context than conventional single-shot editors. We introduce MoviEdit, an end-to-end framework for scalable long-form multi-shot video editing that jointly addresses the challenges of data, modeling, and evaluation. To provide cross-shot supervision data at scale, we develop automatic data-generation pipelines spanning synchronized multi-view footage, real-world multi-shot videos, and generated multi-shot content, yielding 87K paired editing videos without manual annotation. Architecturally, MoviEdit employs a cascaded bidirectional-autoregressive design: a lightweight bidirectional stage first establishes a globally coherent editing draft over a spatiotemporally compressed representation, which is then autoregressively propagated and refined over the full video. We further introduce MoviEdit-Bench, a dedicated benchmark for systematic evaluation of long-form multi-shot video editing. Extensive experiments show that MoviEdit substantially outperforms existing open-source models and performs on par with industry frontier models across text-guided and image-guided editing. Visit our project page for complete results and to access the benchmark: https://multi-shot-edit.github.io/

TLDR: MoviEdit is an end-to-end framework for long-form multi-shot video editing that uses a cascaded bidirectional-autoregressive architecture and a large-scale automatically generated dataset to ensure cross-shot visual coherence.

Latent Diffusion-GAN: Adversarial Learning in the Autoencoded Latent Space

Diffusion models are powerful generative frameworks for producing high-quality images by denoising latent variables from random noise. However, training with likelihood-based objectives, such as denoising score matching, can lead to locally over-smoothed high-frequency details, including fine textures and sharp edges, thereby limiting perceptual fidelity and structural detail. Adversarial training with GANs enhances sharpness but typically requires additional discriminator networks, increasing computational costs and destabilizing training. To this end, we propose Latent Diffusion Generative Adversarial Networks (LD-GAN), a novel framework that seamlessly integrates adversarial learning into diffusion models without modifying their original pipeline. LD-GAN leverages the pretrained variational autoencoder (VAE) in latent diffusion models as an energy-based discriminator, enabling adversarial training without extra parameters and preserving the structured latent priors learned from large datasets. We also introduce a structural consistency energy that aligns encoder and decoder feature representations, thereby enhancing perceptual quality and compatibility with the pretrained latent space. Extensive experiments demonstrate that LD-GAN significantly improves sample fidelity, perceptual sharpness, and diversity over state-of-the-art baseline methods across various generation tasks while ensuring efficient training dynamics.

TLDR: LD-GAN improves diffusion model output fidelity by repurposing the pretrained VAE as an energy-based discriminator, enabling adversarial training without additional parameters or architectural changes.

Flat Features, Detailed Images? Simple Detail Control for Diffusion Transformers

Prompt engineering is commonly used to encourage text-to-image models to generate more detailed images. However, it does not provide reliable, continuous, or disentangled control. We analyze feature tokens produced with and without detail-enhancing prompts across six diffusion transformers and observe a common pattern: these prompts tend to increase the entropy of each token's energy distribution while largely preserving its ordering. That is, flattening the distribution increases the level of detail. Motivated by this counter-intuitive finding, we introduce a simple training-free approach that continuously adjusts the level of detail according to a scalar. Specifically, it shifts the squared channel magnitudes of each token toward a higher entropy distribution with a time-aware scheduler to concentrate the intervention at intermediate sampling steps. Across extensive experiments on different types of diffusion transformers, we show that our approach provides continuous control over detail metrics while largely preserving image quality and semantic structure. It requires only a few lines of code and adds negligible inference time overhead.

TLDR: The paper introduces a training-free method to control image detail in diffusion transformers by mathematically flattening token energy distributions, allowing for continuous adjustment of detail levels with minimal computational overhead.

PixelBench: Can LLMs Understand and Generate Images Pixel by Pixel?

Large language models (LLMs) can generate visual assets through text-based formats such as SVG and CAD, but these symbolic representations describe images using high-level primitives rather than explicit raster values. We ask whether LLMs can instead generate pixel images directly through their existing vocabulary, without a learned image tokenizer or decoder. We introduce PixelBench, a benchmark for text-to-pixel (T2P) and image-to-pixel (I2P) generation across multiple resolutions, together with a complementary pixel-understanding evaluation. Models generate an RGB color palette followed by a grid of indices specifying the palette color of each output pixel. In T2P, the strongest models produce recognizable images directly from text. In I2P, the strongest models outperform palette-matched downsampling and specialized pixelization algorithms at low resolutions. Pixel-understanding performance correlates strongly with generation performance. A resolution-scaling study shows that pixel-generation performance often improves initially before declining at high resolutions. Finally, we introduce Pixel Reconstruction Tuning (PRT), which augments visual instruction tuning with caption-palette-grid trajectories. We post-train Molmo2-4B from its pretraining checkpoint by mixing general instruction data with our curated PRT data, improving performance across 16 visual-centric benchmarks, indicating that explicit pixel reconstruction can provide useful auxiliary supervision.

TLDR: PixelBench investigates if LLMs can generate and understand images at the pixel level without external tokenizers, showing that explicit pixel-based training (PRT) improves general multimodal performance.

TLDR: PixelBench 探讨了大型语言模型在无需外部图像分词器的情况下，直接进行像素级图像生成与理解的能力，并提出像素重建微调（PRT）方法以增强模型的多模态性能。

Do Visual RL Gains Scale at Test Time? A Quantile View of Post-Training

Reinforcement learning (RL) post-training improves the single-sample quality of visual generative models, but deployment often provides additional inference compute through Best-of-N sampling. Whether RL gains transfer under such test-time scaling, particularly on difficult held-out tasks, remains poorly understood in the visual domain. Our central contribution is to systematically identify and characterize a failure mode of scaling transfer in visual RL: across multiple RL methods, model backbones, and evaluations spanning compositional generation, text rendering, and preference alignment, higher single-sample reward does not necessarily imply a better quality-compute frontier. Experiments on SD3.5-M and BAGEL show that QUPO better preserves upper-tail quality and substantially improves scaling on challenging held-out prompts relative to standard RL post-training. Our results suggest that visual generative models should be evaluated and optimized not only for single-sample quality, but for how post-training reshapes the full reward distribution that inference-time scaling exploits.
